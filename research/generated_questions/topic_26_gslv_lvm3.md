## Topic 26 – Indian Launch Vehicles — GSLV and LVM3

### Questions for Topic 26

### Question 1
What does GSLV stand for?

A) General Satellite Launch Vehicle
B) Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle
C) Global Space Launch Vehicle
D) Guided Satellite Launch Vehicle

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GSLV stands for Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle. It is designed to place heavier satellites into Geostationary Transfer Orbit (GTO), from where they can be raised to Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO). This orbit is essential for communication and weather satellites that need to remain stationary over a fixed point on Earth's equator.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
What is the primary purpose of the GSLV?

A) Launching small satellites to Low Earth Orbit
B) Launching heavier communication satellites to Geostationary Transfer Orbit
C) Launching astronauts to the International Space Station
D) Launching interplanetary missions to Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The GSLV was specifically designed to launch heavier satellites (2,000–2,500 kg) to Geostationary Transfer Orbit (GTO). Communication and weather satellites need to be in GEO at 35,786 km altitude, and the GTO is the transfer orbit used to reach GEO. Before GSLV, India had to use foreign rockets (like Ariane) for its heavy communication satellites.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
Why did India need to develop its own cryogenic engine?

A) Cryogenic engines are cheaper than solid engines
B) Russia cancelled the technology transfer for cryogenic engines under US pressure (MTCR)
C) India wanted to beat China in the space race
D) The United Nations required all countries to develop their own engines

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In 1991, ISRO signed a deal with Russia's Glavkosmos for cryogenic engines and technology transfer. The United States pressured Russia to cancel the technology transfer under the Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR). Russia delivered only 7 ready-made KVD-1 engines but no manufacturing know-how. This forced ISRO to develop its own cryogenic engine from scratch — a journey that took nearly 20 years.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / MTCR History

### Question 4
What is a cryogenic engine?

A) An engine that burns solid fuel at very high temperatures
B) An engine that uses extremely cold liquid fuels — liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen
C) An engine powered by nuclear energy
D) An engine that uses solar power for propulsion

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A cryogenic engine uses Liquid Hydrogen (LH₂) at -253°C as fuel and Liquid Oxygen (LOX) at -183°C as oxidiser. The term "cryogenic" comes from "cryo" meaning extremely cold. Cryogenic engines provide the highest efficiency (specific impulse) among chemical rocket engines. The exhaust product is just water (H₂O), making them clean as well.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Rocket Propulsion Science

### Question 5
What is the name of the indigenous cryogenic engine used in GSLV Mk II?

A) Vikas
B) CE-7.5
C) CE-20
D) KVD-1

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The CE-7.5 is India's indigenous cryogenic engine used in the Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS) of GSLV Mk II. It was developed by LPSC (Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre) over nearly 20 years after Russia's technology transfer was cancelled. It produces approximately 73.5 kN of thrust and uses liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen as propellants.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 6
When was the first successful flight of GSLV with an indigenous cryogenic engine?

A) January 5, 2001
B) October 22, 2008
C) January 5, 2014
D) June 5, 2017

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** GSLV-D5, launched on January 5, 2014, was the first successful flight with India's indigenous CE-7.5 cryogenic engine. This was a landmark achievement for ISRO, marking India's entry into the exclusive club of nations with cryogenic rocket engine technology — after nearly two decades of development following the technology denial by Russia under US pressure.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 7
Which Russian cryogenic engine was used in early GSLV flights (GSLV Mk I)?

A) RD-180
B) NK-33
C) KVD-1
D) RD-170

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Early GSLV flights (GSLV Mk I variant) used the Russian KVD-1 cryogenic engine in the upper stage. Russia delivered 7 of these ready-made engines to ISRO as part of the original deal, even though the technology transfer was cancelled. The GSLV Mk I flights using KVD-1 had a mixed success record, further motivating India to develop its own engine.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 8
How many stages does the GSLV have?

A) Two stages
B) Three stages
C) Four stages (like PSLV)
D) Five stages

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GSLV has three main stages: Stage 1 consists of a solid core motor (S139) plus 4 liquid strap-on boosters (L40, each with a Vikas engine); Stage 2 (GS2) is a liquid stage with a Vikas engine; and Stage 3 is the Cryogenic Upper Stage (CUS) using either the Russian KVD-1 (Mk I) or indigenous CE-7.5 (Mk II). This is different from PSLV's four alternating stages.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9
What was LVM3 previously called?

A) PSLV Mk III
B) ASLV Mk III
C) GSLV Mk III
D) SLV Mk III

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** LVM3 (Launch Vehicle Mark-3) was previously called GSLV Mk III. It was renamed because LVM3 is a completely different vehicle from GSLV — not merely an upgrade. The design, configuration, payload capacity, and intended missions are all fundamentally different from the GSLV family. The name change was made to avoid confusion.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What is LVM3's approximate payload capacity to Geostationary Transfer Orbit (GTO)?

A) ~1,425 kg
B) ~2,500 kg
C) ~4,000 kg
D) ~10,000 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** LVM3 can carry approximately 4,000 kg to GTO — significantly more than GSLV Mk II (~2,500 kg) and PSLV (~1,425 kg). To Low Earth Orbit (LEO), LVM3 can carry about 8,000 kg. This heavy-lift capability made it possible for India to launch its heavy communication satellites domestically instead of relying on foreign rockets like Ariane 5.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What are the S200 components in LVM3?

A) The liquid core stage engines
B) Two solid strap-on boosters, each carrying 200 tonnes of propellant
C) The cryogenic upper stage
D) The payload fairing

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The S200 refers to two large solid strap-on boosters that provide the initial thrust for LVM3 at liftoff. Each S200 booster carries 200 tonnes of solid propellant (hence the name "S200"), making them the largest solid rocket boosters ever developed by ISRO. Together, the two S200 boosters produce massive thrust to lift the 640-tonne vehicle off the launch pad.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
What is the L110 component of LVM3?

A) The solid strap-on boosters
B) The liquid core stage with two Vikas engines and 110 tonnes of propellant
C) The cryogenic upper stage
D) The crew module

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** L110 is the liquid core stage of LVM3. It uses two Vikas engines and carries 110 tonnes of liquid propellant (UDMH + N₂O₄). The "L" stands for liquid and "110" indicates the propellant mass in tonnes. The L110 core stage fires along with the S200 boosters at liftoff and continues burning after the solid boosters are jettisoned.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 13
Which cryogenic engine powers the upper stage (C25) of LVM3?

A) CE-7.5
B) KVD-1
C) CE-20
D) Vikas

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The CE-20 is India's most powerful cryogenic engine, developed by LPSC for the C25 upper stage of LVM3. It produces approximately 200 kN of thrust — about 2.7 times more powerful than the CE-7.5 used in GSLV Mk II. The CE-20 uses a Gas Generator cycle and burns liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. "C25" indicates a cryogenic stage with 25 tonnes of propellant.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 14
Which centre developed India's cryogenic engines (CE-7.5 and CE-20)?

A) VSSC (Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre)
B) URSC (U R Rao Satellite Centre)
C) LPSC (Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre)
D) SAC (Space Applications Centre)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with facilities in Valiamala (near Thiruvananthapuram) and Bengaluru, developed both the CE-7.5 and CE-20 cryogenic engines. LPSC also developed the Vikas liquid engine and is working on the upcoming semi-cryogenic engines. The cryogenic engine development at LPSC took nearly 20 years of intensive effort after the technology denial.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 15
What was the first satellite launched by LVM3 into orbit?

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GSAT-19 (weighing 3,136 kg) was the first satellite launched into orbit by LVM3 on its first orbital flight, GSLV MkIII-D1, on June 5, 2017. This mission demonstrated LVM3's capability to place heavy satellites into GTO, ending India's dependence on foreign rockets for launching heavy communication satellites.

### Question 16
LVM3 launched Chandrayaan-2 in 2019. Approximately how heavy was the Chandrayaan-2 spacecraft?

A) ~1,000 kg
B) ~2,500 kg
C) ~3,850 kg
D) ~8,000 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Chandrayaan-2 spacecraft weighed approximately 3,850 kg, consisting of the orbiter, Vikram lander, and Pragyan rover. This was too heavy for PSLV or GSLV Mk II, making LVM3 essential for this lunar mission. LVM3 successfully placed Chandrayaan-2 into its initial Earth orbit on July 22, 2019 (GSLV MkIII-M1 mission).

### Question 18
Which launch vehicle sent Chandrayaan-3 to orbit?

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-3 was launched by LVM3-M4 on July 14, 2023, from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota. LVM3 was required because of the mission's heavy payload weight. ### Question 19
What is the MTCR that led to the denial of cryogenic technology to India?

A) Moon Technology Cooperation Regime
B) Missile Technology Control Regime
C) Maritime Transport and Commerce Regulation
D) Multi-national Technology Collaboration Regime

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The MTCR (Missile Technology Control Regime) is an international agreement aimed at limiting the proliferation of missiles and missile technology. The US used MTCR provisions to pressure Russia into cancelling the cryogenic engine technology transfer to India in the early 1990s, arguing that cryogenic technology could be adapted for ballistic missiles. India later joined the MTCR in 2016.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** MTCR / International Arms Control

### Question 20
How does the CE-20 engine compare to the CE-7.5 in terms of thrust?

A) CE-20 produces about the same thrust as CE-7.5
B) CE-20 produces about half the thrust of CE-7.5
C) CE-20 produces about 2.7 times more thrust than CE-7.5
D) CE-20 produces about 10 times more thrust than CE-7.5

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The CE-20 produces approximately 200 kN of thrust, which is about 2.7 times more powerful than the CE-7.5 (~73.5 kN). This increased thrust is necessary because LVM3's C25 upper stage needs to accelerate a much heavier payload than GSLV Mk II's CUS. The CE-20 uses a Gas Generator cycle, while the CE-7.5 uses staged combustion.

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 21
What is the approximate liftoff mass of LVM3?

A) ~120 tonnes
B) ~320 tonnes
C) ~414 tonnes
D) ~640 tonnes

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** LVM3 has a liftoff mass of approximately 640 tonnes, making it India's heaviest launch vehicle. This compares with ~320 tonnes for PSLV-XL and ~414 tonnes for GSLV Mk II. The large mass is driven by the massive S200 solid boosters (200 tonnes of propellant each), the L110 liquid core stage (110 tonnes), and the C25 cryogenic upper stage (25 tonnes).

### Question 22
What is the key difference between GSLV Mk I and GSLV Mk II?

GSLV Mk I used the Russian-supplied KVD-1 cryogenic engine, while GSLV Mk II uses India's indigenously developed CE-7.5 cryogenic engine. The switch to the CE-7.5 in GSLV Mk II represented India's hard-won mastery of cryogenic technology after nearly 20 years of independent development.

### Question 23
How long did it take ISRO to develop its own cryogenic engine after the technology transfer was denied?

A) About 5 years
B) About 10 years
C) About 20 years
D) About 30 years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** After Russia was forced to cancel the cryogenic technology transfer in the early 1990s, it took ISRO's LPSC nearly 20 years to develop, test, and successfully fly an indigenous cryogenic engine. The CE-7.5 first flew successfully on GSLV-D5 on January 5, 2014. This extended timeline reflects the enormous technical challenges of cryogenic propulsion — handling fuels at -253°C, developing special materials, and building reliable turbopumps.

### Question 24
Which of these Indian launch vehicles does NOT have a cryogenic stage?

A) GSLV Mk II
B) LVM3
C) PSLV
D) GSLV Mk I

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** PSLV does not have a cryogenic stage. It uses four alternating stages: solid-liquid-solid-liquid (S-L-S-L), all using storable (room temperature) propellants. In contrast, GSLV Mk I (Russian KVD-1), GSLV Mk II (CE-7.5), and LVM3 (CE-20) all use cryogenic upper stages with liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 25
What was the LVM3-X/CARE mission?

A) LVM3's first commercial launch
B) An experimental suborbital flight that tested a crew module's atmospheric re-entry
C) The mission that launched Chandrayaan-2
D) A test of the CE-20 cryogenic engine

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** LVM3-X/CARE (December 18, 2014) was an experimental suborbital flight that tested the Crew Module Atmospheric Re-entry Experiment (CARE). The S200 boosters and L110 core stage were tested with a passive (unfuelled) cryogenic upper stage. The CARE crew module successfully separated, re-entered the atmosphere, deployed parachutes, and splashed down in the Bay of Bengal — a key milestone for the Gaganyaan human spaceflight programme.

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 26
LVM3 will be used for India's first human spaceflight programme. What is this programme called?

**Explanation:** Gaganyaan (meaning "Sky Vehicle" in Sanskrit) is India's human spaceflight programme. The CARE re-entry test (2014) and TV-D1 abort test were early technology demonstrations for Gaganyaan. The programme aims to send Indian astronauts (called Vyomanauts) to Low Earth Orbit.

### Question 30
What propellant combination does the GSLV's liquid second stage (GS2) use?

A) Liquid Hydrogen + Liquid Oxygen
B) UDMH + N₂O₄ (Vikas engine)
C) Solid HTPB propellant
D) Kerosene + Liquid Oxygen

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GSLV's liquid second stage (GS2) uses the Vikas engine, which burns UDMH (Unsymmetrical Dimethylhydrazine) fuel with N₂O₄ (Nitrogen Tetroxide) oxidiser. These are hypergolic propellants — they ignite spontaneously on contact, making the engine reliable and simpler to operate. The same Vikas engine is also used in PSLV's second stage and GSLV's liquid strap-on boosters.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 31
What is the approximate payload capacity of LVM3 to Low Earth Orbit (LEO)?

A) ~2,000 kg
B) ~4,000 kg
C) ~8,000 kg
D) ~15,000 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** LVM3 can carry approximately 8,000 kg to Low Earth Orbit (LEO). This is significantly more than its GTO capacity (~4,000 kg) because reaching LEO requires less energy than reaching GTO. This LEO capacity was demonstrated during the OneWeb commercial launches, where LVM3 placed ~5,796 kg of satellites into LEO. The human-rated version for Gaganyaan will utilize this LEO capability.
