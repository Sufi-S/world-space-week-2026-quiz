## Topic 47 – Mars Exploration — International Missions

### Questions for Topic 47

### Question 1
Why is Mars considered the most important planet for exploration?

A) It's the closest planet to Earth
B) It's the most Earth-like planet with evidence of past water, making it the best candidate for finding past life
C) It's the hottest planet
D) It has the most moons

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars is considered the most important target for exploration because it is the most Earth-like planet. It once had liquid water, a thicker atmosphere, and possibly habitable conditions. Evidence of ancient river valleys, lake beds, and minerals that form in water make Mars the prime candidate for finding evidence of past microbial life beyond Earth.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 2
What was the first successful Mars flyby mission?

A) Viking 1 (USA)
B) Mars 3 (USSR)
C) Mariner 4 (USA, 1965) — it took the first close-up photographs of Mars
D) Mangalyaan (India)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mariner 4 (USA) performed the first successful Mars flyby on 15 July 1965, taking the first close-up photographs of another planet. The 22 photos it returned showed a cratered, barren surface — shocking scientists who had hoped to see signs of vegetation or canals. Mariner 4 revealed that Mars was more Moon-like than Earth-like.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 3
What were the Viking 1 and 2 missions famous for?

A) First Mars flybys
B) First successful Mars landers (1976) that searched for signs of life
C) First Mars rovers
D) First Mars orbiters

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Viking 1 and 2 (USA, 1976) were the first spacecraft to successfully land on Mars and operate for an extended period. Each had an orbiter and a lander. They conducted the first biological experiments on Mars, searching for signs of microbial life. The results were inconclusive — tantalising but not conclusive proof of life. Viking 1's lander operated until 1982.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 4
What was the first rover on Mars?

A) Curiosity
B) Opportunity
C) Sojourner — a small rover (1997, size of a microwave oven)
D) Spirit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sojourner was the first Mars rover, delivered by the Mars Pathfinder mission in July 1997. About the size of a microwave oven and weighing just 10.6 kg, it operated for about 83 days on the Martian surface. Despite its tiny size, Sojourner proved that rovers could explore Mars and paved the way for much larger successors.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 5
What record did the Opportunity rover set?

A) First Mars landing
B) It operated for over 14 years (designed for 90 days) and drove 45.16 km on Mars
C) It was the first to find water on Mars
D) It was the fastest rover on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Opportunity (MER-B) landed in January 2004, designed for a 90-day mission, but operated for an incredible 14+ years until a global dust storm in June 2018 covered its solar panels. It drove 45.16 km — a driving record on Mars. Opportunity found strong evidence that Mars once had liquid water, including mineral deposits called "blueberries" that form in water.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 6
What is special about the Curiosity rover?

A) It's the smallest Mars rover
B) It's car-sized, nuclear-powered, and found evidence of ancient habitable conditions in Gale Crater
C) It was the first to land on Mars
D) It's a helicopter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Curiosity (MSL), which landed in Gale Crater in August 2012, is a car-sized rover powered by a radioisotope thermoelectric generator (nuclear battery), allowing it to operate day and night regardless of dust storms. It discovered an ancient lake bed with conditions that could have supported microbial life, organic molecules, and active methane variations in the atmosphere.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 7
What was historic about India's Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM/Mangalyaan)?

A) It was the first Mars mission ever
B) India became the first Asian nation to reach Mars and succeeded on its very first attempt (2014)
C) It landed a rover on Mars
D) It discovered life on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India's MOM/Mangalyaan (launched 5 November 2013, arrived 24 September 2014) made India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit and the first space agency in the world to succeed on its maiden Mars attempt. The mission cost approximately $73 million — less than the budget of the Hollywood movie "Gravity" — demonstrating ISRO's cost-effectiveness.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What is the "Mars Curse"?

A) A superstition about Mars being bad luck
B) The fact that roughly half of all Mars missions have failed — Mars is notoriously difficult to reach
C) A theory about Mars being cursed by aliens
D) A disease astronauts get near Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The "Mars Curse" refers to the very high failure rate of Mars missions — roughly half have failed. Common causes include trajectory errors, entry/descent/landing failures, and communication loss. Mars is particularly challenging because of the 3-24 minute communication delay, thin atmosphere (too thin for parachutes alone, too thick to ignore), and the long journey (6-9 months).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 9
What did China's Tianwen-1 mission accomplish in 2021?

A) First Chinese Mars flyby only
B) Successfully deployed an orbiter, lander, AND rover (Zhurong) on its first Mars mission
C) Returned samples from Mars
D) First landing on Phobos

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Tianwen-1 (China, 2021) was remarkable because it included an orbiter, a lander, and the Zhurong rover — all in one mission. China achieved all three components successfully on its very first Mars mission, a feat no other country had accomplished on a maiden attempt. Zhurong explored the Utopia Planitia region and found evidence of past water activity.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 10
What unique achievement did the Ingenuity helicopter accomplish on Mars?

A) First to photograph Mars from the surface
B) First powered, controlled flight on another planet — completing 72 flights
C) First to drill into Mars soil
D) First to orbit Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Ingenuity, which arrived on Mars with Perseverance in 2021, was the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet. Originally planned for just 5 demonstration flights, it ended up making an astounding 72 flights before its mission ended in January 2024. Flying on Mars is extraordinarily challenging because the atmosphere is only about 1% as dense as Earth's.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 11
What is the Perseverance rover's main mission?

A) Studying Martian weather
B) Searching for signs of ancient microbial life in Jezero Crater and collecting samples for future return to Earth
C) Testing rocket engines on Mars
D) Building a habitat for astronauts

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Perseverance, which landed in Jezero Crater (a former lake bed) in February 2021, is searching for signs of ancient microbial life and collecting rock samples in sealed tubes for a future Mars Sample Return mission. Jezero Crater was chosen because it has a preserved river delta — exactly the kind of place where evidence of past life might be found.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 12
How long does it take to travel from Earth to Mars?

A) About 3 days (like the Moon trip)
B) About 6 to 9 months
C) About 5 years
D) About 1 month

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A trip to Mars typically takes about 6 to 9 months, depending on the alignment of Earth and Mars and the trajectory used. Launch windows to Mars occur only every 26 months, when Earth and Mars are optimally aligned. The distance between Earth and Mars varies from 55 million km at closest approach to 400 million km when on opposite sides of the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 13
What did NASA's InSight mission study?

A) Martian atmosphere
B) The interior of Mars — it was the first mission to study Mars's deep interior, detecting "marsquakes"
C) Martian moons
D) Surface water on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** InSight (Interior Exploration using Seismic Investigations, Geodesy and Heat Transport) landed in 2018 and was the first mission dedicated to studying Mars's deep interior. It deployed a seismometer that detected over 1,300 "marsquakes," revealing that Mars is still geologically active. InSight also measured the thickness of Mars's crust, mantle, and core for the first time.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 14
What are the names of Mars's two moons?

A) Titan and Europa
B) Phobos and Deimos
C) Luna and Sol
D) Ganymede and Callisto

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons: Phobos ("Fear") and Deimos ("Dread"), named after the companions of the Greek god of war, Ares (Mars in Roman mythology). Phobos is the larger one (about 22 km across) and orbits so close to Mars that it's gradually spiralling inward and will eventually either crash into Mars or break apart into a ring.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy

### Question 15
Which Mars rover was named after the astronomer who discovered Mars's atmosphere?

A) Curiosity
B) No rover has this distinction — this is a trick question

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Sojourner was named after Sojourner Truth, the 19th-century American civil rights activist — not after an astronomer. ### Question 16
What is the Mars Sample Return mission?

A) A completed mission that already brought back Mars rocks
B) A planned NASA-ESA mission to return Perseverance's collected rock samples from Mars to Earth
C) A mission to send Earth soil to Mars
D) A Chinese Mars landing mission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars Sample Return is a planned NASA-ESA joint mission to retrieve the sealed sample tubes that Perseverance has been collecting and depositing on Mars's surface in Jezero Crater. If successful, these would be the first Mars samples returned to Earth, allowing detailed laboratory analysis that no rover can perform. The mission concept involves a lander, rover, rocket, and Earth-return orbiter.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / ESA

### Question 17
What makes Mars's atmosphere challenging for landing?

A) It has no atmosphere at all
B) It's too thin for parachutes alone but thick enough to generate extreme heating during entry
C) It's too thick — like Venus
D) It's toxic to spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars's atmosphere is about 1% as dense as Earth's, creating a unique landing challenge. It's thick enough to cause extreme heating during entry (requiring a heat shield), but too thin for parachutes to slow a spacecraft sufficiently on their own. This requires complex landing systems combining heat shields, parachutes, retrorockets, and sometimes sky cranes (as used by Curiosity and Perseverance).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 18
Which orbiter was the first to confirm water ice at Mars's poles?

A) Mars Odyssey (USA, 2001)
B) Mariner 4 (1965)
C) MOM (India, 2014)
D) Mangalyaan (India, 2014)

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** NASA's Mars Odyssey (2001) was the first to find evidence of large amounts of subsurface water ice on Mars, particularly at the polar regions. Still operational after over 20 years, it is the longest-serving spacecraft in Mars orbit. ESA's Mars Express (2003) later confirmed water ice at the poles and also remains operational.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 19
What was Mars 3's distinction?

A) First Mars flyby
B) First spacecraft to land on Mars (USSR, 1971), though it transmitted for only about 20 seconds
C) First Mars rover
D) First to orbit Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars 3 (USSR, December 1971) achieved the first-ever landing on Mars, but the lander transmitted data for only about 20 seconds before going silent — likely destroyed by a massive dust storm. The first fully successful Mars landers were Viking 1 and 2 (USA, 1976), which operated for years on the surface.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Space History

### Question 20
How does Mars's day compare to Earth's?

A) Mars's day is about 12 hours
B) Mars's day (a "sol") is about 24 hours 37 minutes — very close to Earth's
C) Mars's day is about 48 hours
D) Mars's day is about 10 hours like Jupiter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A Martian day (called a "sol") is about 24 hours and 37 minutes — remarkably close to Earth's 24-hour day. This is one of the similarities that makes Mars relatively hospitable compared to other planets. However, a Martian year is about 687 Earth days (nearly twice as long as Earth's year). Mars mission teams often work on "Mars time" to synchronise with their rovers.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA
