## Topic 29 – Indian Communication Satellites (INSAT/GSAT/CMS)

### Questions for Topic 29

### Question 1
What does INSAT stand for?

A) Indian Navigation Satellite System
B) Indian National Satellite System
C) International Navigation and Satellite Technology
D) Indian Natural Satellite Tracker

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** INSAT stands for Indian National Satellite System. It is one of the largest domestic communication satellite systems in the Asia-Pacific region and serves telecommunications, television broadcasting, weather monitoring, and search-and-rescue operations.

What was India's first experimental communication satellite?

B) Bhaskara

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** APPLE (Ariane Passenger Payload Experiment) was India's first experimental communication satellite. It was launched on June 19, 1981, and successfully demonstrated satellite-based TV broadcasting and communication for India, laying the groundwork for the INSAT programme.

### Question 6
At what altitude is a geostationary orbit (GEO) located above the Earth's equator?

A) 2,000 km
B) 20,200 km
C) 35,786 km
D) 42,164 km

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A geostationary orbit is at an altitude of approximately 35,786 km above the Earth's equator. At this specific altitude, a satellite's orbital period exactly matches the Earth's rotation period (about 24 hours), making the satellite appear stationary from the ground. This is ideal for communication satellites because ground antennas can point at a fixed spot without needing tracking mechanisms.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics

### Question 7
Why are communication satellites typically placed in geostationary orbit?

A) Because it is closest to Earth and reduces signal delay
B) Because the satellite appears stationary over one point, so ground antennas need no tracking
C) Because geostationary orbit has zero gravity, protecting satellite electronics
D) Because it is above the Van Allen radiation belts

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In geostationary orbit (35,786 km), a satellite orbits at the same rate the Earth rotates, so it appears to hover over the same point on the equator. This means ground-based dish antennas can be permanently aimed at the satellite without complex tracking systems, making it ideal for continuous communication, TV broadcasting, and weather monitoring.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Satellite Communication Principles

### Question 8

### Question 9
Which satellite in the INSAT-1 series was India's first successful operational multipurpose satellite?

A) INSAT-1A
B) INSAT-1B
C) INSAT-1C
D) INSAT-1D

**Answer:** B

### Question 17
What is a transponder in the context of communication satellites?

A) A device that generates electrical power from solar energy
B) A device that receives, amplifies, and retransmits signals
C) A thruster used to maintain the satellite's orbit
D) An antenna used only for receiving signals from Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A transponder is a key component of a communication satellite that receives signals from Earth (uplink), amplifies them, changes their frequency, and retransmits them back to Earth (downlink). The number and type of transponders on a satellite determine its communication capacity. More transponders mean the satellite can handle more channels and services.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Satellite Communication Technology

### Question 32
A student wants to set up a satellite TV dish at home. Why does the dish antenna point at one fixed direction in the sky and never need to be moved?

A) Because the TV satellite is in low-Earth orbit and moves very slowly
B) Because the satellite is in geostationary orbit at 35,786 km, appearing stationary from the ground
C) Because the satellite is actually moving but the dish has an automatic tracking system
D) Because the satellite is in a polar orbit that always passes over the same point

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Home satellite TV dishes point at a fixed spot because the broadcasting satellite is in geostationary orbit at 35,786 km altitude. At this height, the satellite orbits Earth once every 24 hours — the same time Earth takes to rotate — so it appears to hover motionless over one point on the equator. This is why once a dish is installed and aimed, it never needs to be moved.
