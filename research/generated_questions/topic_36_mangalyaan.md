## Topic 36 – Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan)

### Questions for Topic 36

### Question 1
What is the popular name for India's Mars Orbiter Mission?

A) Chandrayaan
B) Mangalyaan
C) Aditya
D) Gaganyaan

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India's Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM) is popularly known as Mangalyaan, which means "Mars craft" in Hindi/Sanskrit. "Mangal" refers to Mars (also the Hindi word for the planet), and "Yaan" means vehicle or craft. The mission made India the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
When was Mangalyaan launched?

A) 22 October 2008
B) 5 November 2013
C) 24 September 2014
D) 14 July 2023

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan was launched on 5 November 2013 at 2:38 PM IST from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota, aboard PSLV-C25. After spending about 25 days in Earth orbit performing orbit-raising manoeuvres, it departed for Mars on 1 December 2013 and entered Mars orbit on 24 September 2014.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
Which launch vehicle was used for Mangalyaan?

A) GSLV Mk II
B) LVM3
C) PSLV-C25 (XL variant)
D) SSLV

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan was launched by PSLV-C25 using the XL variant with larger strap-on boosters. Since PSLV alone could not send the spacecraft directly to Mars, ISRO used a clever orbit-raising strategy — gradually increasing Earth orbit altitude before performing the trans-Mars injection burn.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
When did Mangalyaan enter orbit around Mars?

A) 5 November 2013
B) 1 December 2013
C) 24 September 2014
D) 2 October 2022

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan successfully entered Mars orbit on 24 September 2014, approximately 10 months after its launch. The orbit insertion was critical — if the engine failed to fire or fired incorrectly, the spacecraft would have flown past Mars and been lost. ISRO's engineers executed the manoeuvre flawlessly on the first attempt.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
India became the ___ space entity to successfully reach Mars.

A) First
B) Second
C) Third
D) Fourth

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** India became the fourth space entity to reach Mars orbit, after NASA (USA), Roscosmos/Soviet space programme (Russia), and ESA (European Space Agency). However, India holds two unique distinctions: it was the first Asian country to reach Mars and the first country to succeed on its very first attempt at a Mars mission.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What historic achievement did India accomplish with Mangalyaan regarding first attempts at Mars?

A) India was the first to land on Mars
B) India was the first country to succeed in reaching Mars on its maiden attempt
C) India was the first to find life on Mars
D) India was the first to orbit both Mars and the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India was the first country in the world to successfully reach Mars orbit on its very first attempt. The USA, Soviet Union, and ESA all experienced failures in their initial Mars missions before eventually succeeding. This remarkable achievement demonstrated ISRO's exceptional engineering precision and mission planning capabilities.

### Question 7
What was the approximate cost of the Mangalyaan mission?

A) About ₹100 crore (~$15 million)
B) About ₹450 crore (~$73 million)
C) About ₹2,000 crore (~$300 million)
D) About ₹10,000 crore (~$1.5 billion)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan cost approximately ₹450 crore (about US$73 million), making it the least expensive Mars mission in history at the time. For comparison, it cost less than the Hollywood movie Gravity ($100 million production budget) and less than the cost per kilometre of many Indian highways. This extraordinary frugality became a symbol of ISRO's cost-effective approach.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
Was Mangalyaan an orbiter or a lander mission?

A) It was a lander mission that touched down on Mars
B) It was an orbiter that circled Mars without landing
C) It was a flyby mission that passed Mars without stopping
D) It was a rover mission on the Martian surface

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan was purely an orbiter mission — it entered and maintained orbit around Mars to study the planet from above. It never landed on the Martian surface. This is a common misconception. MOM was primarily a technology demonstrator to prove India's capability for interplanetary travel, with secondary scientific objectives.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9
How long was Mangalyaan designed to operate?

A) 1 month
B) 6 months
C) 2 years
D) 8 years

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan was designed for a 6-month mission life at Mars (until about March 2015). However, it far exceeded expectations and continued operating for approximately 8 years until communication was lost in October 2022 when onboard fuel was exhausted and eclipse events drained the battery completely.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
How many scientific instruments did Mangalyaan carry?

A) 3
B) 5
C) 8
D) 11

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan carried 5 scientific instruments: Mars Colour Camera (MCC), Thermal Infrared Imaging Spectrometer (TIS), Methane Sensor for Mars (MSM), Mars Exospheric Neutral Composition Analyser (MENCA), and Lyman Alpha Photometer (LAP). These covered imaging, atmospheric science, and the search for methane.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What did the Mars Colour Camera (MCC) on Mangalyaan capture?

A) Only infrared images of Mars
B) Colour images of Mars' surface and its moons Phobos and Deimos
C) Images of Earth from Mars orbit
D) Radar maps of the Martian subsurface

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Mars Colour Camera captured colour images of the Martian surface features, including a famous full-disc image of Mars showing its atmospheric features. MCC also captured images of Mars' two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. These images were widely shared and became iconic representations of India's Mars achievement.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
What was the Methane Sensor for Mars (MSM) looking for, and why is methane important?

A) Methane indicates the presence of water
B) Methane is a potential biosignature that could indicate biological or geological activity
C) Methane is needed for rocket fuel production on Mars
D) Methane indicates the presence of oxygen

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The MSM was designed to detect methane in Mars' atmosphere because methane can be a biosignature — it can be produced by living organisms (methanogenic bacteria) or by geological processes (volcanic activity, water-rock reactions). On Earth, most atmospheric methane comes from biological sources. However, MSM did not make a confirmed detection, only setting an upper limit on Mars' methane concentration.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Mars Science

### Question 13
Why did ISRO use orbit-raising manoeuvres around Earth before sending Mangalyaan to Mars?

A) To test the spacecraft's systems
B) Because PSLV lacked the power to send the spacecraft directly to Mars in a single burn
C) To wait for the correct launch window
D) To collect data about Earth first

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** PSLV is a capable launch vehicle but does not have enough upper-stage thrust to send a 1,337 kg spacecraft directly onto a Mars transfer trajectory. ISRO's solution was to first place MOM in Earth orbit, then perform a series of orbit-raising burns over about 25 days to gradually increase velocity. The final trans-Mars injection burn on 1 December 2013 sent MOM on its way to Mars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 14
What was MENCA and what did it study?

A) A camera for photographing Mars' moons
B) Mars Exospheric Neutral Composition Analyser — studied the composition of Mars' upper atmosphere
C) A magnetometer for measuring Mars' magnetic field
D) A seismometer for detecting marsquakes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** MENCA (Mars Exospheric Neutral Composition Analyser) studied the composition of Mars' exosphere — the outermost, extremely thin layer of its atmosphere. MENCA detected the presence of argon and other gases in the Martian exosphere. Understanding the exosphere helps scientists understand how Mars has been losing its atmosphere over billions of years.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 16
Which other Asian countries attempted Mars missions before India but did not succeed?

A) China and South Korea
B) Japan (Nozomi) and China (Yinghuo-1)
C) Japan and Israel
D) China and UAE

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Japan's Nozomi mission (launched 1998) failed to enter Mars orbit in 2003 due to electrical problems, and China's Yinghuo-1 (launched 2011 with Russia's Phobos-Grunt) was lost when the Russian spacecraft failed to leave Earth orbit. India's success in 2014 made it the first Asian country to reach Mars. China later succeeded with Tianwen-1 in 2021.

### Question 17
In how many months was the Mangalyaan spacecraft designed and built?

A) 6 months
B) 18 months
C) 36 months
D) 60 months

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan was designed and built in a record 18 months after the mission was approved by PM Manmohan Singh on 3 August 2012. This remarkably short development timeline was driven by the need to catch a specific Mars launch window (which occurs approximately every 26 months when Earth and Mars are favourably aligned). ISRO's engineers worked at an extraordinary pace.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 18
What is a trans-Mars injection (TMI)?

A) The launch of the rocket from Earth's surface
B) The propulsive manoeuvre that sends a spacecraft from Earth orbit onto a trajectory to Mars
C) The braking manoeuvre to enter Mars orbit
D) The landing sequence on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Trans-Mars Injection (TMI) is the critical engine burn that accelerates a spacecraft from Earth orbit to the velocity needed to follow a transfer orbit to Mars. For Mangalyaan, the TMI burn was performed on 1 December 2013 after the series of orbit-raising manoeuvres. This burn essentially "slings" the spacecraft away from Earth toward Mars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics / ISRO

### Question 19

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan had a total mass of 1,337 kg at launch (with a dry mass of approximately 500 kg — the rest being fuel). Despite being relatively lightweight compared to other Mars missions, it successfully carried five scientific instruments and operated for 8 years, demonstrating that effective space science doesn't require massive, expensive spacecraft.

### Question 21
What type of Mars orbit did Mangalyaan use?

A) Circular orbit at 500 km
B) Highly elliptical orbit (ranging from ~262 km to ~80,000 km)
C) Sun-synchronous orbit
D) Geostationary orbit around Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mangalyaan used a highly elliptical orbit around Mars, initially ranging from about 421.7 km (closest approach) to about 76,993.6 km (farthest point), later settling to approximately 262 km × 80,000 km. This elliptical orbit, with a period of about 72 hours, allowed the spacecraft to study Mars from different distances and perspectives while conserving fuel.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 22
What did the Lyman Alpha Photometer (LAP) on Mangalyaan measure?

A) Surface temperature of Mars
B) Deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio related to water loss from Mars
C) Magnetic field strength of Mars
D) Dust particle density in Mars' atmosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** LAP measured the ratio of deuterium (heavy hydrogen) to hydrogen in Mars' upper atmosphere. This ratio is important because it reveals how much water Mars has lost over billions of years — deuterium is heavier and escapes less easily, so a high D/H ratio indicates significant water loss. Understanding this helps reconstruct Mars' watery past.

**Source:** ISRO
