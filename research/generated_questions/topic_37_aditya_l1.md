## Topic 37 – Aditya-L1 — India's First Solar Observatory

### Questions for Topic 37

### Question 1
What does "Aditya" mean in Sanskrit?

A) Moon
B) Star
C) Sun
D) Sky

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** "Aditya" means "Sun" in Sanskrit. The mission is named Aditya-L1 because it is India's first solar observatory mission, and it is positioned at the L1 Lagrange point. The name reflects the mission's purpose — to study our nearest star, the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
Where is Aditya-L1 positioned?

A) In orbit around the Sun's surface
B) At the Sun-Earth L1 Lagrange point, about 1.5 million km from Earth
C) In low Earth orbit at 400 km altitude
D) On the Moon, observing the Sun from the lunar surface

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Aditya-L1 is positioned at the Sun-Earth L1 Lagrange point, approximately 1.5 million km from Earth. This is only about 1% of the total Earth-Sun distance (150 million km). A common misconception is that the spacecraft went to the Sun — it is actually much closer to Earth than to the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
When was Aditya-L1 launched?

A) 14 July 2023
B) 23 August 2023
C) 2 September 2023
D) 6 January 2024

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Aditya-L1 was launched on 2 September 2023 from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota, aboard PSLV-C57. It arrived at the L1 point and was inserted into its halo orbit on 6 January 2024, approximately 127 days after launch. Notably, it launched just 10 days after Chandrayaan-3's historic Moon landing.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
Which launch vehicle carried Aditya-L1?

A) LVM3
B) GSLV Mk II
C) PSLV-C57 (XL variant)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Aditya-L1 was launched by PSLV-C57 using the XL variant. PSLV's reliability and the relatively moderate mass of the spacecraft (~1,475 kg) made it suitable for this mission. The XL variant provided enough performance to place the spacecraft on its trajectory toward the L1 point.

**Difficulty:** Easy

### Question 5
What is special about the L1 Lagrange point for a solar observatory?

A) It is the closest point to the Sun
B) It has no gravity, making observations easier
C) A spacecraft there can observe the Sun continuously without Earth blocking the view
D) It is the warmest point in space

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The L1 Lagrange point is a gravitational equilibrium point between Earth and the Sun. A spacecraft at L1 can observe the Sun continuously because Earth never passes between the spacecraft and the Sun — there are no eclipses. This uninterrupted view is ideal for monitoring solar activity like flares and coronal mass ejections that need constant surveillance.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Orbital Mechanics

### Question 6
How many scientific instruments does Aditya-L1 carry?

A) 3
B) 5
C) 7
D) 11

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Aditya-L1 carries 7 scientific payloads: 4 remote sensing instruments (VELC, SUIT, SoLEXS, HEL1OS) that observe the Sun from a distance, and 3 in-situ instruments (ASPEX, PAPA, MAG) that directly measure the solar wind and magnetic field at the L1 point.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
What is VELC, and why is it the primary instrument?

A) A camera for photographing planets
B) Visible Emission Line Coronagraph — studies the Sun's corona, CMEs, and magnetic field
C) A velocity measurement device for the spacecraft
D) A communication relay antenna

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** VELC (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph) is Aditya-L1's primary instrument, built by the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA) in Bengaluru. A coronagraph blocks the Sun's bright disk to observe the much fainter corona (outer atmosphere). VELC studies coronal mass ejections, magnetic field dynamics, and the coronal heating problem.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / IIA

### Question 8
What is a Lagrange point?

A) The point where a rocket's fuel runs out
B) A gravitational equilibrium point where the gravitational forces of two bodies allow a small object to remain relatively stationary
C) The point where a planet's atmosphere ends
D) The centre of the solar system

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Lagrange points are positions in the gravitational field of two large bodies (like Earth and the Sun) where a small object can maintain a relatively stable position. There are 5 Lagrange points (L1 to L5) in any two-body system. L1 is between Earth and Sun, ideal for solar observation. L2 (behind Earth from the Sun) is where the James Webb Space Telescope is positioned.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics

### Question 9
How long did it take Aditya-L1 to travel from Earth to the L1 point?

A) About 3 days
B) About 40 days
C) About 127 days
D) About 300 days

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Aditya-L1 took approximately 127 days to travel from Earth to the L1 Lagrange point. Launched on 2 September 2023, it was inserted into its halo orbit around L1 on 6 January 2024. The spacecraft first orbited Earth with progressively higher orbits before a trans-L1 injection sent it toward the Lagrange point.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What achievement did India accomplish by placing Aditya-L1 at L1?

A) India became the first country to study the Sun from space
B) India became the first Asian country to place a spacecraft at the L1 point
C) India became the first country to reach the L1 point
D) India became the first country to have a solar observatory

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India became the first Asian country to place a spacecraft at the Sun-Earth L1 Lagrange point. Other space agencies had previously placed missions at L1, notably NASA and ESA's SOHO (Solar and Heliospheric Observatory, launched 1995). India's achievement demonstrated its growing capability for complex deep-space missions beyond Earth and the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What is a coronal mass ejection (CME)?

A) A type of asteroid impact on the Sun
B) A massive burst of solar wind, plasma, and magnetic field ejected from the Sun's corona
C) The Sun's normal light emission
D) A process that happens inside the Sun's core

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A CME is a massive eruption of plasma (ionised gas) and magnetic field from the Sun's corona into space. CMEs can travel at speeds of 250-3,000 km/s and, if directed at Earth, can cause geomagnetic storms that damage satellites, disrupt GPS and radio communications, endanger astronauts, and even affect power grids. Monitoring CMEs is a key objective of Aditya-L1.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / ISRO

### Question 12
What is the "coronal heating problem"?

A) The Sun is getting hotter every year
B) The mystery of why the corona is millions of degrees hotter than the Sun's surface
C) The problem of spacecraft overheating near the Sun
D) Difficulty in measuring the Sun's temperature

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The coronal heating problem is one of the great unsolved mysteries in solar physics. The Sun's surface (photosphere) is about 5,500°C, but the corona — the outermost atmosphere above the surface — reaches temperatures of 1-3 million degrees Celsius. This seems to defy common sense (you'd expect temperature to decrease with distance from the heat source). Aditya-L1's instruments aim to help solve this puzzle.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 13
What does SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope) on Aditya-L1 do?

A) Provides a protective suit for astronauts
B) Images the Sun's photosphere and chromosphere in ultraviolet light
C) Measures the Sun's rotation speed
D) Detects solar neutrinos

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope), built by IUCAA (Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics) in Pune, captures images of the Sun in near-ultraviolet wavelengths. It studies the solar photosphere (visible surface) and chromosphere (layer above the photosphere), helping scientists understand solar activity and energy transfer in these layers.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / IUCAA

### Question 14
What do the in-situ instruments (ASPEX, PAPA, MAG) on Aditya-L1 measure?

A) Images of the Sun's surface
B) Solar wind properties, plasma composition, and interplanetary magnetic field at the L1 point
C) The spacecraft's own health and temperature
D) Signals from other spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The three in-situ instruments directly measure the space environment at L1: ASPEX measures solar wind particle properties (protons, alpha particles), PAPA analyses solar wind plasma composition, and MAG measures the interplanetary magnetic field. "In-situ" means measuring at the actual location, as opposed to "remote sensing" which observes from a distance.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 15
Why is space weather monitoring important for modern civilisation?

A) It isn't — space weather has no effect on Earth
B) Solar activity can damage satellites, disrupt communications, affect GPS, endanger astronauts, and damage power grids
C) Space weather only affects the Moon
D) It only matters for astronomy research

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Space weather — conditions caused by solar activity like flares and CMEs — directly affects modern technology. Intense solar storms can damage satellite electronics, disrupt radio communications and GPS signals, endanger astronauts with radiation, induce currents in power grids causing blackouts, and affect aviation routes. As society becomes more technology-dependent, space weather monitoring becomes increasingly critical.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Space Weather Science

### Question 16
What is a halo orbit?

A) An orbit that goes around the Sun's equator
B) A 3D orbit around a Lagrange point (the spacecraft doesn't sit at the exact point but orbits around it)
C) An orbit that creates a ring visible from Earth
D) An orbit that follows Earth's shadow

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A halo orbit is a three-dimensional orbit around a Lagrange point. Aditya-L1 doesn't sit stationary at the exact L1 point — instead, it traces a looping path around L1. This is necessary because L1 is unstable (a spacecraft there would drift away), so the halo orbit uses periodic small corrections to maintain position while providing a stable observing platform.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics

### Question 17
How far is the L1 point from Earth compared to the total Earth-Sun distance?

A) About 50% of the way (halfway)
B) About 25% of the way
C) About 10% of the way
D) About 1% of the way

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The L1 point is approximately 1.5 million km from Earth, while the total Earth-Sun distance is about 150 million km. So L1 is only about 1% of the way from Earth to the Sun. This is a common misconception — many people assume L1 is somewhere near the middle. In reality, Aditya-L1 is much closer to Earth than to the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics

### Question 18
What was Aditya-L1 originally conceived as?

A) A Mars orbiter mission
B) A small coronagraph satellite in low Earth orbit called Aditya-1

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The mission was originally conceived as "Aditya-1" — a small satellite carrying only a coronagraph in low Earth orbit. It was later significantly expanded to become "Aditya-L1" with 7 instruments and repositioned to the L1 Lagrange point, which provides a much better vantage point for continuous solar observation without Earth's interference.

### Question 19
Which of these is NOT one of Aditya-L1's instruments?

A) VELC (Visible Emission Line Coronagraph)
B) SUIT (Solar Ultraviolet Imaging Telescope)
C) M³ (Moon Mineralogy Mapper)
D) MAG (Magnetometer)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** M³ (Moon Mineralogy Mapper) was a NASA instrument on Chandrayaan-1 that discovered water on the Moon — it has nothing to do with the Aditya-L1 solar mission. VELC, SUIT, and MAG are all genuine instruments on Aditya-L1. The other instruments are SoLEXS, HEL1OS, ASPEX, and PAPA.

**Difficulty:** Easy

### Question 20
What is the difference between L1 and L2 Lagrange points?

A) They are the same point
B) L1 is between Earth and Sun; L2 is on Earth's far side from the Sun
C) L1 is near Mars; L2 is near Jupiter
D) L1 is above the North Pole; L2 is above the South Pole

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** L1 is located between Earth and the Sun (ideal for solar observation), while L2 is on the opposite side of Earth from the Sun (ideal for observing deep space away from the Sun's glare). NASA's James Webb Space Telescope is at L2, while ISRO's Aditya-L1 and NASA/ESA's SOHO are at L1. Both are about 1.5 million km from Earth but in opposite directions.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics

### Question 22
What is a solar flare?

A) A gradual dimming of the Sun over years
B) A sudden flash of increased brightness and energy release on the Sun's surface
C) A type of asteroid that passes close to the Sun
D) The Sun's normal radiation

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A solar flare is a sudden, intense flash of electromagnetic radiation from the Sun's surface, typically associated with active regions near sunspots. Flares release enormous energy across the electromagnetic spectrum — from radio waves to gamma rays — in minutes to hours. They can affect Earth's ionosphere, disrupting radio communications and GPS accuracy.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 23
How does studying the Sun help protect satellites and astronauts?

A) It doesn't — the Sun has no effect on them
B) Understanding solar activity enables space weather prediction, allowing warnings before dangerous solar storms reach Earth
C) Studying the Sun makes satellites solar-powered
D) It helps find safer orbits near the Sun

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** By monitoring solar flares and CMEs, scientists can predict space weather events and issue warnings. This allows satellite operators to put satellites into safe mode, astronauts to take shelter in shielded areas, airlines to reroute polar flights, and power grid operators to prepare for geomagnetically induced currents. Aditya-L1's position at L1 gives approximately 1 hour of advance warning for solar wind events.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Space Weather
