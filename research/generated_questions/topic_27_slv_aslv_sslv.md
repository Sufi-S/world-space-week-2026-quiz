## Topic 27 – Indian Launch Vehicles — SLV, ASLV, SSLV and Sounding Rockets

### Questions for Topic 27

### Question 1
What is a sounding rocket?

A) A rocket that creates a sonic boom to study sound waves
B) A sub-orbital rocket designed to carry scientific instruments to the upper atmosphere
C) A rocket that is launched underwater to study ocean acoustics
D) A type of satellite that monitors sound pollution

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A sounding rocket is a sub-orbital rocket designed to carry scientific instruments to the upper atmosphere (30–200+ km altitude). It does NOT reach orbit — it goes up, takes measurements of atmospheric and space conditions, and comes back down. Sounding rockets are simpler and cheaper than orbital launch vehicles, making them ideal for scientific research.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Rocket Science

### Question 2
Why are they called "sounding" rockets?

A) Because they make a loud sound when launched
B) From the nautical term "to sound," meaning to take measurements
C) Because they carry sound-recording equipment
D) Because they test the sound barrier

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The word "sounding" in sounding rockets comes from the old nautical term "to sound," which means to take measurements — originally referring to measuring ocean depth. Similarly, sounding rockets "sound" or measure the upper atmosphere. They carry scientific instruments to study atmospheric conditions, cosmic rays, and other phenomena at high altitudes.

**Source:** Atmospheric Science / Etymology

### Question 3
When and where was the first sounding rocket launched in India?

A) January 26, 1950, from Sriharikota
B) November 21, 1963, from Thumba
C) August 15, 1969, from Bengaluru
D) July 18, 1980, from Sriharikota

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The first sounding rocket in India — a Nike-Apache — was launched from the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) on November 21, 1963. This event is considered the birth of India's space programme. The launch was overseen by Vikram Sarabhai's INCOSPAR. Famously, rocket parts were transported on a bicycle due to limited infrastructure at the time.

### Question 4
What was special about the Nike-Apache rocket launched from Thumba in 1963?

A) It was fully designed and built in India
B) It was an American-made rocket — India's first indigenous rocket came later
C) It was the world's first sounding rocket ever launched
D) It placed India's first satellite into orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Nike-Apache launched from Thumba on November 21, 1963, was an American-made sounding rocket. India had not yet developed its own rockets. The first Indian-made sounding rocket, the Rohini-75 (RH-75), came four years later in 1967. India's approach was to start with foreign rockets, learn from them, and then build indigenous capability step by step.

### Question 5
What was India's first indigenously made sounding rocket?

A) Nike-Apache
B) SLV-3
C) Rohini-75 (RH-75)
D) ASLV

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Rohini-75 (RH-75) was India's first indigenously designed and built sounding rocket, launched in 1967. Its name comes from "Rohini," the Indian name for the star Aldebaran. The "75" refers to its 75 mm diameter. The Rohini series grew into a family of progressively larger sounding rockets — RH-200, RH-300, and RH-560 — still used today.

### Question 7
What does SLV-3 stand for, and what was its significance?

A) Space Launch Vehicle-3 — India's first communication satellite
B) Satellite Launch Vehicle-3 — India's first indigenous orbital rocket
C) Standard Launch Vehicle-3 — India's first military missile
D) Scientific Launch Vehicle-3 — India's first weather rocket

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLV-3 stands for Satellite Launch Vehicle-3. It was India's first indigenously built satellite launch vehicle, designed to place a 40 kg satellite into Low Earth Orbit (~400 km). Its successful launch in 1980 made India only the sixth nation in the world to independently launch a satellite into orbit, marking a major milestone in India's space programme.

### Question 9
What type of propulsion did SLV-3 use?

A) Liquid propellant in all stages
B) Four-stage, all-solid propellant
C) Hybrid propulsion (solid + liquid)
D) Nuclear thermal propulsion

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLV-3 was a four-stage, all-solid-propellant rocket. It was about 22 metres tall with a mass of approximately 17 tonnes. Solid propellant was chosen because it is simpler to develop and handle — appropriate for a nation building its first orbital rocket. The mastery of solid rocket technology through SLV-3 provided the foundation for all subsequent Indian launch vehicles.

### Question 10

### Question 11

### Question 12
After the SLV-3 success, India became the ___ nation to independently launch a satellite.

A) 3rd
B) 4th
C) 6th

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** With the successful launch of SLV-3 E2 on July 18, 1980, India became the sixth nation to independently launch a satellite into orbit. The order was: USSR (1957), USA (1958), France (1965), Japan (1970), China (1970), and India (1980). This achievement demonstrated India's ability to design, build, and operate a complete launch vehicle system on its own.

### Question 13
What leadership lesson is associated with Satish Dhawan and the SLV-3 programme?

A) He fired the entire SLV-3 team after the first failure
B) When SLV-3 E1 failed, he took the blame publicly; when E2 succeeded, he pushed Kalam to take the credit
C) He refused to allow a second launch attempt

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When SLV-3 E1 failed in August 1979, ISRO Chairman Satish Dhawan personally addressed the press and took full responsibility for the failure, shielding project director Kalam and the team. When SLV-3 E2 succeeded in July 1980, Dhawan pushed Kalam to face the press and receive the credit. This "take blame for failure, give credit for success" leadership style is legendary in Indian space history.

### Question 14
What does ASLV stand for?

A) Advanced Satellite Launch Vehicle
B) Augmented Satellite Launch Vehicle
C) Automatic Space Launch Vehicle
D) Assisted Satellite Launch Vehicle

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ASLV stands for Augmented Satellite Launch Vehicle. It was an augmented version of SLV-3, designed to carry heavier payloads (150 kg to LEO) by adding two strap-on boosters (which were essentially SLV-3 first stages strapped to the core). ASLV had five stages and was about 24 metres tall with a mass of approximately 40 tonnes.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 15
How did ASLV differ from SLV-3 in its design?

A) ASLV used liquid propellant instead of solid
B) ASLV added two strap-on boosters using SLV-3 first stages
C) ASLV was smaller and lighter than SLV-3
D) ASLV was identical to SLV-3 in every way

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ASLV was an augmented version of SLV-3 that added two strap-on boosters to increase payload capacity from 40 kg (SLV-3) to 150 kg to LEO. The strap-on boosters were essentially SLV-3 first stages attached to the core vehicle. This gave ASLV five stages total, compared to SLV-3's four, and increased its mass from ~17 tonnes to ~40 tonnes.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Launch Vehicle Design

### Question 16
How many ASLV flights were there, and what were the results?

A) All four flights were successful
B) D1 and D2 failed; D3 and D4 succeeded
C) Only the first flight was successful
D) All four flights failed

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ASLV had four development flights: D1 (March 1987) and D2 (July 1988) both failed, while D3 (May 1992) and D4 (May 1994) succeeded. D3 launched the SROSS-C satellite and D4 launched SROSS-C2. The early failures were disappointing, but the lessons learned — particularly about strap-on booster technology and guidance systems — were crucial for PSLV's development.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 17

A) It was India's most commercially successful rocket
B) Its failures provided crucial learning that enabled PSLV's success
C) It was the first rocket to reach the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Although ASLV had a troubled development with two early failures, it served as a critical learning experience for ISRO. ASLV proved that learning from failure is essential for progress.

### Question 18
What does SSLV stand for?

### Question 20
What type of propulsion does SSLV use?

A) All liquid propellant
B) Three solid stages plus a liquid-propulsion Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)
C) Cryogenic propulsion in all stages
D) Nuclear thermal propulsion

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SSLV has three stages using solid propellant, plus a fourth component — a liquid-propulsion-based Velocity Trimming Module (VTM) — for precise orbit insertion. The solid stages provide the main thrust, while the VTM makes fine adjustments to place the satellite in exactly the right orbit. This hybrid approach balances simplicity (solid stages) with precision (liquid VTM).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SSLV Technical Specifications

### Question 21
What happened during SSLV's first launch (SSLV-D1)?

A) It was a complete success
B) It was a partial failure — satellites were placed in the wrong orbit and decayed
C) It exploded on the launch pad
D) The launch was cancelled

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SSLV-D1 was launched on August 7, 2022, and was a partial failure. The satellites were placed in an incorrect elliptical orbit instead of the intended circular orbit, causing them to decay and re-enter the atmosphere. The issue was traced to a problem in the guidance and control system. ISRO investigated the failure thoroughly and made corrections for the next flight.

### Question 24
What is the RLV-TD?

A) A reusable launch vehicle technology demonstrator being developed by ISRO
B) A Russian launch vehicle used by ISRO

**Answer:** A

### Question 25

### Question 28
What is the correct chronological order of India's orbital launch vehicle development?

A) ASLV → SLV-3 → PSLV → GSLV → SSLV

C) PSLV → SLV-3 → ASLV → GSLV → SSLV

Each step built on lessons from the previous one.
