## Topic 28 – Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota (SDSC SHAR)

### Questions for Topic 28

### Question 1
In which Indian state is the Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC SHAR) located?

A) Tamil Nadu
B) Kerala
C) Andhra Pradesh
D) Karnataka

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** SDSC SHAR is located on Sriharikota island in Nellore district, Andhra Pradesh. It is situated on the east coast of India, about 80 km north of Chennai. Despite being close to Tamil Nadu, the spaceport is in Andhra Pradesh.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 2
What body of water lies to the east of Sriharikota island?

A) Arabian Sea
B) Indian Ocean
C) Bay of Bengal
D) Pulicat Lake

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sriharikota island has the Bay of Bengal to its east and Pulicat Lake to its west. This east coast location is strategically important because rockets launched eastward fly over the open sea (Bay of Bengal), ensuring that no populated land areas are in the flight path.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 3
When was the Sriharikota Range (SHAR) originally established?

A) 1962
B) 1969
C) 1971
D) 1975

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sriharikota Range was established in 1971. The first launch from SHAR was a Rohini sounding rocket on October 9, 1971. It has since grown into India's primary and only orbital launch site, hosting all Indian satellite launches.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 4
After whom is the Satish Dhawan Space Centre named?

**Explanation:** The centre is named after Prof. Satish Dhawan, who served as ISRO Chairman from 1972 to 1984. ### Question 10
Is SDSC SHAR India's only operational orbital launch site?

B) No, there are two other launch sites in Karnataka

### Question 11
Why was the east coast of India chosen over the west coast for the launch site?

A) The east coast has better weather year-round
B) Rockets launched eastward fly over the sea, avoiding populated areas
C) The west coast is too mountainous for rocket launches
D) The east coast has stronger winds that help rockets

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The east coast was chosen because rockets are typically launched eastward to take advantage of Earth's rotational velocity. When launching from the east coast, the rocket's eastward trajectory takes it over the Bay of Bengal — open sea with no populated areas underneath. A west coast launch going east would fly over the Indian landmass, posing a safety risk.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 12
What natural advantage does Sriharikota's proximity to the equator provide for rocket launches?

A) Stronger magnetic field for navigation
B) Less atmospheric resistance
C) Earth's rotational velocity provides a free speed boost
D) Closer distance to geostationary orbit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Being close to the equator (approximately 13.7°N latitude) means the launch site benefits from Earth's higher rotational velocity at lower latitudes. At the equator, this velocity is about 465 m/s; at Sriharikota it is approximately 450 m/s. This "free" speed boost reduces the fuel needed for launches, especially for eastward geostationary orbit insertions.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Orbital Mechanics

### Question 13

The Second Launch Pad (SLP) handles GSLV and LVM3 missions, while the Third Launch Pad (TLP) was built specifically for the smaller SSLV.

### Question 18
What is the Vehicle Assembly Building (VAB) at SDSC SHAR used for?

A) Manufacturing satellite components
B) Assembling rockets vertically before launch
C) Testing rocket engines at full thrust
D) Training astronauts for Gaganyaan

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Vehicle Assembly Building (VAB) is where rockets are assembled vertically. The different stages of the launch vehicle are stacked together in the VAB, and the satellite payload is integrated on top. Once assembly is complete, the rocket is transported to the launch pad for final preparations.

### Question 21
What famous leadership lesson is associated with Satish Dhawan during the SLV-3 programme?

### Question 23

B) Thumba launches only sounding rockets while Sriharikota handles all orbital launches
C) Both are orbital launch sites but Thumba is older

### Question 24
What was the first satellite launch attempt from Sriharikota, and what was its outcome?

B) SLV-3 on August 10, 1979 — failed
C) ASLV-D1 in 1987 — succeeded
D) Aryabhata on April 19, 1975 — succeeded

The second attempt on July 18, 1980 succeeded, placing the Rohini RS-1 satellite into orbit and making India the seventh nation to achieve orbital launch capability. Note that Aryabhata (1975) was launched by a Soviet rocket from the USSR, not from Sriharikota.

### Question 25
What is the STEX facility at SDSC SHAR used for?

B) Static testing and evaluation of rocket motors before flight
C) Space telecommunications exchange
D) Storing spent rocket stages

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** STEX stands for Static Test and Evaluation Complex. This critical facility tests rocket motors before they are approved for actual flight missions. During a static test, a rocket motor is mounted in a test stand and fired at full thrust while measurements are taken of performance, vibration, and structural integrity — all without the motor actually going anywhere.

### Question 28
What approximate speed boost does Earth's rotation provide to rockets launched from near the equator?

A) About 50 m/s
B) About 465 m/s
C) About 1,500 m/s
D) About 7,800 m/s

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's rotational velocity at the equator is approximately 465 m/s. At Sriharikota's latitude (~13.7°N), this reduces slightly to about 450 m/s. This "free" velocity is added to rockets launched eastward, meaning less fuel is needed to reach orbital velocity (about 7,800 m/s for LEO). While ~465 m/s may seem small compared to orbital velocity, it translates to significant fuel savings due to the exponential nature of the rocket equation.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics / ISRO

Why is Sriharikota being an island location advantageous for a launch site?
