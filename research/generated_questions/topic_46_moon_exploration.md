## Topic 46 – Moon Exploration — International Missions

### Questions for Topic 46

### Question 1
What was the first spacecraft to reach the Moon?

A) Luna 9 (USSR)
B) Luna 2 (USSR) — it impacted the Moon in 1959
C) Ranger 7 (USA)
D) Apollo 11 (USA)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Luna 2 (USSR) was the first human-made object to reach the Moon, impacting its surface on 14 September 1959. This was just two years after Sputnik 1. Luna 1, launched earlier that year, was the first spacecraft to fly past the Moon but missed its target by about 6,000 km.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 2
What did Luna 3 achieve in 1959?

A) First soft landing on the Moon
B) First photographs of the far side of the Moon
C) First sample return from the Moon
D) First humans to see the Moon up close

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Luna 3 (USSR, 1959) took the first photographs of the far side of the Moon — the side that never faces Earth due to tidal locking. This was a major milestone because no human had ever seen the far side before. The photos revealed that the far side is very different from the near side, with far fewer dark "maria" (plains).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 3
Which mission achieved the first soft landing on the Moon?

A) Surveyor 1 (USA)
B) Apollo 11 (USA)
C) Luna 9 (USSR) — in February 1966
D) Chandrayaan-3 (India)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Luna 9 (USSR) made the first-ever soft landing on the Moon on 3 February 1966. It transmitted the first photographs from the lunar surface, proving that the surface was solid enough to support a lander (there had been fears that spacecraft might sink into deep dust). The first US soft landing followed four months later with Surveyor 1.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 4
How many people have walked on the Moon?

A) 2
B) 6
C) 12
D) 24

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Twelve people have walked on the Moon — all American astronauts during the Apollo programme (1969-1972). There were 6 successful Moon landing missions: Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17. Each mission had 2 astronauts walk on the surface while a third orbited above in the Command Module.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 5
What was Lunokhod 1?

A) The first human Moon mission
B) The first robotic rover on the Moon (USSR, 1970)
C) A Chinese lunar lander
D) An Indian Moon orbiter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Lunokhod 1 (USSR, 1970) was the first robotic rover to operate on another celestial body. It was remotely controlled from Earth and drove approximately 10.5 km over the lunar surface during its 322 days of operation. It was delivered to the Moon by the Luna 17 mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 6
What did the Apollo 15 mission introduce for the first time?

A) The first Moon landing
B) The Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV) — an electric car on the Moon
C) The first spacewalk
D) The first sample return

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Apollo 15 (July 1971) was the first mission to use the Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), an electric car that allowed astronauts to travel much farther from the lander. The LRV had a top speed of about 13 km/h on the Moon. Apollo 15, 16, and 17 all used rovers, which are still sitting on the Moon today.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 7
Which mission discovered water molecules on the Moon?

A) Apollo 11 (1969)
B) Luna 16 (1970)
C) Chandrayaan-1 (India, 2008) — using its Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³) instrument
D) Chang'e 3 (2013)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** India's Chandrayaan-1 made a groundbreaking discovery in 2008-2009: its Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³, a NASA instrument) detected water molecules (H₂O) and hydroxyl (OH) on the lunar surface. This overturned decades of belief that the Moon was completely dry. The discovery was confirmed by NASA's LCROSS mission in 2009, which found water ice in permanently shadowed craters.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / NASA

### Question 8
What was the significance of China's Chang'e 3 mission (2013)?

A) First ever Moon landing
B) China's first lunar lander and rover (Yutu), making China the 3rd country to soft-land on the Moon
C) First far-side landing
D) First lunar sample return since 1976

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chang'e 3 (December 2013) was China's first lunar lander, deploying the Yutu ("Jade Rabbit") rover. This made China the third country ever to achieve a soft landing on the Moon, after the USSR and USA. It was the first soft landing on the Moon since the Soviet Union's Luna 24 in 1976 — a gap of 37 years.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 9
Why was China's Chang'e 4 landing (2019) historic?

A) It was the first Chinese Moon mission
B) It was the first-ever landing on the far side of the Moon
C) It carried the first human to the Moon
D) It was the first to find water on the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chang'e 4 (January 2019) achieved the first-ever soft landing on the far side of the Moon, in the Von Kármán crater. Because the far side always faces away from Earth, direct communication is impossible. China solved this by first placing the Queqiao relay satellite at the L2 Lagrange point behind the Moon. The Yutu-2 rover explored the far side terrain.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 10
Why is the lunar south pole so important for future exploration?

A) It's the warmest area on the Moon
B) It may contain water ice in permanently shadowed craters, useful for fuel and life support
C) It's the only flat area on the Moon
D) It has the most sunlight of any area

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The lunar south pole is a prime target because permanently shadowed craters there may contain water ice — a potential resource for drinking, oxygen production, and rocket fuel (hydrogen). Additionally, elevated crater rims receive near-continuous sunlight, ideal for solar power. This is why Chandrayaan-3's landing near the south pole in 2023 was so scientifically significant.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 11
Which was the first robotic sample return from the Moon?

A) Apollo 11 (1969)
B) Luna 16 (USSR, 1970)
C) Chang'e 5 (China, 2020)
D) Chandrayaan-1 (India, 2008)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Luna 16 (USSR, 1970) was the first robotic (unmanned) mission to return samples from the Moon — about 101 grams of lunar soil. This was significant because it demonstrated that sample return could be achieved without sending humans. The Apollo missions returned far more (382 kg total) but used crewed spacecraft.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 12
What was the gap between the last Soviet Moon mission and China's Chang'e 5 sample return?

A) 10 years
B) 24 years
C) 44 years — from Luna 24 (1976) to Chang'e 5 (2020)
D) 60 years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** There was a 44-year gap between the USSR's Luna 24 (1976, the last Soviet Moon mission) and China's Chang'e 5 (2020), which was the next mission to return lunar samples. Chang'e 5 returned approximately 1.73 kg of Moon soil. This long gap reflects how the world's interest in Moon missions faded after the Space Race, only to revive in the 21st century.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History / CNSA

### Question 13
What is a common misconception about the "dark side" of the Moon?

A) It doesn't exist
B) People think it's always dark, but it actually gets equal sunlight — "dark" means "unseen from Earth"
C) It's darker than the near side
D) Only China has photographed it

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The "dark side" is more correctly called the "far side." It is not permanently dark — it receives just as much sunlight as the near side. "Dark" in this context means "unknown" or "unseen from Earth" because the Moon is tidally locked (its rotation period equals its orbital period), so the same face always points toward Earth.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy

### Question 14
Which five countries have achieved soft landings on the Moon (as of 2024)?

A) USA, Russia, China, India, Japan
B) USSR, USA, China, India, Japan
C) USA, China, India, Japan, South Korea
D) USA, Russia, China, Japan, UK

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Five countries have achieved soft landings on the Moon: the USSR (Luna 9, 1966), USA (Surveyor 1, 1966; Apollo missions), China (Chang'e 3, 2013; Chang'e 4, 2019), India (Chandrayaan-3, 2023), and Japan (SLIM, 2024). Note: the distinction is USSR, not "Russia" — these landings were achieved by the Soviet Union. Additionally, the private company Intuitive Machines achieved a landing in 2024.

### Question 15
What was ESA's SMART-1 mission?

A) A Mars orbiter
B) ESA's first Moon mission, which tested ion propulsion technology (2003-2006)
C) A space station module
D) A Venus lander

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SMART-1 (2003-2006) was ESA's first mission to the Moon. It was primarily a technology demonstrator that tested solar-electric (ion) propulsion for interplanetary travel. Using its ion engine, SMART-1 spiralled outward from Earth to reach lunar orbit — a slow but highly fuel-efficient approach. It mapped the lunar surface before its planned impact in 2006.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ESA

### Question 16
What is the Outer Space Treaty, and how does it relate to Moon exploration?

A) A trade agreement between space agencies
B) The 1967 UN treaty that states no nation can claim sovereignty over the Moon or any celestial body
C) A treaty banning all Moon landings
D) An agreement that only the USA can explore the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Outer Space Treaty (signed 10 October 1967 — the date World Space Week ends) establishes that outer space, including the Moon, cannot be claimed as territory by any nation. It also prohibits placing nuclear weapons in space and states that astronauts are "envoys of mankind." Over 110 countries have ratified this treaty, making it the foundation of international space law.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** United Nations

### Question 17
What did NASA's LCROSS mission confirm in 2009?

A) The Moon has no atmosphere
B) Water ice exists in permanently shadowed craters at the lunar south pole
C) The Moon is older than Earth
D) The Moon has active volcanoes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's LCROSS (Lunar Crater Observation and Sensing Satellite) deliberately crashed a rocket stage into the permanently shadowed Cabeus crater near the Moon's south pole in 2009. Instruments analysed the ejected plume and confirmed the presence of water ice. This confirmed Chandrayaan-1's earlier detection and proved that significant water resources exist on the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 18
What was Chang'e 6's historic achievement in 2024?

A) First Chinese lunar landing
B) The first-ever sample return from the far side of the Moon, collecting about 1.9 kg of material
C) First rover on the far side
D) First detection of water on the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chang'e 6 (2024) achieved the first-ever sample return from the Moon's far side, collecting approximately 1.9 kg of lunar material. Since the far side never faces Earth, this required the Queqiao-2 relay satellite for communication. Far-side samples are especially valuable because that terrain has a different geological history than the near side, providing unique scientific insights.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 19
How did Apollo 13 demonstrate the dangers of Moon exploration?

A) It proved the Moon has no gravity
B) An oxygen tank exploded en route to the Moon, forcing the crew to abort and return using the lunar module as a lifeboat
C) It crashed on the Moon's surface
D) It ran out of fuel in orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Apollo 13 (April 1970) suffered an oxygen tank explosion on the way to the Moon, crippling the spacecraft. The crew — Jim Lovell, Fred Haise, and Jack Swigert — used the lunar module as a lifeboat and swung around the Moon to return safely to Earth. It is called a "successful failure" because the mission failed but the crew survived through extraordinary problem-solving.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 20
What is Japan's SLIM mission known for?

A) Japan's first Mars mission
B) Achieving a precision landing within 55 metres of the target, making Japan the 5th country to soft-land on the Moon
C) First Japanese satellite
D) A space station module

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLIM (Smart Lander for Investigating Moon), which landed in January 2024, made Japan the fifth country to soft-land on the Moon. Its key achievement was precision landing — it touched down within just 55 metres of its intended target, a remarkable feat. The lander landed upside-down but still managed to operate and return data. Japan called it the "Moon Sniper" mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** JAXA
