## Topic 3 – The Sun — Our Star

### Questions for Topic 3

### Question 1
What type of star is the Sun classified as?

A) Red dwarf
B) Blue supergiant
C) G2V main-sequence star (yellow dwarf)
D) White dwarf

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sun is classified as a G2V main-sequence star, commonly called a yellow dwarf. "G2" refers to its spectral class (surface temperature around 5,500°C), "V" indicates it is a main-sequence star (still fusing hydrogen in its core). Despite the name "yellow dwarf," the Sun actually appears white from space.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics / Standard Stellar Classification

### Question 2
Approximately how old is the Sun?

A) 1 billion years
B) 4.6 billion years
C) 10 billion years
D) 13.8 billion years

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Sun is approximately 4.6 billion years old, making it a middle-aged star. It formed from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust (solar nebula) and is expected to continue shining for about another 5 billion years before exhausting its hydrogen fuel.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 3
What is the approximate diameter of the Sun?

A) 140,000 km
B) 1.4 million km
C) 14 million km
D) 150 million km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Sun's diameter is approximately 1.4 million km, which is about 109 times Earth's diameter. This enormous size means that about 1.3 million Earths could fit inside the Sun. The 150 million km figure is the Earth-Sun distance (1 AU), not the Sun's diameter.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 4
What percentage of the total mass of the solar system is contained in the Sun?

A) 50%
B) 75%
C) 90%
D) 99.86%

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The Sun contains approximately 99.86% of the total mass of the entire solar system. All the planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and other objects together make up only about 0.14% of the solar system's mass. This dominant mass is why the Sun's gravity holds everything in orbit.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar System Science

### Question 5
What is the primary composition of the Sun?

A) Mostly iron and nickel
B) Mostly carbon and oxygen
C) Mostly hydrogen and helium
D) Mostly nitrogen and oxygen

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sun is composed of approximately 73% hydrogen and 25% helium, with the remaining 2% being heavier elements. This is similar to the composition of other stars and the overall Universe. The hydrogen in the Sun's core is the fuel for nuclear fusion.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 6
How long does light from the Sun take to reach Earth?

A) About 1 second
B) About 8 minutes 20 seconds
C) About 1 hour
D) About 1 day

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Light from the Sun takes approximately 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth. The Sun is about 150 million km away (1 AU), and light travels at approximately 300,000 km per second. This means that when you look at the Sun, you are seeing it as it was about 8 minutes ago.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomical Units / Standard Physics

### Question 7
What is the process that powers the Sun and produces its energy?

A) Chemical combustion (burning)
B) Nuclear fission
C) Nuclear fusion
D) Gravitational contraction only

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sun is powered by nuclear fusion — the process of joining lighter atomic nuclei to form heavier ones. In the Sun's core, four hydrogen nuclei (protons) fuse to form one helium nucleus, releasing enormous energy according to Einstein's equation E=mc². This is fundamentally different from burning (combustion) or nuclear fission (splitting atoms).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Nuclear Physics / Solar Physics

### Question 8
In the Sun's core, what is fused together to produce energy?

A) Helium atoms fuse into carbon
B) Iron atoms fuse into gold
C) Hydrogen atoms fuse into helium
D) Oxygen atoms fuse into nitrogen

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** In the Sun's core, hydrogen nuclei (protons) fuse together to form helium nuclei. During this process, a small amount of mass is converted to energy (E=mc²). The Sun converts approximately 600 million tonnes of hydrogen into helium every second, with about 4 million tonnes of mass being converted to pure energy each second.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Nuclear Physics / Solar Physics

### Question 9
What is the approximate temperature at the Sun's core?

A) 5,500°C
B) 20,000°C
C) 1 million °C
D) 15 million °C

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The temperature at the Sun's core is approximately 15 million degrees Celsius, with a pressure of about 250 billion atmospheres. These extreme conditions are necessary for nuclear fusion to occur. The 5,500°C figure is the temperature of the photosphere (visible surface), not the core.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 10
Which layer of the Sun is its visible "surface"?

A) Corona
B) Chromosphere
C) Photosphere
D) Convective zone

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The photosphere is the visible "surface" of the Sun — the layer from which light is emitted that we can see. It has a temperature of approximately 5,500°C and is about 500 km thick. Sunspots appear on the photosphere. The corona and chromosphere are above the photosphere, while the convective zone is below it.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 11
What is the temperature of the Sun's photosphere (visible surface)?

A) About 2,700°C
B) About 5,500°C
C) About 20,000°C
D) About 1 million °C

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The photosphere has a temperature of approximately 5,500°C (about 5,778 K). This is much cooler than the core (15 million°C) but also much cooler than the corona (1-3 million°C). The fact that the corona is much hotter than the surface is one of the great unsolved mysteries of solar physics.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 12
What is unusual about the temperature of the Sun's corona compared to its surface?

A) The corona is cooler than the surface
B) The corona has the same temperature as the surface
C) The corona is much hotter than the surface
D) The corona has no measurable temperature

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The corona, the Sun's outermost atmosphere, has a temperature of 1-3 million degrees Celsius — far hotter than the visible surface (photosphere) at about 5,500°C. This is known as the "coronal heating problem" and remains one of the biggest mysteries in solar physics. ISRO's Aditya-L1 mission is studying this phenomenon.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / ISRO Aditya-L1

### Question 13
In which layer of the Sun does nuclear fusion occur?

A) Photosphere
B) Corona
C) Convective zone
D) Core

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Nuclear fusion occurs only in the Sun's core, where temperatures reach about 15 million°C and pressures are about 250 billion atmospheres. These extreme conditions are necessary for hydrogen nuclei to overcome their natural repulsion and fuse together. No other layer of the Sun has conditions extreme enough for fusion.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 14
How does energy travel through the radiative zone of the Sun?

A) Through convection currents (hot gas rising)
B) Through radiation (photons bouncing around)
C) Through sound waves
D) Through magnetic field lines

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In the radiative zone, energy travels outward as radiation — photons are continuously absorbed and re-emitted by the dense solar material. Because photons bounce around so many times in the dense plasma, it takes a photon approximately 170,000 years to travel through the radiative zone to reach the convective zone above it.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 15
What are sunspots?

A) Holes in the Sun's surface
B) Areas hotter than the surrounding photosphere
C) Dark patches on the photosphere that are cooler than surrounding areas
D) Regions where nuclear fusion is happening on the surface

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sunspots are dark patches on the photosphere that are cooler than their surroundings — approximately 3,500°C compared to the normal 5,500°C. They appear dark by contrast. Sunspots are caused by intense magnetic field activity that inhibits convection, reducing the temperature in those areas. They typically appear in pairs (magnetic dipoles).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 16
Approximately how long is a solar cycle (from one solar maximum to the next)?

A) About 1 year
B) About 11 years
C) About 76 years
D) About 100 years

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The solar cycle is approximately 11 years long, during which the number of sunspots varies from a minimum to a maximum and back. At solar maximum, there are many sunspots and increased solar activity (flares, CMEs). At solar minimum, the Sun is relatively quiet. The full magnetic cycle (including polarity reversal) is about 22 years.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 17
What is a solar flare?

A) A dark spot on the Sun's surface
B) A sudden, intense burst of radiation from the Sun
C) A stream of particles continuously flowing from the Sun
D) A large bubble of plasma ejected from the corona

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A solar flare is a sudden, intense burst of radiation (across the electromagnetic spectrum) from the Sun's surface. Solar flares are caused by the sudden release of magnetic energy stored in the Sun's atmosphere. They can affect Earth's ionosphere and disrupt radio communications, GPS signals, and satellite operations.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / Space Weather

### Question 18
What is a Coronal Mass Ejection (CME)?

A) A dark spot on the photosphere
B) The normal flow of charged particles from the Sun
C) A huge bubble of plasma and magnetic field ejected from the Sun's corona
D) A flash of visible light from the Sun

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A Coronal Mass Ejection (CME) is a massive bubble of magnetized plasma ejected from the Sun's corona into space. CMEs can contain billions of tonnes of matter and travel at speeds of hundreds to thousands of km/s. When directed at Earth, they can reach us in 1-3 days and cause geomagnetic storms that affect satellites, power grids, and produce auroras.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / Space Weather

### Question 19
What causes the beautiful auroras (Northern and Southern Lights) on Earth?

A) Moonlight reflecting off ice crystals
B) Solar wind and CME particles interacting with Earth's magnetosphere and atmosphere
C) Volcanic eruptions releasing gases
D) Lightning in the upper atmosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Auroras are caused when charged particles from the solar wind and coronal mass ejections interact with Earth's magnetic field. These particles are channeled toward the poles along magnetic field lines, where they collide with atoms and molecules in the upper atmosphere, causing them to emit light — creating the spectacular Aurora Borealis (north) and Aurora Australis (south).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Weather / Solar-Terrestrial Physics

### Question 20
What is the solar wind?

A) Wind caused by the Sun heating Earth's atmosphere
B) A continuous stream of charged particles flowing from the Sun
C) The rotation of the Sun causing space disturbance
D) Sound waves from the Sun traveling through space

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The solar wind is a continuous stream of charged particles (mostly protons and electrons) flowing outward from the Sun at speeds of about 400-800 km/s. It extends throughout the solar system, shaping the heliosphere. Earth's magnetic field protects us from most solar wind particles. The solar wind is also responsible for shaping comet tails to always point away from the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / Space Weather

### Question 21
What protects Earth from most of the solar wind?

A) The ozone layer
B) Earth's thick atmosphere
C) Earth's magnetic field (magnetosphere)
D) The Moon blocking the particles

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth's magnetic field, generated by the motion of liquid iron in the outer core (dynamo effect), creates a protective magnetosphere around our planet. This magnetosphere deflects most of the charged particles in the solar wind. Without it, the solar wind could strip away our atmosphere over time, similar to what happened to Mars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Physics / Space Weather

### Question 22
Why do comet tails always point away from the Sun?

A) Because comets always travel away from the Sun
B) Because the solar wind and radiation pressure push the tail material away from the Sun
C) Because of the gravitational pull of outer planets
D) Because comets rotate in a specific direction

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A comet's tail always points away from the Sun regardless of the comet's direction of travel. This is because the solar wind (stream of charged particles) and solar radiation pressure push the gas and dust away from the Sun. When a comet is moving away from the Sun, its tail actually leads in front of it!

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics / Cometary Science

### Question 23
What happens during a total solar eclipse?

A) The Earth passes between the Sun and Moon
B) The Moon completely covers the Sun's disk as seen from Earth
C) The Sun goes dark for no reason
D) The Sun moves behind another star

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** During a total solar eclipse, the Moon passes directly between the Earth and the Sun, completely blocking the Sun's disk. This is possible because, by a remarkable coincidence, the Sun is about 400 times larger than the Moon but also about 400 times farther away, making them appear almost the same size in our sky. During totality, the Sun's corona becomes visible.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy / Eclipse Science

### Question 24
What is the difference between a total and an annular solar eclipse?

A) A total eclipse is longer than an annular eclipse
B) In a total eclipse the Moon fully covers the Sun; in an annular eclipse a ring of sunlight remains visible
C) Annular eclipses happen at night
D) There is no difference

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In a total solar eclipse, the Moon completely covers the Sun's disk. In an annular eclipse, the Moon is too far from Earth (appearing smaller in the sky) to completely cover the Sun, so a bright ring or "annulus" of sunlight remains visible around the Moon's silhouette. The Moon's distance from Earth varies because its orbit is elliptical.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy / Eclipse Science

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sun converts approximately 600 million tonnes of hydrogen into helium every second through nuclear fusion in its core. ### Question 30
The Sun will eventually die. What will be its final state?

A) It will explode as a supernova
B) It will become a black hole
C) It will become a white dwarf
D) It will become a neutron star

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Sun is not massive enough to explode as a supernova or collapse into a black hole or neutron star. In about 5 billion years, it will exhaust its hydrogen fuel, expand into a red giant (engulfing Mercury and Venus), then shed its outer layers to form a planetary nebula, leaving behind a hot, dense white dwarf that will slowly cool over billions of years.

### Question 31
What is the energy equation that describes how mass is converted to energy in the Sun?

A) F = ma
B) E = mc²
C) PV = nRT
D) V = IR

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Einstein's famous equation E = mc² describes how mass and energy are related. In the Sun's core, when hydrogen fuses into helium, a small amount of mass is "lost" — it is converted into energy. Because the speed of light (c) is enormous, even a tiny amount of mass produces a vast amount of energy. This is the principle behind both nuclear fusion and fission.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Einstein / Nuclear Physics

### Question 32
Which of the following correctly lists the Sun's layers from inside to outside?

A) Photosphere → Core → Corona → Chromosphere
B) Core → Radiative Zone → Convective Zone → Photosphere → Chromosphere → Corona
C) Corona → Chromosphere → Photosphere → Core
D) Core → Convective Zone → Radiative Zone → Photosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The correct order from inside out is: Core (where fusion occurs) → Radiative Zone (energy travels as radiation) → Convective Zone (energy carried by convection) → Photosphere (visible surface) → Chromosphere (thin reddish layer) → Corona (outermost atmosphere, extends millions of km). The radiative zone is between the core and convective zone, not the other way around.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar Physics

### Question 33
Why do sunspots appear dark?

A) They are holes in the Sun
B) They are cooler than the surrounding photosphere, so they appear darker by contrast
C) They absorb all light
D) They are shadows cast by planets

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sunspots appear dark because they are about 1,500-2,000°C cooler than the surrounding photosphere (approximately 3,500°C vs 5,500°C). They are still incredibly hot and luminous — if you could isolate a sunspot against the dark sky, it would actually be brighter than the full Moon. They appear dark only by contrast with the even hotter surrounding surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 34
During which phase of a total solar eclipse does the corona become visible?

A) Before the Moon starts covering the Sun
B) When the Moon partially covers the Sun
C) During totality, when the Moon completely blocks the Sun's disk
D) After the eclipse is completely over

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The corona is normally invisible because it is vastly dimmer than the photosphere. During totality (when the Moon completely blocks the Sun's bright disk), the much fainter corona becomes visible as a beautiful pearly white halo around the eclipsed Sun. This is one of the reasons total solar eclipses are scientifically valuable and visually spectacular.

### Question 35
The chromosphere is a thin layer of the Sun's atmosphere. How can it sometimes be seen?

A) It is always visible from Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The chromosphere (meaning "color sphere") is a thin layer of the Sun's atmosphere above the photosphere. It gets its name from its reddish appearance, which can be seen briefly during solar eclipses — particularly at the beginning and end of totality. It can also be observed using special filters (H-alpha filters) that isolate specific wavelengths of light.

### Question 36
What would happen to Earth if the Sun suddenly had no solar wind?

A) Nothing — the solar wind has no effect on Earth
B) Comet tails would not be pushed away from the Sun, and Earth's magnetosphere shape would change
C) Earth would freeze immediately
D) All water on Earth would evaporate

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The solar wind interacts with Earth's magnetosphere, compressing it on the Sun-facing side and stretching it on the opposite side. Without solar wind, the magnetosphere would become more spherical. Also, comet tails, which are shaped by solar wind and radiation pressure to always point away from the Sun, would behave differently. Earth's climate would not be directly affected since the Sun's light energy is far more important than the solar wind's energy.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Space Physics / Solar-Terrestrial Interactions

### Question 37

### Question 39
In the Sun's convective zone, energy is transported by:

A) Radiation (photons)
B) Conduction (direct contact)
C) Convection (hot gas rising, cool gas sinking)
D) Nuclear fusion

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** In the convective zone (the outermost of the Sun's interior layers), energy is carried outward by convection — hot plasma rises toward the surface, releases its energy, cools, and sinks back down. These convection cells are visible on the Sun's surface as granulation patterns. This is similar to how water heats in a pot — hot water rises, cool water sinks.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 41

### Question 42
Approximately how long does it take a photon to travel from the Sun's core to the surface (through the radiative zone)?

A) 8 minutes
B) About 1 year
C) About 170,000 years
D) About 4.6 billion years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Although light travels at 300,000 km/s, a photon produced in the Sun's core takes approximately 170,000 years to reach the surface. This is because in the extremely dense radiative zone, photons are constantly absorbed and re-emitted in random directions, taking a "random walk" through the solar interior. Once the photon reaches the surface, it then takes only about 8 minutes to reach Earth.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Solar Physics
