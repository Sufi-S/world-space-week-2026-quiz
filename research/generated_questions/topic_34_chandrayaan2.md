## Topic 34 – Chandrayaan-2 — Orbiter, Lander, and Rover

### Questions for Topic 34

### Question 1
When was Chandrayaan-2 launched?

A) 22 October 2008
B) 5 November 2013
C) 22 July 2019
D) 14 July 2023

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-2 was launched on 22 July 2019 from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota. The original launch date was 15 July 2019, but it was scrubbed 56 minutes before liftoff due to a technical issue — a helium leak was detected. The launch was rescheduled successfully for 22 July.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
What were the three main components of the Chandrayaan-2 mission?

A) Orbiter, Lander, and Rover
B) Orbiter, Relay Satellite, and Lander
C) Orbiter, Sample Return Module, and Rover
D) Lander, Rover, and Communication Satellite

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-2 had three components: the Orbiter (2,379 kg, carrying 8 instruments), the Vikram lander (1,471 kg including the rover), and the Pragyan rover (27 kg, 6-wheeled and solar-powered). Together they had a total mission mass of approximately 3,850 kg.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
Which launch vehicle was used for Chandrayaan-2?

A) PSLV-XL
B) GSLV Mk II
C) LVM3 (formerly GSLV Mk III)
D) SSLV

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-2 was launched by LVM3-M1 (at the time still called GSLV Mk III). LVM3 was needed because the total mission mass of approximately 3,850 kg was far too heavy for PSLV, which was used for the lighter Chandrayaan-1. LVM3 is India's heaviest operational launch vehicle, capable of placing about 4,000 kg in GTO.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What happened during Chandrayaan-2's landing attempt on 7 September 2019?

A) Vikram landed successfully but the rover failed to deploy
B) Vikram lost communication at approximately 2.1 km altitude and crash-landed
C) The entire mission was aborted before the landing attempt
D) Vikram landed upside-down but remained intact

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** During the landing attempt on 7 September 2019, the Vikram lander lost communication with ground control at approximately 2.1 km above the lunar surface during the fine braking phase. A software issue in the guidance system caused the lander to deviate from its planned trajectory and tilt beyond recoverable limits. It crash-landed about 500 metres from its intended site.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
Was the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter a success or failure?

A) It failed along with the lander
B) It was a major success and continues to operate years beyond its design life
C) It operated for only a few months
D) It never reached lunar orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Chandrayaan-2 orbiter has been a resounding success. Designed for a 1-year mission, it has enough fuel to operate for 7+ years. It orbits at 100 km altitude and carries 8 scientific instruments that continue to map the lunar surface, detect water ice signatures, and conduct other science. The orbiter even served as the communication relay for Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander in 2023.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What caused the Vikram lander to crash during its descent?

A) Engine failure during the rough braking phase
B) A software issue in the guidance system during the fine braking phase
C) Running out of fuel before reaching the surface
D) A collision with a lunar boulder

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The engines worked correctly — the issue was in the software. During the fine braking phase (below 2.1 km altitude), a software issue in the guidance and navigation system caused the lander's trajectory to deviate significantly. The lander tilted beyond recoverable limits and crash-landed. The rough braking phase (30 km to 7.4 km) had worked correctly.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9

A) About ₹100 crore (~$15 million)

D) About ₹10,000 crore (~$1.4 billion)

For context, it cost less than many Hollywood blockbuster films and was cheaper than the Avengers: Endgame movie budget.

### Question 10
How many scientific instruments does the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter carry?

A) 3
B) 5
C) 8
D) 11

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Chandrayaan-2 orbiter carries 8 scientific instruments: TMC-2 (terrain mapping), CLASS (X-ray spectrometer), XSM (solar X-ray monitor), OHRC (high-resolution camera), DFSAR (dual-frequency SAR), CHACE-2 (atmospheric composition), DFRS (radio science), and IIRS (infrared spectrometer). These instruments continue to map and analyse the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What makes the OHRC on Chandrayaan-2 special?

A) It is the cheapest camera ever sent to the Moon
B) It has the highest resolution (0.3 m) of any camera sent to the Moon from lunar orbit
C) It can see through the lunar surface
D) It was built by NASA

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Orbiter High Resolution Camera (OHRC) on Chandrayaan-2 achieves 0.3 metre resolution — meaning it can distinguish objects as small as 30 centimetres on the lunar surface. This makes it the highest-resolution camera orbiting the Moon. The OHRC even captured images of the Vikram lander debris on the surface after the crash-landing.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12

### Question 13

### Question 14

A) Near the equator

**Answer:** C

### Question 16
What is the difference between the "rough braking" and "fine braking" phases of a lunar landing?

A) They are the same phase with different names
B) Rough braking decelerates from high altitude (30 km to ~7 km); fine braking handles the precision descent from ~7 km to touchdown
C) Rough braking uses parachutes; fine braking uses engines
D) Fine braking happens first, followed by rough braking

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** During a lunar landing, the rough braking phase covers the initial deceleration from orbital velocity at high altitude (about 30 km down to about 7.4 km). The fine braking phase handles the critical, precision-controlled descent from there to the surface, where the lander must precisely control its attitude and descent rate. Vikram's rough braking worked correctly, but the fine braking phase is where the guidance software failed.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 17
What instruments did the Pragyan rover carry?

A) Cameras and a drill
B) APXS (Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer) and LIBS (Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscope)
C) VHRR and SAR
D) Magnetometer and seismometer

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pragyan carried two instruments: APXS (Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer) to determine the elemental composition of lunar soil by bombarding it with alpha particles, and LIBS (Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscope) which fires a powerful laser at rocks/soil and analyses the emitted light to identify elements. These same instruments were later successfully used on Chandrayaan-3's Pragyan rover.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 19
What is the DFSAR instrument on the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter?

A) A camera for photographing the lunar surface
B) Dual Frequency Synthetic Aperture Radar for subsurface and surface studies
C) A radio transmitter for communicating with Earth
D) A laser for measuring surface elevation

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** DFSAR (Dual Frequency Synthetic Aperture Radar) on the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter uses radar signals at two frequencies (L-band and S-band) to study the lunar surface and subsurface. Radar can penetrate below the surface to reveal buried features and ice deposits. It is particularly useful for detecting water ice in permanently shadowed regions that cannot be imaged by optical cameras.

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 21
What was CHACE-2 on the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter?

A) A camera for mapping the surface
B) An atmospheric composition instrument, successor to Chandrayaan-1's CHACE
C) A communication antenna
D) A heat shield

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CHACE-2 (Chandrayaan-2 Atmospheric Compositional Explorer 2) is the successor to the CHACE instrument that flew on Chandrayaan-1's Moon Impact Probe. While CHACE on MIP measured composition during a brief descent, CHACE-2 on the orbiter continuously measures the composition of the Moon's extremely thin atmosphere (exosphere) from orbit, providing long-term data.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 22
Which of the following statements about Chandrayaan-2 is FALSE?

A) The orbiter has operated far beyond its 1-year design life
B) Vikram crash-landed due to engine failure
C) It was launched by LVM3 (GSLV Mk III)
D) The mission cost approximately ₹978 crore

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The false statement is B. Vikram's engines worked correctly. The crash was caused by a software issue in the guidance and navigation system during the fine braking phase. The engines fired as expected during both rough braking and fine braking — it was the trajectory guidance that went wrong, causing the lander to tilt beyond recoverable limits.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 23
Why was LVM3 used for Chandrayaan-2 instead of PSLV?

A) PSLV was unavailable at the time
B) The total mission mass (~3,850 kg) was too heavy for PSLV's capacity
C) LVM3 is cheaper than PSLV
D) PSLV cannot reach the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-2's total mass of approximately 3,850 kg (orbiter + lander + rover + fuel) was far beyond PSLV's capacity. PSLV can place about 1,750 kg to SSO in its XL variant. LVM3, which can handle about 4,000 kg to GTO, was the only Indian launch vehicle capable of carrying such a heavy payload. Chandrayaan-1 used PSLV because it was lighter (1,380 kg) as an orbiter-only mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 24
What NASA instrument was carried on the Vikram lander?

A) Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³)
B) Mini-SAR
C) Laser Retroreflector Array (LRA)
D) Mars Hand Lens Imager

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's Laser Retroreflector Array (LRA) was a passive instrument on the Vikram lander. Unlike active instruments that need power, LRA is a set of corner-cube reflectors that bounce laser beams back to their source, enabling precise distance measurements from Earth. Even though Vikram crash-landed, the LRA (being a passive, rugged device) is still on the lunar surface and potentially usable.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 25
What was the intended design life of the Chandrayaan-2 orbiter?

A) 6 months
B) 1 year
C) 5 years
D) 10 years

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Chandrayaan-2 orbiter was designed for a 1-year mission life. However, because of precise orbit insertion by LVM3 (which saved onboard fuel) and efficient mission operations, the orbiter has enough fuel to operate for over 7 years. It continues to orbit at 100 km altitude, conducting science and even supporting the Chandrayaan-3 mission in 2023.

**Difficulty:** Medium
