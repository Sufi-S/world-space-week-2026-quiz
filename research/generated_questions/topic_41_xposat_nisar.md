## Topic 41 – XPoSat, NISAR, and Other Recent/Upcoming ISRO Missions

### Questions for Topic 41

### Question 1
What is XPoSat?

A) India's X-ray Polarimeter Satellite — the world's second dedicated X-ray polarimetry mission
B) India's first communication satellite
C) A satellite for studying the Moon's X-ray emissions
D) A defence surveillance satellite

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** XPoSat (X-ray Polarimeter Satellite) is India's first and the world's second dedicated X-ray polarimetry mission (after NASA's IXPE in 2021). Launched on 1 January 2024 by PSLV-C58, it studies the polarization of X-rays from celestial sources like black holes, neutron stars, and magnetars to understand their extreme physics.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
When was XPoSat launched?

A) 23 August 2023
B) 2 September 2023
C) 1 January 2024
D) 30 December 2024

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** XPoSat was launched on 1 January 2024 from SDSC SHAR aboard PSLV-C58. It was placed in a low-inclination orbit at approximately 650 km altitude. The PSLV's fourth stage was repurposed as POEM-3 carrying 10 additional experimental payloads.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
What does X-ray polarimetry measure?

A) The temperature of X-ray sources
B) The direction of oscillation (polarization) of X-rays from cosmic sources
C) The number of X-rays hitting Earth
D) The distance to X-ray sources

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** X-ray polarimetry measures the polarization of X-rays — the direction in which the X-ray light waves oscillate. This reveals information about the geometry and physics of extreme environments like the magnetic field structure around black holes, the emission mechanisms in pulsars, and the nature of radiation from magnetars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Astrophysics

### Question 4
What is NISAR?

A) A navigation satellite for India
B) A joint NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar satellite for Earth observation
C) An Indian weather satellite
D) A deep-space telescope

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) is a joint mission between NASA and ISRO, and one of the most significant US-India space collaborations. It uses dual-frequency SAR (L-band from NASA/JPL and S-band from ISRO) to map the entire Earth every 12 days, monitoring earthquakes, ice sheets, deforestation, and other surface changes.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 5
What makes NISAR unique among Earth observation satellites?

A) It is the smallest Earth observation satellite
B) It is the first satellite to carry dual-frequency (L-band and S-band) synthetic aperture radar
C) It only observes India
D) It uses optical cameras instead of radar

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NISAR is unique because it carries both L-band (provided by NASA) and S-band (provided by ISRO) synthetic aperture radar — the first Earth observation satellite to combine both frequencies. L-band penetrates deeper into vegetation and ice while S-band provides complementary surface information, enabling comprehensive monitoring of Earth's changing surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 6
What is Chandrayaan-4's main objective?

A) Landing another rover on the Moon
B) Collecting lunar soil samples and returning them to Earth
C) Building a permanent base on the Moon
D) Mapping the Moon from orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-4 is India's planned lunar sample return mission. It will involve a lander collecting samples from the Moon's surface, an ascent module launching from the Moon with the samples, docking in lunar orbit with a return vehicle, and bringing the samples back to Earth. This makes it far more complex than previous Chandrayaan missions.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
What critical technology does Chandrayaan-4 need that SpaDeX demonstrated?

A) Solar panel deployment
B) In-orbit docking — the ascent module must dock with the return vehicle in lunar orbit
C) Rocket engine ignition
D) Parachute deployment

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-4 requires in-orbit docking in lunar orbit because the ascent module carrying samples from the surface must rendezvous and dock with the orbiter/return vehicle that will bring the samples back to Earth. SpaDeX's successful demonstration of autonomous docking technology was a critical precursor for this mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What is Shukrayaan?

A) A follow-up mission to Mars
B) India's planned Venus orbiter mission
C) A satellite to study Jupiter
D) A new type of launch vehicle

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Shukrayaan is India's proposed mission to Venus. It would study Venus's atmosphere, surface, and interaction with solar radiation. Venus is a fascinating target because despite being similar in size to Earth, it has extreme conditions — a surface temperature of 460°C, atmospheric pressure 90 times Earth's, and clouds of sulphuric acid. The mission is planned for the late 2020s.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9
What is the Bharatiya Antariksha Station (BAS)?

A) ISRO's mission control centre
B) India's planned modular space station
C) A proposed Moon colony
D) A new rocket engine testing facility

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** BAS (Bharatiya Antariksha Station, meaning "Indian Space Station") is India's planned space station. The first module (BAS-1) is planned for launch by around 2028. It will be built incrementally over multiple launches using docking technology proven by SpaDeX, and will support crew stays of 15-20 days initially. It builds on Gaganyaan technology.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What are NVS-01 and NVS-02?

A) New navigation satellites launched by ISRO for India's NavIC system
B) Venus orbiters
C) Weather satellites
D) Lunar communication satellites

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** NVS-01 (launched May 2023 on GSLV-F12) and NVS-02 (launched January 2025 on GSLV-F15) are second-generation NavIC navigation satellites. They replace aging first-generation IRNSS satellites and include an L1 signal compatible with civilian GPS receivers, improving NavIC's accessibility for regular users.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What is NSIL (NewSpace India Limited)?

A) A private Indian space company
B) ISRO's commercial arm for technology transfer and commercial launches
C) A space agency of another country
D) A satellite manufacturing company in the USA

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NSIL (NewSpace India Limited) is ISRO's commercial and technology transfer arm. It has conducted commercial launches including deploying OneWeb broadband satellites using LVM3 (36 satellites each in October 2022 and March 2023), demonstrating India's capability in the global commercial launch market.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / NSIL

### Question 12
Which of these XPoSat instruments is the primary payload?

A) XSPECT
B) POLIX
C) VELC
D) SUIT

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** POLIX (Polarimeter Instrument in X-rays) is XPoSat's primary payload, built by the Raman Research Institute (RRI) in Bengaluru. It measures X-ray polarization in the 8-30 keV energy range. XSPECT is the secondary instrument for X-ray spectroscopy and timing. VELC and SUIT are instruments on Aditya-L1, not XPoSat.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO / RRI

### Question 13
Which countries have previously returned samples from the Moon?

A) Only the USA
B) USA, Soviet Union, and China
C) USA and China only
D) USA, Russia, China, and Japan

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Three countries have returned samples from the Moon: the USA (Apollo programme, 1969-1972, returned 382 kg), the Soviet Union (Luna 16/20/24, 1970-1976, returned ~326 grams via robotic missions), and China (Chang'e-5, December 2020, returned 1,731 grams). If Chandrayaan-4 succeeds, India would join this exclusive group.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 15
What was INSAT-3DS launched for?

A) Navigation
B) Advanced weather forecasting, disaster warning, and search-and-rescue
C) Communication only
D) Scientific research on black holes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** INSAT-3DS, launched on 17 February 2024 on GSLV-F14, is an advanced meteorological satellite following in the footsteps of INSAT-3D and INSAT-3DR. It carries an imager, sounder, data relay transponder, and satellite-aided search and rescue transponder for weather forecasting, disaster warning, and maritime safety.

### Question 16
How does XPoSat compare to NASA's IXPE?

A) They are identical missions
B) XPoSat is the world's second X-ray polarimetry satellite; IXPE was the first (2021). They observe complementary energy ranges
C) XPoSat was launched before IXPE
D) They have no relation to each other

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's IXPE (Imaging X-ray Polarimetry Explorer), launched in December 2021, was the world's first dedicated X-ray polarimetry satellite, observing in the 2-8 keV range. XPoSat is the second, with POLIX covering 8-30 keV — a higher energy range that complements IXPE. Together they provide polarimetric coverage across a wider X-ray energy spectrum.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO / NASA

### Question 17
What does NISAR map, and how often?

A) The Moon's surface every month
B) The entire Earth's surface every 12 days
C) Mars every 6 months
D) India only, every day

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NISAR will map the entire Earth's surface every 12 days using its dual-frequency SAR. This frequent repeat coverage allows scientists to detect and track changes — deforestation, glacier movement, ground deformation from earthquakes, volcanic activity, urban subsidence — on timescales of weeks. The 12-day revisit rate makes NISAR one of the most ambitious Earth observation missions ever.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 18
Which of the following about Indian space missions is FALSE?

A) XPoSat studies X-ray polarization from cosmic sources
B) Shukrayaan targets Venus
C) NISAR is a purely Indian mission with no international collaboration
D) Chandrayaan-4 plans to return lunar samples to Earth

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The false statement is C. NISAR is a joint NASA-ISRO mission, not a purely Indian one. NASA provided the L-band SAR and ISRO provided the S-band SAR and spacecraft bus. At approximately $1.5 billion combined investment, it is one of the most significant US-India space collaborations. All other statements are correct.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 20
Why is lunar sample return (Chandrayaan-4) significantly more complex than a landing mission (Chandrayaan-3)?

A) It isn't — they are equally complex
B) It requires landing, sample collection, launching from the Moon, docking in lunar orbit, and returning to Earth
C) It only requires a larger rover
D) It just needs a bigger rocket

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A sample return mission involves multiple critical phases beyond a landing: the lander must collect samples, an ascent module must launch from the lunar surface (a first for India), the ascent module must find and dock with an orbiting return vehicle in lunar orbit, and finally the return capsule must survive re-entry into Earth's atmosphere. Each phase is independently complex and failure at any point loses the mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO
