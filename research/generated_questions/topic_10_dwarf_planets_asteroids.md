## Topic 10 – Dwarf Planets, Asteroids, Comets and Meteors

### Questions for Topic 10

### Question 1
According to the IAU (International Astronomical Union), what distinguishes a dwarf planet from a full planet?

A) A dwarf planet is smaller than 1,000 km
B) A dwarf planet has not cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit
C) A dwarf planet does not orbit the Sun
D) A dwarf planet is not spherical

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The IAU defined in 2006 that a planet must: (1) orbit the Sun, (2) have enough mass for gravity to make it roughly spherical, and (3) have cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit. A dwarf planet meets the first two criteria but NOT the third — it has not gravitationally dominated its orbital zone by clearing away other objects.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** IAU 2006 / Planetary Science

### Question 2
When was Pluto reclassified from a planet to a dwarf planet?

A) 1930
B) 1992
C) 2006
D) 2015

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006 by the International Astronomical Union (IAU). Pluto had been considered the 9th planet since its discovery in 1930, but the discovery of similar-sized objects in the Kuiper Belt (especially Eris in 2005) prompted the IAU to create a formal definition of "planet" — which Pluto did not fully meet because it has not cleared its orbital neighbourhood.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** IAU 2006

### Question 3
Who discovered Pluto and in what year?

A) William Herschel in 1781
B) Clyde Tombaugh in 1930
C) Johann Galle in 1846
D) Galileo Galilei in 1610

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pluto was discovered by American astronomer Clyde Tombaugh on February 18, 1930, at the Lowell Observatory in Arizona. Tombaugh was systematically comparing photographic plates of the night sky, looking for the predicted "Planet X." The name "Pluto" was suggested by an 11-year-old English girl, Venetia Burney.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy History

### Question 4
How does Pluto's size compare to Earth's Moon?

A) Pluto is much larger than the Moon
B) Pluto is about the same size as the Moon
C) Pluto is smaller than the Moon
D) Pluto is exactly half the Moon's size

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Pluto's diameter is approximately 2,377 km, which is significantly smaller than Earth's Moon (3,474 km diameter). Pluto is only about 68% the diameter of our Moon. This small size was one factor that led to questions about whether Pluto truly deserved planet status, contributing to its 2006 reclassification.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 5
What is the name of Pluto's largest moon?

A) Titan
B) Charon
C) Triton
D) Europa

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Charon is Pluto's largest moon with a diameter of about 1,212 km — nearly half of Pluto's own diameter. This makes the Pluto-Charon system almost a "double dwarf planet," as they orbit a common center of gravity (barycenter) that lies between them in space. Pluto also has four smaller moons: Nix, Hydra, Kerberos, and Styx.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 6
Which NASA mission flew by Pluto in 2015 and sent back the first close-up images?

A) Voyager 2
B) Cassini
C) New Horizons
D) OSIRIS-REx

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew by Pluto on July 14, 2015, after a journey of over 9 years from Earth. It provided the first detailed close-up images of Pluto, revealing a heart-shaped nitrogen ice plain called Tombaugh Regio (named after Pluto's discoverer), mountains made of water ice, and a more complex and geologically active world than anyone expected.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA New Horizons

### Question 7
What is Ceres?

A) The largest asteroid and a dwarf planet in the asteroid belt
B) A comet that visits the inner solar system every 76 years
C) Neptune's largest moon
D) A planet beyond Neptune

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt with a diameter of approximately 940 km. It is classified as both an asteroid and a dwarf planet — making it the only dwarf planet in the inner solar system. Discovered by Giuseppe Piazzi in 1801, Ceres was originally classified as a planet. NASA's Dawn mission orbited Ceres from 2015 to 2018, finding bright spots (sodium carbonate deposits).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA Dawn / Planetary Science

### Question 8
Where is the asteroid belt located?

A) Between Earth and Mars
B) Between Mars and Jupiter
C) Between Jupiter and Saturn
D) Beyond Neptune

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The asteroid belt is located between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter, approximately 2.2 to 3.2 AU from the Sun. It contains millions of asteroids — rocky and metallic objects that are remnants from the early solar system. Jupiter's strong gravity prevented these objects from coalescing into a full planet.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 9
Despite what movies often show, why can spacecraft safely pass through the asteroid belt?

A) Asteroids are very small and invisible
B) Asteroids are widely spaced — millions of kilometers apart
C) Spacecraft use shields to deflect asteroids
D) The asteroid belt only exists sometimes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Despite popular depictions in science fiction films, asteroids in the asteroid belt are extremely widely spaced — typically millions of kilometers apart. The total mass of all asteroids in the belt is only about 4% of the Moon's mass, spread across an enormous volume of space. Multiple spacecraft have passed through the asteroid belt safely with no need for evasive maneuvers.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 10
What are asteroids primarily made of?

A) Ice and gas
B) Rock and metal
C) Liquid water
D) Diamond and crystal

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Asteroids are primarily composed of rock and metal — they are remnants of the early solar system's building materials that never formed into a planet. Different types of asteroids have different compositions: C-type (carbonaceous, dark, most common), S-type (silicate/stony), and M-type (metallic, rich in iron and nickel). This distinguishes them from comets, which are primarily ice and dust.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 11
What are Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs)?

A) Asteroids that have crashed into Earth
B) Asteroids whose orbits bring them close to Earth, potentially hazardous if large enough
C) Asteroids visible from Earth with the naked eye
D) Asteroids in the asteroid belt closest to Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Near-Earth Asteroids (NEAs) are asteroids whose orbits bring them into the vicinity of Earth's orbit. Some are classified as Potentially Hazardous Asteroids (PHAs) if they are large enough (>140 meters) and their orbits pass close enough to Earth. NASA's Center for Near Earth Object Studies (CNEOS) and other agencies track these objects to provide advance warning of any potential impact threats.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA CNEOS / Planetary Defense

### Question 12
What was the DART mission?

A) NASA's first Mars sample return mission
B) NASA's first planetary defense test — it deliberately crashed into asteroid Dimorphos to change its orbit
C) A European mission to study dark matter
D) A JAXA asteroid sample return mission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test) was NASA's first planetary defense test, which on September 26, 2022, deliberately crashed a spacecraft into Dimorphos (a small moon of asteroid Didymos) at about 22,000 km/h. The impact successfully changed Dimorphos's orbital period by about 33 minutes — demonstrating that kinetic impact could deflect a potentially hazardous asteroid.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA DART Mission

### Question 13
What are comets primarily made of?

A) Rock and metal like asteroids
B) Pure water ice
C) Ice, dust, and rock — often called "dirty snowballs"
D) Gas and plasma

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Comets are composed of a mixture of ice (water ice, frozen CO₂, frozen methane, ammonia), dust, and rock — often described as "dirty snowballs" or "icy dirt balls." When a comet approaches the Sun, the ice sublimates (turns directly from solid to gas), creating the characteristic coma and tails. This composition distinguishes comets from the primarily rocky/metallic asteroids.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Cometary Science

### Question 14
What are the parts of a comet when it is near the Sun?

A) Core, surface, and atmosphere
B) Nucleus, coma, ion tail, and dust tail
C) Head, body, and legs
D) Center, rings, and moons

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When near the Sun, a comet has four main parts: (1) Nucleus — the solid core of ice and rock (1-50 km); (2) Coma — a cloud of gas and dust surrounding the nucleus as ice sublimates; (3) Ion tail (gas tail) — bluish, straight, pushed directly away from the Sun by solar wind; (4) Dust tail — whitish, slightly curved, following the comet's orbital path.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Cometary Science

### Question 15
A comet's tail always points:

A) In the direction the comet is traveling
B) Opposite to the direction the comet is traveling
C) Away from the Sun, regardless of the comet's direction of travel
D) Toward the nearest planet

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A comet's tails always point away from the Sun, regardless of which direction the comet is traveling. This is because the tails are pushed outward by solar wind (for the ion tail) and solar radiation pressure (for the dust tail). When a comet is moving away from the Sun, its tails actually extend in front of it — a counterintuitive fact.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Cometary Science / Solar Physics

### Question 16
Where do short-period comets (orbital period less than 200 years) originate?

A) The Oort Cloud
B) The Kuiper Belt
C) The asteroid belt
D) The Sun's corona

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Short-period comets, with orbital periods less than 200 years, generally originate from the Kuiper Belt — a region of icy bodies extending from about 30 to 50 AU from the Sun, beyond Neptune's orbit. Gravitational interactions (particularly with Neptune) can send Kuiper Belt objects into the inner solar system as short-period comets. Halley's Comet (~76-year period) is the most famous example.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Cometary Science / Solar System Science

### Question 17
Where do long-period comets (orbital period greater than 200 years) originate?

A) The Kuiper Belt
B) The asteroid belt
C) The Oort Cloud
D) Other star systems

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Long-period comets originate from the Oort Cloud, a theoretical spherical shell of icy bodies extending from roughly 2,000 to 100,000 AU from the Sun. The Oort Cloud is so distant that passing stars and galactic tides can disturb objects in it, sending them on long, elliptical orbits into the inner solar system. These comets can take thousands or even millions of years to complete one orbit.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Cometary Science / Solar System Science

### Question 18
Halley's Comet returns to the inner solar system approximately every:

A) 12 years
B) 29 years
C) 76 years
D) 200 years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Halley's Comet has an orbital period of approximately 76 years, making it the most famous periodic comet. It last appeared in 1986 and will next be visible from Earth in 2061. Halley's Comet has been observed and recorded for over 2,000 years — ancient Chinese, Babylonian, and European records describe its appearances.

**Difficulty:** Easy

### Question 19
What was special about the Rosetta mission?

A) It was the first mission to orbit the Sun
B) It achieved the first landing on a comet
C) It was the first mission to photograph an asteroid
D) It discovered the first exoplanet

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ESA's Rosetta mission achieved a historic first by landing its Philae probe on Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko on November 12, 2014 — the first controlled landing on a comet. Rosetta orbited the comet for over two years, studying its composition, surface, and activity as it approached the Sun. The mission revealed comets to be far more complex than previously thought.

### Question 20
What is the correct distinction between a meteoroid, a meteor, and a meteorite?

A) They are three different names for the same object
B) Meteoroid is in space; meteor is the streak of light in Earth's atmosphere; meteorite is what survives to reach the ground
C) A meteor is larger than a meteoroid, and a meteorite is largest
D) Meteoroid orbits the Sun; meteor orbits Earth; meteorite orbits the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** These three terms describe the same type of object at different stages: A meteoroid is a small rocky or metallic object in space (dust-sized to about 1 meter). A meteor is the streak of light ("shooting star") produced when a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere and burns up due to friction. A meteorite is a meteoroid that survives its fiery passage through the atmosphere and lands on Earth's surface.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / Meteor Science

### Question 21
Meteor showers occur when:

A) Asteroids collide in the asteroid belt
B) Earth passes through debris left behind by a comet
C) The Sun sends extra particles toward Earth
D) Planets align and pull meteoroids toward Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Meteor showers occur when Earth's orbit crosses a trail of debris left behind by a comet (or occasionally an asteroid). As Earth plows through this debris, many meteoroids enter the atmosphere and burn up as meteors, creating the appearance of a "shower." Major annual meteor showers include the Perseids (August, from Comet Swift-Tuttle) and Geminids (December, from asteroid 3200 Phaethon).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Meteor Science

### Question 22
The Perseids meteor shower occurs in August and is associated with which comet?

A) Halley's Comet
B) Comet Hale-Bopp
C) Comet Swift-Tuttle
D) Comet Tempel-Tuttle

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Perseids meteor shower, one of the most popular annual meteor showers visible in August, is caused by Earth passing through debris left by Comet Swift-Tuttle. The meteors appear to radiate from the constellation Perseus, hence the name "Perseids." The Leonids (November) are from Comet Tempel-Tuttle, and the Geminids (December) are unusually from an asteroid, not a comet.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Meteor Science / Observational Astronomy

### Question 23
What makes the Geminids meteor shower unusual compared to most other meteor showers?

A) It is the longest-lasting meteor shower
B) It is caused by debris from an asteroid (3200 Phaethon) rather than a comet
C) It can only be seen from the Southern Hemisphere
D) It produces meteors that travel upward

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Geminids meteor shower (December) is unusual because it is produced by debris from asteroid 3200 Phaethon rather than a comet. Most meteor showers are caused by cometary debris. The reason 3200 Phaethon sheds debris like a comet is debated — it may be a "rock comet" (an asteroid that behaves partly like a comet when close to the Sun).

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Meteor Science

### Question 24
Which of the following is a recognized dwarf planet?

A) Ceres, Pluto, Eris, Makemake, and Haumea
B) Only Pluto and Ceres
C) Pluto, Ceres, and Sedna
D) Only Pluto

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** As of the IAU's current classification, five objects are officially recognized as dwarf planets: Pluto, Eris, Ceres, Makemake, and Haumea. Pluto, Eris, Makemake, and Haumea are in the outer solar system (Kuiper Belt region), while Ceres is in the asteroid belt. There are likely many more dwarf planets awaiting formal classification, particularly in the Kuiper Belt.

### Question 25
What discovery triggered the debate that led to Pluto's reclassification?

A) The discovery of the asteroid belt
B) The discovery of Eris in 2005, which is slightly more massive than Pluto
C) The Voyager 2 flyby of Neptune
D) The discovery of water on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The discovery of Eris in 2005 by Mike Brown's team directly triggered the "what is a planet?" debate. Eris is slightly more massive than Pluto (though slightly smaller in diameter), and it raised the question: if Pluto is a planet, shouldn't Eris be too? This led the IAU in 2006 to create a formal definition of "planet" that excluded both Pluto and Eris, classifying them as dwarf planets.

### Question 28

### Question 29

### Question 30
What is the estimated distance range of the Oort Cloud from the Sun?

A) 30-50 AU
B) 100-1,000 AU
C) 2,000-100,000 AU
D) Over 1 million AU

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Oort Cloud is estimated to extend from roughly 2,000 to 100,000 AU from the Sun — so distant that it is nearly a quarter of the way to the nearest star (Proxima Centauri at ~4.2 light years ≈ 265,000 AU). No spacecraft has ever reached the Oort Cloud, and its existence is inferred from the orbits of long-period comets. The Kuiper Belt (30-50 AU) is much closer.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Solar System Science

### Question 31
NASA's Dawn mission discovered bright spots on Ceres. What were they identified as?

A) Water ice pools
B) Sodium carbonate deposits
C) Diamond formations
D) Reflected sunlight from metal

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's Dawn mission (2015-2018) discovered puzzling bright spots in Occator Crater on Ceres. Analysis revealed they were primarily sodium carbonate deposits — a type of salt that likely reached the surface through cryovolcanic activity. Briny water from beneath Ceres's surface welled up through fractures and evaporated, leaving behind these bright salt deposits. This suggested Ceres had (or has) a subsurface ocean.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA Dawn Mission

### Question 32
In the Kuiper Belt, objects orbit the Sun at approximately what distance?

A) 1-5 AU
B) 5-30 AU
C) 30-50 AU
D) 100-1,000 AU

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Kuiper Belt extends from approximately 30 to 50 AU from the Sun, beginning just beyond Neptune's orbit. It contains thousands of icy bodies, including dwarf planets (Pluto, Eris, Makemake, Haumea) and is the source of short-period comets. The Kuiper Belt is fundamentally different from the more distant Oort Cloud (2,000-100,000 AU), which is the source of long-period comets.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Solar System Science

### Question 33
Why was Pluto's orbit considered unusual even when it was classified as a planet?

A) Pluto orbits in the opposite direction to other planets
B) Pluto's orbit is highly elliptical and inclined 17° to the ecliptic plane
C) Pluto orbits between Earth and Mars
D) Pluto's orbit is perfectly circular

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pluto's orbit is significantly different from the eight planets. It is highly elliptical (sometimes bringing Pluto closer to the Sun than Neptune) and inclined about 17° to the ecliptic plane (the plane in which most planets orbit). This unusual orbit was one of the clues that Pluto was different from the other planets and might belong to a different population of objects (the Kuiper Belt).

### Question 35
A "shooting star" is actually:

A) A star falling from the sky
B) A meteor — a meteoroid burning up in Earth's atmosphere
C) A comet passing close to Earth
D) A satellite re-entering the atmosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A "shooting star" is actually a meteor — the streak of light produced when a small meteoroid (typically grain-of-sand to pebble-sized) enters Earth's atmosphere at high speed (11-72 km/s) and burns up due to friction with air molecules. Despite the name, shooting stars have nothing to do with actual stars. On any clear dark night, you can typically see 5-10 meteors per hour.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Meteor Science / Basic Astronomy
