## Topic 32 – Indian Meteorological and Scientific Satellites

### Questions for Topic 32

### Question 1
What was India's first dedicated meteorological (weather) satellite?

A) INSAT-1A
B) Aryabhata
C) Kalpana-1
D) INSAT-3D

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Kalpana-1, launched on September 12, 2002, by PSLV-C4, was India's first satellite exclusively dedicated to weather monitoring. While earlier INSAT communication satellites carried weather instruments as secondary payloads, Kalpana-1 was the first satellite designed solely for meteorological observation.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / IMD

### Question 2
After whom was the Kalpana-1 satellite named?

A) Kalpana Saroj, an Indian businesswoman
B) Kalpana Chawla, the Indian-American astronaut
C) Kalpana Lajmi, an Indian filmmaker
D) Kalpana Devi, a mythological figure

Kalpana Chawla was the first woman of Indian origin to go to space.

### Question 3

### Question 4
What does VHRR stand for in the context of weather satellites?

A) Very High Range Radar
B) Very High Resolution Radiometer
C) Variable Heat Resistant Receiver
D) Visible and Hidden Range Recorder

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** VHRR stands for Very High Resolution Radiometer. It is a key weather monitoring instrument carried on INSAT and Kalpana-1 satellites. VHRR captures images of cloud patterns, atmospheric moisture, and surface temperature in visible and infrared wavelengths, providing the data used for weather forecasting and cyclone tracking.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
How did India's weather monitoring from space originally begin?

A) With a dedicated weather satellite programme from the start
B) As secondary weather payloads on INSAT communication satellites
C) By purchasing weather data from foreign countries
D) Through ground-based radar systems only

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India's space-based weather monitoring began as a "secondary payload" on INSAT communication satellites. INSAT-1B (1983) and subsequent INSAT satellites carried VHRR instruments alongside their communication payloads. This dual-purpose approach allowed India to build weather monitoring capability while the communication satellites served their primary function.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
Which are India's advanced dedicated meteorological satellites currently in operation?

A) IRS-1A and IRS-1B
B) INSAT-3D and INSAT-3DR
C) Cartosat-1 and Cartosat-2
D) RISAT-1 and RISAT-2

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** INSAT-3D (launched 2013) and INSAT-3DR (launched 2016) are India's current advanced dedicated meteorological satellites. They carry a 6-channel imager, 19-channel sounder for atmospheric profiling, Data Relay Transponder, and Satellite Aided Search and Rescue (SAS&R) equipment. They provide critical data for weather forecasting, cyclone tracking, and fog detection.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / IMD

### Question 7
What is the Megha-Tropiques satellite, and which countries collaborated on it?

A) An Indian navigation satellite built with Russian help
B) An Indo-French joint mission studying tropical weather and climate
C) A Chinese-Indian joint Mars mission
D) A Japanese-Indian satellite for ocean monitoring

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Megha-Tropiques is a joint mission between ISRO (India) and CNES (the French space agency), launched in October 2011 by PSLV-C18. It studies tropical weather — particularly the water cycle and energy budget in the tropics. "Megha" means "cloud" in Sanskrit, and "Tropiques" means "tropics" in French, reflecting the bilateral collaboration.

### Question 8
What special orbit does Megha-Tropiques use, and why?

A) Geostationary orbit to remain fixed over India
B) Low-inclination orbit (20°) to spend most time over the tropics
C) Polar orbit to cover the entire Earth
D) Highly elliptical Molniya orbit for northern hemisphere coverage

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Megha-Tropiques orbits at a low inclination of 20°, which means it spends most of its orbital time passing over tropical regions between roughly 20°N and 20°S latitude. This is deliberate — since the mission studies tropical weather and climate, a low-inclination orbit maximises the time the satellite spends over the tropics, providing frequent observations of tropical weather systems.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO / CNES

### Question 9
How has India's cyclone prediction improved thanks to weather satellites?

A) Cyclone prediction has not improved significantly
B) Prediction error reduced from 100+ km to approximately 50 km, and advance warnings now save thousands of lives
C) India can now prevent cyclones from forming
D) Cyclone prediction improved only due to ground-based radar, not satellites

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India's cyclone prediction accuracy has dramatically improved, with track prediction error reducing from over 100 km to approximately 50 km, largely due to data from INSAT-3D/3DR satellites. The contrast is stark: the 1999 Odisha super cyclone killed approximately 10,000 people due to poor warnings, while Cyclone Phailin (2013) caused only 47 casualties thanks to satellite-based advance warnings enabling massive evacuations.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** IMD / ISRO

### Question 10
What is the SARAL satellite, and what does it measure?

A) A navigation satellite measuring precise positions
B) An Indo-French satellite measuring ocean surface height (altimetry)
C) A communication satellite for rural India
D) A defence surveillance satellite

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SARAL (Satellite with ARgos and ALtika) is an Indo-French joint mission between ISRO and CNES, launched in February 2013 by PSLV-C20. It carries the AltiKa instrument — a Ka-band altimeter that measures ocean surface height with high precision. This data is used for studying ocean circulation, monitoring sea level changes, and coastal zone management.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / CNES

### Question 11
What instrument does SCATSAT-1 carry, and what does it measure?

A) A camera for photographing Earth's surface
B) A scatterometer for measuring ocean surface wind speed and direction
C) A spectrometer for studying star composition
D) A magnetometer for measuring Earth's magnetic field

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SCATSAT-1, launched in 2016 by PSLV-C35, carries a scatterometer — an instrument that sends microwave pulses toward the ocean surface and measures the scattered return signal. The pattern of scattering reveals wind speed and direction over the ocean surface. This data is critical for weather forecasting, cyclone prediction, and monsoon monitoring.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
What special feature do INSAT-3D and INSAT-3DR carry for maritime safety?

A) Anti-piracy radar
B) Satellite Aided Search and Rescue (SAS&R) equipment
C) Automated ship navigation control
D) Underwater sonar

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** INSAT-3D and INSAT-3DR carry Satellite Aided Search and Rescue (SAS&R) transponders. These detect distress signals transmitted by emergency beacons on ships and aircraft. When a vessel or aircraft sends a distress signal, the satellite relays the signal and location information to rescue coordination centres, enabling rapid search and rescue operations.

### Question 17

### Question 18
What orbit type are India's dedicated weather satellites (Kalpana-1, INSAT-3D) placed in?

A) Low Earth Orbit (LEO)
B) Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)
C) Geostationary Orbit (GEO)
D) Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** India's dedicated weather satellites are placed in Geostationary Orbit (GEO) at approximately 35,786 km altitude. In GEO, the satellite appears stationary over a fixed point on the equator, providing continuous, uninterrupted observation of weather patterns over the Indian region. This is essential for real-time weather monitoring and cyclone tracking — you need to watch the weather develop, not just take snapshots.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 22
What is an altimeter in the context of ocean-observing satellites like SARAL?

A) An instrument that measures altitude of aircraft
B) An instrument that precisely measures ocean surface height from space
C) An instrument that measures atmospheric temperature
D) An instrument that measures wind speed

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In ocean-observing satellites, an altimeter sends radar or microwave pulses down to the ocean surface and measures the precise time for the signal to bounce back. This determines the ocean surface height with centimetre-level accuracy. Changes in ocean surface height reveal ocean currents, sea level rise, tidal patterns, and undersea geological features.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / CNES

### Question 23
What makes a scatterometer different from a regular camera on a satellite?

A) Scatterometers are just higher-resolution cameras
B) Scatterometers emit microwave signals and analyse the return scatter pattern to measure wind
C) Scatterometers can only work during daytime
D) There is no difference — they are the same technology

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A scatterometer is an active microwave instrument — it emits microwave pulses toward the ocean surface and analyses how the signals scatter back. The roughness of the ocean surface (caused by wind) affects the scatter pattern, allowing the instrument to determine wind speed and direction. Unlike cameras that passively record light, scatterometers actively probe the surface, working in all weather conditions and at night.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 27
Which of the following statements about Indian weather satellites is FALSE?
