## Topic 43 – Human Spaceflight — History and Key Missions

### Questions for Topic 43

### Question 1
Who was the first human to travel to space?

A) Neil Armstrong
B) Alan Shepard
C) Yuri Gagarin
D) John Glenn

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Yuri Gagarin of the Soviet Union became the first human in space on 12 April 1961 aboard Vostok 1. His flight lasted 108 minutes and completed a single orbit of Earth. This date is celebrated internationally as "Yuri's Night" and Cosmonautics Day in Russia.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 2
Who was the first woman in space?

A) Sally Ride
B) Valentina Tereshkova
C) Kalpana Chawla
D) Sunita Williams

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Valentina Tereshkova of the Soviet Union became the first woman in space on 16 June 1963 aboard Vostok 6. She orbited Earth 48 times over nearly 3 days. Sally Ride became the first American woman in space 20 years later in 1983.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 3
Who were the first humans to walk on the Moon, and when?

A) Yuri Gagarin and Valentina Tereshkova in 1961
B) Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on 20 July 1969
C) John Glenn and Alan Shepard in 1962
D) Jim Lovell and Fred Haise in 1970

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon on 20 July 1969 during the Apollo 11 mission, while Michael Collins orbited above in the Command Module. Armstrong's first words on the Moon — "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind" — became one of the most famous quotes in history.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / Space History

### Question 4
How many people have walked on the Moon?

A) 2
B) 6
C) 12
D) 24

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A total of 12 people have walked on the Moon, all of them American men who flew on Apollo missions between 1969 and 1972. There were 6 successful Moon landing missions (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17), with 2 astronauts walking on the surface during each mission while a third orbited above.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 5
Who was the first Indian citizen to travel to space?

A) Kalpana Chawla
B) Sunita Williams
C) Rakesh Sharma
D) Shubhanshu Shukla

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Wing Commander Rakesh Sharma became the first Indian citizen in space on 3 April 1984, flying aboard the Soviet Soyuz T-11 spacecraft to the Salyut 7 space station. He spent 7 days 21 hours in space. Kalpana Chawla, while of Indian origin, was a US citizen — an Indian-American astronaut.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History / ISRO

### Question 6
What was Rakesh Sharma's famous reply when PM Indira Gandhi asked how India looked from space?

A) "India is beautiful"
B) "Saare jahan se achha" (Better than the whole world)
C) "Very green and blue"
D) "Like a diamond"

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked Rakesh Sharma how India looked from space, he replied "Saare jahan se achha" — a line from the famous patriotic poem by Muhammad Iqbal, meaning "Better than the whole world." This response has become one of the most quoted moments in Indian space history.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 7
What was the Apollo 13 mission famous for?

A) The first Moon landing
B) A "successful failure" — the crew survived after an oxygen tank explosion
C) The longest Moon walk
D) The first space docking

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Apollo 13 (1970) is called a "successful failure" because an oxygen tank exploded en route to the Moon, forcing the crew (Jim Lovell, Fred Haise, Jack Swigert) to abort the lunar landing. Using the lunar module as a lifeboat, the crew returned safely to Earth through extraordinary problem-solving by the crew and Mission Control. The phrase "Houston, we've had a problem" comes from this mission.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 8
What was the Space Shuttle?

A) A Soviet rocket used to reach the Moon
B) NASA's reusable spacecraft that launched like a rocket and landed like a glider
C) An Indian launch vehicle
D) A Chinese space station module

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Space Shuttle was NASA's partially reusable spacecraft that flew 135 missions from 1981 to 2011. It launched vertically like a rocket but landed on a runway like a glider. Five orbiters were built: Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis, and Endeavour. The Shuttle carried out satellite deployments, ISS construction, and Hubble servicing missions.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 9
What caused the Challenger disaster in 1986?

A) A computer malfunction
B) An O-ring seal failure in a solid rocket booster due to cold weather
C) Running out of fuel
D) A collision with space debris

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch on 28 January 1986, killing all 7 crew members. The cause was the failure of an O-ring rubber seal in one of the solid rocket boosters. Cold temperatures on launch day made the rubber seal brittle and unable to contain hot gases, which burned through and caused structural failure.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 10
What happened in the Columbia disaster, and which Indian-American astronaut was aboard?

A) Columbia failed to launch — no casualties
B) Columbia broke apart during re-entry in 2003; Kalpana Chawla was among the 7 crew killed
C) Columbia was struck by a meteor during orbit
D) Columbia's engines failed; Sunita Williams was aboard

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Space Shuttle Columbia broke apart during re-entry on 1 February 2003, killing all 7 crew members including Kalpana Chawla, an Indian-American astronaut on her second space mission. The cause was damage to the heat shield tiles on the wing's leading edge from foam insulation that broke off the external tank during launch.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 11
When did China first independently send a human into space?

A) 1984
B) 1998
C) 2003
D) 2011

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Yang Liwei became China's first astronaut (taikonaut) on 15 October 2003 aboard Shenzhou 5. This made China the third nation (after the Soviet Union/Russia and the USA) to independently launch a human into space. China has since conducted numerous crewed missions and built its own Tiangong space station.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 12
Who performed the first spacewalk (EVA)?

A) Neil Armstrong
B) Alexei Leonov
C) Ed White
D) Buzz Aldrin

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Soviet cosmonaut Alexei Leonov performed the first spacewalk on 18 March 1965 during the Voskhod 2 mission. He spent about 12 minutes outside the spacecraft. Famously, his space suit inflated in the vacuum of space, making it extremely difficult for him to get back through the airlock — he had to partially deflate his suit to fit back inside.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 13
What is the Artemis programme?

A) NASA's programme to return humans to the Moon
B) ISRO's Mars exploration programme
C) ESA's Jupiter exploration programme
D) China's Venus programme

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Artemis is NASA's programme to return humans to the Moon for the first time since Apollo 17 in 1972. Artemis I (2022) was an uncrewed test flight. Artemis II (April 2026) was the first crewed flight around the Moon since Apollo. Artemis IV (targeted 2028) plans the first lunar landing of the programme.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 14
What was Dennis Tito's claim to fame?

A) First person on Mars
B) First space tourist (2001, paid approximately $20 million to visit the ISS)
C) First commercial pilot to go to space
D) First person to orbit the Moon alone

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** American businessman Dennis Tito became the first space tourist in April 2001, paying approximately $20 million for a trip to the International Space Station aboard a Russian Soyuz spacecraft. His visit marked the beginning of space tourism, which has since grown with companies like SpaceX, Blue Origin, and Virgin Galactic offering civilian spaceflights.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 15
What was the last Apollo Moon landing mission?

A) Apollo 11
B) Apollo 13
C) Apollo 17
D) Apollo 20

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Apollo 17 (December 1972) was the last Apollo Moon landing and the most recent time humans walked on the Moon. Eugene Cernan was the last person to walk on the lunar surface. The Apollo programme was cancelled after Apollo 17 due to budget cuts and shifting priorities, leaving the final planned missions (18, 19, 20) unflown.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 16
What is the SpaceX Crew Dragon?

A) A military spacecraft
B) The first operational commercial crew transport vehicle carrying astronauts to the ISS
C) A Chinese space station module
D) A Russian cargo spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SpaceX's Crew Dragon is the first operational commercial spacecraft to carry astronauts to the ISS under NASA's Commercial Crew Program. Its Demo-2 mission (May 2020) carried Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken, marking the first crewed launch from US soil since the Space Shuttle's retirement in 2011.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / SpaceX

### Question 17
Which of these is the correct order of the first three nations to independently send humans to space?

A) USA, Soviet Union, China
B) Soviet Union, USA, China
C) China, Soviet Union, USA
D) Soviet Union, China, USA

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Soviet Union was first (Yuri Gagarin, 1961), followed by the USA (Alan Shepard, 1961), and then China (Yang Liwei, 2003). There was a 42-year gap between the USA's first crewed flight and China's, during which no new nation achieved independent human spaceflight capability. India aims to become the fourth with Gaganyaan.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 18
How much lunar rock and soil did the Apollo missions bring back to Earth?

A) About 5 kg
B) About 50 kg
C) About 382 kg
D) About 1,000 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The six Apollo Moon landings brought back a total of approximately 382 kg of lunar rocks, soil, and core samples. These samples have been studied by scientists worldwide for decades and continue to provide new insights into the Moon's formation, composition, and history. They remain some of the most valuable scientific specimens ever collected.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA
