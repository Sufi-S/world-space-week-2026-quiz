## Topic 45 – International Space Agencies (ESA, Roscosmos, CNSA, JAXA)

### Questions for Topic 45

### Question 1
What is ESA?

A) Earth Science Academy
B) European Space Agency — a 22-member-state space organisation headquartered in Paris
C) Eastern Space Alliance — an Asian space group
D) Extraterrestrial Search Association

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ESA (European Space Agency) is an intergovernmental organisation with 22 member states, headquartered in Paris, France. Founded in 1975, ESA conducts space exploration, Earth observation, and science missions. It launches rockets from the Guiana Space Centre in French Guiana, near the equator.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ESA

### Question 2
What was the Rosetta mission?

A) A Mars landing mission
B) An ESA mission that orbited a comet and landed the Philae probe on it
C) A NASA telescope
D) A Russian Moon mission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Rosetta was a landmark ESA mission (2004-2016) that orbited comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko and deployed the Philae lander, which made the first-ever landing on a comet on 12 November 2014. This achievement gave scientists unprecedented close-up data about comets, which are remnants from the solar system's formation.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ESA

### Question 3
What is the Soyuz spacecraft, and which country operates it?

A) A Chinese space station module
B) Russia's workhorse crewed spacecraft, used since 1967
C) An American cargo ship
D) India's manned spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Soyuz is Russia's (formerly Soviet Union's) long-serving crewed spacecraft, in use since 1967. It is part of the most-launched rocket family in history. For many years, especially after the Space Shuttle's retirement in 2011, Soyuz was the only way to transport crew to the International Space Station until SpaceX's Crew Dragon became operational in 2020.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Roscosmos

### Question 4
What was the first human-made object in space?

A) Explorer 1 (USA)
B) Sputnik 1 (Soviet Union)
C) Vostok 1 (Soviet Union)
D) Mercury capsule (USA)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957, was the first artificial satellite to orbit Earth. This basketball-sized satellite shocked the world and triggered the Space Race between the USA and USSR. Sputnik's radio signal could be picked up by amateur radio operators worldwide — proof that it was really up there.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 5
What historic first did China's Chang'e 4 achieve?

A) First lunar sample return
B) First-ever landing on the far side of the Moon
C) First Chinese satellite in orbit
D) First Mars landing

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** China's Chang'e 4 achieved the first-ever soft landing on the far side of the Moon on 3 January 2019, deploying the Yutu-2 rover. The far side (sometimes incorrectly called the "dark side") never faces Earth, so a relay satellite (Queqiao) was needed for communication. This was a historic achievement that no other country had attempted.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 6
What is China's space station called?

A) Soyuz
B) Mir
C) Tiangong
D) Skylab

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Tiangong ("Heavenly Palace") is China's modular space station, completed in 2022. It consists of three modules: Tianhe (core), Wentian (lab), and Mengtian (lab). Tiangong is independently built and operated by China and has been permanently crewed since June 2022.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 7
What did JAXA's Hayabusa2 mission accomplish?

A) Landed on Mars
B) Returned samples from asteroid Ryugu
C) Orbited Jupiter
D) Built a space station

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** JAXA's Hayabusa2 mission (2014-2020) successfully returned samples from asteroid Ryugu (162173). The spacecraft landed on the asteroid multiple times and even fired an impactor to create an artificial crater, collecting subsurface material. The samples returned to Earth in December 2020 and contain pristine material from the early solar system.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** JAXA

### Question 8
Where is the world's first and largest space launch facility?

A) Kennedy Space Center, Florida, USA
B) Baikonur Cosmodrome, Kazakhstan
C) Guiana Space Centre, French Guiana
D) Satish Dhawan Space Centre, India

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan is the world's first and largest operational space launch facility. Sputnik 1 was launched from here in 1957, and Yuri Gagarin launched from here in 1961. It remains Russia's primary launch site for crewed missions, leased from Kazakhstan under a long-term agreement.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Roscosmos

### Question 9
What was significant about JAXA's SLIM mission in January 2024?

A) First Japanese satellite in orbit
B) Japan became the 5th country to soft-land on the Moon, achieving precision landing within 55 metres of the target
C) Japan's first Mars mission
D) First Japanese astronaut on the ISS

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLIM (Smart Lander for Investigating Moon) landed on the Moon on 19 January 2024, making Japan the fifth country to achieve a soft landing (after USSR, USA, China, and India). Remarkably, SLIM achieved pinpoint precision landing within just 55 metres of its target — a major technical achievement. The lander landed upside-down but still operated.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** JAXA

### Question 10
What did China's Chang'e 6 achieve in 2024?

A) First Chinese Moon landing
B) First-ever sample return from the far side of the Moon
C) First Chinese space station
D) First Chinese Mars rover

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chang'e 6 (2024) achieved the first-ever sample return from the Moon's far side, collecting approximately 1.9 kg of material. This was an extraordinary technical feat because the far side never faces Earth, requiring a relay satellite for communication. The samples from the far side have a different geological history than near-side samples, making them scientifically invaluable.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 11
Where does ESA launch its rockets, and why?

A) From Paris, France, because that's the headquarters
B) From French Guiana (near equator at ~5°N) for maximum rotational velocity advantage
C) From London, UK, the largest member country
D) From Germany, where most rockets are built

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ESA launches from the Guiana Space Centre in Kourou, French Guiana, in South America. At approximately 5°N latitude, it is very close to the equator, providing maximum benefit from Earth's rotational velocity for launches — especially for geostationary orbit missions. This gives ESA a significant advantage over launch sites at higher latitudes.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ESA

### Question 12
What was the Soviet Venera programme known for?

A) Moon landings
B) Multiple Venus landers, including the first-ever landing on another planet
C) Jupiter flybys
D) Space station construction

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Soviet Venera programme achieved multiple firsts at Venus: Venera 7 (1970) was the first spacecraft to land on another planet, and Venera 9 (1975) returned the first photographs from Venus's surface. Despite Venus's extreme conditions (460°C, 90 atmospheres pressure, sulphuric acid clouds), several Venera landers survived long enough to transmit data.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 13
Which countries have soft-landed on the Moon as of 2024?

A) Only USA and Soviet Union
B) Soviet Union, USA, China, India, and Japan
C) USA, China, and India only
D) Soviet Union and USA only

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Five countries have achieved soft landings on the Moon: the Soviet Union (Luna 9, 1966), the USA (Surveyor 1, 1966; Apollo programme), China (Chang'e 3, 2013; Chang'e 4, 2019), India (Chandrayaan-3, 2023), and Japan (SLIM, 2024). India was the fourth and Japan the fifth to join this exclusive club.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 14
What is BeiDou?

A) China's space station
B) China's global navigation satellite system (similar to GPS)
C) China's Mars rover
D) China's Moon programme

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** BeiDou (BDS) is China's global navigation satellite system, comparable to the USA's GPS. The third generation (BDS-3), completed in 2020, provides global coverage with approximately 35 satellites. "BeiDou" refers to the Big Dipper constellation in Chinese. Unlike India's regional NavIC, BeiDou provides worldwide coverage.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 15
What is the Canadarm?

A) A Canadian rocket
B) A robotic arm on the ISS built by Canada
C) A Canadian satellite
D) A Canadian space suit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Canadarm (and its successor Canadarm2 on the ISS) is a robotic arm built by the Canadian Space Agency (CSA). Canadarm2 on the ISS is used for moving equipment, capturing visiting spacecraft (berthing), and assisting astronauts during spacewalks. It is one of Canada's most recognisable contributions to space exploration.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** CSA

### Question 16
What is JUICE?

A) A liquid fuel used in European rockets
B) ESA's Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer, launched in 2023 to study Ganymede, Callisto, and Europa
C) A Japanese asteroid sampling tool
D) A Russian space station module

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** JUICE (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) is an ESA mission launched in April 2023, en route to the Jupiter system. It will study three of Jupiter's largest moons — Ganymede, Callisto, and Europa — which are believed to have subsurface oceans beneath their icy surfaces, making them potential habitats for life. JUICE will arrive at Jupiter in 2031.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ESA

### Question 17
What is China's Zhurong rover?

A) A lunar rover
B) China's first Mars rover, part of the Tianwen-1 mission
C) A space station module
D) A navigation satellite

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Zhurong is China's first Mars rover, which landed on Mars in May 2021 as part of the Tianwen-1 mission. Named after the ancient Chinese god of fire, Zhurong explored the Utopia Planitia region of Mars. Remarkably, China achieved an orbiter, lander, AND rover in its very first Mars mission — a feat no other country has accomplished on a maiden attempt.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** CNSA

### Question 18
Which of the following correctly pairs a space agency with its country?

A) JAXA — China
B) CNSA — Japan
C) Roscosmos — Russia
D) ESA — United States

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Roscosmos is Russia's space agency. The correct pairings are: JAXA (Japan), CNSA (China), Roscosmos (Russia), ESA (European, 22 member states), NASA (USA), ISRO (India), and CSA (Canada). ESA is not a single country's agency — it is a consortium of 22 European nations.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** International Space Agencies
