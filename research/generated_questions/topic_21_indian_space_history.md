## Topic 21 – The Indian Space Programme — History and Vision

### Questions for Topic 21

### Question 1
What was the predecessor organisation to ISRO, established in 1962?

A) Department of Space (DOS)
B) INCOSPAR (Indian National Committee for Space Research)
C) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL)
D) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** INCOSPAR (Indian National Committee for Space Research) was established in 1962 under the Department of Atomic Energy, with Dr. Vikram Sarabhai as the driving force and support from Dr. Homi Bhabha. INCOSPAR was later reorganised into ISRO in 1969.

### Question 2
When was ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) formally established?

A) November 21, 1963
B) August 15, 1969
C) April 19, 1975
D) July 18, 1980

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISRO was formally established on August 15, 1969 — India's Independence Day. It replaced INCOSPAR and was founded with Dr. Vikram Sarabhai as its first Chairman. The date was symbolically chosen to link India's quest for space self-reliance with its national independence.

B) Thumba, Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala

**Explanation:** India's first sounding rocket was launched from the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, on November 21, 1963. Thumba was chosen because of its proximity to the magnetic equator, which is ideal for studying the equatorial ionosphere.

### Question 7
What was India's first satellite, and when was it launched?

A) Rohini RS-1, launched in 1980
B) APPLE, launched in 1981
C) Aryabhata, launched on April 19, 1975
D) Bhaskara-I, launched in 1979

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Aryabhata was India's first satellite, launched on April 19, 1975. It was named after the ancient Indian mathematician Aryabhata. The satellite weighed 360 kg and conducted experiments in X-ray astronomy, aeronomy, and solar physics. Importantly, it was launched by a Soviet Kosmos-3M rocket from Kapustin Yar, USSR — India did not yet have its own launch vehicle.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
Who launched India's first satellite Aryabhata into space?

A) India, using the SLV-3
B) The United States, using a Delta rocket
C) The Soviet Union, using a Kosmos-3M rocket
D) France, using an Ariane rocket

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Although Aryabhata was designed and built in India, it was launched by the Soviet Union using a Kosmos-3M rocket from Kapustin Yar on April 19, 1975. India did not develop its own orbital launch capability until the SLV-3 success in 1980. This is an important distinction — India built the satellite but relied on the USSR for launch.

### Question 9

A) 1975, with Aryabhata
B) July 18, 1980, with SLV-3 launching Rohini RS-1

The project director was APJ Abdul Kalam, who later became the President of India. India joined the USSR, USA, France, Japan, and China in this exclusive club.

### Question 14

### Question 18

### Question 24
What is the concept of "self-reliance" (Atmanirbharta) in the context of India's space programme?

A) India buys all its technology from other countries
B) When denied technology by others, India develops its own — as demonstrated by the cryogenic engine and NavIC
C) India only launches satellites for itself, never for other countries
D) India does not cooperate with any other space agency

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Self-reliance is a defining trait of India's space programme. When Russia was pressured to cancel cryogenic technology transfer under MTCR, ISRO developed its own cryogenic engine over ~20 years. When India was denied GPS precision data during the Kargil War (1999), India built its own navigation system, NavIC. This spirit of developing indigenous alternatives when denied technology exemplifies Atmanirbharta.

### Question 38
In the early 1990s, why did Russia cancel the transfer of cryogenic engine technology to India?

A) India could not afford the cost
B) The USA pressured Russia under MTCR (Missile Technology Control Regime), and Russia cancelled the technology transfer
C) The technology did not work
D) India decided it did not need the technology

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In 1991, India signed a deal with Russia's Glavkosmos for cryogenic engines and technology transfer. However, the United States pressured Russia under the Missile Technology Control Regime (MTCR), arguing that the technology could be used for missile development. Russia cancelled the technology transfer, delivering only 7 ready-made engines without know-how. This forced ISRO to develop its own cryogenic engine — a journey that took nearly 20 years but resulted in India mastering this critical technology independently.

**Source:** ISRO / Space Policy History
