## Topic 4 – Mercury and Venus — The Inner Planets

### Questions for Topic 4

### Question 1
Which planet is the closest to the Sun?

A) Venus
B) Mars
C) Mercury
D) Earth

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mercury is the first planet from the Sun, orbiting at an average distance of about 58 million km. Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury is not the hottest planet — Venus holds that record due to its extreme greenhouse effect.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 2
Which is the smallest planet in the solar system?

A) Mars
B) Pluto
C) Mercury
D) Venus

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system with a diameter of about 4,879 km (about 38% of Earth's diameter). Pluto, which is even smaller, was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006. Before Pluto's reclassification, Mercury was the second smallest.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 3
Although Mercury is closest to the Sun, which planet is actually the hottest?

A) Mercury
B) Venus
C) Mars
D) Jupiter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Venus is the hottest planet with a surface temperature of about 462°C, even hotter than Mercury. This is because Venus has an extremely thick atmosphere (mostly CO₂) that creates an intense greenhouse effect, trapping heat. Mercury, with virtually no atmosphere, cannot retain heat — its night side drops to -180°C.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 4
How many moons does Mercury have?

A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Mercury has no moons (natural satellites) and no rings. This is likely due to its small size, weak gravity, and proximity to the Sun — the Sun's strong gravitational influence would make it difficult for Mercury to capture and retain a moon in a stable orbit.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 5
How long is one year on Mercury (its orbital period)?

A) About 28 Earth days
B) About 88 Earth days
C) About 225 Earth days
D) About 365 Earth days

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mercury's orbital period is approximately 88 Earth days, making it the shortest year of any planet. This is because Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and therefore travels its orbital path most quickly. Interestingly, Mercury's day-night cycle (176 Earth days) is longer than two of its years!

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 6
Mercury has an extremely unusual day-night cycle. How long is one full day-night cycle on Mercury?

A) About 24 Earth hours
B) About 59 Earth days
C) About 88 Earth days
D) About 176 Earth days

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** One complete day-night cycle on Mercury lasts about 176 Earth days — that's about two Mercury years! This happens because Mercury rotates very slowly on its axis (once every 59 Earth days) while orbiting the Sun relatively quickly (every 88 days). The combination means the Sun rises and sets very infrequently on Mercury.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 7
What is the Caloris Basin on Mercury?

A) A large ocean on Mercury's surface
B) One of the largest impact craters in the solar system
C) A volcano
D) A flat plain of ice

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Caloris Basin is one of the largest impact craters in the solar system, approximately 1,550 km wide. It was formed by a massive asteroid impact early in Mercury's history. The name comes from the Latin word for "heat" because it was one of the first features identified near Mercury's "hot pole" — the point that faces the Sun during Mercury's closest approach.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA MESSENGER

### Question 8
What is surprising about Mercury's interior?

A) It has no core
B) It has a proportionally enormous iron core — about 85% of the planet's radius
C) Its core is made entirely of ice
D) It has a liquid water layer

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mercury has a disproportionately large iron core that takes up about 85% of the planet's radius — the largest core relative to body size of any planet. This gives Mercury a relatively high density despite its small size. The reason for this oversized core is debated — theories include a giant impact stripping away the mantle or the early Sun vaporizing lighter materials.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA MESSENGER

### Question 9
What is found in permanently shadowed craters near Mercury's poles?

A) Liquid water
B) Water ice
C) Diamonds
D) Nothing — they are completely empty

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Despite Mercury's extreme daytime temperatures (up to 430°C), water ice exists in permanently shadowed craters near its poles. These craters never receive direct sunlight, so temperatures there remain below -170°C. This ice was detected by radar from Earth and confirmed by NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft. It likely came from comet and asteroid impacts.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA MESSENGER / Planetary Science

### Question 10
Why is Venus sometimes called "Earth's twin" or "sister planet"?

A) Because it has the same atmosphere as Earth
B) Because it has liquid water like Earth
C) Because it is nearly the same size as Earth
D) Because it has the same temperature as Earth

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venus is called Earth's twin because it is nearly the same size as Earth — its diameter is about 12,104 km, or 95% of Earth's diameter. However, Venus is extremely different in almost every other way: its surface temperature is 462°C, it has a crushing CO₂ atmosphere, sulfuric acid clouds, and no liquid water. It's sometimes called Earth's "evil twin."

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 11
What is the surface temperature of Venus?

A) About 100°C
B) About 260°C
C) About 462°C
D) About 1,000°C

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venus has a surface temperature of approximately 462°C — hot enough to melt lead. This makes it the hottest planet in the solar system, even hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun. The extreme temperature is caused by a runaway greenhouse effect from Venus's thick CO₂ atmosphere.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 12
What causes Venus to be so extremely hot?

A) Venus is closest to the Sun
B) Venus has active volcanoes heating its surface
C) Venus has a thick CO₂ atmosphere that creates an extreme greenhouse effect
D) Venus's core is much hotter than other planets

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venus's extreme heat is caused by a runaway greenhouse effect. Its thick atmosphere is about 96.5% carbon dioxide, which traps enormous amounts of heat from the Sun. The surface pressure is about 90 times Earth's atmospheric pressure. This greenhouse effect is so powerful that Venus is hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is much closer to the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / Atmospheric Science

### Question 13
What is unusual about Venus's rotation?

A) It does not rotate at all
B) It rotates backward (retrograde) compared to most other planets
C) It rotates at exactly the same speed as Earth
D) It changes direction of rotation every year

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Venus has retrograde rotation — it spins clockwise when viewed from above its north pole, which is the opposite direction to most other planets. This means the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east on Venus. The cause is uncertain — it may have been flipped by a large impact or gradually reversed by atmospheric tidal effects.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 14
On Venus, which lasts longer — a day or a year?

A) A day is much shorter than a year
B) They are exactly the same length
C) A day (243 Earth days) is longer than a year (225 Earth days)
D) This question makes no sense

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venus rotates on its axis once every 243 Earth days, but orbits the Sun once every 225 Earth days. This means a Venusian day (one rotation) is actually longer than a Venusian year (one orbit). Venus is the only planet where this is the case. Combined with its retrograde rotation, Venus has the most unusual day-night cycle in the solar system.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 15
Venus is commonly known by which poetic names?

A) The Red Planet and the War Star
B) The Morning Star and the Evening Star
C) The Blue Planet and the Water World
D) The Ringed Planet and the Jewel

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Venus is called the "Morning Star" when it appears in the eastern sky before sunrise, and the "Evening Star" when it appears in the western sky after sunset. It is the brightest natural object in the night sky after the Sun and Moon, thanks to its thick clouds reflecting about 70% of sunlight. Ancient civilizations often thought these were two different objects.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy / Observational Science

### Question 16
What are Venus's clouds primarily made of?

A) Water vapor
B) Ammonia
C) Sulfuric acid
D) Carbon monoxide

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Venus's thick cloud layers are composed primarily of sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) droplets. These clouds completely obscure the surface in visible light, reflecting about 70% of sunlight and making Venus extremely bright. The clouds extend from about 45 to 70 km altitude and are driven by hurricane-force winds that circle the planet in about 4 Earth days.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / Venus Atmospheric Studies

### Question 17
Which was the first spacecraft to successfully land on another planet?

A) Mariner 2 on Venus
B) Viking 1 on Mars
C) Venera 7 on Venus
D) Luna 2 on the Moon

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Soviet Venera 7 was the first spacecraft to successfully land on another planet, touching down on Venus on December 15, 1970. It transmitted data from the surface for about 23 minutes. The extreme conditions on Venus (462°C, 90 atmospheres pressure) made this an extraordinary engineering achievement. Later Venera missions (9, 13, 14) sent back the first surface photographs.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Exploration History / USSR Space Programme

### Question 18
Which NASA mission mapped 98% of Venus's surface using radar?

A) Voyager 2
B) Pioneer Venus
C) Magellan
D) Cassini

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's Magellan spacecraft orbited Venus from 1990 to 1994 and used synthetic aperture radar (SAR) to map about 98% of Venus's surface through its thick clouds. Radar can penetrate the sulfuric acid clouds that make optical imaging impossible. Magellan revealed volcanic plains, mountains, and the highest point on Venus — Maxwell Montes.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 19
What is the highest mountain on Venus?

A) Olympus Mons
B) Maxwell Montes
C) Mount Everest
D) Maat Mons

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Maxwell Montes is the highest mountain on Venus, rising about 11 km above the mean surface level. It is located in the Ishtar Terra highland region near the north pole. Interestingly, it is the only major feature on Venus named after a male (James Clerk Maxwell) — by convention, most features on Venus are named after women and goddesses.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Magellan

### Question 20
What is ISRO's planned Venus mission called?

A) Aditya-V
B) Shukrayaan
C) Venus Express
D) Mangalyaan-2

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISRO's planned Venus mission is called Shukrayaan, where "Shukra" means Venus in Hindi/Sanskrit (named after the planet Venus in Hindu astronomy). The mission would study Venus's atmosphere, surface, and subsurface. This follows ISRO's tradition of Sanskrit names: Chandrayaan (Moon), Mangalyaan (Mars), Gaganyaan (Sky/Space).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 21
Which spacecraft is currently en route to Mercury and expected to arrive around 2025?

A) New Horizons
B) MESSENGER
C) BepiColombo
D) Juno

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** BepiColombo is a joint ESA/JAXA mission launched in 2018 and currently traveling to Mercury with an expected arrival around 2025. It consists of two orbiters that will study Mercury's surface, interior, and magnetosphere. It is named after Italian mathematician Giuseppe (Bepi) Colombo, who made key contributions to understanding Mercury's orbital mechanics.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ESA / JAXA

### Question 22
The surface pressure on Venus is approximately:

A) Same as Earth (1 atmosphere)
B) About 10 times Earth's
C) About 90 times Earth's
D) About 900 times Earth's

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The atmospheric pressure at Venus's surface is approximately 90 times that of Earth — equivalent to the pressure found about 900 meters underwater on Earth. This extreme pressure, combined with the 462°C temperature and corrosive sulfuric acid clouds, makes Venus's surface one of the most hostile environments in the solar system. Soviet Venera landers survived only about 1-2 hours.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 23
Why does Mercury have such extreme temperature differences between day and night (-180°C to 430°C)?

A) Mercury is very far from the Sun
B) Mercury has practically no atmosphere to retain or distribute heat
C) Mercury has a very strong magnetic field
D) Mercury rotates very quickly

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mercury has virtually no atmosphere (only a thin exosphere). Without an atmosphere to trap heat (like Venus's greenhouse effect) or distribute it around the planet, the Sun-facing side becomes extremely hot (up to 430°C) while the night side radiates heat away into space, dropping to about -180°C. This 610°C temperature swing is the largest of any planet.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 24
How many moons does Venus have?

A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 4

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Venus has no moons, making it one of only two planets in our solar system without natural satellites (the other being Mercury). The reason Venus lacks moons may be related to its slow, retrograde rotation — any captured moon would likely have been destabilized and either crashed into Venus or escaped its gravity.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 25
Which space programme achieved the first surface photographs of another planet?

A) NASA's Mariner programme
B) USSR's Venera programme
C) ESA's Venus Express
D) JAXA's Akatsuki

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Soviet Union's Venera programme achieved many firsts at Venus, including the first surface photographs of another planet. Venera 9 (1975) and later Venera 13 and 14 (1982) transmitted photographs of Venus's surface, showing a rocky, desolate landscape under orange-hued light filtered through the thick atmosphere. These remain the only surface images of Venus ever taken.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Exploration History / USSR Venera Programme

### Question 26
Scientists believe Venus may have once been habitable. What happened?

A) An asteroid destroyed its water
B) A runaway greenhouse effect boiled away its oceans
C) Its magnetic field reversed and destroyed the atmosphere
D) The Sun's gravity pulled away its atmosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Scientists believe Venus may have had liquid water oceans and more Earth-like conditions billions of years ago. However, a runaway greenhouse effect gradually heated the planet — water evaporated, adding more greenhouse gas (water vapor) to the atmosphere, which trapped more heat, evaporating more water, in a self-reinforcing cycle. Eventually all water was lost to space, leaving the hostile world we see today.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Climate Science

### Question 27
Mercury's surface appearance most closely resembles:

A) Earth's oceans
B) Mars's red deserts
C) The Moon's cratered surface
D) Jupiter's cloudy atmosphere

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mercury's surface is heavily cratered and looks very similar to our Moon's surface. Like the Moon, Mercury lacks a substantial atmosphere and geological activity to erode craters, so impacts from billions of years ago are still preserved. Both bodies also lack liquid water and plate tectonics, which contributes to their similar ancient, cratered appearances.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA MESSENGER

### Question 28
NASA's MESSENGER mission to Mercury operated from:

A) 1974-1975
B) 1990-1994
C) 2004-2015
D) 2018-present

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** MESSENGER (MErcury Surface, Space ENvironment, GEochemistry, and Ranging) was launched in 2004 and entered orbit around Mercury in 2011, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit Mercury. It mapped Mercury's entire surface, studied its composition and magnetic field, and confirmed water ice in polar craters. The mission ended in 2015 when the spacecraft impacted Mercury's surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 29
Which Japanese spacecraft is currently studying Venus's atmosphere?

A) Hayabusa2
B) Akatsuki
C) Kaguya
D) SLIM

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Akatsuki (meaning "Dawn" in Japanese) is a JAXA Venus orbiter that has been studying Venus's atmosphere since 2015. Originally launched in 2010, it missed its initial orbit insertion but succeeded five years later on a second attempt. Akatsuki studies Venus's atmospheric dynamics, cloud structure, and weather patterns using cameras that observe in multiple wavelengths.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** JAXA

### Question 30
Which of the following statements about Mercury is FALSE?

A) Mercury has the largest iron core relative to its size of any planet
B) Mercury is the hottest planet in the solar system
C) Mercury has virtually no atmosphere
D) Mercury has water ice at its poles

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The false statement is B — Mercury is NOT the hottest planet. Despite being closest to the Sun, Venus is the hottest planet (462°C) due to its extreme greenhouse effect. All other statements are true: Mercury does have the proportionally largest iron core (~85% of its radius), essentially no atmosphere (thin exosphere), and water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science
