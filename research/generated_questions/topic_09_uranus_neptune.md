## Topic 9 – Uranus and Neptune — The Ice Giants

### Questions for Topic 9

### Question 1
Uranus and Neptune are classified as what type of planets?

A) Gas giants
B) Rocky planets
C) Ice giants
D) Dwarf planets

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Uranus and Neptune are classified as ice giants, distinct from the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn. While gas giants are mainly hydrogen and helium, ice giants contain significant amounts of "ices" — water, ammonia, and methane — along with hydrogen and helium. This compositional difference led astronomers to create the separate "ice giant" category.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 2
Who discovered Uranus, and what was special about this discovery?

A) Galileo Galilei — first planet seen through a telescope
B) William Herschel — first planet discovered with a telescope
C) Johann Galle — first planet found by mathematical prediction
D) Isaac Newton — first planet discovered using gravity calculations

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** William Herschel discovered Uranus on March 13, 1781, making it the first planet discovered using a telescope. All planets up to Saturn had been known since antiquity because they are visible to the naked eye. Herschel initially thought he had found a comet, but further observations confirmed it was a new planet.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy History

### Question 3
What is the most distinctive physical feature of Uranus?

A) Its spectacular ring system
B) Its extreme axial tilt of approximately 98°
C) Its Great Dark Spot
D) Its intense volcanic activity

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Uranus has an extreme axial tilt of approximately 98° — it essentially rolls on its side as it orbits the Sun. This means Uranus's poles take turns pointing almost directly at the Sun. The cause of this extreme tilt is uncertain, but it may have resulted from a collision with an Earth-sized object early in the solar system's history.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 4
How does Uranus's extreme tilt affect its seasons?

A) Uranus has no seasons
B) Each pole gets about 42 years of continuous sunlight, then 42 years of darkness
C) Seasons last only a few Earth days
D) The entire planet has the same temperature year-round

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Because of its ~98° axial tilt, Uranus has the most extreme seasons in the solar system. Each pole alternately faces the Sun for about 42 Earth years (half of its 84-year orbital period), receiving continuous sunlight, while the other pole is in complete darkness. The equatorial regions experience rapid day-night cycles only during the equinoxes.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 5
Why do Uranus and Neptune appear blue-green in colour?

A) Because they have large oceans on their surfaces
B) Because methane in their atmospheres absorbs red light and reflects blue-green
C) Because they are very cold
D) Because of chemical reactions in their cores

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Both Uranus and Neptune appear blue-green because methane (CH₄) in their upper atmospheres absorbs red and orange wavelengths of sunlight while reflecting blue and green light back to space. Neptune appears a deeper blue than Uranus, possibly due to an additional unknown atmospheric component that enhances its blue colour.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / Atmospheric Science

### Question 6
How was Neptune discovered?

A) By accident through a telescope
B) By mathematical prediction based on irregularities in Uranus's orbit
C) By spacecraft observation
D) By ancient astronomers studying the night sky

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Neptune was the first planet discovered through mathematical prediction rather than direct observation. Astronomers Urbain Le Verrier (France) and John Couch Adams (England) independently calculated that an unknown planet's gravity was causing irregularities in Uranus's orbit. Johann Galle then found Neptune on September 23, 1846, very close to the predicted position — a triumph of Newtonian physics.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy History

### Question 7
Neptune holds a remarkable record in the solar system. What is it?

A) Coldest temperature
B) Strongest winds — up to 2,100 km/h
C) Most moons
D) Largest volcano

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Neptune has the strongest winds in the entire solar system, reaching speeds of up to 2,100 km/h — supersonic by Earth standards. This is remarkable because Neptune receives very little energy from the Sun (being so far away). The energy driving these extreme winds comes primarily from Neptune's internal heat source — it radiates 2.6 times more energy than it receives from the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 8
Which spacecraft is the ONLY one to have visited Uranus?

A) Pioneer 11
B) Voyager 1
C) Voyager 2
D) New Horizons

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus, flying by on January 24, 1986. During this brief encounter, it discovered 10 new moons, studied Uranus's rings, and captured images of its atmosphere. No dedicated orbiter mission has ever been sent to Uranus, making it one of the least-explored planets in our solar system.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / Voyager Programme

### Question 9
Which spacecraft is the ONLY one to have visited Neptune?

A) Pioneer 10
B) Voyager 1
C) Voyager 2
D) Cassini

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Voyager 2 is also the only spacecraft to have visited Neptune, flying by on August 25, 1989. This made Voyager 2 the only spacecraft to have visited all four outer planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune). During the Neptune flyby, it discovered the Great Dark Spot, measured Neptune's winds, and studied its moon Triton.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / Voyager Programme

### Question 10
What is unusual about Neptune compared to Uranus regarding internal heat?

A) Neptune has no internal heat at all
B) Neptune radiates 2.6 times more energy than it receives from the Sun, while Uranus barely radiates excess heat
C) Neptune is powered by nuclear fusion
D) Both planets radiate exactly the same amount of heat

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Neptune radiates about 2.6 times more energy than it receives from the Sun, indicating a significant internal heat source. Uranus, by contrast, radiates very little excess heat — the reason for this difference is poorly understood. Neptune's internal heat is thought to drive its extreme winds (up to 2,100 km/h), while Uranus's lack of internal heat makes it the coldest planet despite being closer to the Sun.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 11
How long is one year on Uranus?

A) About 12 Earth years
B) About 30 Earth years
C) About 84 Earth years
D) About 165 Earth years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** One orbit of Uranus around the Sun takes approximately 84 Earth years. This means that since its discovery in 1781, Uranus has completed less than three full orbits around the Sun. A person born when Uranus was at one point in its orbit would be 84 years old before Uranus returned to the same position.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 12
How long is one year on Neptune?

A) About 30 Earth years
B) About 84 Earth years
C) About 165 Earth years
D) About 248 Earth years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** One orbit of Neptune around the Sun takes approximately 165 Earth years. Neptune completed its first full orbit since discovery in 2011, having been discovered in 1846. This incredibly long orbital period is due to Neptune's vast distance from the Sun — about 30 AU (4.5 billion km).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 13
Which of Uranus's moons are named after characters from Shakespeare and Alexander Pope?

A) None — they are named after Greek gods
B) Only the largest moon
C) All 28 known moons
D) Only the first 5 discovered

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** All 28 known moons of Uranus are named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope. Major moons include Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania (the largest), and Oberon — all Shakespearean characters. This naming convention is unique to Uranus; other planets' moons are typically named after figures from Greek or Roman mythology.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy / IAU Naming Conventions

### Question 14
What is Neptune's moon Triton known for?

A) Being the largest moon in the solar system
B) Having a retrograde orbit, suggesting it was captured from the Kuiper Belt
C) Being the only moon with a thick atmosphere
D) Having active volcanoes like Jupiter's Io

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Triton, Neptune's largest moon (~2,707 km diameter), has a retrograde orbit — it orbits Neptune in the opposite direction to Neptune's rotation. This strongly suggests Triton was not formed alongside Neptune but was captured from the Kuiper Belt. Triton also has nitrogen geysers (cryovolcanism), an extremely cold surface (~-235°C), and may have a subsurface ocean.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Voyager 2

### Question 15
Despite being closer to the Sun than Neptune, Uranus is actually colder. Why?

A) Neptune reflects more heat from the Sun
B) Uranus lacks a significant internal heat source, unlike Neptune
C) Uranus has a thicker atmosphere that blocks sunlight
D) Uranus is shielded by Saturn's magnetic field

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Uranus has the coldest planetary atmosphere in the solar system (~-224°C), even colder than the more distant Neptune (~-214°C). The key difference is that Neptune has a strong internal heat source (radiating 2.6× more energy than it receives from the Sun), while Uranus barely radiates excess heat. The reason Uranus lacks this internal heat is one of the unsolved mysteries of planetary science.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 16
How many rings does Uranus have?

A) None
B) 5
C) 13
D) Hundreds like Saturn

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Uranus has 13 known rings, all of which are faint and narrow. They were discovered in 1977 during a stellar occultation (when Uranus passed in front of a distant star). The rings are mostly composed of dark particles and are very different from Saturn's bright, icy rings. They were further studied during the Voyager 2 flyby in 1986.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 17
What was the Great Dark Spot observed on Neptune?

A) A permanent mountain on Neptune's surface
B) A large storm system observed by Voyager 2 in 1989
C) A crater from an asteroid impact
D) A region of dark ice

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Great Dark Spot was a large storm system on Neptune observed by Voyager 2 during its 1989 flyby. Similar in nature to Jupiter's Great Red Spot, it was an anticyclonic storm roughly the size of Earth. However, unlike Jupiter's persistent storm, Neptune's Great Dark Spot disappeared within a few years and new dark spots have appeared since — indicating Neptune's atmosphere is very dynamic.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA Voyager 2 / Planetary Science

### Question 18
How many moons does Neptune have?

A) 2
B) 8
C) 16

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Neptune has 16 known moons. The largest by far is Triton, which accounts for more than 99.5% of the total mass orbiting Neptune. Other notable moons include Nereid (with an extremely elliptical orbit) and several small moons discovered by Voyager 2 and Earth-based telescopes.

**Explanation:** Uranus has a diameter of approximately 51,118 km, making it about 4 times the diameter of Earth. ### Question 20
Which statement correctly distinguishes ice giants from gas giants?

A) Ice giants are closer to the Sun than gas giants
B) Gas giants have more moons than ice giants
C) Ice giants contain significant amounts of water, ammonia, and methane "ices," while gas giants are mostly hydrogen and helium
D) Ice giants have solid surfaces while gas giants do not

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The key distinction is composition. Gas giants (Jupiter, Saturn) are primarily hydrogen and helium — similar to the Sun. Ice giants (Uranus, Neptune) contain significant proportions of heavier compounds called "ices" — water (H₂O), ammonia (NH₃), and methane (CH₄) — in addition to hydrogen and helium. This different composition leads to different internal structures and properties.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 21
Since Pluto's reclassification in 2006, which planet is now considered the farthest from the Sun?

A) Uranus
B) Neptune
C) Pluto
D) Eris

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Since Pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) in 2006, Neptune is now the farthest planet from the Sun at an average distance of about 30 AU (4.5 billion km). Pluto and Eris are dwarf planets, not full planets, so they don't count in this ranking.

### Question 22
What day length (rotation period) does Uranus have?

A) About 10 hours
B) About 17 hours 14 minutes
C) About 24 hours
D) About 84 hours

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Uranus rotates on its axis once every approximately 17 hours and 14 minutes. Despite its extreme axial tilt of ~98°, its day length is actually quite moderate compared to some other planets. Venus (243 Earth days) and Mercury (59 Earth days) both rotate much more slowly than Uranus.

**Difficulty:** Hard

### Question 23
Voyager 2 visited all four outer planets. In what order did it visit them?

A) Saturn, Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune
B) Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune
C) Uranus, Neptune, Jupiter, Saturn
D) Jupiter, Saturn, Neptune, Uranus

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Voyager 2 visited the outer planets in order of their distance from the Sun: Jupiter (1979), Saturn (1981), Uranus (1986), and Neptune (1989). This "Grand Tour" was possible because of a rare planetary alignment that occurs only once every 175 years, allowing the spacecraft to use gravity assists from each planet to reach the next one.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA Voyager Programme

### Question 24
Triton's surface temperature is approximately -235°C, making it:

A) The hottest moon in the solar system
B) About the same temperature as Earth's Moon
C) One of the coldest known objects in the solar system
D) Warm enough to have liquid water

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Triton has a surface temperature of approximately -235°C (38 K), making it one of the coldest known objects in the solar system. Despite this extreme cold, Triton shows geological activity — nitrogen geysers erupt from its surface, spraying dark material up to 8 km high. This is an example of cryovolcanism — volcanic activity at extremely low temperatures.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA Voyager 2 / Planetary Science

### Question 25
Which of the following is TRUE about Uranus's ring system?

A) Its rings are as bright and spectacular as Saturn's
B) Its rings were discovered by the Hubble Space Telescope in 2005
C) Its rings are faint and narrow, discovered in 1977 during a stellar occultation
D) It has no rings at all

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Uranus's 13 rings are faint and narrow, quite unlike Saturn's spectacular ring system. They were discovered in 1977 when astronomers observed Uranus pass in front of a distant star (stellar occultation) and noticed the star's light dimmed briefly before and after Uranus blocked it — revealing the rings. Voyager 2 later imaged them directly in 1986.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Observational Astronomy
