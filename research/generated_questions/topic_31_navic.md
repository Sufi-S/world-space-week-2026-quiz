## Topic 31 – NavIC — Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System

### Questions for Topic 31

### Question 1
What does NavIC stand for?

A) National Aviation and Intelligence Constellation
B) Navigation with Indian Constellation
C) Naval Information Command
D) Navigational Instruments for Communication

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC stands for Navigation with Indian Constellation. It is India's own regional satellite navigation system. The name "NavIC" also has a deeper meaning — it translates to "navigator" or "sailor" in Hindi/Sanskrit. The name was coined by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
What was NavIC previously known as?

A) GAGAN
B) IRNSS
C) INSAT
D) GSAT

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC was previously known as IRNSS (Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System). The name was changed to NavIC to give the system a more recognisable Indian identity. IRNSS is still used as a technical designation, but NavIC is the popular name.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
Which event was the primary motivation for India to develop its own navigation satellite system?

A) The launch of Sputnik in 1957
B) The success of Chandrayaan-1 in 2008
C) The USA's denial of GPS data during the Kargil War in 1999
D) The failure of GSLV's first flight in 2001

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** During the Kargil War (1999), India requested GPS data from the United States to track Pakistani intrusions in the mountainous terrain. The USA denied India access to accurate GPS signals. This was a critical wake-up call that convinced India it could not depend on foreign navigation systems for national security, leading to the development of IRNSS/NavIC.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Defence History

### Question 4
How many satellites make up the NavIC constellation?

A) 4
B) 7
C) 24
D) 31

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC uses 7 satellites in its primary constellation — much fewer than GPS (31 satellites) or GLONASS (24 satellites). Despite having fewer satellites, NavIC achieves reliable coverage over India by placing its satellites in geostationary (GEO) and geosynchronous (GSO) orbits where they remain constantly visible over the Indian region.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
Is NavIC a global or regional navigation system?

A) Global — it covers the entire Earth
B) Regional — it covers India and approximately 1,500 km around India
C) Local — it only covers major Indian cities
D) Continental — it covers all of Asia

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC is a regional navigation system, covering India and a surrounding area extending approximately 1,500 km beyond India's borders. This covers the Indian subcontinent, nearby countries, and surrounding seas (the Indian Ocean Region). Unlike GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou, which are global systems, NavIC is designed specifically for the Indian region.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What type of orbits do NavIC satellites use?

A) All in Low Earth Orbit (LEO)
B) All in Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)
C) 3 in Geostationary (GEO) and 4 in inclined Geosynchronous (GSO)
D) All in polar sun-synchronous orbit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NavIC's 7 satellites use a combination of two orbit types: 3 satellites in Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) at 35,786 km over the equator, and 4 satellites in inclined Geosynchronous Orbit (GSO) at 29° inclination. This combination ensures that all 7 satellites are always visible from India, providing continuous navigation coverage.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What mathematical method do navigation satellite receivers use to determine position?

A) Triangulation using angles
B) Trilateration using distances from multiple satellites
C) Parallax measurement
D) Doppler shift calculation

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Navigation receivers use trilateration — they measure the time it takes for signals from at least 4 satellites to reach the receiver. Since the signals travel at the speed of light and each satellite's position is known, the time differences translate into distances. With distances from 4 or more satellites, the receiver calculates its exact position (latitude, longitude, altitude) and precise time.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Navigation Science

### Question 11

### Question 14
What is NVS-01?

A) The first NavIC satellite ever launched
B) A replenishment/replacement satellite for the NavIC constellation
C) An experimental navigation satellite for testing new technology
D) A communication satellite that also carries navigation signals

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NVS-01 (Navigation Satellite-01), launched in May 2023 by GSLV, is a replenishment satellite for the NavIC constellation. As the original IRNSS satellites age and their components degrade (particularly the atomic clocks), newer NVS-series satellites are launched to maintain the constellation's operational capability. NVS-01 replaced aging capacity in the constellation.

### Question 15
Why does NavIC use GEO and inclined GSO orbits instead of MEO orbits like GPS?

A) GEO orbits are cheaper to reach
B) These orbits ensure the satellites are always visible over the Indian region with just 7 satellites
C) MEO technology was not available to India
D) GEO orbits provide faster signal transmission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC uses a combination of GEO and inclined GSO orbits because these keep all 7 satellites permanently visible over the Indian region. GPS uses 31 satellites in MEO, where each satellite orbits the Earth twice per day and is only visible from a given location for part of the time. NavIC's approach achieves reliable regional coverage with far fewer satellites by keeping them "parked" over India.

### Question 18
What critical component do navigation satellites carry to ensure extremely precise timing?

A) Solar panels
B) Atomic clocks
C) Cameras
D) Radar systems

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Navigation satellites carry atomic clocks — ultra-precise timekeeping devices that are accurate to billionths of a second. Precise timing is fundamental to satellite navigation because position is calculated from the time it takes signals to travel from satellite to receiver. Even tiny timing errors (nanoseconds) translate to position errors of metres, so atomic clocks are essential for accuracy.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Navigation Science

### Question 22
What altitude are the GEO satellites in the NavIC constellation?

A) About 400 km (Low Earth Orbit)
B) About 2,000 km (Medium Earth Orbit)
C) About 20,200 km (GPS orbit altitude)
D) About 35,786 km (Geostationary orbit)

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The three GEO satellites in NavIC orbit at approximately 35,786 km — the geostationary altitude where a satellite's orbital period matches Earth's rotation. At this altitude, GEO satellites appear stationary over a fixed point on the equator. The four inclined GSO satellites orbit at the same altitude but are tilted at 29° to the equator, so they trace a figure-eight pattern over India.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO / Orbital Mechanics

### Question 23
Why can't NavIC be used for navigation in, say, Europe or the Americas?

A) NavIC signals are encrypted and only work in India
B) NavIC satellites are positioned over/near India and their signals don't reach those regions
C) NavIC uses a different technology incompatible with European receivers
D) International law prohibits India from providing navigation signals outside its territory

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC is a regional system with all 7 satellites positioned in orbits that keep them above or near the Indian region. Since the satellites are in GEO and inclined GSO orbits over India, their signals cover India and approximately 1,500 km beyond its borders — but not distant regions like Europe or the Americas. A global system like GPS uses 31 satellites distributed around the Earth to achieve worldwide coverage.

### Question 24
How many satellites must a navigation receiver pick up signals from to determine a 3D position (latitude, longitude, and altitude)?

A) At least 1
B) At least 2
C) At least 3
D) At least 4

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** A receiver needs signals from at least 4 satellites to determine a 3D position. Three satellites provide latitude, longitude, and altitude through trilateration, while the fourth satellite is needed to solve for an additional unknown — the clock error in the receiver (since consumer receivers don't have atomic clocks). With more satellites in view, accuracy improves further.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Navigation Science

### Question 29
At what inclination angle do the GSO satellites in the NavIC constellation orbit?

A) 0° (equatorial)
B) 29°
C) 45°
D) 90° (polar)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The four GSO satellites in NavIC orbit at an inclination of 29° to the equator. This means they are not directly over the equator (like the GEO satellites) but instead trace a figure-eight ground track that extends north and south of the equator. This inclination is chosen to optimise coverage over India, which extends from about 8°N to 37°N latitude.
