## Topic 33 – Chandrayaan-1 — India's First Lunar Mission

### Questions for Topic 33

### Question 1
When was Chandrayaan-1 launched?

A) 15 August 2003
B) 22 October 2008
C) 14 November 2008
D) 5 November 2013

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 was launched on 22 October 2008 from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC SHAR), Sriharikota. It was India's first mission to the Moon, marking India's entry into lunar exploration. The launch date should not be confused with 14 November 2008, which is when the Moon Impact Probe (MIP) was released from the orbiter.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
Which launch vehicle carried Chandrayaan-1 to space?

A) GSLV Mk II
B) LVM3
C) PSLV-C11
D) SSLV

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 was launched by PSLV-C11, using the PSLV-XL variant with larger strap-on boosters to provide the extra thrust needed for a lunar mission. PSLV's reliability made it the natural choice, and the XL variant gave it sufficient payload capacity to send the 1,380 kg spacecraft toward the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
What was Chandrayaan-1 primarily designed for?

A) Landing a rover on the Moon
B) Chemical, mineralogical, and photo-geologic mapping of the Moon from orbit
C) Bringing back lunar soil samples to Earth
D) Testing India's first manned spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 was an orbiter mission designed for scientific mapping of the Moon. It carried 11 instruments to study the Moon's surface chemistry, mineral composition, and topography from orbit. It was NOT designed to land on or return from the Moon — the Moon Impact Probe (MIP) intentionally crashed into the surface but did not soft-land.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
How many scientific instruments did Chandrayaan-1 carry in total?

A) 5
B) 8
C) 11
D) 15

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 carried 11 scientific instruments: 5 built in India and 6 provided by international partners (NASA/USA, ESA/UK, ESA/Germany, ESA/Sweden, and Bulgaria). This international collaboration was significant — it showed India could lead a multinational lunar science mission and demonstrated the global interest in India's Moon exploration capability.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
What was the Moon Impact Probe (MIP)?

A) A soft-landing module that gently touched down on the Moon
B) A 34 kg probe released from Chandrayaan-1 that intentionally crashed into the lunar surface
C) A satellite that orbited the Moon independently
D) An instrument that measured the Moon's magnetic field

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Moon Impact Probe (MIP) was a 34 kg probe that separated from the Chandrayaan-1 orbiter on 14 November 2008 and intentionally crashed into the lunar surface near the Shackleton Crater at the south pole. It was NOT a soft landing — it was a controlled hard impact. The MIP carried three instruments and was painted with the Indian tricolour flag.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What is Chandrayaan-1's most famous scientific discovery?

A) Discovery of a new element on the Moon
B) Detection of water (H₂O) and hydroxyl (OH) molecules on the lunar surface
C) Discovery of volcanic activity on the Moon
D) Finding evidence of alien life on the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The most groundbreaking discovery from Chandrayaan-1 was the detection of water (H₂O) and hydroxyl (OH) molecules on the lunar surface, particularly in polar regions. This was announced in September 2009 and fundamentally changed our understanding of the Moon, which had long been considered completely dry. The discovery was made primarily by NASA's Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³) instrument aboard Chandrayaan-1.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / NASA

### Question 7
Which instrument on Chandrayaan-1 made the famous water discovery on the Moon?

A) Terrain Mapping Camera (TMC)
B) Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³) — a NASA instrument
C) Hyper Spectral Imager (HySI)
D) Lunar Laser Ranging Instrument (LLRI)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Moon Mineralogy Mapper (M³), an instrument built by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) but flown on India's Chandrayaan-1 spacecraft, detected signatures of water and hydroxyl molecules on the lunar surface. India's own CHACE instrument on the MIP also independently detected water molecules during its descent. The discovery was a collaborative achievement.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 8

The choice of this date was symbolic, as the mission represented India's aspirations in space exploration and was meant to inspire young people.

### Question 9
Near which lunar crater did the Moon Impact Probe crash?

A) Copernicus Crater
B) Tycho Crater
C) Shackleton Crater (south pole region)
D) Aristarchus Crater

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The MIP impacted near the Shackleton Crater in the Moon's south pole region. This location was scientifically significant because the lunar south pole contains permanently shadowed craters where water ice was expected to exist. The choice of this site foreshadowed India's continued interest in the lunar south pole, which later became the target for Chandrayaan-3's historic landing.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What type of lunar orbit did Chandrayaan-1 use?

A) Equatorial orbit at 100 km altitude
B) Circular polar orbit, initially at 100 km altitude
C) Highly elliptical orbit with a 500 km apoapsis
D) Geostationary orbit around the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 orbited the Moon in a circular polar orbit at an initial altitude of 100 km. A polar orbit passes over both the north and south poles of the Moon, which is ideal for complete surface mapping — as the Moon slowly rotates beneath the orbiting spacecraft, every part of the surface eventually passes under the satellite's instruments.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
Why was Chandrayaan-1's orbit later raised from 100 km to 200 km?

A) To take wider-angle photographs
B) To extend mission life by reducing thermal stress on the spacecraft
C) To communicate better with Earth
D) To get closer to the Sun

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In May 2009, the orbit was raised to 200 km because the spacecraft was experiencing thermal (overheating) issues at the lower altitude. The closer orbit meant more intense infrared radiation from the lunar surface was hitting the spacecraft. Raising the orbit reduced thermal stress and helped extend the mission's operational life, although contact was eventually lost in August 2009.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
How long did Chandrayaan-1 operate before contact was lost?

A) About 30 days
B) About 6 months
C) About 312 days (approximately 10 months)
D) Exactly 2 years as planned

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Contact with Chandrayaan-1 was lost on 29 August 2009, after approximately 312 days (about 10 months) of operation. The mission was originally planned for 2 years but ended early due to overheating and a power bus failure. Despite this, the mission was considered highly successful because 95% of its scientific objectives were achieved.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 13
What percentage of its scientific objectives did Chandrayaan-1 achieve despite ending early?

A) About 50%
B) About 75%
C) About 95%
D) 100%

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 achieved approximately 95% of its mission objectives during its 312 days of operation, even though the planned mission duration was 2 years. The spacecraft completed over 3,400 orbits around the Moon and collected an enormous amount of scientific data including the historic water discovery. This is why the mission is considered a major success.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 14
With the MIP impact, India became the ___ country to place its flag on the Moon.

A) First
B) Second
C) Third
D) Fourth

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** When the Moon Impact Probe (painted with the Indian tricolour) crashed into the lunar surface, India became the fourth country to place its flag on the Moon, after the Soviet Union (Luna programme), the United States (Apollo programme), and Japan (SELENE/Kaguya mission). This was a proud moment for India's space programme.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 15
What was the CHACE instrument on the Moon Impact Probe?

A) A camera for photographing the lunar surface
B) A mass spectrometer that detected water molecules during the MIP's descent
C) A seismometer for detecting moonquakes
D) A drill for collecting soil samples

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CHACE (Chandra's Altitudinal Composition Explorer) was a mass spectrometer carried on the Moon Impact Probe. During the MIP's descent toward the lunar surface, CHACE analysed the composition of the Moon's extremely thin atmosphere (exosphere) and detected water molecules. This was one of the earliest direct detections of water on the Moon, independent of the M³ discovery.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 16
Which of the following countries did NOT contribute an instrument to Chandrayaan-1?

A) USA (NASA)
B) Bulgaria
C) France
D) Germany

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** France did not contribute an instrument to Chandrayaan-1. The international payloads came from: USA/NASA (Moon Mineralogy Mapper M³ and Mini-SAR), UK/ESA (C1XS X-ray Spectrometer), Germany/ESA (Near Infrared Spectrometer SIR-2), Sweden/ESA (SARA), and Bulgaria (RADOM radiation dose monitor). France has collaborated with ISRO on other missions like Megha-Tropiques and SARAL.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 17
What did NASA's Mini-SAR instrument on Chandrayaan-1 find?

A) New craters on the far side of the Moon
B) Evidence of water ice in permanently shadowed craters near the north pole
C) Volcanic activity beneath the surface
D) Remnants of an ancient magnetic field

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's Mini-SAR (Miniature Synthetic Aperture Radar) instrument on Chandrayaan-1 found evidence of water ice deposits in permanently shadowed craters near the Moon's north pole. These craters never receive sunlight, so temperatures are extremely cold (below -200°C), allowing water ice to persist. This finding complemented the M³ water discovery and confirmed water existed at both lunar poles.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 18
What was the launch mass of Chandrayaan-1?

A) 34 kg
B) 590 kg
C) 1,380 kg
D) 3,800 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 had a launch mass of 1,380 kg. After expending fuel for orbit manoeuvres and reaching lunar orbit, the spacecraft's mass reduced to about 590 kg. The 34 kg figure is the mass of the Moon Impact Probe, not the whole spacecraft. For context, the PSLV-XL variant had to be used to handle this relatively heavy payload for a lunar mission.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 19
How many orbits did Chandrayaan-1 complete around the Moon?

A) About 100
B) About 500
C) Over 3,400
D) About 10,000

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 completed over 3,400 orbits around the Moon during its approximately 10-month operational life. Each orbit at 100 km altitude took about 2 hours. These thousands of orbits allowed the instruments to build comprehensive maps of the lunar surface chemistry, mineralogy, and topography.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 20
What remarkable discovery did NASA's JPL make about Chandrayaan-1 in 2016?

A) The spacecraft had soft-landed on the Moon
B) Chandrayaan-1 was still orbiting the Moon and was detected using ground-based radar
C) The spacecraft had left the Moon's orbit and was heading toward Mars
D) Chandrayaan-1 had collided with another spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In 2016 — seven years after contact was lost — NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) detected Chandrayaan-1 still orbiting the Moon using powerful ground-based radar at the Goldstone Deep Space Communications Complex. The spacecraft continued its orbit around the Moon even though all communication had been lost in 2009, demonstrating the stability of its orbital trajectory.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA JPL

### Question 21
Which of the following is a COMMON MISCONCEPTION about Chandrayaan-1?

A) It carried international instruments
B) It landed on the Moon
C) It discovered water on the Moon
D) It used the PSLV-XL variant

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A common misconception is that Chandrayaan-1 "landed on the Moon." In reality, Chandrayaan-1 was purely an orbiter mission. Only the Moon Impact Probe (a small 34 kg probe) reached the surface, and it crashed intentionally — it did not soft-land. India's first soft landing on the Moon came much later with Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander in August 2023.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 22
What was the Terrain Mapping Camera (TMC) on Chandrayaan-1 used for?

A) Measuring the Moon's gravity field
B) Creating a 3D topographic map of the lunar surface using stereo imaging
C) Detecting water molecules in the atmosphere
D) Communicating with the Moon Impact Probe

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Terrain Mapping Camera (TMC) was an Indian-built instrument on Chandrayaan-1 that used stereo imaging to create a high-resolution three-dimensional atlas of the lunar surface. By capturing images from slightly different angles, TMC could calculate the height of surface features like mountains, crater rims, and valleys, producing detailed topographic maps.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 23
What caused Chandrayaan-1's mission to end prematurely?

A) A collision with an asteroid
B) Running out of fuel during orbit insertion
C) Overheating and a power bus failure leading to loss of contact
D) Deliberate deorbiting by ISRO

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1's mission ended on 29 August 2009 when contact was lost due to overheating and a subsequent power bus failure. The spacecraft's thermal control system struggled with the intense infrared radiation from the lunar surface. Although the orbit was raised to 200 km to reduce thermal stress, the damage eventually led to communication failure.

### Question 24
Why is a polar orbit ideal for mapping a celestial body like the Moon?

A) It is the easiest orbit to achieve
B) It requires the least fuel
C) As the body rotates, every part of the surface eventually passes under the satellite
D) It keeps the satellite over the same region permanently

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A polar orbit passes over both poles while the Moon rotates slowly beneath the satellite. Over time, every part of the Moon's surface passes under the orbiting spacecraft, allowing its instruments to scan and map the entire surface. This makes polar orbits ideal for comprehensive mapping missions — which is exactly why Chandrayaan-1 used this orbit type.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Orbital Mechanics / ISRO

### Question 25
The water discovery on the Moon by Chandrayaan-1 was:

A) Made entirely by Indian instruments with no international involvement
B) Made entirely by NASA instruments with no Indian contribution
C) A collaborative discovery — NASA's M³ and India's CHACE both detected water independently
D) Later proven to be a measurement error

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The water discovery was a collaborative achievement. NASA's M³ instrument detected water and hydroxyl signatures on the surface, NASA's Mini-SAR found water ice in polar craters, and India's CHACE instrument on the MIP independently detected water molecules during its descent. While M³ was the most publicised detector, multiple instruments confirmed the finding, making it a joint Indian-international discovery.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 26
How does Chandrayaan-1 connect to Chandrayaan-3?

A) They are the same mission with the same spacecraft
B) Chandrayaan-1's discoveries, especially the south pole water findings, motivated the Chandrayaan-3 south pole landing
C) Chandrayaan-3 was launched to rescue the Chandrayaan-1 spacecraft
D) There is no connection between the two missions

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1's discovery of water molecules on the lunar surface — especially evidence of water ice near the south pole — helped motivate Chandrayaan-3's mission to land near the lunar south pole. The scientific interest in the south pole region, first revealed by Chandrayaan-1's data, became the driving rationale for Chandrayaan-3's successful soft landing in August 2023.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 27
Which PSLV variant was used for the Chandrayaan-1 mission, and why?

A) PSLV-CA (Core Alone) — for a lighter mission
B) PSLV-XL (eXtra Large boosters) — for the extra thrust needed for a lunar mission
C) PSLV-DL (Dual boosters) — for moderate performance
D) PSLV-G (Generic) — the standard version

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-1 used the PSLV-XL variant, which has six larger PSOM-XL strap-on boosters (12.2 tonnes each instead of the 9-tonne standard ones). The extra thrust was necessary because sending a 1,380 kg spacecraft to the Moon requires significantly more energy than placing a satellite in Earth orbit. The XL variant can deliver approximately 1,750 kg to a 600 km Sun-synchronous orbit.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO
