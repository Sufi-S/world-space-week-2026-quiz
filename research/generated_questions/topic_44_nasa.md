## Topic 44 – NASA and the US Space Programme

### Questions for Topic 44

### Question 1
What does NASA stand for?

A) National Astronomy and Space Agency
B) National Aeronautics and Space Administration
C) North American Space Association
D) National Air and Space Authority

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA stands for National Aeronautics and Space Administration. It was founded on 29 July 1958, succeeding NACA (National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics). NASA is headquartered in Washington, D.C. and is responsible for aeronautics research, space exploration, Earth science, and space technology development.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 2
Which NASA centre handles launch operations for missions like the Space Shuttle and Artemis?

A) Johnson Space Center (JSC), Houston
B) Kennedy Space Center (KSC), Cape Canaveral, Florida
C) Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), California
D) Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC), Maryland

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Kennedy Space Center (KSC) at Cape Canaveral, Florida, is NASA's primary launch facility. All Space Shuttle missions, SLS/Artemis launches, and many other NASA missions launched from KSC. Its coastal Florida location allows rockets to launch eastward over the Atlantic Ocean for safety.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 3
What is the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) known for?

A) Human spaceflight operations
B) Robotic planetary missions like Mars rovers, Voyager, and Cassini
C) Astronaut training
D) Aircraft design

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** JPL in Pasadena, California, is NASA's centre for robotic planetary missions. It has designed and operated iconic missions including the Voyager probes, Mars rovers (Curiosity, Perseverance), Cassini at Saturn, and many others. Uniquely, JPL is managed by Caltech (a private university) on behalf of NASA.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 4
What is Voyager 1's current distinction?

A) It is orbiting Mars
B) It is the farthest human-made object from Earth, in interstellar space
C) It is currently exploring Jupiter
D) It has returned to Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is the farthest human-made object from Earth — over 24 billion km away. In 2012, it crossed the heliopause and entered interstellar space, becoming the first spacecraft to leave the Sun's sphere of influence. Despite being over 45 years old, it continues to transmit data back to Earth.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 5
What is the Golden Record carried by the Voyager spacecraft?

A) A gold medal given to the mission team
B) A phonograph record containing sounds and images of Earth for potential extraterrestrial finders
C) A record-breaking flight log
D) A gold-plated computer chip

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Both Voyager 1 and 2 carry a Golden Record — a gold-plated copper phonograph record containing sounds and images selected to portray the diversity of life and culture on Earth. It includes music, greetings in 55 languages, sounds of nature, and images. It was designed as a message to any intelligent extraterrestrial life that might find the spacecraft.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 6
What is the Hubble Space Telescope?

A) A ground-based telescope in Hawaii
B) A space telescope orbiting Earth at about 540 km, with a 2.4 m mirror, that revolutionised astronomy
C) A radio telescope in Puerto Rico
D) A telescope on the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Hubble Space Telescope, launched in 1990, orbits Earth at approximately 540 km altitude. With its 2.4-metre mirror, it has captured some of the most iconic astronomical images ever taken, including the Hubble Deep Field showing galaxies billions of light-years away. Hubble continues to operate alongside the newer James Webb Space Telescope.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 7
What is the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)?

A) A replacement for the ISS
B) An infrared space telescope at Lagrange Point L2 with a 6.5 m mirror
C) A radar satellite for Earth observation
D) A lunar rover

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** JWST, launched on 25 December 2021 (a joint NASA/ESA/CSA mission), is the largest and most powerful space telescope ever built. Its 6.5-metre gold-coated mirror (18 hexagonal segments) observes in infrared wavelengths from the L2 Lagrange point, about 1.5 million km from Earth. It studies the earliest galaxies, exoplanet atmospheres, and star formation.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / ESA / CSA

### Question 8
What was the first Mars rover, and when did it land?

A) Curiosity in 2012
B) Spirit in 2004
C) Sojourner in 1997
D) Perseverance in 2021

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sojourner was the first Mars rover, landing as part of NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission in July 1997. It was small (about the size of a microwave oven) and operated for about 83 days. Since then, NASA has landed increasingly capable rovers: Spirit and Opportunity (2004), Curiosity (2012), and Perseverance (2021).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 9
What was special about the Ingenuity helicopter?

A) It was the first helicopter to fly on another planet (Mars)
B) It was the first helicopter to fly to the Moon
C) It was the largest drone in space
D) It was the first helicopter to fly in Earth's stratosphere

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Ingenuity, which arrived on Mars with the Perseverance rover in 2021, was the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet. Originally planned for just 5 flights as a technology demonstration, Ingenuity made an remarkable 72 flights on Mars before its mission ended in 2024. Flying on Mars is extremely challenging because the atmosphere is only about 1% as dense as Earth's.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 10
Which rover found evidence of ancient water on Mars in Gale Crater?

A) Sojourner
B) Opportunity
C) Curiosity
D) Spirit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Curiosity, which landed in Gale Crater in 2012, found compelling evidence that Mars once had conditions suitable for microbial life, including ancient stream beds and mineral deposits that form in water. Curiosity is car-sized and nuclear-powered (using a radioisotope thermoelectric generator), allowing it to operate day and night.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 11
How does JWST differ from Hubble in terms of wavelength and location?

A) They observe the same wavelengths from the same location
B) Hubble is in LEO and observes mainly visible/UV light; JWST is at L2 and observes mainly infrared
C) JWST is in LEO; Hubble is at L2
D) Both observe only X-rays

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Hubble orbits in low Earth orbit (~540 km) and primarily observes in visible and ultraviolet light. JWST is at the L2 Lagrange point (~1.5 million km from Earth) and observes mainly in infrared wavelengths. Infrared allows JWST to see through cosmic dust clouds and observe very distant, ancient galaxies whose light has been stretched to infrared by the expansion of the universe.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 12
What was the Mercury programme?

A) A mission to the planet Mercury
B) NASA's first human spaceflight programme with single-astronaut capsules
C) A communication satellite programme
D) A Mars exploration programme

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Project Mercury (1958-1963) was NASA's first human spaceflight programme. It used small, single-astronaut capsules launched on Atlas and Redstone rockets. Alan Shepard made the first American suborbital flight (May 1961), and John Glenn made the first American orbital flight (February 1962). Mercury proved Americans could safely fly in space.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 13
What was the Gemini programme's main purpose?

A) Landing on the Moon
B) Practising rendezvous, docking, and spacewalks needed for Apollo
C) Building a space station
D) Exploring Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Gemini programme (1965-1966) used two-astronaut spacecraft to practise the critical skills needed for the Apollo Moon landing programme: rendezvous (bringing two spacecraft together), docking (connecting them), and spacewalks (EVAs). These capabilities were essential for Apollo's complex lunar orbit rendezvous landing approach.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 14
Which Mars rover lasted an incredible 14 years on the Martian surface?

A) Curiosity
B) Spirit
C) Opportunity
D) Sojourner

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Opportunity landed on Mars in January 2004 and operated for an incredible 14 years until a massive dust storm in June 2018 blocked its solar panels. Originally designed for a 90-day mission, Opportunity drove over 45 km on Mars — a remarkable testament to engineering quality. Its twin, Spirit, operated for about 6 years.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 15
What unique feature does Voyager 2 have that no other spacecraft shares?

A) It is the fastest spacecraft ever
B) It is the only spacecraft to have visited all four giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)
C) It was the first to leave the solar system
D) It is the heaviest spacecraft ever launched

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited all four giant planets of our solar system — Jupiter (1979), Saturn (1981), Uranus (1986), and Neptune (1989). Its "Grand Tour" trajectory used gravity assists from each planet to reach the next. It remains the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus and Neptune.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / JPL

### Question 16
Where is NASA's Mission Control Center located?

A) Kennedy Space Center, Florida
B) Johnson Space Center, Houston, Texas
C) Jet Propulsion Laboratory, California
D) Goddard Space Flight Center, Maryland

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's Mission Control Center is at the Johnson Space Center (JSC) in Houston, Texas. This is where flight controllers monitor and direct crewed space missions. The famous phrase "Houston, we have a problem" from Apollo 13 refers to communication with Mission Control at JSC. JSC also handles astronaut training and human spaceflight operations.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 17
What is the Space Launch System (SLS)?

A) A satellite tracking system
B) NASA's heavy-lift rocket for the Artemis programme to return humans to the Moon
C) A space station life support system
D) A weather forecasting system using satellites

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLS is NASA's powerful heavy-lift rocket designed specifically for the Artemis programme. It is the most powerful rocket NASA has ever built, capable of sending the Orion crew capsule and payloads beyond low Earth orbit toward the Moon. Artemis I (2022) was the first uncrewed SLS flight, successfully sending Orion around the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 18
Which of the following is NOT a NASA Mars rover?

A) Curiosity
B) Perseverance
C) Ingenuity
D) Spirit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Ingenuity is a Mars helicopter (rotorcraft), not a rover. It flew on Mars but did not drive on the surface like a rover. The NASA Mars rovers are: Sojourner (1997), Spirit (2004), Opportunity (2004), Curiosity (2012), and Perseverance (2021). Ingenuity rode to Mars attached to Perseverance and conducted 72 flights.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / JPL
