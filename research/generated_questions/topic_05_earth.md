## Topic 5 – Earth — Our Home Planet

### Questions for Topic 5

### Question 1
What is Earth's position from the Sun?

A) 1st planet
B) 2nd planet
C) 3rd planet
D) 4th planet

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth is the third planet from the Sun, orbiting at an average distance of approximately 150 million km (1 Astronomical Unit). This position places Earth in the "Goldilocks zone" — the habitable zone where conditions are right for liquid water to exist on the surface.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 2
What is the approximate diameter of Earth?

A) ~6,371 km
B) ~12,742 km
C) ~25,000 km
D) ~40,075 km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's diameter is approximately 12,742 km. The value 6,371 km is Earth's radius (half the diameter), while 40,075 km is Earth's circumference at the equator. Earth is the largest of the four terrestrial (rocky) planets in our solar system.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 3
How much of Earth's surface is covered by water?

A) About 29%
B) About 50%
C) About 71%
D) About 90%

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Approximately 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water (oceans, seas, lakes, and rivers), while about 29% is land. This is why Earth appears predominantly blue when seen from space. The presence of abundant liquid water is one of the key factors making Earth habitable — it is the only planet known to have permanent liquid water on its surface.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Earth Science

### Question 4
What are the two most abundant gases in Earth's atmosphere?

A) Carbon dioxide and oxygen
B) Nitrogen and oxygen
C) Hydrogen and helium
D) Oxygen and argon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's atmosphere is composed of approximately 78% nitrogen (N₂) and 21% oxygen (O₂), with the remaining 1% being mostly argon and trace amounts of carbon dioxide, water vapour, and other gases. Oxygen, essential for animal life, is maintained by photosynthesis in plants and algae.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Atmospheric Science

### Question 5
How many natural satellites (moons) does Earth have?

A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth has exactly one natural satellite — the Moon. It is the fifth-largest moon in the solar system with a diameter of about 3,474 km. The Moon stabilizes Earth's axial tilt, influences ocean tides, and has been a subject of exploration by both NASA (Apollo programme) and ISRO (Chandrayaan missions).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 6
What is Earth's average distance from the Sun?

A) About 15 million km
B) About 150 million km
C) About 1.5 billion km
D) About 384,400 km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth orbits the Sun at an average distance of approximately 150 million km, defined as 1 Astronomical Unit (AU). This distance is the standard reference for measuring distances within the solar system. Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to cover this distance.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomical Units / Planetary Science

### Question 7
How long does Earth take to complete one orbit around the Sun?

A) 24 hours
B) 27.3 days
C) 365.25 days
D) 687 days

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth takes approximately 365.25 days to orbit the Sun once. The extra 0.25 day is why we have a leap year every four years — an extra day (February 29) is added to keep the calendar aligned with Earth's orbital period. Without leap years, the calendar would gradually drift out of sync with the seasons.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 8
What is Earth's sidereal rotation period (one complete spin on its axis)?

A) Exactly 24 hours
B) About 23 hours and 56 minutes
C) About 25 hours
D) About 12 hours

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's sidereal rotation period (one complete 360° spin relative to distant stars) is approximately 23 hours and 56 minutes. The solar day (noon to noon) is about 24 hours because Earth must rotate slightly more than 360° to compensate for its orbital motion around the Sun. This 4-minute difference accumulates to one full extra rotation per year.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy / Earth Science

### Question 9
What causes the seasons on Earth?

A) Earth's varying distance from the Sun during its orbit
B) Earth's 23.5° axial tilt
C) The Moon's gravitational pull
D) Changes in the Sun's energy output

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Seasons are caused by Earth's 23.5° axial tilt, NOT by changes in distance from the Sun. When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, it receives more direct sunlight and experiences summer, while the Southern Hemisphere experiences winter — and vice versa. In fact, Earth is actually slightly closer to the Sun during the Northern Hemisphere's winter!

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Earth Science / Astronomy

### Question 10
Earth's axial tilt is approximately:

A) 0° (no tilt)
B) 23.5°
C) 45°
D) 98°

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's axis is tilted at approximately 23.5° relative to the plane of its orbit around the Sun. This tilt is the fundamental reason Earth has seasons. For comparison, Uranus has an extreme tilt of about 98° (essentially rolling on its side), while Mercury has almost no tilt (~2°). Earth's tilt is stabilized by the gravitational influence of the Moon.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy / Earth Science

### Question 11
Why is Earth sometimes called the "Goldilocks planet"?

A) Because it is gold in colour from space
B) Because it is at just the right distance from the Sun for liquid water to exist
C) Because it was discovered by a scientist named Goldilocks
D) Because it is the third of three rocky planets

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth is in the "Goldilocks zone" (habitable zone) — the region around a star where conditions are "just right" for liquid water to exist on a planet's surface. Not too hot (like Venus) and not too cold (like Mars). The name comes from the fairy tale "Goldilocks and the Three Bears." Liquid water is considered essential for life as we know it.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astrobiology / Planetary Science

### Question 12
Which layer of Earth generates its magnetic field?

A) The crust
B) The mantle
C) The outer core (liquid iron-nickel)
D) The inner core (solid iron-nickel)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth's magnetic field is generated by the motion of liquid iron and nickel in the outer core — a process called the dynamo effect. The outer core is about 2,200 km thick and the convective motion of this electrically conducting liquid creates electric currents that produce the magnetic field. The inner core is solid and cannot generate the field by itself.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Earth Science / Geophysics

### Question 13
What are the Van Allen radiation belts?

A) Layers of the atmosphere that block UV radiation
B) Two doughnut-shaped zones of charged particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field
C) Volcanic regions on Earth's surface
D) Bands of asteroids orbiting Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Van Allen radiation belts are two doughnut-shaped (toroidal) zones of energetic charged particles (mainly protons and electrons) trapped by Earth's magnetic field. The inner belt extends from about 1,000 to 5,000 km altitude, and the outer belt from about 15,000 to 25,000 km. They were discovered in 1958 by James Van Allen using data from the Explorer 1 satellite.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Physics / Geophysics

### Question 14
What is Earth's magnetosphere?

A) The region of visible aurora lights
B) The protective magnetic shield surrounding Earth created by its magnetic field
C) A layer of the atmosphere
D) The region between the crust and mantle

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The magnetosphere is the region of space around Earth dominated by its magnetic field. It extends tens of thousands of kilometres into space and acts as a shield, deflecting most of the charged particles in the solar wind. Without the magnetosphere, the solar wind could strip away Earth's atmosphere over geological time, similar to what likely happened to Mars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Physics

### Question 15
What causes auroras (Northern and Southern Lights)?

A) Reflection of sunlight off polar ice
B) Charged particles from the Sun interacting with gases in Earth's atmosphere near the magnetic poles
C) Volcanic gases glowing at the poles
D) Bioluminescent organisms in polar oceans

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Auroras occur when charged particles from the solar wind are channelled along Earth's magnetic field lines toward the poles. When these particles collide with gas molecules in the upper atmosphere, the molecules become excited and emit light — green and red from oxygen, blue and purple from nitrogen. Auroras are called Aurora Borealis (north) and Aurora Australis (south).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Weather / Atmospheric Physics

### Question 16
What are the four main layers of Earth from outermost to innermost?

A) Crust, mantle, outer core, inner core
B) Lithosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, core
C) Crust, asthenosphere, mantle, core
D) Surface, middle layer, outer layer, centre

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Earth's four main layers from outside to inside are: the crust (thin outer shell, 5–70 km thick), the mantle (thick semi-solid layer, ~2,900 km), the outer core (liquid iron-nickel, ~2,200 km thick, generates the magnetic field), and the inner core (solid iron-nickel ball, ~1,200 km radius, temperature ~5,400°C).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Earth Science / Geology

### Question 17
What is the temperature at Earth's inner core?

A) About 100°C
B) About 1,000°C
C) About 5,400°C
D) About 15 million °C

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The temperature at Earth's inner core is approximately 5,400°C — comparable to the temperature of the Sun's surface (photosphere at ~5,500°C). Despite this extreme heat, the inner core remains solid because the immense pressure at the centre of Earth (about 3.6 million atmospheres) prevents the iron-nickel from melting.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Geophysics / Earth Science

### Question 18
Why is Earth's inner core solid despite being extremely hot?

A) It is made of a material that never melts
B) The extreme pressure at the centre keeps it solid
C) It is cooled by the outer core
D) It only recently formed and hasn't heated up yet

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Although the inner core is at about 5,400°C (hot enough to melt iron at the surface), the immense pressure at Earth's centre — approximately 3.6 million atmospheres — raises the melting point of iron-nickel above its actual temperature. This keeps the inner core solid. The outer core, at lower pressure, remains liquid.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Geophysics

### Question 19
Why do we have a leap year approximately every four years?

A) Because the Moon's orbit takes 4 years to complete
B) Because Earth's rotation slows down every four years
C) Because Earth's orbital period is about 365.25 days, so the extra quarter-days accumulate
D) Because the calendar was designed that way for convenience

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth takes approximately 365.25 days to orbit the Sun — about 6 hours longer than the 365-day calendar year. These extra quarter-days accumulate, and every four years an extra day (February 29) is added to the calendar to keep it synchronised with Earth's orbit. Without leap years, the calendar would drift about 24 days per century relative to the seasons.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy / Calendar Science

### Question 20
Earth's atmosphere protects life in several ways. Which of the following is NOT one of them?

A) The ozone layer blocks harmful ultraviolet radiation
B) The atmosphere provides breathable oxygen
C) The atmosphere prevents all meteoroids from reaching the surface
D) The atmosphere helps maintain a habitable temperature range

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** While the atmosphere does burn up most small meteoroids (creating "shooting stars"), it does NOT prevent ALL meteoroids from reaching the surface. Some survive passage through the atmosphere and land as meteorites. The atmosphere does, however, block harmful UV radiation (via the ozone layer), provide oxygen for breathing, and regulate temperature through the greenhouse effect.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Atmospheric Science / Earth Science

### Question 21

B) A famous photograph of Earth taken by the Apollo 17 crew in 1972
C) The first satellite image of Earth taken by Sputnik
D) An image of Earth taken by the Hubble Space Telescope

**Explanation:** The "Blue Marble" is one of the most famous photographs ever taken. It was captured by the crew of Apollo 17 on December 7, 1972, showing Earth as a complete, illuminated sphere — a fragile blue marble against the blackness of space. The image became an icon of the environmental movement and showed humanity its home planet in a completely new perspective.

### Question 23
What process on Earth recycles minerals and creates diverse habitats?

A) Solar radiation
B) Plate tectonics
C) Ocean currents
D) Atmospheric circulation

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Plate tectonics — the movement of large sections of Earth's crust — is a key factor in making Earth habitable. It recycles minerals through the carbon cycle, creates diverse habitats (mountains, valleys, ocean trenches), generates volcanic activity that releases gases into the atmosphere, and enables the rock cycle. Earth is the only planet known to have active plate tectonics.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 24
Why is the existence of liquid water so important for life on Earth?

A) Water is needed to generate Earth's magnetic field
B) Water is a universal solvent essential for biochemical reactions and is required for all known life
C) Water produces the oxygen in the atmosphere
D) Water keeps the planet spinning on its axis

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Liquid water is considered essential for all known forms of life because it is an excellent solvent — it can dissolve a wide range of substances, enabling the complex chemical reactions necessary for living organisms. Water also regulates temperature, transports nutrients, and provides a medium for biological processes. This is why the search for extraterrestrial life often focuses on finding liquid water.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 25
What is the difference between a sidereal day and a solar day on Earth?

A) A sidereal day is longer than a solar day
B) A sidereal day is about 4 minutes shorter than a solar day because it measures rotation relative to stars, not the Sun
C) They are exactly the same
D) A sidereal day is 12 hours while a solar day is 24 hours

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A sidereal day (~23h 56m) is the time for one complete 360° rotation relative to distant stars. A solar day (24h) is the time between two consecutive noons. The solar day is about 4 minutes longer because Earth must rotate slightly more than 360° to compensate for its orbital motion around the Sun. Over a full year, this extra ~4 minutes per day adds up to one full extra rotation.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Astronomy

### Question 26
If Earth had no axial tilt (0°), what would happen to the seasons?

A) Seasons would be more extreme
B) There would be no seasons — every day would have roughly equal day and night, and temperatures would remain relatively constant year-round
C) Only the poles would have seasons
D) Seasons would reverse — summer would become winter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Without axial tilt, the Sun would always be directly above the equator, and every location on Earth would receive the same amount of sunlight throughout the year. There would be no variation in daylight hours and no seasonal temperature changes. The equator would always be warmest, the poles always coldest, but neither would experience the dramatic seasonal shifts we know.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Astronomy / Earth Science

### Question 27
Why does Mars lack a strong magnetic field like Earth's?

A) Mars has no iron in its core
B) Mars's core has likely solidified and no longer has the convective liquid motion needed for a dynamo
C) Mars is too far from the Sun
D) Mars's rotation is too slow

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's magnetic field is generated by convective motion of liquid iron in its outer core (the dynamo effect). Mars, being smaller, cooled faster over geological time, and its core likely solidified — stopping the dynamo process. Without a global magnetic field, Mars lost its magnetosphere, allowing the solar wind to gradually strip away much of its atmosphere.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Comparative Planetology

### Question 28
Earth's crust comes in two types. Which correctly describes them?

A) Oceanic crust is thicker but lighter; continental crust is thinner but denser
B) Oceanic crust is thinner and denser; continental crust is thicker and lighter
C) Both types of crust are identical in thickness and density
D) Oceanic crust is made of granite; continental crust is made of basalt

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth has two types of crust: oceanic crust (thinner, about 5–10 km, denser, primarily basalt) and continental crust (thicker, about 30–70 km, lighter, primarily granite). Because continental crust is lighter, it "floats" higher on the mantle, forming landmasses. When oceanic and continental plates collide, the denser oceanic plate is typically forced beneath the continental plate (subduction).

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Geology / Earth Science

### Question 29
What role does Earth's ozone layer play?

A) It generates oxygen for breathing
B) It traps heat to keep Earth warm
C) It absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation from the Sun
D) It produces the magnetic field

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The ozone layer, located in the stratosphere at about 15–35 km altitude, absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation, particularly UV-B and UV-C. Without it, this radiation would reach the surface and cause increased rates of skin cancer, cataracts, and damage to ecosystems. The ozone layer is made of O₃ (three oxygen atoms bonded together).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Atmospheric Science

### Question 30
Which statement about Earth is TRUE?

A) Earth is the largest planet in the solar system
B) Earth is the only planet known to support life
C) Earth has two natural satellites
D) Earth's atmosphere is mostly carbon dioxide

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth is the only planet in the solar system (and the only body in the universe) currently known to support life. Earth is NOT the largest planet (Jupiter is), it has only one natural satellite (the Moon, not two), and its atmosphere is mostly nitrogen (78%) and oxygen (21%), not carbon dioxide. Earth's unique combination of liquid water, atmosphere, magnetic field, and habitable temperature makes life possible.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / Astrobiology

What was ISRO's IRS (Indian Remote Sensing) satellite programme designed for?

### Question 32
If Earth's magnetic field suddenly disappeared, what would be the most significant long-term consequence?

A) The Moon would drift away
B) Seasons would stop
C) The solar wind could gradually strip away Earth's atmosphere, making the planet uninhabitable
D) Volcanoes would stop erupting

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Without Earth's magnetic field and the protective magnetosphere it creates, the solar wind — a constant stream of charged particles from the Sun — would directly impact the upper atmosphere. Over geological time, this could strip away significant portions of the atmosphere, as likely happened on Mars. In the short term, increased radiation would also endanger surface life and damage electronics and satellites.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Space Physics / Planetary Science
