## Topic 23 – Vikram Sarabhai — Father of the Indian Space Programme

### Questions for Topic 23

### Question 1
Who is known as the "Father of the Indian Space Programme"?

A) Homi Bhabha
B) APJ Abdul Kalam
C) Vikram Sarabhai
D) Satish Dhawan

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Vikram Ambalal Sarabhai is universally recognised as the "Father of the Indian Space Programme." He established INCOSPAR in 1962, oversaw the first sounding rocket launch from Thumba in 1963, and became the first Chairman of ISRO when it was formed in 1969. His vision that space technology should serve national development — not prestige — shaped India's entire approach to space.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Indian Space History

### Question 6
Which institution did Vikram Sarabhai found in 1947 that is known as the "cradle of space science in India"?

A) Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)
B) Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC)
C) Physical Research Laboratory (PRL)
D) Space Applications Centre (SAC)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sarabhai founded the Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) in Ahmedabad in 1947, at the young age of 28. PRL started in just two rooms at M.G. Science Institute and grew into India's premier research laboratory for physics, astronomy, and space science. It is often called the "cradle of space science in India" because it nurtured much of the early research that led to India's space programme.

### Question 7
What committee did Vikram Sarabhai establish in 1962 that was the precursor to ISRO?

A) Department of Space (DoS)
B) Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR)
C) Space Commission
D) National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sarabhai convinced the Indian government to establish the Indian National Committee for Space Research (INCOSPAR) in 1962. INCOSPAR was responsible for organising and coordinating India's space research activities. It oversaw the establishment of the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station and the first sounding rocket launches. INCOSPAR was later reorganised into ISRO on August 15, 1969.

### Question 8

A) 1962, Homi Bhabha
B) August 15, 1969, Vikram Sarabhai
C) 1972, Satish Dhawan
D) 1975, U R Rao

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) was formed on August 15, 1969, India's Independence Day, as a reorganisation of INCOSPAR. Vikram Sarabhai became its first Chairman and served until his untimely death on December 30, 1971. Under his leadership, ISRO laid the groundwork for India's satellite and launch vehicle programmes.

### Question 9
What was the first sounding rocket launched from Thumba, and when?

A) Rohini-75, November 1967
B) Nike-Apache, November 21, 1963
C) RH-200, January 1970
D) SLV-3, August 1979

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The first sounding rocket launched from the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) was the Nike-Apache, an American-made rocket, on November 21, 1963. This event is celebrated as the beginning of India's space programme. The launch was overseen by Vikram Sarabhai's INCOSPAR. Famously, parts of the rocket were transported on a bicycle due to limited infrastructure.

### Question 10
What was Vikram Sarabhai's core vision for India's space programme?

A) To compete with the USA and USSR in manned space exploration
B) To build nuclear-powered rockets for military supremacy
C) To use space technology for national development — communication, education, weather, and resource management
D) To colonise the Moon and Mars before other nations

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sarabhai's vision was that space technology should serve as a tool for national development in a developing country like India. He advocated using space for television broadcasting to remote villages, weather forecasting to help farmers, telecommunications to connect a vast nation, and resource mapping for planning. This development-first approach, rather than prestige-driven space exploration, became the guiding philosophy of ISRO.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Sarabhai's Speeches

### Question 11
Which famous quote by Vikram Sarabhai best captures his vision for the Indian space programme?

A) "One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind"
B) "There are some who question the relevance of space activities in a developing nation. To us, there is no ambiguity of purpose."
C) "Space is the final frontier"
D) "We choose to go to the Moon in this decade"

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sarabhai's most famous statement began: "There are some who question the relevance of space activities in a developing nation. To us, there is no ambiguity of purpose." He went on to say India did not seek to compete with advanced nations in moon exploration or manned spaceflight, but was convinced that space technology must be applied to "the real problems of man and society." This quote encapsulates ISRO's entire philosophy.

A) The orbiter

### Question 21

This strategic choice reflects Sarabhai's scientific approach to building India's space capability.
