## Topic 30 – Indian Earth Observation Satellites (IRS/EOS)

### Questions for Topic 30

### Question 1
What does IRS stand for in the context of Indian space satellites?

A) International Research Satellite
B) Indian Remote Sensing
C) Integrated Radar System
D) Indian Reconnaissance Satellite

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** IRS stands for Indian Remote Sensing. The IRS series forms one of the largest constellations of Earth observation satellites in the world. These satellites carry sensors that look down at Earth's surface to collect data used for agriculture, forestry, water resources, disaster management, and many other applications.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
What was India's first operational remote sensing satellite?

A) Aryabhata
B) INSAT-1A
C) IRS-1A
D) Cartosat-1

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** IRS-1A, launched in 1988, was India's first operational remote sensing satellite. It was launched aboard a Soviet Vostok rocket since India did not yet have a launch vehicle capable of placing it in the required orbit. IRS-1A carried cameras to image the Indian landmass for resource mapping.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
What do Earth observation satellites primarily observe?

A) Deep space objects like galaxies and nebulae
B) Other planets in the solar system
C) Earth's surface, atmosphere, and oceans
D) The Sun's activity and solar flares

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Earth observation satellites carry sensors that look DOWN at Earth, not outward at space. They collect data about Earth's surface features, vegetation, water bodies, urban areas, atmospheric conditions, and ocean properties. This data supports agriculture, disaster management, weather forecasting, and many other applications.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
Which of these is a modern naming convention that ISRO adopted for its Earth observation satellites?

A) GSAT
B) INSAT
C) EOS
D) CMS

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** ISRO transitioned to the EOS (Earth Observation Satellite) naming convention for its newer Earth observation missions. Earlier satellites used names like IRS, Cartosat, Resourcesat, and Oceansat. Under the new system, satellites are designated as EOS-01, EOS-02, EOS-04, EOS-06, etc. The older programme-specific names are sometimes still used alongside EOS designations.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
What is the primary purpose of the Cartosat series of satellites?

A) Communication and broadcasting
B) Weather monitoring
C) High-resolution cartography and mapping
D) Navigation

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Cartosat series provides high-resolution images for cartography (map-making) and terrain mapping. Starting with Cartosat-1 in 2005 which offered stereo imaging, the series advanced to Cartosat-3 in 2019 which achieves sub-metre resolution of approximately 0.25 metres in panchromatic mode — among the best civilian imaging capabilities in the world.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What does SAR stand for, and which Indian satellite series uses this technology?

A) Satellite Altimetry Radar — Oceansat series
B) Synthetic Aperture Radar — RISAT series
C) Space Antenna Receiver — INSAT series
D) Standard Atmospheric Radar — Cartosat series

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SAR stands for Synthetic Aperture Radar, and it is the key technology used in the RISAT (Radar Imaging Satellite) series. SAR uses microwave signals rather than visible light, which gives it a crucial advantage: it can image Earth's surface through clouds and in total darkness, making it invaluable for monitoring during cyclones, floods, and at night.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
What is the main advantage of radar imaging satellites (RISAT) over optical imaging satellites?

A) They produce colour images
B) They can image through clouds and in darkness
C) They are cheaper to build
D) They have higher resolution than optical satellites

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The main advantage of radar imaging satellites like RISAT is their all-weather, day-and-night imaging capability. Optical satellites depend on sunlight and clear skies to capture images. SAR satellites emit their own microwave signals that penetrate clouds and do not need sunlight, making them essential for monitoring floods, cyclones, and other events where clouds typically obstruct optical imaging.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
Which satellite series monitors India's oceans, including sea surface temperature and potential fishing zones?

A) Cartosat
B) RISAT
C) Oceansat

**Explanation:** The Oceansat series is dedicated to ocean studies. Starting with Oceansat-1 (1999), followed by Oceansat-2 (2009) and Oceansat-3/EOS-06 (2022), these satellites measure ocean colour, sea surface temperature, and wind speed. One of their most impactful applications is identifying Potential Fishing Zones (PFZ), which directly helps fishermen locate fish-rich areas.

### Question 11
Which satellite is a joint mission between NASA and ISRO for advanced radar Earth observation?

A) Chandrayaan-1
B) NISAR
C) AstroSat
D) Aditya-L1

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) is a joint mission between NASA and ISRO. It carries a dual-frequency SAR system — L-band radar built by NASA/JPL and S-band radar built by ISRO. NISAR is designed to map the entire Earth every 12 days, monitoring ecosystems, ice sheets, earthquakes, volcanoes, and groundwater with unprecedented detail.

### Question 12

### Question 15
What is RISAT-1's significance in India's satellite programme?

A) It was India's first communication satellite
B) It was India's first indigenous SAR (radar imaging) satellite
C) It was India's first satellite launched to the Moon
D) It was India's first navigation satellite

**Answer:** B

### Question 16
What is the difference between panchromatic and multispectral imaging?

A) Panchromatic uses radar while multispectral uses cameras
B) Panchromatic captures a single broad band (black-and-white, higher resolution) while multispectral captures several colour bands
C) Panchromatic works only in daylight while multispectral works at night
D) There is no difference — they are the same technology

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Panchromatic imaging captures a single broad wavelength band, producing black-and-white images at higher spatial resolution. Multispectral imaging captures images in several distinct colour bands (such as red, green, near-infrared), enabling analysis of surface composition — for example, healthy vegetation reflects strongly in near-infrared. Satellites often carry both types of cameras for complementary data.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Remote Sensing Science

### Question 17
What special capability does hyperspectral imaging (as used in HySIS) provide compared to multispectral imaging?

A) It can only image very small areas
B) It images in many narrow spectral bands (55 in HySIS), enabling very detailed compositional analysis
C) It works only in visible light
D) It produces lower resolution images

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** HySIS (Hyper Spectral Imaging Satellite), launched in 2018, images in 55 narrow spectral bands compared to the 3-4 bands typical of multispectral sensors. This fine spectral resolution allows very detailed analysis of surface composition — for example, distinguishing between different crop types, assessing plant health at a detailed level, identifying specific minerals, and detecting water pollution.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 18

C) Ka-band (NASA) and Ku-band (ISRO)

Together, they enable comprehensive monitoring of Earth's land surfaces, ice sheets, and geological activity.

### Question 32
