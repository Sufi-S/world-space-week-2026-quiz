## Topic 50 – World Space Week and the "Rocket Revolution" Theme

### Questions for Topic 50

### Question 1
When is World Space Week celebrated?

A) January 1–7
B) October 4–10 every year
C) July 20–26
D) December 1–7

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** World Space Week is celebrated every year from October 4 to October 10. October 4 marks the launch of Sputnik 1 (1957), the first human-made satellite, and October 10 marks the signing of the Outer Space Treaty (1967). It was declared by the United Nations General Assembly on 6 December 1999.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** UNOOSA / WSWA

### Question 2
Why does World Space Week start on October 4?

A) It's the birthday of a famous astronaut
B) October 4, 1957 is the date Sputnik 1 was launched — the first human-made satellite
C) It's the anniversary of the first Moon landing
D) It's when NASA was founded

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** October 4, 1957 was when the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the first human-made satellite to orbit Earth. This event shocked the world, triggered the Space Race between the USA and USSR, and is considered the beginning of the Space Age. The choice of this date as the start of World Space Week commemorates this transformative moment in human history.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History / WSWA

### Question 3
Why does World Space Week end on October 10?

A) It's 7 days after Sputnik
B) October 10, 1967 is when the Outer Space Treaty was signed
C) It's the anniversary of the last Apollo mission
D) It's the UN's founding date

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** October 10, 1967 marks the signing of the Outer Space Treaty — the foundation of international space law. The treaty established that outer space cannot be claimed by any nation, prohibited placing weapons of mass destruction in space, and declared that astronauts are "envoys of mankind." Over 110 countries have ratified this treaty.

### Question 5
What was Sputnik 1?

A) The first human in space
B) The first human-made satellite — a 83.6 kg sphere launched by the USSR in 1957
C) The first Moon landing mission
D) The first Mars mission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957, was the first human-made satellite. It was a polished metal sphere about 58 cm in diameter, weighing 83.6 kg. It orbited Earth every 96 minutes at about 29,000 km/h, transmitting a "beep-beep" radio signal for 21 days. It burned up on re-entry on 4 January 1958. Sputnik's launch triggered the Space Race and led to the creation of NASA in 1958.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 6
Who is credited with inventing gunpowder rockets?

A) Robert Goddard (USA)
B) Ancient China (approximately 13th century)
C) Werner von Braun (Germany)
D) India

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Gunpowder rockets were invented in ancient China around the 13th century. They were initially used as weapons and fireworks. From China, rocket technology spread along trade routes to the Middle East, Europe, and India. Tipu Sultan of Mysore (18th century India) developed advanced iron-cased military rockets that were among the most sophisticated of their time.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 7
What was significant about Tipu Sultan's Mysorean rockets?

A) They were the first to reach space
B) They were among the first iron-cased rockets used in warfare, and influenced British rocket development
C) They were used to launch satellites
D) They were only used for fireworks

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Tipu Sultan's Mysorean rockets (18th century, India) were among the first iron-cased rockets used as weapons — a significant advance over earlier paper or bamboo-cased rockets. The iron casing allowed larger charges, greater range, and more destructive power. After defeating Tipu Sultan, the British studied captured Mysorean rockets and developed the Congreve rocket, which was used in the Napoleonic Wars.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History / Indian History

### Question 8
Who launched the first liquid-fuelled rocket?

A) Wernher von Braun (Germany)
B) Robert Goddard (USA, 1926)
C) Konstantin Tsiolkovsky (Russia)
D) Tipu Sultan (India)

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Robert Goddard (USA) launched the first liquid-fuelled rocket on 16 March 1926 in Auburn, Massachusetts. The small rocket flew for about 2.5 seconds, reaching an altitude of 12.5 metres. While modest by today's standards, this was a revolutionary demonstration that liquid propellants could power rockets — the principle behind every major launch vehicle since, including Saturn V, PSLV, and Falcon 9.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 9
What was the Saturn V rocket?

A) A modern SpaceX rocket
B) The most powerful rocket of the Apollo era — it carried astronauts to the Moon
C) India's first launch vehicle
D) A Soviet space station

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Saturn V was the massive rocket that powered NASA's Apollo programme (1967-1973). Standing about 111 metres tall (taller than the Statue of Liberty) and producing approximately 34 million Newtons of thrust, it was the most powerful rocket ever successfully flown until recent years. Designed by Wernher von Braun's team, Saturn V launched all crewed Moon missions and the Skylab space station.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 10
What was the first rocket launched from India, and when?

A) SLV-3 in 1980
B) A Nike-Apache sounding rocket from Thumba in 1963
C) PSLV in 1993
D) Rohini in 1967

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The first rocket launched from India was an American-made Nike-Apache sounding rocket, launched from the Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) on 21 November 1963. This small beginning, under the leadership of Vikram Sarabhai, marked the start of India's space programme. India's first indigenous rockets — the Rohini sounding rockets — followed from 1967 onwards.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What was India's first satellite launch vehicle?

A) PSLV
B) SLV-3 (1980) — with A.P.J. Abdul Kalam as project director
C) GSLV
D) LVM3

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SLV-3 (Satellite Launch Vehicle-3) was India's first satellite launch vehicle, which successfully placed the Rohini satellite into orbit on 18 July 1980. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam (who later became India's President) was the project director. India became only the 7th nation to develop indigenous satellite launch capability. SLV-3 was a small, 4-stage, all-solid-fuel rocket.

### Question 12
What makes SpaceX's Falcon 9 revolutionary?

A) It's the largest rocket ever built
B) It was the first orbital-class rocket to land and fly again, dramatically reducing launch costs
C) It was the first rocket to reach space
D) It's the fastest rocket ever

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SpaceX's Falcon 9 was the first orbital-class rocket to successfully land its first stage booster and reuse it for subsequent missions. This reusability has dramatically reduced launch costs — from roughly $10,000/kg to about $2,700/kg. Before Falcon 9, all orbital rockets were expendable (thrown away after one use). Routine booster landing and reuse is the core of the modern "Rocket Revolution."

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** SpaceX

### Question 13
What is the Outer Space Treaty's key principle about celestial bodies?

A) Only the USA can claim the Moon
B) No nation can claim sovereignty over any celestial body — space is free for exploration by all
C) Only UN member nations can explore space
D) Celestial bodies can be sold to private companies

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Outer Space Treaty (1967) establishes that outer space, including the Moon and other celestial bodies, is not subject to national appropriation — no nation can claim them as territory. Key principles include: space is free for exploration by all nations, no weapons of mass destruction in space, astronauts are "envoys of mankind," and nations are responsible for their space activities (including those of private companies).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** United Nations

### Question 14
Trace India's rocket evolution from the 1960s to today.

A) India always had large rockets
B) Nike-Apache (1963) → Rohini sounding rockets → SLV-3 (1980) → ASLV → PSLV → GSLV → LVM3
C) India only has one type of rocket
D) India's rockets were all bought from other countries

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** India's rocket journey spans 60+ years: starting with a small American Nike-Apache (1963) at Thumba, then indigenous Rohini sounding rockets (1967+), SLV-3 — the first satellite launcher (1980), ASLV — augmented SLV (1987, limited success), PSLV — the workhorse with 50+ consecutive successes (1993+), GSLV with indigenous cryogenic engine (2001+), and LVM3 — the heaviest launcher for Chandrayaan and commercial missions. Future plans include NGLV and reusable vehicles.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 15
What is SpaceX's Starship?

A) A small satellite
B) The largest and most powerful rocket ever built, designed to be fully reusable
C) A space station
D) A Moon rover

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SpaceX's Starship is the largest and most powerful rocket ever built, standing about 121 metres tall (taller than Saturn V). It is designed to be fully reusable — both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage are intended to land and fly again. If successful, full reusability could further revolutionise launch costs and is intended to enable crewed missions to Mars.

### Question 16
Which body declared World Space Week, and when?

A) NASA in 1969
B) The United Nations General Assembly on 6 December 1999
C) ESA in 2000
D) ISRO in 2005

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** World Space Week was declared by the United Nations General Assembly on 6 December 1999 (Resolution 54/68). It is described as the largest annual space event in the world, celebrated in over 95 nations with more than 8,000 events. It is coordinated by the World Space Week Association (WSWA), a non-governmental, non-profit organisation, in collaboration with the United Nations.

### Question 17
What was the V-2 rocket's claim to fame?

A) First liquid-fuelled rocket
B) First rocket to reach space (~100 km altitude, 1944) — developed by Germany during World War II
C) First orbital rocket
D) First rocket used in India

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The V-2 (Vergeltungswaffe 2), developed by Wernher von Braun's team in Germany during WWII, was the first rocket to reach space, crossing the Kármán line (~100 km) in 1944. It was also the first long-range ballistic missile. After the war, V-2 technology and captured German scientists (including von Braun) were crucial for both American and Soviet rocket programmes that eventually led to the Space Age.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 18
What is Konstantin Tsiolkovsky known for?

A) Building the first rocket
B) Being the theoretical "father of astronautics" — he mathematically proved multi-stage rockets could reach space (1903)
C) Walking on the Moon
D) Founding NASA

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Konstantin Tsiolkovsky (Russia, 1857-1935) is known as the "father of astronautics." In 1903, he published work mathematically proving that multi-stage rockets using liquid propellants could achieve orbital velocity. His famous "rocket equation" (Tsiolkovsky equation) remains fundamental to rocket science. He theorised about space stations, air locks, and even space elevators — decades before any of these became possible.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 19
How has reusability changed the economics of spaceflight?

A) It hasn't changed anything
B) Reusing rocket boosters (like Falcon 9) has reduced launch costs from ~$10,000/kg to ~$2,700/kg
C) It has made launches more expensive
D) Only government agencies benefit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Before reusable rockets, every launch vehicle was thrown away after a single use — like throwing away an aeroplane after one flight. SpaceX's Falcon 9 pioneered routine booster reuse, cutting costs dramatically (from roughly $10,000/kg to about $2,700/kg). This has made satellite launches, space station resupply, and even space tourism more economically viable, opening space to more participants and applications.

**Source:** Space Industry

### Question 20
What are ISRO's future rocket plans?

A) ISRO has no future plans
B) Semi-cryogenic engine (SCE-200), Reusable Launch Vehicle (RLV) demonstrator, and Next Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV)
C) ISRO will only use PSLV forever

### Question 21
What is the significance of the R-7 rocket in space history?

A) It was the first Indian rocket
B) It was the Soviet ICBM that launched Sputnik 1 — the rocket that started the Space Age
C) It was a NASA rocket
D) It was the first Chinese launch vehicle

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The R-7, designed by Sergei Korolev, was the Soviet Union's first intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM). On 4 October 1957, a modified R-7 launched Sputnik 1 into orbit, making it the rocket that started the Space Age. The R-7 went on to launch Yuri Gagarin and is the ancestor of the Soyuz rocket family — the most-launched rocket family in history, still in use today.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History
