## Topic 42 – Space Stations and Life in Space

### Questions for Topic 42

### Question 1
What was the world's first space station?

A) Skylab
B) Mir
C) Salyut 1
D) ISS

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Salyut 1, launched by the Soviet Union in 1971, was the world's first space station. Tragically, the three cosmonauts who visited it aboard Soyuz 11 died during re-entry due to cabin depressurisation. Despite this tragedy, Salyut 1 proved that humans could live and work in orbit for extended periods.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Space History

### Question 2
What is the ISS, and how many nations are involved?

A) Indian Space Station — operated by India alone
B) International Space Station — a partnership of 15 nations
C) International Satellite System — a network of communication satellites
D) Interplanetary Space Ship — a deep-space vessel

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The ISS (International Space Station) is the largest structure ever built in space, involving 15 nations. The five partner agencies are NASA (USA), Roscosmos (Russia), ESA (Europe), JAXA (Japan), and CSA (Canada). It has been continuously inhabited since November 2, 2000 — over 25 years of unbroken human presence in orbit.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA / ISS

### Question 3
At approximately what altitude does the ISS orbit?

A) About 100 km
B) About 400 km
C) About 36,000 km
D) About 384,000 km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The ISS orbits at approximately 400 km (250 miles) above Earth. At this altitude, it travels at about 28,000 km/h and circles the Earth every approximately 90 minutes. This means astronauts see 16 sunrises and sunsets every day.

### Question 5
What was the first modular space station?

A) Salyut 1
B) Skylab
C) Mir
D) ISS

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mir, operated by the Soviet Union and then Russia from 1986 to 2001, was the first modular space station — assembled in orbit from multiple separately launched modules. This modular approach allowed the station to grow over time and became the model for building the ISS. Mir was de-orbited in 2001.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History

### Question 6
What is the name of China's current space station?

A) Shenzhou
B) Tiangong
C) Tianwen
D) Chang'e

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Tiangong ("Heavenly Palace") is China's current modular space station. Completed in 2022, it consists of three modules: Tianhe (core module, launched 2021), Wentian (laboratory, July 2022), and Mengtian (laboratory, October 2022). It has been permanently crewed since June 2022 and has a mass of approximately 100,000 kg.

### Question 8
Why must astronauts on the ISS exercise about 2-2.5 hours every day?

A) To stay entertained during free time
B) To combat bone density loss and muscle atrophy caused by microgravity
C) To generate electrical power for the station
D) To maintain the station's orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** In microgravity, bones lose approximately 1-2% of their density per month and muscles atrophy because they don't need to work against gravity. Mandatory daily exercise (about 2-2.5 hours) using specially designed equipment — a treadmill with harness, stationary bicycle, and resistance exercise device — helps slow these losses and keeps astronauts healthy enough to readapt to Earth's gravity upon return.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 9
How does the ISS get its electrical power?

A) Nuclear reactors
B) 8 large solar panel arrays generating approximately 240 kW
C) Fuel cells that burn hydrogen
D) Batteries charged before launch

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The ISS generates electrical power from 8 large solar panel arrays that convert sunlight into electricity, producing approximately 240 kW of power. The solar arrays span about 73 metres end-to-end, making them among the largest structures on the station. Batteries store power for use when the station is in Earth's shadow (about 35 minutes of each 90-minute orbit).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 10
What is the record for the longest continuous stay in space by a single person?

A) 100 days on the ISS
B) 437 days on Mir by Valeri Polyakov
C) 365 days on the ISS by Scott Kelly
D) 500 days on Tiangong

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Russian cosmonaut Valeri Polyakov holds the record for the longest single continuous stay in space — 437 days aboard the Mir space station from January 1994 to March 1995. This record has stood for over 30 years. Scott Kelly's 340-day ISS mission (2015-2016) was notable but shorter than Polyakov's record.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space Records

### Question 11
How does the ISS handle its water supply?

A) It carries all water from Earth and never reuses it
B) It recycles approximately 90% of water, including from sweat and urine
C) It generates water from hydrogen and oxygen on board
D) It receives fresh water every day from Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The ISS recycles approximately 90% of its water through sophisticated recycling systems that process humidity from the air, sweat, and even urine into clean drinking water. Transporting water from Earth costs about $10,000 per kilogram, so recycling is essential. The water recycling system produces water that is cleaner than most municipal tap water.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 12
How do astronauts sleep in the ISS?

A) In regular beds with gravity
B) Floating freely in the middle of the station
C) In sleeping bags attached to the wall inside small crew quarters
D) Only during specific orbital positions

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Astronauts sleep in small personal crew quarters in sleeping bags that are attached to the wall. In microgravity, there is no "up" or "down," so you can sleep in any orientation — the sleeping bag prevents you from drifting into equipment. Earplugs are commonly used because the station's equipment produces significant background noise.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** NASA

### Question 13
What is microgravity?

A) Complete absence of gravity in space
B) Near-weightlessness experienced in orbit, where gravity is still present but objects are in free fall
C) Gravity that only exists on small planets
D) Artificial gravity created by spinning spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Microgravity is NOT zero gravity. At the ISS altitude of 400 km, gravity is still about 90% of what it is on Earth's surface. Astronauts feel weightless because they are in continuous free fall — the station and everyone inside it are falling toward Earth together while moving forward fast enough to keep missing it (orbit). This creates the sensation of floating.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 14
What was Skylab?

A) A Soviet space station
B) The first American space station, launched in 1973 using a modified Saturn V
C) China's first space station
D) A space telescope

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Skylab was the first American space station, launched in 1973 using a modified Saturn V rocket (left over from the Apollo programme). Three crews visited Skylab, with the longest stay being 84 days (Skylab 4). It fell back to Earth in 1979, scattering debris over Western Australia — some pieces were famously found by locals.

### Question 16
What is an EVA?

A) Extra Vehicle Attachment — connecting modules in space
B) Extra-Vehicular Activity — a spacewalk outside the spacecraft
C) Emergency Vacuum Alert — a safety warning
D) Electric Voltage Alternator — power system component

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** EVA stands for Extra-Vehicular Activity, commonly called a spacewalk. During an EVA, an astronaut goes outside the spacecraft in a protective space suit to perform tasks like repairing equipment, installing new components, or conducting experiments. Over 259 EVAs have been conducted from the ISS since 1998.

### Question 18
What is a space suit essentially designed to be?

A) Just protective clothing
B) A one-person spacecraft providing oxygen, pressure, temperature control, and protection
C) A fashion item for astronauts
D) A communication device

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A space suit is essentially a one-person spacecraft. It must provide breathable oxygen, remove CO₂, maintain internal pressure (space is a vacuum), regulate temperature (extreme hot/cold in space), and protect against micrometeorites and harmful radiation. EVA suits like NASA's EMU weigh about 127 kg on Earth and have approximately 14 protective layers.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 19
What is the approximate mass of the ISS?

A) About 10,000 kg
B) About 100,000 kg
C) About 420,000 kg
D) About 1,000,000 kg

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The ISS has a mass of approximately 420,000 kg (420 tonnes), making it the largest and most massive structure ever assembled in space. Its pressurised volume is about 916 m³ — roughly the size of a 6-bedroom house. The station spans about 109 metres end-to-end with its solar arrays extended — about the size of a football field.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA

### Question 20
Why do astronauts need to "pre-breathe" pure oxygen before a spacewalk?

A) To get more energy
B) To avoid decompression sickness, since EVA suits operate at lower pressure than the station
C) To clean their lungs
D) To test the suit's oxygen supply

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** EVA suits operate at a lower pressure (about 4.3 psi) than the station interior (about 14.7 psi). If astronauts went directly from higher to lower pressure, dissolved nitrogen in their blood could form bubbles — decompression sickness (the "bends"), similar to what deep-sea divers can experience. Pre-breathing pure oxygen for several hours removes nitrogen from the body.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA
