## Topic 38 – AstroSat — India's First Multi-Wavelength Space Observatory

### Questions for Topic 38

### Question 1
What is AstroSat?

A) India's first weather satellite
B) India's first multi-wavelength space observatory
C) India's first navigation satellite
D) India's first Mars orbiter

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat is India's first dedicated multi-wavelength space observatory. Launched in 2015, it observes the universe simultaneously in visible, ultraviolet, and X-ray wavelengths. This multi-wavelength capability allows comprehensive study of celestial objects that would be impossible from ground-based telescopes alone.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
When was AstroSat launched?

A) 22 October 2008
B) 5 November 2013
C) 28 September 2015
D) 2 September 2023

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** AstroSat was launched on 28 September 2015 from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota, aboard PSLV-C30. Despite being designed for a 5-year mission life, it continues to operate as of 2026, making it one of India's longest-serving science satellites with over 10 years of observations.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
In how many wavelength bands can AstroSat observe simultaneously?

A) Only visible light
B) Two bands (visible and infrared)
C) Five bands (visible, UV, soft X-ray, hard X-ray, and sky monitoring)
D) Only X-rays

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** AstroSat's unique capability is simultaneous observation in five different wavelength bands using its five instruments: visible light and UV (UVIT), soft X-rays (SXT), hard X-rays (LAXPC and CZTI), and X-ray sky monitoring (SSM). Very few space observatories in the world can observe across such a wide range simultaneously.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
Why must X-ray and UV observations be made from space rather than from the ground?

A) Ground telescopes are too expensive
B) Earth's atmosphere blocks X-rays and most UV radiation
C) X-rays are too faint to detect from the ground
D) Ground telescopes can observe all wavelengths equally well

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Earth's atmosphere absorbs most electromagnetic radiation except visible light and radio waves. X-rays, ultraviolet, and gamma rays from space are blocked by the atmosphere and cannot reach ground-based telescopes. This is why space observatories like AstroSat are essential for studying the universe in these wavelengths.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy Science

### Question 5
How many scientific instruments does AstroSat carry?

A) 3
B) 5
C) 7
D) 11

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat carries 5 scientific instruments: UVIT (Ultra-Violet Imaging Telescope), LAXPC (Large Area X-ray Proportional Counter), SXT (Soft X-ray Telescope), CZTI (Cadmium Zinc Telluride Imager), and SSM (Scanning Sky Monitor). Each covers a different part of the electromagnetic spectrum.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
What does UVIT stand for, and what does it do?

A) Universal Visible Imaging Technology — photographs Earth's surface
B) Ultra-Violet Imaging Telescope — images celestial objects in UV and visible light
C) Underwater Vehicle Imaging Technology — studies ocean floors
D) Ultra-Violet Infrared Telescope — studies heat from stars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** UVIT (Ultra-Violet Imaging Telescope) consists of twin 38 cm telescopes that image celestial objects in far-ultraviolet (130-180 nm), near-ultraviolet (200-300 nm), and visible (320-530 nm) light. It has produced stunning images of galaxies and star-forming regions, and is AstroSat's most publicly visible instrument.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
What is the altitude and orbit of AstroSat?

A) 36,000 km geostationary orbit
B) 650 km low Earth orbit with 6° inclination
C) 100 km lunar orbit
D) 1.5 million km at the L1 point

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat orbits at 650 km altitude in a near-equatorial orbit with 6° inclination. This low Earth orbit is well above the densest parts of the atmosphere (allowing clear observations) while being low enough for the PSLV to place it precisely. It is NOT in geostationary orbit, which would be at approximately 36,000 km.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What remarkable detection did AstroSat's CZTI instrument make related to gravitational waves?

A) It directly detected gravitational waves
B) It detected gamma-ray burst signals that were electromagnetic counterparts of LIGO gravitational wave events
C) It measured the speed of gravitational waves
D) It disproved the existence of gravitational waves

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat's CZTI (Cadmium Zinc Telluride Imager) detected gamma-ray burst (GRB) signals that corresponded to events also detected by LIGO as gravitational waves. When two neutron stars merge, they produce both gravitational waves (detected by LIGO) and electromagnetic radiation (detected by telescopes like AstroSat). Detecting both from the same event is called multi-messenger astronomy.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO / LIGO

### Question 9
What is special about AstroSat's Guest Observer programme?

A) It allows Indian scientists priority access
B) It allows any astronomer worldwide to propose observations using AstroSat
C) It lets tourists view through AstroSat's telescope
D) It is a training programme for ISRO engineers

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat has an open Guest Observer programme that allows astronomers from any country to submit proposals to use AstroSat for their research. This was a first for an Indian astronomy satellite and reflects ISRO's commitment to contributing to global science. The programme is managed through the AstroSat Science Support Cell at IUCAA, Pune.

### Question 11
What is a gamma-ray burst (GRB)?

A) A burst of visible light from the Sun
B) An extremely energetic explosion in distant galaxies — among the most luminous events in the universe
C) A type of radar signal
D) A release of energy from nuclear reactors

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Gamma-ray bursts are the most energetic explosions in the universe, releasing more energy in seconds than the Sun will emit in its entire lifetime. They occur in distant galaxies and are associated with the collapse of massive stars or the merger of compact objects like neutron stars. AstroSat's CZTI has detected several GRBs, contributing to our understanding of these extreme events.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Astronomy Science

### Question 12
What was the mass of AstroSat?

A) 500 kg
B) 1,515 kg
C) 3,850 kg
D) 5,000 kg

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat has a mass of 1,515 kg. This is relatively modest for a space observatory — for comparison, the Hubble Space Telescope weighs about 11,000 kg and the JWST about 6,200 kg. Despite its smaller size, AstroSat's unique multi-wavelength capability makes it a valuable asset for the global astronomy community.

### Question 13
How does AstroSat compare to the Hubble Space Telescope?

A) They are identical in capability
B) AstroSat is much smaller but uniquely covers UV and X-ray bands simultaneously; Hubble focuses mainly on visible and near-UV/IR
C) AstroSat is larger and more capable than Hubble
D) Hubble does X-ray astronomy while AstroSat does visible light

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** AstroSat is much smaller than Hubble and not comparable in optical resolution. However, AstroSat's strength is its simultaneous multi-wavelength coverage from visible through UV to X-rays — something Hubble cannot do. The two are complementary: UVIT images have provided UV data that complements Hubble's optical observations of the same objects.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / NASA

### Question 15
Which of the following is a correct achievement of AstroSat?

A) It discovered a new planet in the solar system
B) It discovered a new galaxy cluster in UV and detected extreme UV galaxies
C) It was the first satellite to photograph the Moon

**Answer:** B

These discoveries showcase the value of UV observations from space, which reveal star-forming regions and galactic structures not visible in other wavelengths.

### Question 16
What does the LAXPC instrument on AstroSat study?

A) Lunar surface composition
B) X-ray timing from compact objects like neutron stars and black holes
C) Solar wind particles
D) Earth's atmosphere

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** LAXPC (Large Area X-ray Proportional Counter) consists of three co-aligned proportional counters designed for X-ray timing studies in the 3-80 keV energy range. It is particularly useful for studying rapidly varying X-ray sources like neutron stars, black hole binaries, and X-ray pulsars. At launch, it had the largest collecting area of any X-ray timing instrument in space.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 17
What is multi-wavelength astronomy?

A) Studying the same object using different telescope sizes
B) Observing celestial objects in multiple wavelength bands (visible, UV, X-ray, etc.) to get a complete picture
C) Using multiple telescopes on the ground simultaneously
D) Studying only radio waves from space

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Multi-wavelength astronomy means observing the same celestial object in different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum. Different wavelengths reveal different physical processes — visible light shows stellar surfaces, UV reveals hot young stars and gas, X-rays expose extremely hot material near black holes and neutron stars. AstroSat's ability to observe in multiple bands simultaneously makes it exceptionally valuable.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Astronomy Science

### Question 18
Which of these statements about AstroSat is FALSE?

A) It is in geostationary orbit at 36,000 km
B) It carries 5 scientific instruments
C) It has an open Guest Observer programme
D) It was launched by PSLV-C30

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The false statement is A. AstroSat is NOT in geostationary orbit. It orbits at 650 km altitude in a near-equatorial orbit with 6° inclination — a low Earth orbit. Geostationary orbit (36,000 km) is used by communication and weather satellites. All other statements are correct.
