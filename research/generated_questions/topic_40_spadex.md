## Topic 40 – SpaDeX — Space Docking Experiment

### Questions for Topic 40

### Question 1
What does SpaDeX stand for?

A) Space Defence Experiment
B) Space Docking Experiment
C) Spacecraft Design Excellence
D) Space Data Exchange

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SpaDeX stands for Space Docking Experiment. It is an ISRO mission that demonstrated autonomous orbital docking of two spacecraft, making India the fourth country to achieve in-space docking technology, after Russia, the USA, and China.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
When was SpaDeX launched?

A) 2 September 2023
B) 1 January 2024
C) 30 December 2024
D) 16 January 2025

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** SpaDeX was launched on 30 December 2024 from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota, aboard PSLV-C60. The docking was achieved on 16 January 2025, about 17 days after launch. The 30 December date was the launch, and 16 January was the successful docking.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
What are the two satellites in the SpaDeX mission called?

A) Vikram and Pragyan
B) SDX01 (Chaser) and SDX02 (Target)
C) Alpha and Beta
D) Orbiter-1 and Orbiter-2

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SpaDeX deployed two satellites: SDX01 (the Chaser, approximately 220 kg) and SDX02 (the Target, approximately 300 kg). In docking terminology, the Chaser actively approaches and docks with the Target, which maintains a passive position. This is standard terminology used worldwide for docking missions.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
India became the ___ country to achieve in-space docking.

A) First
B) Second
C) Third
D) Fourth

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** India became the fourth country to demonstrate in-space docking, after Russia (Kosmos 186/188, 1967), the USA (Gemini 8/Agena, 1966), and China (Shenzhou-8/Tiangong-1, 2011). This technology is critical for future human spaceflight, space station construction, and complex missions like lunar sample return.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 5
Why is space docking technology important?

A) Only for satellite communication
B) For building space stations, human spaceflight, sample return missions, and satellite servicing
C) Only for destroying old satellites
D) Only for launching heavier rockets

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Space docking is critical for multiple advanced space activities: building and maintaining space stations (like India's planned BAS), crew transfer between spacecraft (Gaganyaan), sample return missions (Chandrayaan-4 needs docking in lunar orbit), on-orbit satellite servicing and repair, and assembling large spacecraft in orbit for deep-space missions.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
When was the successful docking achieved?

A) 30 December 2024
B) 1 January 2025
C) 16 January 2025
D) 26 January 2025

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Successful docking of SDX01 and SDX02 was achieved on 16 January 2025. After launch on 30 December 2024, the two satellites performed multiple rendezvous and proximity operations over several days before the autonomous docking sequence was executed. The spacecraft then operated as a single rigid body.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 7
Which launch vehicle was used for SpaDeX?

A) GSLV Mk II
B) LVM3
C) PSLV-C60
D) SSLV

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** SpaDeX was launched by PSLV-C60. Both satellites (SDX01 and SDX02) were placed in a 475 km circular orbit. The PSLV's spent fourth stage also carried 24 secondary payloads as POEM-4 (PSLV Orbital Experimental Module), continuing ISRO's innovative reuse of the fourth stage.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What does "autonomous docking" mean in the context of SpaDeX?

A) Docking performed manually by astronauts
B) Docking controlled entirely by onboard software without human intervention
C) Docking guided by ground controllers via remote control
D) Docking using magnetic attraction

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Autonomous docking means the entire docking sequence — approach, alignment, contact, and capture — was controlled by onboard computers and software without any human intervention or ground control commands during the critical phase. The spacecraft's sensors and algorithms guided it autonomously, which is essential for future operations where communication delays make real-time ground control impractical.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9
What additional capability was demonstrated after docking?

A) Launching a third satellite
B) Electrical power transfer between the two docked spacecraft
C) Satellite manufacturing in orbit
D) 3D printing in space

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** After successful docking, SpaDeX demonstrated electrical power transfer between the two docked spacecraft. This is critical for future space station operations — when a supply or crew vehicle docks with a station, power sharing between modules is essential. SpaDeX also demonstrated successful undocking and re-docking.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
How does SpaDeX technology relate to the Chandrayaan-4 mission?

A) It doesn't relate at all
B) Chandrayaan-4's lunar sample return requires docking in lunar orbit to transfer samples to the return vehicle
C) SpaDeX will carry samples to the Moon
D) Chandrayaan-4 will use SpaDeX as a relay satellite

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-4, India's planned lunar sample return mission, requires docking technology in lunar orbit. A lander will collect samples from the Moon's surface, then an ascent module will carry them to lunar orbit where it must dock with a return vehicle that brings the samples back to Earth. This complex sequence absolutely requires the docking capability demonstrated by SpaDeX.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What is the Bharatiya Antariksha Station (BAS)?

A) An ISRO research centre on Earth
B) India's planned space station that will require docking technology
C) A proposed Moon base
D) India's satellite manufacturing facility

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** BAS (Bharatiya Antariksha Station, meaning "Indian Space Station") is India's planned space station. Building and operating a space station requires regular docking of crew vehicles, supply capsules, and modules — exactly the technology SpaDeX demonstrated. BAS is a long-term goal that builds on Gaganyaan and SpaDeX capabilities.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
At what altitude did the SpaDeX satellites operate?

A) 200 km
B) 475 km
C) 36,000 km
D) 1.5 million km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Both SDX01 and SDX02 were placed in a 475 km circular orbit. This is a low Earth orbit altitude, high enough to be stable and above atmospheric drag but low enough for PSLV to deliver both satellites efficiently. The docking operations were conducted at this altitude.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 13
What is the difference between "docking" and "berthing" in space operations?

A) They are the same thing
B) Docking: two spacecraft fly together and connect; Berthing: a robotic arm grabs a spacecraft and connects it
C) Docking is for cargo; berthing is for crew
D) Docking is permanent; berthing is temporary

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Docking occurs when two spacecraft actively fly toward each other and connect using their own propulsion and docking mechanisms. Berthing is when a spacecraft approaches a station, and a robotic arm (like the ISS's Canadarm2) reaches out, grabs it, and attaches it to a port. SpaDeX demonstrated autonomous docking, the more autonomous and technically demanding approach.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Space Operations

### Question 14
What is POEM-4, and how was it part of the SpaDeX mission?

A) A poem written by ISRO scientists about space
B) PSLV Orbital Experimental Module — the reused fourth stage carrying 24 secondary payloads
C) A new satellite launched separately
D) A communication system for the docking experiment

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** POEM-4 (PSLV Orbital Experimental Module 4) used the spent fourth stage of PSLV-C60 as a platform for 24 secondary payloads. After deploying SDX01 and SDX02, the fourth stage was repurposed as an orbiting experimental platform with solar power — continuing ISRO's innovative practice of turning "space junk" into useful orbital laboratories.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 15
Which of the following was NOT demonstrated by SpaDeX?

A) Autonomous docking of two spacecraft
B) Undocking and separation
C) Electrical power transfer between docked spacecraft
D) Human crew transfer between spacecraft

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** SpaDeX was an uncrewed technology demonstration mission. It did not involve human crew transfer. It successfully demonstrated autonomous docking, undocking, re-docking, and electrical power transfer. Human crew transfer will come with future Gaganyaan missions and India's planned space station, building on the foundation SpaDeX established.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 16
What technologies does the "Chaser" spacecraft need for autonomous docking?

A) Only a powerful rocket engine
B) Precise sensors, navigation systems, onboard computers, and a docking mechanism
C) Only a camera and radio
D) Only fuel tanks

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Autonomous docking requires the Chaser to have: precise sensors (to detect and track the Target's position and orientation), navigation and guidance computers (to calculate approach trajectories), attitude control thrusters (for fine positioning), and a docking mechanism (to physically capture and lock onto the Target). All these systems must work together flawlessly.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 17
Which three countries achieved space docking before India?

A) USA, Japan, and France
B) Russia, USA, and China
C) Russia, USA, and ESA
D) USA, China, and Japan

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The three countries that achieved in-space docking before India were: Russia (first automated docking: Kosmos 186/188, 1967), the USA (first crewed docking: Gemini 8/Agena, 1966), and China (Shenzhou-8/Tiangong-1, 2011). India became the fourth with SpaDeX in January 2025.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Space History
