## Topic 35 – Chandrayaan-3 — India Lands on the Moon

### Questions for Topic 35

### Question 1
When did Chandrayaan-3 successfully land on the Moon?

A) 14 July 2023
B) 23 August 2023
C) 7 September 2019
D) 22 October 2008

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander touched down on the Moon on 23 August 2023 at 6:04 PM IST. This date has been declared National Space Day by the Government of India. The mission was launched on 14 July 2023, and took about 40 days to travel to the Moon and descend to the surface.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
India became the ___ country to soft-land on the Moon.

A) Second
B) Third
C) Fourth
D) Fifth

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** With Chandrayaan-3's successful landing, India became the fourth country in the world to achieve a soft landing on the Moon, after the Soviet Union (Luna 9, 1966), the United States (Surveyor 1, 1966), and China (Chang'e 3, 2013). India is also the first country to soft-land near the lunar south pole.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
What is the special significance of Chandrayaan-3's landing location?

A) It landed on the far side of the Moon
B) It was the first soft landing near the lunar south pole
C) It landed at the exact same spot as Apollo 11
D) It landed in the deepest crater on the Moon

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-3 achieved the first-ever soft landing near the Moon's south pole (at 69.37°S, 32.35°E). The south polar region is scientifically valuable because permanently shadowed craters there may contain water ice. No previous mission had successfully soft-landed in this challenging region, making India's achievement particularly significant.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 4
What launch vehicle was used for Chandrayaan-3?

A) PSLV-XL
B) GSLV Mk II
C) LVM3-M4

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Chandrayaan-3 was launched by LVM3-M4, India's heaviest operational rocket. LVM3 was necessary because of the mission's significant mass. This was the same type of launch vehicle used for Chandrayaan-2, demonstrating LVM3's proven capability for lunar missions.

### Question 7
How was Chandrayaan-3 different from Chandrayaan-2 in terms of mission components?

A) Chandrayaan-3 had a larger orbiter
B) Chandrayaan-3 used a Propulsion Module instead of a new orbiter, relying on Chandrayaan-2's orbiter for communication
C) Chandrayaan-3 had no rover
D) Chandrayaan-3 was identical to Chandrayaan-2

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Unlike Chandrayaan-2 (which had its own orbiter, lander, and rover), Chandrayaan-3 used a Propulsion Module to carry the lander to lunar orbit and then the still-operational Chandrayaan-2 orbiter for communication relay. This simplified the mission, reduced cost, and leveraged an existing asset already in lunar orbit.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
What was the "failure-based design" approach used in Chandrayaan-3?

A) Designing the mission to fail safely
B) Engineering the spacecraft to succeed even if individual components fail
C) Accepting that the mission would likely fail
D) Building multiple spacecraft as backups

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** "Failure-based design" means the engineers designed the spacecraft to achieve its objectives even if some components failed. This included redundant systems, wider margins, expanded landing zones (4 km × 2.5 km instead of 500 m × 500 m), extra fuel reserves, improved software algorithms, and stronger landing legs. The philosophy: assume things will go wrong, and ensure the mission still succeeds.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 9
What element did the Pragyan rover's LIBS instrument detect for the first time at the lunar south pole?

A) Gold
B) Helium-3
C) Sulphur
D) Uranium

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Pragyan's LIBS (Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscope) instrument made the first in-situ confirmation of sulphur at the lunar south pole. LIBS also detected aluminium, calcium, iron, chromium, titanium, manganese, silicon, and oxygen. The sulphur detection was particularly significant because it was the first direct measurement at this location.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
What did the ChaSTE instrument on the Vikram lander measure?

A) Chemical composition of lunar rocks
B) Lunar surface temperature profile at different depths
C) Seismic vibrations
D) Solar wind particles

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ChaSTE (Chandra's Surface Thermophysical Experiment) measured the temperature profile of the lunar surface and subsurface. It found that temperatures varied dramatically — about +50°C at the surface dropping to approximately –10°C at just 80 mm depth. This was the first such measurement at the lunar south pole and provides valuable data about the thermal properties of lunar regolith.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 11
What did ILSA, the seismometer on Vikram, accomplish?

A) It measured the Moon's magnetic field
B) It made the first seismic measurements at the lunar south pole, detecting moonquakes and rover movement
C) It measured atmospheric pressure
D) It detected underground water

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ILSA (Instrument for Lunar Seismic Activity) recorded the first-ever seismic measurements at the Moon's south pole. It detected natural moonquakes as well as vibrations from the Pragyan rover's movement across the surface. This seismic data helps scientists understand the Moon's interior structure and geological activity in the south polar region.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 12
What was the SHAPE experiment on the Propulsion Module?

A) An instrument to study the shape of the Moon
B) An experiment studying Earth's spectral signatures from lunar orbit to help identify habitable exoplanets
C) A camera to photograph the landing
D) A thruster test for future missions

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SHAPE (Spectro-polarimetry of HAbitable Planet Earth) observed Earth from lunar distance, studying our planet's spectral and polarimetric signatures. The goal was to develop techniques for identifying habitable exoplanets — if we can characterise what a habitable planet (Earth) looks like from afar, we can look for similar signatures around other stars.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 13
How far did the Pragyan rover travel on the lunar surface?

A) About 10 metres
B) About 100 metres
C) About 1 kilometre
D) About 5 kilometres

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pragyan traversed approximately 100 metres on the lunar surface during its roughly two-week operational period. Along the way, it analysed the composition of lunar soil at multiple points using its APXS and LIBS instruments. The rover moved slowly and deliberately to navigate safely in the challenging terrain near the south pole.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 14
What happened to Vikram and Pragyan when lunar night began?

A) They continued operating normally
B) They were put into sleep mode and did not reawaken after lunar night
C) They were deliberately destroyed
D) They were remotely moved to a sunlit area

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When the 14-Earth-day lunar night began in early September 2023, Vikram and Pragyan were put into sleep mode because they relied on solar power and could not operate in darkness. The extreme cold during lunar night (approximately –200°C) exceeded the survival limits of their electronics. ISRO hoped they might reawaken when sunlight returned, but they did not respond.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 15
What was the "hop" experiment performed by Vikram?

A) Vikram hopped between different craters
B) Vikram briefly fired its engines, hopping about 40 cm vertically before settling nearby
C) Pragyan hopped over rocks
D) Vikram launched a secondary probe

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** On 3 September 2023, just before entering sleep mode, ISRO conducted a "kick-start" experiment where Vikram's engines were briefly fired. The lander hopped approximately 40 cm vertically and landed about 30-40 cm away from its original position. This demonstrated engine re-ignition capability on the lunar surface — technology valuable for future sample return missions.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 16
What improvements did ISRO make to Chandrayaan-3's landing system compared to Chandrayaan-2?

A) No changes were made
B) Expanded landing zone, extra fuel, improved guidance software, hazard detection, and stronger legs
C) Only the landing legs were changed
D) A parachute system was added

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISRO made multiple critical improvements: the acceptable landing zone was expanded from 500m × 500m to 4 km × 2.5 km; extra fuel was added for contingencies; guidance and navigation software algorithms were improved; hazard detection and avoidance capability was added; landing legs were strengthened for higher impact velocities; and a laser Doppler velocimeter was added for precise velocity measurement.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 20
How long does a lunar night last?

A) About 12 hours
B) About 1 Earth day
C) About 14 Earth days
D) About 1 Earth month

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A lunar night lasts approximately 14 Earth days because the Moon takes about 27.3 Earth days to complete one rotation. During the 14-day night, surface temperatures plummet to approximately –200°C in the south polar region. Vikram and Pragyan, designed for solar-powered operation during the 14-day lunar day, could not survive this prolonged, extreme cold.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Lunar Science

### Question 21
What two instruments did the Pragyan rover carry?

A) RAMBHA and ChaSTE
B) APXS and LIBS
C) ILSA and LRA
D) SHAPE and DFSAR

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Pragyan carried APXS (Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer) for determining elemental composition by bombarding soil with alpha particles, and LIBS (Laser Induced Breakdown Spectroscope) which fires a laser at the surface and analyses the emitted light spectrum. These are the same instruments that were on Chandrayaan-2's Pragyan, and they successfully detected sulphur and multiple other elements.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 22
Which countries had previously soft-landed on the Moon before India?

A) USA and Russia only
B) USSR, USA, and China
C) USSR, USA, China, and Japan

**Answer:** B

### Question 23
Russia's Luna-25 mission attempted to reach the Moon's south pole around the same time as Chandrayaan-3. What happened?

A) Luna-25 landed successfully before Chandrayaan-3
B) Luna-25 crashed on the Moon on 19 August 2023, four days before Chandrayaan-3's successful landing
C) Luna-25 was never launched
D) Luna-25 entered lunar orbit but did not attempt landing

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Russia's Luna-25 launched on 11 August 2023 (after Chandrayaan-3's 14 July launch) and attempted to reach the south pole first. However, Luna-25 crashed into the Moon on 19 August 2023 due to a thruster firing error. Four days later, on 23 August 2023, India's Chandrayaan-3 successfully achieved what Luna-25 could not — a soft landing near the south pole.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Roscosmos / ISRO

### Question 24
What is the NASA Laser Retroreflector Array (LRA) on Vikram, and how long can it function?

A) An active radar system that will last 1 year
B) A passive reflector that can bounce laser beams back to Earth and work for decades
C) A communication antenna that transmits data
D) A solar panel for emergency power

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The LRA is a passive instrument — a set of corner-cube reflectors that bounce laser beams back to their source with no need for power. Scientists on Earth can fire lasers at the reflector to precisely measure the Moon's distance. Because it has no electronic components, it can function for decades. Similar reflectors placed by Apollo astronauts are still used today, over 50 years later.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA / ISRO

### Question 25
What was the landing zone expansion in Chandrayaan-3 compared to Chandrayaan-2?

A) From 100 m × 100 m to 200 m × 200 m
B) From 500 m × 500 m to 4 km × 2.5 km
C) From 1 km × 1 km to 2 km × 2 km
D) No change was made

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISRO expanded the acceptable landing zone from 500 m × 500 m in Chandrayaan-2 to 4 km × 2.5 km in Chandrayaan-3. This much larger zone gave the guidance system more room to find a safe landing spot, even if the spacecraft drifted during descent. Combined with improved hazard detection, this was one of the key design changes that ensured success.
