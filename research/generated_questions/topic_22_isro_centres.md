## Topic 22 – ISRO — Organisation, Centres and Infrastructure

### Questions for Topic 22

### Question 1
Where is the headquarters of ISRO located?

A) Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala
B) Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh
C) Bengaluru, Karnataka
D) Ahmedabad, Gujarat

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** ISRO's headquarters is located at Antariksh Bhavan (meaning "Space Building") on New BEL Road, Bengaluru, Karnataka. This is sometimes confused with Sriharikota, which is the launch centre, or Thiruvananthapuram, which houses the rocket design centre (VSSC).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 2
What does the name "Antariksh Bhavan" mean?

A) Star Building
B) Space Building
C) Rocket House
D) Mission Centre

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Antariksh Bhavan translates to "Space Building" in English. "Antariksh" means "space" or "sky" in Hindi/Sanskrit. It is the official headquarters of ISRO, the Department of Space (DOS), and the Indian Space Commission, all located in Bengaluru.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 3
ISRO operates under which government body?

A) Ministry of Science and Technology
B) Ministry of Defence
C) Department of Space (DOS), which reports directly to the Prime Minister
D) Ministry of Education

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** ISRO is the primary R&D organisation under the Department of Space (DOS). Uniquely, DOS reports directly to the Prime Minister rather than being under any ministry. The ISRO Chairman also serves simultaneously as the Secretary of DOS and Chairman of the Space Commission.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Government of India

### Question 4
The ISRO Chairman holds which additional positions simultaneously?

A) Secretary of DOS and Chairman of the Space Commission
B) Defence Secretary and Chief of Defence Staff
C) Director of VSSC and Director of URSC
D) President of PRL and Director of SAC

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The ISRO Chairman holds three key positions simultaneously: Chairman of ISRO, Secretary of the Department of Space (DOS), and Chairman of the Space Commission. This combined role ensures unified leadership across India's space policy, administration, and research.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / Government of India

### Question 5
Which ISRO centre is responsible for designing and developing rockets (launch vehicles)?

A) URSC, Bengaluru
B) VSSC, Thiruvananthapuram
C) SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota
D) SAC, Ahmedabad

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC) in Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum), Kerala, is responsible for the design and development of all Indian launch vehicles. All major rockets — SLV, ASLV, PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, and SSLV — were developed here. VSSC is named after Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, the founder of the Indian space programme.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 6
Which ISRO centre designs and builds all Indian satellites?

A) VSSC, Thiruvananthapuram
B) SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota
C) URSC, Bengaluru
D) LPSC, Valiamala

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC) in Bengaluru is where all Indian satellites are designed, developed, and fabricated — including INSAT, IRS, GSAT, Chandrayaan, Mangalyaan, AstroSat, and Aditya-L1. It is named after Prof. U R Rao, who built India's satellite programme. URSC is also the centre organising World Space Week 2026.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / URSC

### Question 7
What was the earlier name of URSC before it was renamed in 2018?

A) VSSC
B) ISAC (ISRO Satellite Centre)
C) SAC
D) NRSC

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** URSC was formerly called ISAC — ISRO Satellite Centre. It was renamed U R Rao Satellite Centre in 2018 in honour of Prof. Udupi Ramachandra Rao, who was ISRO Chairman from 1984 to 1994 and was the project director of India's first satellite, Aryabhata (1975).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 8
From which location do all Indian orbital launches take place?

A) Thumba, Thiruvananthapuram
B) SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota
C) Bengaluru
D) Ahmedabad

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** All Indian orbital launches take place from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC SHAR) on Sriharikota island, Andhra Pradesh. Located at approximately 13.7°N latitude between Pulicat Lake and the Bay of Bengal, it provides a good launch trajectory over the sea (no populated areas underneath) and is relatively close to the equator for an eastward launch advantage.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 9
After whom is the Satish Dhawan Space Centre named?

A) Vikram Sarabhai — founder of ISRO
B) Satish Dhawan — ISRO's 2nd Chairman
C) U R Rao — satellite programme builder
D) APJ Abdul Kalam — SLV-3 project director

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Satish Dhawan Space Centre was renamed in 2002 after Prof. Satish Dhawan, ISRO's 2nd Chairman (1972–1984). Dhawan is famous for his leadership style — he personally took the blame when SLV-3 E1 failed in 1979 but pushed APJ Abdul Kalam to face the press when SLV-3 succeeded in 1980. He is also called the "Father of Experimental Fluid Dynamics in India."

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO

### Question 10
How many operational launch pads does SDSC SHAR currently have?

A) One
B) Two, plus a third for SSLV
C) Five
D) Ten

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SDSC SHAR has two main operational launch pads — the First Launch Pad (FLP) and the Second Launch Pad (SLP) — used for PSLV, GSLV, and LVM3 missions. A Third Launch Pad has been built specifically for the SSLV (Small Satellite Launch Vehicle). The centre also has vehicle assembly buildings and propellant processing facilities.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 11
Why was Sriharikota chosen as India's spaceport?

A) It is at the highest elevation in India
B) It is close to the equator, on the east coast over the Bay of Bengal for safe launches
C) It is the closest point to the Moon
D) It is surrounded by mountains for wind protection

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Sriharikota was chosen for several strategic reasons: it is at approximately 13.7°N latitude (relatively close to the equator, giving an eastward velocity boost), it is on the east coast (rockets launch eastward over the Bay of Bengal where no populated areas are at risk), and it is an island location with good infrastructure access.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 12
Which ISRO centre develops liquid and cryogenic rocket engines?

A) VSSC
B) LPSC
C) SAC
D) NRSC

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC), with facilities in Valiamala (near Thiruvananthapuram) and Bengaluru, is responsible for developing all liquid and cryogenic rocket engines. LPSC developed the Vikas engine, the indigenous cryogenic engines CE-7.5 and CE-20, and is developing future semi-cryogenic engines. It played a critical role in India's cryogenic engine success story.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 13
What is the role of SAC (Space Applications Centre)?

A) Launching rockets
B) Building satellites
C) Developing payloads (satellite instruments) and space applications
D) Training astronauts

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Space Applications Centre (SAC) in Ahmedabad, Gujarat, develops payloads — the instruments and sensors carried by satellites. This includes communication transponders, cameras, sensors, and SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) instruments. SAC also develops ground-based applications like telemedicine, tele-education, and disaster management systems.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SAC

### Question 14
Which centre provides mission control and operates the Indian Deep Space Network (IDSN)?

A) NRSC, Hyderabad
B) ISTRAC, Bengaluru
C) VSSC, Thiruvananthapuram
D) URSC, Bengaluru

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** ISTRAC (ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network), headquartered in Bengaluru, provides mission control and tracking support for all ISRO missions. It operates the Indian Deep Space Network (IDSN) with a 32-metre antenna at Byalalu, near Bengaluru. The IDSN tracked Chandrayaan-1, MOM, Chandrayaan-2/3, and Aditya-L1.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / ISTRAC

### Question 15
The Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) in Ahmedabad is known as the "cradle of space science in India." Who founded it?

A) APJ Abdul Kalam in 1980
B) Satish Dhawan in 1972
C) Vikram Sarabhai in 1947
D) U R Rao in 1962

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** PRL was founded by Dr. Vikram Sarabhai in 1947 — when he was just 28 years old — starting with just two rooms at M.G. Science Institute in Ahmedabad. PRL became India's premier research laboratory for physics, astronomy, and space science and is called the "cradle of space science in India." It is supported by the Department of Space.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** PRL / ISRO

### Question 16
What is IN-SPACe and when was it established?

A) A rocket testing facility, established in 2010
B) An agency to promote and authorise private sector participation in space, established in 2020
C) A satellite manufacturing plant, established in 2015
D) A deep space tracking station, established in 2005

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** IN-SPACe (Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre) was established in 2020 in Ahmedabad. It acts as a single-window agency to promote and authorise private sector participation in Indian space activities, allowing private companies to use ISRO facilities and launch services. This represents India's effort to open its space sector.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / IN-SPACe

### Question 17
What is NSIL (New Space India Limited)?

A) A new ISRO research laboratory
B) ISRO's commercial arm, handling commercial launches and technology transfer
C) A private space company competing with ISRO
D) A satellite navigation system

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NSIL (New Space India Limited) is ISRO's commercial arm, established in 2019 and based in Bengaluru. It handles commercial launches, satellite-based services, and technology transfer to Indian industry. NSIL replaced some functions of Antrix Corporation, which had served as ISRO's commercial arm since 1992.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / NSIL

### Question 18
Which ISRO centre is located in Hyderabad and handles remote sensing data?

A) ISTRAC
B) LPSC
C) NRSC
D) IIRS

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC) is located in Hyderabad, Telangana. It is responsible for acquiring, processing, and distributing remote sensing data from India's Earth observation satellites. NRSC provides data used for agriculture, water resources, forestry, urban planning, and disaster management across India.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / NRSC

### Question 19
The Indian Institute of Remote Sensing (IIRS), which provides training in remote sensing and GIS, is located in:

A) Bengaluru
B) Ahmedabad

**Explanation:** IIRS is located in Dehradun, Uttarakhand. It is a premier institution providing training and education in remote sensing, Geographic Information Systems (GIS), and space technology. It offers various courses and degree programmes to develop skilled professionals in the use of space-based Earth observation data.

### Question 21
Which of the following correctly matches ISRO centres with their primary function?

A) VSSC — satellites; URSC — rockets; SDSC — engines
B) VSSC — rockets; URSC — satellites; SDSC — launches
C) VSSC — launches; URSC — engines; SDSC — satellites
D) VSSC — engines; URSC — launches; SDSC — rockets

This division of responsibilities allows each centre to specialise.

### Question 22
The Indian Deep Space Network (IDSN) antenna at Byalalu tracked which of these missions?

A) Only Chandrayaan-1
B) Only Mangalyaan (MOM)
C) Chandrayaan-1, MOM, Chandrayaan-2/3, and Aditya-L1
D) Only international missions for NASA and ESA

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The IDSN 32-metre antenna at Byalalu, near Bengaluru, has been vital for tracking all of India's deep space missions: Chandrayaan-1, Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan), Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, and Aditya-L1. IDSN is operated by ISTRAC and also participates in international deep space tracking networks.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 23
What distinguishes Antrix Corporation from NSIL?

A) They are the same organisation with different names
B) Antrix (1992) was the earlier commercial arm; NSIL (2019) is the newer one that replaced some of its functions
C) Antrix makes rockets; NSIL makes satellites

**Explanation:** Antrix Corporation was established in 1992 as ISRO's first commercial arm, handling marketing and international cooperation. NSIL (New Space India Limited) was established in 2019 as a newer commercial entity that took over some of Antrix's functions, including commercial launches and technology transfer. Both are based in Bengaluru.

### Question 25
A key advantage of SDSC SHAR's east coast location is:

A) It receives more sunlight for solar-powered launches
B) Rockets launched eastward fly over the Bay of Bengal, avoiding populated areas
C) The east coast has stronger winds to help rockets gain speed
D) It is closer to other country's launch sites for cooperation

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Rockets are typically launched eastward (to take advantage of Earth's rotation). Sriharikota's east coast location means eastward launches fly over the Bay of Bengal — open sea with no populated areas underneath, ensuring safety. If a rocket were to malfunction, debris would fall into the ocean rather than on land. This is a critical safety factor in choosing launch sites.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / SDSC SHAR

### Question 26
Which ISRO centre developed the CE-7.5 and CE-20 cryogenic engines?

A) VSSC
B) URSC
C) LPSC
D) SAC

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (LPSC) developed India's indigenous cryogenic engines — the CE-7.5 (used in GSLV Mk II upper stage) and the more powerful CE-20 (used in LVM3 upper stage). LPSC's work was critical in overcoming the technology denial India faced when Russia was pressured to cancel cryogenic technology transfer in the 1990s.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / LPSC

### Question 27
The Indian Space Commission, which makes policy for India's space programme, is chaired by:

A) The President of India
B) The ISRO Chairman
C) The Prime Minister

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Indian Space Commission is the policy-making body for India's space programme and is chaired by the Prime Minister. This direct linkage to the PM's office underscores the importance India places on its space programme. The Department of Space also reports directly to the PM, rather than through any ministry.

**Difficulty:** Medium
