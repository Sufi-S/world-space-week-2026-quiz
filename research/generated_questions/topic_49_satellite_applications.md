## Topic 49 – Satellite Applications and Space Technology

### Questions for Topic 49

### Question 1
How does GPS (Global Positioning System) determine your location?

A) By tracking your phone's camera
B) By receiving signals from at least 4 satellites and calculating distances based on signal travel time
C) By measuring the magnetic field of Earth
D) By using a single satellite to track you directly

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GPS works by receiving signals from at least 4 satellites simultaneously. Each satellite transmits its position and the precise time of transmission. Your GPS receiver measures how long each signal took to arrive, calculates the distance to each satellite, and uses triangulation (technically "trilateration") to pinpoint your position. Four satellites are needed to determine latitude, longitude, altitude, and correct for clock errors.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** GPS / Navigation

### Question 2
What is remote sensing?

A) Controlling a satellite remotely
B) Observing Earth from space using sensors without physical contact — including cameras, infrared, and radar
C) Sensing the weather from the ground
D) A type of satellite communication

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Remote sensing is the science of observing Earth (or other objects) from a distance using sensors on satellites or aircraft. Types include optical (visible light photos), infrared (detects heat — useful for fires and vegetation health), and radar/SAR (works through clouds and at night). India's IRS series is one of the world's largest civilian remote sensing constellations.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** ISRO / Remote Sensing

### Question 4
How have satellites helped reduce cyclone deaths in India?

A) They haven't made a significant difference
B) Satellite-based tracking and early warning systems have reduced cyclone deaths from thousands to dozens
C) Satellites destroy cyclones before they hit land
D) Satellites only help track cyclones after they've hit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Satellite-based cyclone tracking and early warning systems have dramatically reduced cyclone deaths in India. In past decades, major cyclones killed thousands (e.g., the 1999 Odisha super cyclone killed over 10,000). Today, with satellite tracking, accurate prediction, and timely evacuations, major cyclones like Fani (2019) and Biparjoy (2023) caused far fewer deaths — from thousands down to dozens.

### Question 6
What are the five major satellite navigation systems in the world?

A) Only GPS exists
B) GPS (USA), GLONASS (Russia), Galileo (EU), BeiDou (China), and NavIC (India)
C) GPS, WiFi, Bluetooth, 5G, and 4G
D) Only GPS and GLONASS

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** There are five major satellite navigation systems: GPS (USA, global, operational since 1995), GLONASS (Russia, global), Galileo (EU/ESA, global, designed for higher civilian precision), BeiDou (China, global, completed 2020), and NavIC/IRNSS (India, regional — covers India and about 1,500 km around it). The umbrella term for all of them is GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Navigation Systems

### Question 7
How is NavIC different from GPS?

A) NavIC is more accurate than GPS everywhere
B) NavIC is India's regional system covering India and ~1,500 km around it, while GPS is global
C) NavIC and GPS are the same system
D) NavIC only works on military devices

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation, also called IRNSS) is India's own regional navigation system. It covers India and extends about 1,500 km beyond India's borders. Unlike GPS, which is global (works everywhere on Earth), NavIC provides service only in this regional area. NavIC uses 7 satellites and offers both restricted (military) and standard (civilian) positioning services.

**Difficulty:** Easy

### Question 8
What everyday technology originated from NASA's space research?

A) Television
B) Memory foam — developed for spacecraft seat cushions to absorb impact
C) The telephone
D) The printing press

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Memory foam was originally developed by NASA in the 1960s to improve crash protection for aircraft seats. It was later used in spacecraft applications. Today, it is widely used in mattresses, pillows, shoe insoles, and medical equipment. Other space spinoffs include scratch-resistant lens coatings, CMOS image sensors (used in smartphone cameras), cordless power tools, and water purification systems.

### Question 9
What is SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar)?

A) A type of optical camera
B) A radar imaging technology that works through clouds and at night, creating detailed surface images
C) A type of solar panel
D) A communication protocol

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** SAR (Synthetic Aperture Radar) uses radar signals (microwaves) to create detailed images of Earth's surface. Unlike optical cameras, SAR works regardless of clouds, rain, or darkness, making it invaluable for monitoring areas with persistent cloud cover. India's RISAT satellites use SAR technology, and the NASA-ISRO NISAR mission will use both L-band and S-band SAR for comprehensive Earth observation.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** ISRO / NASA

### Question 15
What is the difference between geostationary and polar-orbiting weather satellites?

A) They are the same type
B) Geostationary satellites stay over one point providing continuous regional coverage; polar satellites orbit pole-to-pole giving global coverage twice daily
C) Geostationary satellites move faster
D) Polar satellites only photograph the poles

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Geostationary weather satellites orbit at about 36,000 km and remain fixed above one point on the equator, providing continuous coverage of one region — ideal for monitoring rapidly changing weather like cyclones. Polar-orbiting satellites orbit much lower (about 850 km), passing over the poles and covering the entire globe twice daily, providing detailed global data. India uses both types: INSAT series (geostationary) and Oceansat/Megha-Tropiques (polar).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** IMD / ISRO

### Question 16
How do smartphone cameras relate to space technology?

A) They don't
B) CMOS image sensors used in smartphone cameras were developed from technology originally created for space applications
C) Smartphones use satellite cameras directly
D) NASA designed the iPhone

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** CMOS (Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor) image sensors, which are used in virtually every smartphone camera today, were originally developed for space applications. NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory developed CMOS sensors in the 1990s as smaller, lower-power alternatives to CCD sensors for spacecraft cameras. This technology was then adapted for consumer electronics, revolutionising digital photography.

**Source:** NASA Spinoffs / JPL

A) A type of satellite engine

**Answer:** B
