## Topic 7 – Mars — The Red Planet

### Questions for Topic 7

### Question 1
Which position does Mars hold from the Sun?

A) 3rd planet
B) 4th planet
C) 5th planet
D) 6th planet

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars is the 4th planet from the Sun, sitting between Earth (3rd) and Jupiter (5th). It orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 228 million km. Mars is the outermost of the terrestrial (rocky) planets.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 2
Why is Mars called the "Red Planet"?

A) Because it is very hot and glowing
B) Because of iron oxide (rust) on its surface
C) Because its atmosphere filters sunlight to appear red
D) Because it reflects red light from the Sun

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars appears red because its surface is rich in iron oxide — essentially rust. Fine particles of this reddish dust cover the surface and are carried into the thin atmosphere by winds, giving the entire planet a distinctive reddish-orange hue visible even from Earth with the naked eye.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 3
What is the approximate diameter of Mars?

A) About 3,400 km
B) About 6,779 km
C) About 12,742 km
D) About 49,500 km

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars has a diameter of approximately 6,779 km, which is about half of Earth's diameter (12,742 km). Despite being the second-smallest planet after Mercury, Mars has a surface area roughly equal to the total land area of Earth (since Mars has no oceans).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 4
How long is a day on Mars?

A) About 10 hours
B) About 24 hours 37 minutes
C) About 48 hours
D) About 687 hours

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A Martian day, called a "sol," lasts approximately 24 hours and 37 minutes — remarkably similar to an Earth day. This makes Mars the planet with the day length most similar to Earth's. A Martian sol is only about 2.7% longer than an Earth day, which would make it relatively easy for future human settlers to adapt to.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 5
How long is one year on Mars?

A) About 225 Earth days
B) About 365 Earth days
C) About 687 Earth days
D) About 1,000 Earth days

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** One Martian year is about 687 Earth days (roughly 1.88 Earth years). This longer year is because Mars is farther from the Sun than Earth, so it has a longer orbital path and moves more slowly in its orbit. Mars also has seasons like Earth because its axis is tilted at about 25° (similar to Earth's 23.5°).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 6
What is the main component of Mars's atmosphere?

A) Nitrogen (N₂)
B) Oxygen (O₂)
C) Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
D) Hydrogen (H₂)

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mars's atmosphere is about 95.3% carbon dioxide (CO₂), with small amounts of nitrogen (2.7%) and argon (1.6%). The atmosphere is extremely thin — surface pressure is only about 1% of Earth's atmospheric pressure. This thin CO₂ atmosphere cannot sustain liquid water on the surface or support human breathing.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 7
How many moons does Mars have?

A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 4

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons: Phobos (the larger one, about 22 km across) and Deimos (the smaller one, about 12 km across). Both are thought to be captured asteroids because of their irregular shapes and composition. Their names come from Greek mythology — Phobos (fear) and Deimos (terror) were the sons of Ares (Mars).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 8
What is Olympus Mons?

A) The deepest ocean on Mars
B) The tallest volcano and mountain in the solar system
C) A large crater on Mars
D) A dust storm on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Olympus Mons is the tallest volcano and mountain in the entire solar system, standing approximately 21.9 km high with a base diameter of about 600 km. It is a shield volcano about 2.5 times taller than Mount Everest. Its enormous size is partly because Mars lacks plate tectonics, so the volcano could keep building up over a single hot spot for hundreds of millions of years.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 9
What is Valles Marineris?

A) A volcano chain
B) A system of dried-up lakes
C) The largest canyon system in the solar system
D) A polar ice cap

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Valles Marineris is the largest canyon system in the solar system, stretching about 4,000 km long and reaching up to 7 km deep. It would stretch across the entire continental United States. It is much larger than Earth's Grand Canyon. It was likely formed by tectonic processes and possibly widened by erosion from ancient water.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 10
What are Mars's polar ice caps made of?

A) Only frozen carbon dioxide (dry ice)
B) Only water ice
C) Both water ice and frozen carbon dioxide (dry ice)
D) Liquid nitrogen

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Mars's polar ice caps are composed of both water ice and frozen carbon dioxide (dry ice). The permanent caps contain water ice, while seasonal CO₂ frost accumulates on top during winter. The caps grow and shrink with the Martian seasons — in winter, CO₂ from the atmosphere freezes onto the caps, and in summer it sublimates back into gas.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 11
What evidence suggests that liquid water once existed on Mars?

A) Liquid water can currently be seen on the surface
B) River channels, lake beds, and water-related mineral deposits have been found
C) Mars has a thick atmosphere that could support liquid water
D) Mars's moons contain liquid water

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Multiple lines of evidence indicate that Mars once had liquid water: dried-up river channels and valley networks, ancient lake beds (like Jezero Crater), and mineral deposits (such as clays and sulfates) that only form in the presence of water. Mars was likely warmer and wetter billions of years ago with a thicker atmosphere, but lost most of its atmosphere over time.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Mars Exploration

### Question 12
What is the surface gravity on Mars compared to Earth?

A) About the same as Earth
B) About 38% of Earth's gravity
C) About 10% of Earth's gravity
D) About 80% of Earth's gravity

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars's surface gravity is about 38% of Earth's. This means a person weighing 60 kg on Earth would weigh only about 22.7 kg on Mars. The lower gravity is due to Mars's smaller mass (about 11% of Earth's). This reduced gravity is an important consideration for future human missions, affecting everything from walking to the human body's health.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 13
What is the average surface temperature on Mars?

A) About 15°C
B) About -60°C
C) About -200°C
D) About 100°C

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The average surface temperature on Mars is about -60°C, with a range from approximately -140°C at the poles in winter to about 20°C near the equator at midday in summer. The thin atmosphere provides very little insulation, so temperatures drop dramatically at night and between seasons.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 14
Which of Mars's two moons is larger?

A) Deimos
B) Phobos
C) They are the same size
D) Mars has no moons

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Phobos is the larger of Mars's two moons at about 22 km across, compared to Deimos at about 12 km. Phobos orbits very close to Mars (about 6,000 km above the surface) and is gradually spiraling inward — in about 50 million years, it will either crash into Mars or break apart into a ring system. Deimos orbits much farther out.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 15
What is unique about Mars's dust storms?

A) They only occur at the poles
B) They can grow large enough to engulf the entire planet
C) They produce lightning that can be seen from Earth
D) They only last a few minutes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars experiences dust storms that can grow to enormous sizes, sometimes engulfing the entire planet in a global dust storm. These events can last for weeks or months and significantly reduce sunlight reaching the surface. A planet-engulfing dust storm in 2018 ended the Opportunity rover's mission by blocking sunlight from its solar panels.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Mars Exploration

### Question 16
Which were the first spacecraft to successfully land on Mars?

A) Spirit and Opportunity
B) Curiosity and Perseverance
C) Viking 1 and Viking 2
D) Pathfinder and Sojourner

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's Viking 1 and Viking 2 achieved the first successful Mars landings in 1976. Viking 1 landed on July 20, 1976, and Viking 2 on September 3, 1976. Both landers included experiments to search for signs of life in the Martian soil. They also included orbiters that mapped the planet's surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA / Space Exploration History

### Question 17
What was the first rover to operate on Mars?

A) Spirit
B) Curiosity
C) Sojourner
D) Perseverance

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Sojourner was the first rover to operate on Mars, arriving as part of NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission in 1997. It was a small rover, only about 65 cm tall, and operated for about 83 sols (Martian days). Sojourner demonstrated that a mobile robot could explore the Martian surface, paving the way for larger, more capable rovers like Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 18
NASA's Opportunity rover was designed for a 90-day mission. How long did it actually operate?

A) About 1 year
B) About 6 years
C) About 14 years
D) About 20 years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Opportunity operated for over 14 years (2004–2018), vastly exceeding its planned 90-day mission. It travelled over 45 km on Mars — the farthest any off-Earth wheeled vehicle has travelled. Its mission finally ended in June 2018 when a massive planet-wide dust storm blocked sunlight from its solar panels and it lost power.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 19
What is NASA's Curiosity rover primarily studying?

A) Mars's moons
B) The Gale Crater area, looking for organic molecules and past habitable conditions
C) The polar ice caps
D) Mars's magnetic field

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Curiosity, a car-sized rover, has been exploring Gale Crater since August 2012. Its primary mission is to study Mars's climate and geology, investigate whether the Gale Crater area ever had conditions favourable for microbial life, and assess the planet's habitability. Curiosity has found organic molecules and evidence that Gale Crater once held a lake.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 20
What is special about NASA's Ingenuity helicopter?

A) It discovered water on Mars
B) It was the first powered aircraft to fly on another planet
C) It landed on Mars's moon Phobos
D) It returned samples to Earth

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Ingenuity made history as the first powered, controlled flight on another planet. It arrived on Mars with the Perseverance rover in February 2021 and completed 72 flights before its mission ended in January 2024 due to rotor blade damage. Flying on Mars is extremely challenging because the atmosphere is only about 1% as dense as Earth's, requiring extremely fast rotor speeds.

### Question 21
Where is NASA's Perseverance rover exploring, and what is it collecting?

A) Olympus Mons; rock samples for on-site analysis only
B) Jezero Crater; rock and soil samples for potential return to Earth
C) Valles Marineris; atmospheric samples
D) The Martian south pole; ice samples

Jezero was chosen because it was once an ancient lake with a river delta — an ideal place to search for signs of ancient microbial life (biosignatures). ### Question 22
What was India's Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM/Mangalyaan)?

A) India's first lunar mission
B) India's first interplanetary mission, an orbiter sent to Mars
C) India's human spaceflight programme
D) A joint India-NASA rover mission

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM), also known as Mangalyaan ("Mars vehicle" in Hindi), was India's first interplanetary mission. Launched by ISRO on November 5, 2013, using the PSLV-C25 rocket, it successfully entered Mars orbit on September 24, 2014. India became the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit and the first space agency to succeed on its first attempt.

### Question 27
What type of volcano is Olympus Mons?

A) Stratovolcano (like Mount Fuji)
B) Shield volcano (like those in Hawaii)
C) Cinder cone volcano
D) Caldera volcano

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Olympus Mons is a shield volcano, similar in shape to Hawaiian volcanoes like Mauna Loa but enormously larger. Shield volcanoes have gently sloping sides built up by many layers of fluid lava flows. Olympus Mons is about 600 km across at its base and 21.9 km high. It grew so large partly because Mars lacks plate tectonics — the crust stayed over the hot spot.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Geology

### Question 28
Why can't liquid water exist on the surface of Mars today?

A) Mars is too close to the Sun
B) Mars's atmospheric pressure is too low — water would boil or freeze
C) Mars has too much oxygen which prevents water from forming
D) Mars's gravity is too strong

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Mars's atmospheric pressure is only about 1% of Earth's — well below the triple point of water (the minimum pressure needed for liquid water to exist). At such low pressures, water either freezes or sublimates directly to vapor without passing through a liquid phase. Billions of years ago, when Mars had a thicker atmosphere, liquid water could exist on the surface.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Atmospheric Physics

### Question 29
What did NASA's Phoenix lander confirm in 2008?

A) The presence of liquid water on Mars's surface
B) The existence of water ice in Mars's soil
C) That Mars once had plate tectonics
D) The existence of microbial life

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's Phoenix lander, which touched down near Mars's north pole in May 2008, directly confirmed the presence of water ice in the Martian soil. It observed white material exposed by its landing thrusters and scoop that sublimated (turned from solid directly to vapor) over several days — proof that it was ice, not salt or another solid.

### Question 32
NASA's InSight lander (2018–2022) was unique because it studied:

A) Mars's atmosphere and weather patterns
B) Mars's interior, including detecting "marsquakes"
C) Mars's moons Phobos and Deimos
D) The possibility of growing plants on Mars

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** InSight (Interior Exploration using Seismic Investigations, Geodesy and Heat Transport) was designed to study Mars's deep interior. It deployed a seismometer that detected hundreds of "marsquakes" — seismic events on Mars — providing the first direct measurements of Mars's internal structure. InSight revealed details about the thickness and composition of Mars's crust, mantle, and core.

### Question 34

B) About 23 kg

**Explanation:** Mars's surface gravity is about 38% of Earth's. So a person weighing 60 kg on Earth would weigh approximately 60 × 0.38 = 22.8 kg on Mars (about 23 kg). You would feel much lighter and could jump much higher. This reduced gravity is a factor in designing habitats and equipment for future Mars missions.

### Question 35

B) Opportunity

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** NASA's Opportunity rover lost contact in June 2018 during a massive global dust storm that blocked sunlight from reaching its solar panels. ### Question 36

### Question 37
Why does Mars lack a global magnetic field like Earth?

A) Mars is too close to the Sun
B) Mars's core has cooled and solidified, stopping the dynamo effect
C) Mars rotates too quickly
D) Mars has too much iron in its crust

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Unlike Earth, Mars does not have a strong global magnetic field. Scientists believe Mars once had one, but its smaller size caused its core to cool more quickly. Without a liquid outer core to generate a magnetic dynamo, the global magnetic field faded. This loss had severe consequences — without magnetic protection, the solar wind gradually stripped away much of Mars's atmosphere over billions of years.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Mars Atmospheric Studies
