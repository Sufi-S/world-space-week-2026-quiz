## Topic 8 – Jupiter and Saturn — The Gas Giants

### Questions for Topic 8

### Question 1
Which is the largest planet in the solar system?

A) Saturn
B) Neptune
C) Jupiter
D) Uranus

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system with a diameter of approximately 139,820 km — about 11 times Earth's diameter. It is so large that more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. Jupiter is also more massive than all the other planets combined.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 2
What position does Jupiter occupy from the Sun?

A) 4th
B) 5th
C) 6th
D) 7th

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Jupiter is the 5th planet from the Sun, located beyond the asteroid belt. It orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 778 million km (roughly 5.2 AU). Jupiter is the first of the outer planets and the first of the two gas giants.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 3
What is Jupiter primarily composed of?

A) Rock and metal
B) Water and ammonia ice
C) Hydrogen and helium
D) Carbon dioxide and nitrogen

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Jupiter is composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, giving it a composition similar to the Sun. It is classified as a gas giant because it does not have a solid surface — the gaseous atmosphere gradually transitions to liquid and then possibly a solid core under immense pressure deep within the planet.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 4
How long does it take Jupiter to rotate once on its axis?

A) About 9 hours 56 minutes
B) About 24 hours
C) About 10 days
D) About 30 hours

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Jupiter has the fastest rotation of any planet in the solar system, completing one full rotation in approximately 9 hours and 56 minutes. Despite being the largest planet, it spins the fastest. This rapid rotation causes Jupiter to bulge noticeably at its equator and contributes to its banded cloud pattern.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 5
What is Jupiter's Great Red Spot?

A) A massive volcano on Jupiter's surface
B) A huge anticyclonic storm larger than Earth that has raged for over 350 years
C) A red-coloured crater from an ancient impact
D) A landing site for spacecraft

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Great Red Spot is a massive anticyclonic (high-pressure) storm in Jupiter's atmosphere that has been observed for at least 350 years. It is so large that Earth could fit inside it. The storm's red colour may come from chemical reactions in the upper atmosphere. In recent decades, the Great Red Spot has been gradually shrinking.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Juno

### Question 6
How many known moons does Jupiter have (as of 2023)?

A) 16
B) 27
C) 95
D) 146

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** As of 2023, Jupiter has 95 known moons. The four largest — Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — are called the Galilean moons because they were discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610. The remaining moons are much smaller and many are believed to be captured asteroids.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 7
Who discovered Jupiter's four largest moons, and in what year?

A) Isaac Newton in 1687
B) Galileo Galilei in 1610
C) Nicolaus Copernicus in 1543
D) William Herschel in 1781

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Galileo Galilei discovered Jupiter's four largest moons in January 1610 using one of the first telescopes. These were the first moons ever discovered around another planet, providing crucial evidence that not everything orbited Earth — supporting the heliocentric model. The moons are now called the Galilean moons in his honour.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** History of Astronomy

### Question 8
What are Jupiter's four Galilean moons?

A) Titan, Enceladus, Mimas, Iapetus
B) Phobos, Deimos, Triton, Charon
C) Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto
D) Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The four Galilean moons are Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto, named after figures associated with the god Jupiter/Zeus in mythology. They were the first moons discovered around another planet and are among the most scientifically interesting objects in the solar system, each with unique characteristics.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science / History of Astronomy

### Question 9
Which Galilean moon is the most volcanically active body in the solar system?

A) Europa
B) Ganymede
C) Callisto
D) Io

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Io is the most volcanically active body in the entire solar system, with hundreds of active volcanoes. Its volcanism is driven by tidal heating — the enormous gravitational forces from Jupiter and the other Galilean moons repeatedly squeeze and stretch Io, generating internal heat. Some of Io's volcanoes eject material hundreds of kilometres above the surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Galileo and Juno missions

### Question 10
Why is Europa considered one of the best candidates for extraterrestrial life?

A) It has a thick oxygen atmosphere
B) It has a subsurface ocean of liquid water beneath an icy crust
C) Its surface temperature is similar to Earth's
D) It has forests of alien organisms visible from space

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Europa is considered one of the most promising places to search for life because it has a global ocean of liquid water beneath its icy crust. This ocean is kept liquid by tidal heating from Jupiter's gravity. Where there is liquid water, energy, and the right chemistry, life as we know it may be possible — even deep beneath the ice, similar to life found near hydrothermal vents on Earth's ocean floor.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / Astrobiology

### Question 11
Which is the largest moon in the entire solar system?

A) Titan
B) Europa
C) Ganymede
D) Triton

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Ganymede, a moon of Jupiter, is the largest moon in the solar system with a diameter of about 5,268 km — larger than the planet Mercury (4,879 km)! Ganymede is also the only moon known to have its own magnetic field. It may also harbour a subsurface ocean beneath its icy surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 12
Jupiter's magnetic field is the strongest of any planet. How does it compare to Earth's?

A) About the same strength
B) About 100 times stronger
C) About 20,000 times stronger
D) About 1 million times stronger

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Jupiter's magnetic field is approximately 20,000 times stronger than Earth's, making it the most powerful planetary magnetic field in the solar system. This field creates an enormous magnetosphere that traps charged particles and produces intense radiation belts. Jupiter's magnetic field extends millions of kilometres into space and even affects the orbits of its moons.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Juno

### Question 13
Why is Jupiter sometimes called a "cosmic vacuum cleaner"?

A) It absorbs all light that reaches it
B) Its strong gravity captures or deflects asteroids and comets that might otherwise hit inner planets like Earth
C) It generates powerful winds that sweep up debris
D) It pulls planets closer to the Sun

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Jupiter's enormous mass (318× Earth) gives it a very strong gravitational pull that can capture or deflect asteroids and comets heading toward the inner solar system. This "vacuum cleaner" effect may have reduced the number of catastrophic impacts on Earth over billions of years, potentially helping life to develop and survive.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / Solar System Dynamics

### Question 14
Does Jupiter have a solid surface?

A) Yes, a rocky surface like Earth
B) Yes, an icy surface
C) No — its gas gradually transitions to liquid under immense pressure
D) No — it is entirely empty inside

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Jupiter has no solid surface. Its atmosphere of hydrogen and helium gas gradually becomes denser with depth, transitioning into liquid hydrogen, then into a metallic hydrogen layer (hydrogen under such extreme pressure that it behaves like a metal). There may be a small rocky/icy core at the very centre, but there is no defined surface boundary.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 15
Does Jupiter have rings?

A) No, only Saturn has rings
B) Yes, a faint, thin ring system discovered in 1979
C) Yes, spectacular rings visible from Earth with binoculars
D) It used to have rings but they disappeared

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Jupiter does have a ring system, but it is very faint and thin — nothing like Saturn's spectacular rings. Jupiter's rings were discovered in 1979 by the Voyager 1 spacecraft. They are composed mainly of dust particles knocked off Jupiter's small inner moons by micrometeorite impacts. In fact, all four gas and ice giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) have ring systems.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Voyager

### Question 16
How long is one year on Jupiter?

A) About 5 Earth years
B) About 11.86 Earth years
C) About 29 Earth years
D) About 84 Earth years

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Jupiter takes approximately 11.86 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun. This relatively long orbital period is because Jupiter orbits at a much greater distance from the Sun (about 5.2 AU) than the inner planets. Despite having a long year, Jupiter has the shortest day of any planet — just under 10 hours.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 17
What causes Jupiter's banded appearance of alternating light and dark stripes?

A) Different rock layers on its surface
B) Alternating light zones and dark belts of clouds driven by Jupiter's rapid rotation
C) Shadows cast by its many moons
D) Reflection from its ring system

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Jupiter's banded appearance comes from alternating light-coloured zones and dark-coloured belts of clouds in its atmosphere. The rapid rotation (under 10 hours) and powerful atmospheric circulation create these bands. Light zones are regions of rising gas (higher, cooler clouds), while dark belts are regions of sinking gas (lower, warmer clouds).

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / Atmospheric Science

### Question 18
Which spacecraft has been orbiting Jupiter since 2016, studying its interior, atmosphere, and magnetosphere?

A) Galileo
B) Cassini
C) Juno
D) New Horizons

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** NASA's Juno spacecraft arrived at Jupiter on July 4, 2016, and has been studying the planet ever since. Juno orbits Jupiter in a highly elliptical polar orbit and has provided unprecedented data about Jupiter's interior structure, magnetic field, atmospheric dynamics, and the Great Red Spot. Galileo orbited Jupiter earlier (1995–2003) but its mission ended.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA

### Question 19
ESA launched the JUICE mission in 2023. What does it aim to study?

A) Jupiter's rings
B) Jupiter's Galilean moons, particularly icy moons with potential for life
C) Jupiter's Great Red Spot only
D) Saturn's moon Titan

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** JUICE (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) is an ESA mission launched in April 2023, expected to arrive at Jupiter around 2031. Its primary targets are the Galilean moons Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — particularly their subsurface oceans and potential habitability. It will eventually orbit Ganymede, becoming the first spacecraft to orbit a moon other than Earth's Moon.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** ESA

### Question 20
What position does Saturn occupy from the Sun?

A) 5th
B) 6th
C) 7th
D) 8th

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Saturn is the 6th planet from the Sun, orbiting at an average distance of about 1.4 billion km (9.5 AU). It is the second of the two gas giants (after Jupiter) and the most distant planet easily visible to the naked eye from Earth.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 21
Saturn is the second largest planet. What is its most famous feature?

A) A giant storm like the Great Red Spot
B) Its spectacular ring system
C) Its red surface colour
D) Its thousands of active volcanoes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Saturn is most famous for its spectacular ring system, which is the most extensive and visible of any planet. The rings are made of billions of particles of ice and rock, ranging from tiny dust grains to house-sized chunks. Saturn's rings are visible from Earth through even a small telescope and have been observed since Galileo first saw them in 1610 (though he couldn't resolve them clearly).

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 22
What are Saturn's rings primarily made of?

A) Gas and dust only
B) Solid rock
C) Billions of particles of ice and rock
D) Liquid metal

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Saturn's rings are composed of billions of individual particles made of ice and rock. These particles range enormously in size — from microscopic dust grains to chunks the size of houses or even small buildings. The rings reflect sunlight brightly because of the high ice content, making them visible from Earth.

### Question 23

**Explanation:** As of 2023, Saturn has 146 known moons — the most of any planet in the solar system, surpassing even Jupiter's 95. Saturn's moons range from the massive Titan (larger than Mercury) to tiny moonlets embedded within the ring system. Many of the smaller moons were discovered by the Cassini spacecraft.

### Question 24
What is remarkable about Saturn's density?

A) It is the densest planet in the solar system
B) Its density is less than water — theoretically it would float
C) It has the same density as Earth
D) It has no measurable density because it is entirely gas

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Saturn has a density of only 0.687 g/cm³, which is less than the density of water (1.0 g/cm³). This means that if you had a bathtub large enough, Saturn would theoretically float! Saturn is the least dense planet in the solar system. This low density comes from its composition — mostly hydrogen and helium gas.

**Difficulty:** Easy

**Source:** Planetary Science

### Question 25
What is the Cassini Division?

A) The gap between Saturn and its rings
B) The prominent gap between Saturn's A and B rings, about 4,800 km wide
C) The name of a spacecraft
D) The boundary between Jupiter and Saturn's orbits

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The Cassini Division is a prominent gap approximately 4,800 km wide between Saturn's A and B rings. It was discovered by Giovanni Cassini in 1675 and is visible through a telescope from Earth. Despite appearing empty, the division actually contains sparse ring material. The gap is maintained partly by the gravitational influence of Saturn's moon Mimas.

### Question 26

A) A ring
B) B ring

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The B ring is the brightest and most massive of Saturn's rings. The rings were named alphabetically in the order they were discovered (not their distance from Saturn), so the letter doesn't correspond to position. The main rings from innermost to outermost are D, C, B, A, F, G, and E — but B and A were discovered first.

### Question 27
Approximately how thick are Saturn's rings in some places?

A) About 10 metres
B) About 10 kilometres
C) About 1,000 kilometres
D) About 100,000 kilometres

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Despite extending up to 80,000 km above Saturn's equator, Saturn's rings are remarkably thin — in some places only about 10 metres thick. To put this in perspective, if the rings were shrunk to the size of a football field, they would be thinner than a razor blade. This extreme thinness is one of the most surprising facts about Saturn's rings.

### Question 28
Which of Saturn's moons is the second largest moon in the solar system?

A) Enceladus
B) Mimas
C) Titan
D) Iapetus

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Titan is Saturn's largest moon and the second largest moon in the entire solar system (after Jupiter's Ganymede), with a diameter of about 5,150 km — larger than the planet Mercury. Titan is unique among all moons in having a thick, dense atmosphere composed primarily of nitrogen.

**Difficulty:** Easy

### Question 29
What makes Titan unique among all moons in the solar system?

A) It has active volcanoes
B) It is the only moon with a thick atmosphere
C) It has the strongest magnetic field of any moon
D) It is the only moon that orbits backward

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Titan is the only moon in the solar system with a thick, substantial atmosphere. Its atmosphere is primarily nitrogen (about 95%) with some methane, and is even denser than Earth's — surface pressure is about 1.5 times Earth's. This thick atmosphere supports weather systems and has methane rain, rivers, and lakes on Titan's surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Cassini-Huygens

### Question 30
What liquid is found on Titan's surface in the form of lakes and rivers?

A) Liquid water
B) Liquid oxygen
C) Liquid methane and ethane
D) Liquid mercury

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Titan has lakes and rivers of liquid methane (CH₄) and ethane (C₂H₆) on its surface. Titan is so cold (about -179°C) that methane, which is a gas on Earth, exists as a liquid there. These hydrocarbon lakes make Titan the only body in the solar system besides Earth known to have stable liquid on its surface.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Cassini-Huygens

### Question 31
Which spacecraft probe landed on Titan, and when?

A) Voyager 1 in 1980
B) Galileo probe in 1995
C) Huygens probe on January 14, 2005
D) Philae lander in 2014

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** ESA's Huygens probe, carried to Saturn by NASA's Cassini spacecraft, landed on Titan on January 14, 2005. It was the most distant landing ever achieved at the time. During its descent and after landing, Huygens transmitted data and images for about 72 minutes, revealing a landscape with rounded pebbles and channels carved by flowing liquid methane.

### Question 32
What makes Saturn's moon Enceladus scientifically exciting?

A) It is the largest moon in the solar system
B) It has geysers of water ice erupting from its south pole, suggesting a subsurface ocean
C) It has a thick atmosphere with oxygen
D) It orbits Saturn backward

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Enceladus has dramatic geysers of water ice and vapour erupting from fractures near its south pole (cryovolcanism). These plumes, discovered by the Cassini spacecraft, contain water, salts, and organic molecules — evidence of a subsurface ocean beneath the icy crust. This makes Enceladus one of the top candidates in the solar system for harbouring extraterrestrial life.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** NASA Cassini / Astrobiology

### Question 34
The Cassini-Huygens mission studied the Saturn system for how many years?

A) 3 years
B) 7 years
C) 13 years
D) 20 years

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The Cassini-Huygens mission was one of the most ambitious and successful space missions ever. Launched in 1997, Cassini arrived at Saturn in 2004 and orbited for 13 years until September 2017, when it was deliberately plunged into Saturn's atmosphere to prevent contaminating potentially habitable moons like Enceladus. During this time it made groundbreaking discoveries about Saturn, its rings, and its moons.

**Difficulty:** Medium

### Question 35
How was the Cassini mission deliberately ended in 2017?

A) It ran out of fuel and drifted away
B) It was deliberately plunged into Saturn's atmosphere
C) It was redirected to Jupiter
D) It was shut down remotely and left in orbit

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cassini's mission ended with the "Grand Finale" — a series of daring orbits between Saturn and its rings, followed by a deliberate plunge into Saturn's atmosphere on September 15, 2017. This was done to prevent the spacecraft from accidentally contaminating Enceladus or Titan (which may have conditions for life) after it eventually ran out of fuel.

### Question 38
Which Galilean moon has its own magnetic field — the only moon in the solar system known to do so?

A) Io
B) Europa
C) Ganymede
D) Callisto

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Ganymede is the only moon in the solar system known to generate its own magnetic field. This is thought to be produced by convection in a liquid iron core, similar to how Earth generates its magnetic field. Ganymede's magnetic field creates a small magnetosphere within Jupiter's much larger one — a unique nested magnetosphere system.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / NASA Galileo

### Question 39
What drives the intense volcanic activity on Jupiter's moon Io?

A) Radioactive decay in Io's core
B) Tidal heating caused by Jupiter's enormous gravitational pull
C) Chemical reactions on Io's surface
D) Heat from the Sun

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Io's extreme volcanism is powered by tidal heating. Jupiter's immense gravity, combined with gravitational interactions with Europa and Ganymede (orbital resonance), repeatedly squeezes and stretches Io as it orbits. This constant flexing generates enormous internal friction and heat, driving hundreds of active volcanoes — far more than any other body in the solar system.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** Planetary Science / Tidal Dynamics

### Question 40
Scientists believe Saturn's rings may be surprisingly young. How old might they be?

A) As old as the solar system (4.6 billion years)
B) About 1 billion years old
C) Possibly only 100–400 million years old
D) About 10,000 years old

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Recent research, particularly from the Cassini mission, suggests Saturn's rings may be only 100–400 million years old — surprisingly young compared to Saturn itself (4.6 billion years). Evidence includes the rings' brightness (they haven't accumulated much dark debris), their mass, and gravitational measurements. This means dinosaurs may have roamed Earth before Saturn had its famous rings.

**Difficulty:** Hard

**Source:** NASA Cassini / Planetary Science

### Question 41
Both Jupiter and Saturn are classified as gas giants. What primarily distinguishes them from ice giants like Uranus and Neptune?

A) Gas giants have rings but ice giants do not
B) Gas giants are composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, while ice giants contain more water, ammonia, and methane ices
C) Gas giants are closer to the Sun
D) Gas giants have no moons

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Gas giants (Jupiter and Saturn) are primarily composed of hydrogen and helium, similar to the Sun. Ice giants (Uranus and Neptune) contain significantly more "ices" — water, ammonia, and methane — along with hydrogen and helium. This compositional difference is why they are classified differently, even though all four are much larger than the terrestrial planets.

**Difficulty:** Medium

**Source:** Planetary Science
