# Topic 21: The Indian Space Programme — History and Vision

### Learn This Topic

#### The Beginning
India's space programme began not with rockets and satellites, but with a vision: that space technology could transform a developing nation. While other countries pursued space for military prestige, India's founders envisioned space as a tool for **national development** — communication, weather forecasting, resource management, education, and disaster warning.

#### Key Timeline of the Indian Space Programme

| Year | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| **1962** | **INCOSPAR** (Indian National Committee for Space Research) established by Government of India, under the Department of Atomic Energy. Dr. Vikram Sarabhai was the driving force. Dr. Homi Bhabha, then chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission, supported the vision. |
| **Nov 21, 1963** | **First sounding rocket** launched from Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) in Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala. It was a Nike-Apache rocket (American-made). The famous story: rocket parts were transported by **bicycle** and assembled in a **church** (St. Mary Magdalene Church, now a space museum). |
| **1965** | Space Science and Technology Centre (SSTC) established in Thumba |
| **1967** | First Indian-made sounding rocket (Rohini-75) launched |
| **Aug 15, 1969** | **ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation)** formally established, replacing INCOSPAR. Founded on India's Independence Day! First Chairman: Vikram Sarabhai. |
| **1971** | Dr. Vikram Sarabhai passes away (Dec 30, 1971). Satish Dhawan later takes over. |
| **1972** | **Space Commission** and **Department of Space (DOS)** created. DOS reports directly to the Prime Minister. ISRO brought under DOS. |
| **1975** | **Aryabhata** — India's first satellite, launched on April 19, 1975 by a Soviet Kosmos-3M rocket from Kapustin Yar, USSR. Named after the ancient Indian mathematician Aryabhata. Weighed 360 kg. Conducted experiments in X-ray astronomy, aeronomy, and solar physics. |
| **1979** | **Bhaskara-I** — experimental Earth observation satellite (launched by USSR). Rohini Technology Payload launched by SLV-3 (first test flight failed to reach orbit). |
| **July 18, 1980** | **SLV-3** successfully launches **Rohini RS-1** satellite into orbit. India becomes the **6th nation** with independent satellite launch capability (after USSR, USA, France, Japan, China). APJ Abdul Kalam was the project director. |
| **1981** | **APPLE** (Ariane Passenger Payload Experiment) — India's first communication satellite, launched by ESA's Ariane-1. Famous photo of APPLE being transported on a bullock cart! |
| **1983** | **INSAT-1B** — India's first operational multipurpose communication and weather satellite |
| **1988** | **IRS-1A** — first operational Indian Remote Sensing satellite. Beginning of India's world-class remote sensing programme. |
| **1992** | **INSAT-2A** — first fully indigenous communication satellite |
| **Sep 20, 1993** | **PSLV-D1** — first developmental flight. Failed (guidance problem). |
| **Oct 15, 1994** | **PSLV-D2** — success! Beginning of PSLV era. |
| **2001** | **GSLV-D1** — first GSLV flight (with Russian cryogenic upper stage) |
| **Oct 22, 2008** | **Chandrayaan-1** — India's first Moon mission. Discovered water on the Moon! |
| **Nov 5, 2013** | **Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM/Mangalyaan)** launched — India's first interplanetary mission |
| **Sep 24, 2014** | MOM enters Mars orbit. India becomes first Asian nation to reach Mars, first in the world to succeed on first attempt. |
| **Jan 5, 2014** | **GSLV-D5** — first flight with indigenous cryogenic engine (CE-7.5). India masters cryogenic technology! |
| **Sep 28, 2015** | **AstroSat** — India's first multi-wavelength space observatory |
| **Feb 15, 2017** | **PSLV-C37** launches **104 satellites** in a single mission — world record at the time! |
| **July 22, 2019** | **Chandrayaan-2** — orbiter successful, Vikram lander crash-landed |
| **Aug 23, 2023** | **Chandrayaan-3** lands on Moon! India becomes 4th country to soft-land on the Moon, 1st to land near the south pole. Aug 23 declared **National Space Day**. |
| **Sep 2, 2023** | **Aditya-L1** launched — India's first solar mission |
| **Jan 1, 2024** | **XPoSat** launched — X-ray polarimetry mission |
| **Dec 30, 2024** | **SpaDeX** — India demonstrates space docking technology |
| **Future** | **Gaganyaan** (human spaceflight), **Chandrayaan-4** (sample return), **Shukrayaan** (Venus), **BAS** (Indian Space Station) |

#### Vision and Philosophy
- **Space for national development**: Vikram Sarabhai's vision was to use space technology to solve real problems — communication in remote areas, weather prediction for farmers, resource mapping, disaster management, telemedicine, tele-education
- **Self-reliance**: When denied cryogenic technology, India built its own. When denied GPS precision during Kargil War, India built NavIC. This spirit of self-reliance (Atmanirbharta) defines ISRO.
- **Cost-effectiveness**: ISRO is renowned for achieving missions at remarkably low costs. MOM cost less than the movie Gravity!
- **NOT a weapons programme**: Unlike some other space programmes, India's civilian space programme is separate from its military activities. ISRO focuses on peaceful applications.

#### Thumba — Where It All Began
- Thumba is in Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum), Kerala
- Located near the magnetic equator — ideal for studying the equatorial ionosphere
- The first rockets were assembled in the **St. Mary Magdalene Church** and the Bishop's house served as the office
- Rocket parts were transported on **bicycles** and **bullock carts**
- Today, TERLS is part of the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC)
- The church has been preserved as the **Thumba Space Museum**

### Key Facts
- INCOSPAR was established in 1962; ISRO was formed on August 15, 1969
- First sounding rocket launched from Thumba on November 21, 1963
- Aryabhata (1975) was India's first satellite — launched by the USSR
- SLV-3 success on July 18, 1980 made India the 6th nation with own launch capability
- ISRO operates under the Department of Space, which reports directly to the PM
- India's space programme philosophy: space technology for national development, not weapons
- The famous Thumba church is now a space museum

### Important Terms
- **INCOSPAR**: Indian National Committee for Space Research (1962, predecessor to ISRO)
- **ISRO**: Indian Space Research Organisation (est. August 15, 1969)
- **DOS**: Department of Space (est. 1972, under PMO)
- **TERLS**: Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station
- **Aryabhata**: India's first satellite (1975)
- **SLV-3**: Satellite Launch Vehicle-3, India's first indigenous launch vehicle

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| ISRO was founded in 1962 | INCOSPAR was founded in 1962. ISRO replaced it in 1969 |
| India's first satellite was launched by India | Aryabhata was launched by the USSR. India's own launch capability came in 1980 (SLV-3) |
| ISRO was founded by Vikram Sarabhai alone | Sarabhai was the key visionary, but Homi Bhabha also played a crucial supporting role |
| India's space programme started with satellites | It started with sounding rockets (1963), satellites came later (1975) |
| The first rocket from Thumba was Indian-made | It was a US-made Nike-Apache. Indian Rohini rockets came later |

# Topic 22: ISRO — Organisation, Centres and Infrastructure

### Learn This Topic

#### Organisational Structure
- **Indian Space Commission**: policy-making body, chaired by PM
- **Department of Space (DOS)**: under the PM, implements space policy
- **ISRO**: primary R&D organisation under DOS
- **ISRO Chairman** also serves as **Secretary, DOS** and **Chairman, Space Commission**
- Headquarters: **Antariksh Bhavan** (meaning "Space Building"), New BEL Road, Bengaluru

#### Major ISRO Centres

**1. VSSC — Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre**
- Location: Thiruvananthapuram (Trivandrum), Kerala
- Named after: **Vikram Sarabhai**, founder of Indian space programme
- Role: **Launch vehicle design and development** — this is where rockets are designed
- All Indian launch vehicles (SLV, ASLV, PSLV, GSLV, LVM3, SSLV) were developed here
- Also houses TERLS (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station)
- Includes propellant and solid motor processing facilities

**2. URSC — U R Rao Satellite Centre**
- Location: Bengaluru, Karnataka
- Named after: **Prof. U R Rao**, who built India's satellite programme (renamed from ISAC in 2018)
- Former name: ISAC (ISRO Satellite Centre)
- Role: **Design, development, and fabrication of satellites** — this is where all Indian satellites are built!
- **This is the centre organising WSW 2026!** (the quiz event)
- Built all Indian satellites: INSAT, IRS, GSAT, Chandrayaan, MOM, AstroSat, Aditya-L1, etc.

**3. SDSC SHAR — Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota**
- Location: Sriharikota island, Andhra Pradesh (between Pulicat Lake and Bay of Bengal)
- Named after: **Satish Dhawan**, ISRO's 2nd Chairman (renamed in 2002)
- Role: **India's spaceport** — all orbital launches happen from here
- Has **two operational launch pads** (First Launch Pad and Second Launch Pad) plus a **Third Launch Pad** for SSLV
- Also has vehicle assembly buildings, solid propellant plant, rocket motor static test facilities
- Located at ~13.7°N latitude — close to equator for launch advantage
- East coast location allows launches over the Bay of Bengal (safe — no populated areas underneath)

**4. SAC — Space Applications Centre**
- Location: Ahmedabad, Gujarat
- Role: Development of **payloads** (instruments carried by satellites) and **space applications**
- Develops communication transponders, cameras, sensors, SAR instruments
- Also develops ground-based applications: telemedicine, tele-education, disaster management systems

**5. LPSC — Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre**
- Locations: Valiamala (near Thiruvananthapuram) and Bengaluru
- Role: Development of **liquid and cryogenic rocket engines**
- Developed the Vikas engine, CE-7.5, CE-20, and upcoming semi-cryogenic engines
- Critical role in India's cryogenic engine success story

**6. NRSC — National Remote Sensing Centre**
- Location: Hyderabad, Telangana
- Role: **Acquisition, processing, and distribution of remote sensing data**
- Operates ground stations that receive data from Earth observation satellites
- Provides data for agriculture, water resources, forestry, urban planning, disaster management

**7. ISTRAC — ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network**
- Location: Headquarters in Bengaluru; network of ground stations across India and globally
- Role: **Mission control** — provides tracking, telemetry, and command support for satellite and launch vehicle missions
- Operates the Indian Deep Space Network (IDSN) with a 32m antenna at Byalalu, near Bengaluru
- IDSN tracked Chandrayaan-1, MOM, Chandrayaan-2/3, Aditya-L1

**8. IIRS — Indian Institute of Remote Sensing**
- Location: Dehradun, Uttarakhand
- Role: **Training and education** in remote sensing, GIS, and space technology
- Offers courses and degree programmes

**9. PRL — Physical Research Laboratory**
- Location: Ahmedabad, Gujarat
- Founded by: **Vikram Sarabhai** (1947)
- Role: **Fundamental research** in physics, astronomy, space science, planetary science
- Considered the "cradle of space science" in India
- Supported by DOS

**10. NARL — National Atmospheric Research Laboratory**
- Location: Gadanki, Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh
- Role: Research in atmospheric and space sciences
- Operates MST Radar and other instruments

**11. IN-SPACe — Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre**
- Location: Ahmedabad
- Established: 2020
- Role: **Promotes and authorises private sector participation** in Indian space activities
- Acts as a single-window agency for private companies wanting to use ISRO facilities or launch services

**12. NSIL — New Space India Limited**
- Location: Bengaluru
- Established: 2019
- Role: **Commercial arm of ISRO** — handles commercial launches, satellite-based services, technology transfer
- Replaced some functions of Antrix Corporation

**13. Antrix Corporation**
- Location: Bengaluru
- Established: 1992
- Role: Earlier commercial arm of ISRO (some functions now with NSIL)
- Handles international cooperation and marketing of ISRO capabilities

### Key Facts
- ISRO HQ is at Antariksh Bhavan, Bengaluru
- VSSC (Thiruvananthapuram) designs rockets; URSC (Bengaluru) builds satellites
- All Indian orbital launches happen from SDSC SHAR, Sriharikota
- URSC is the centre organising WSW 2026 — it was renamed from ISAC after Prof. U R Rao
- PRL was founded by Vikram Sarabhai in 1947 — the "cradle of space science"
- IN-SPACe was created to open India's space sector to private companies
- SDSC SHAR was renamed after Satish Dhawan in 2002

### Important Terms
- **VSSC**: Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (rockets)
- **URSC**: U R Rao Satellite Centre (satellites)
- **SDSC SHAR**: Satish Dhawan Space Centre (spaceport)
- **SAC**: Space Applications Centre (payloads)
- **LPSC**: Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre (engines)
- **ISTRAC**: Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network
- **IDSN**: Indian Deep Space Network
- **IN-SPACe**: Private sector authorisation
- **NSIL**: New Space India Limited (commercial arm)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| ISRO builds both rockets and satellites | VSSC builds rockets, URSC builds satellites — different centres! |
| URSC has always been called URSC | It was called ISAC until 2018, then renamed after Prof. U R Rao |
| Sriharikota is ISRO headquarters | Sriharikota is the launch centre. ISRO HQ is in Bengaluru |
| India has only one launch pad | SDSC SHAR has multiple launch pads (FLP, SLP, and a third for SSLV) |
| NSIL and Antrix are the same | NSIL (2019) is the newer commercial arm; Antrix (1992) was the earlier one |

# Topic 23: Vikram Sarabhai — Father of the Indian Space Programme

### Learn This Topic

#### Personal Details
- **Full Name**: Vikram Ambalal Sarabhai
- **Born**: August 12, 1919, in Ahmedabad, Gujarat
- **Died**: December 30, 1971, in Kovalam, Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala (age 52)
- **Family**: Born into the wealthy Sarabhai family of industrialists. Father: Ambalal Sarabhai. Wife: Mrinalini Sarabhai (classical dancer). Daughter: Mallika Sarabhai (dancer and activist). Son: Kartikeya Sarabhai (environmentalist).

#### Education
- Studied at Gujarat College, Ahmedabad
- Went to **Cambridge University**, England — studied Natural Sciences Tripos
- His PhD research was on **cosmic rays** at Cambridge
- His mentor/teacher at the Indian Institute of Science was **Sir C.V. Raman** (Nobel laureate in Physics, 1930)

#### Major Achievements and Contributions

**1. Physical Research Laboratory (PRL)**
- Founded PRL in Ahmedabad in **1947** (at age 28!)
- Started in just two rooms at M.G. Science Institute
- PRL became India's premier research lab for physics, astronomy, and space science
- Known as the "cradle of space science in India"

**2. INCOSPAR and India's Space Programme**
- Convinced the Indian government to establish **INCOSPAR** (1962) for space research
- Set up the **Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station** (TERLS)
- Oversaw the first sounding rocket launch from Thumba (November 21, 1963)
- INCOSPAR later became **ISRO** (August 15, 1969) with Sarabhai as first Chairman

**3. Vision for Space Technology**
- His most famous contribution was his **vision**: space technology must serve national development
- He argued that a developing country like India needed space technology for:
  - Television broadcasting to remote villages (education)
  - Weather forecasting (helping farmers)
  - Communication (connecting a vast country)
  - Natural resource mapping (development planning)
- His famous statement: *"There are some who question the relevance of space activities in a developing nation. To us, there is no ambiguity of purpose. We do not have the fantasy of competing with the economically advanced nations in the exploration of the Moon or the planets or manned space-flight. But we are convinced that if we are to play a meaningful role nationally, and in the community of nations, we must be second to none in the application of advanced technologies to the real problems of man and society."*

**4. Other Institutions Founded**
- **IIM Ahmedabad** (Indian Institute of Management, 1961) — co-founded
- **ATIRA** (Ahmedabad Textile Industry's Research Association)
- **CEPT University** (Centre for Environmental Planning and Technology)
- Helped establish the **Nehru Foundation for Development**
- Instrumental in setting up **Community Science Centre**, Ahmedabad
- Was involved with the Indian pharmaceutical industry (Sarabhai Chemicals)

#### Awards
- **Padma Bhushan** (1966) — India's third-highest civilian honour
- **Padma Vibhushan** (1972, posthumous) — India's second-highest civilian honour
- **Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize** (1962)

#### Legacy
- Called the **"Father of the Indian Space Programme"**
- VSSC (Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre) in Thiruvananthapuram named after him
- **Vikram** — the lander in Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3 was named after him
- Vikram Sarabhai Space Exhibition in Ahmedabad
- His vision continues to guide ISRO's approach: technology for societal benefit
- The Thumba church where rockets were first assembled is now a museum in his memory

#### The Thumba Church Story
- When Sarabhai chose Thumba for the launch site, the area had a fishing village and a church
- The villagers had to be relocated — the Bishop of the diocese, Rt. Rev. Peter Bernard Pereira, helped convince the community
- The church (St. Mary Magdalene) became the rocket assembly workshop
- The Bishop's house became the office
- This humble beginning — a church turned into a rocket workshop — symbolises India's grassroots approach to space

### Key Facts
- Born August 12, 1919 in Ahmedabad; died December 30, 1971 in Kovalam
- Studied at Cambridge, his mentor was C.V. Raman
- Founded PRL (1947), INCOSPAR (1962), helped found IIM Ahmedabad
- First Chairman of ISRO (1969-1971)
- Vision: space technology for national development, not prestige or weapons
- Received Padma Bhushan (1966) and Padma Vibhushan (posthumous, 1972)
- Chandrayaan landers named "Vikram" in his honour

### Important Terms
- **PRL**: Physical Research Laboratory (founded 1947, Ahmedabad)
- **INCOSPAR**: Indian National Committee for Space Research
- **TERLS**: Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station
- **Cosmic rays**: high-energy particles from space (Sarabhai's research area)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Sarabhai founded ISRO | He founded INCOSPAR (1962). ISRO was formed in 1969 as a reorganisation of INCOSPAR. He was its first Chairman. |
| Sarabhai launched the first Indian satellite | He died in 1971; Aryabhata was launched in 1975. He laid the groundwork but didn't live to see it. |
| Sarabhai's teacher was Homi Bhabha | His mentor at IISc was C.V. Raman. Bhabha was a colleague and supporter, not his teacher. |
| Sarabhai only worked on space | He was also a major institution builder — IIM Ahmedabad, ATIRA, pharmaceutical business, and more |

# Topic 24: Key Indian Space Scientists and Leaders

### Learn This Topic

#### ISRO Chairpersons — Complete List

**1. Vikram Sarabhai (1963–1971)**
- First Chairman of INCOSPAR/ISRO
- The visionary who started it all
- See Topic 23 for detailed coverage
- Died on December 30, 1971

**2. M.G.K. Menon (1972)**
- Served briefly as acting Chairman after Sarabhai's death
- Distinguished physicist, later became Minister of State for Science & Technology
- Bridged the transition period

**3. Satish Dhawan (1972–1984)**
- **Called "Father of Experimental Fluid Dynamics in India"**
- Was a professor and Director of **IISc (Indian Institute of Science), Bengaluru**
- Oversaw the SLV-3 programme
- **Famous leadership story**: When SLV-3 E1 (first experimental flight, Aug 1979) **failed**, Dhawan personally addressed the press and **took full blame**. When SLV-3 E2 (July 18, 1980) **succeeded**, he pushed **APJ Abdul Kalam** (the project director) to face the press and receive the credit. This "take blame for failure, give credit for success" leadership style is legendary.
- Oversaw INSAT and IRS programmes
- **Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC SHAR)** in Sriharikota named after him (2002)
- Died: January 3, 2002

**4. Prof. Udupi Ramachandra Rao (U R Rao) (1984–1994)**
- **Built India's satellite programme**
- As a young scientist, was project director of India's first satellite **Aryabhata** (1975)
- As Chairman, expanded INSAT system, established IRS programme
- Oversaw PSLV development
- Under his leadership, India's satellite capabilities became world-class
- **ISAC was renamed U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC)** in his honour (2018)
- Received Padma Bhushan and Padma Vibhushan
- Died: July 24, 2017

**5. K. Kasturirangan (1994–2003)**
- Oversaw PSLV becoming operational and reliable
- IRS-P series of remote sensing satellites
- Initiated Chandrayaan-1 project
- Later headed the New Education Policy (NEP 2020) drafting committee
- Received Padma Vibhushan

**6. G. Madhavan Nair (2003–2009)**
- **Chandrayaan-1 era Chairman** — oversaw India's first Moon mission
- Continued GSLV development
- Expanded international cooperation

**7. K. Radhakrishnan (2009–2014)**
- Oversaw the **Mars Orbiter Mission (MOM)** — from concept to launch in record time
- MOM entered Mars orbit just 2 days after his term ended (Sep 24, 2014)
- Also oversaw GSLV-D5 success (indigenous cryogenic engine, Jan 2014)
- Continued Chandrayaan and GSLV programmes

**8. A.S. Kiran Kumar (2015–2018)**
- AstroSat launch (2015)
- NavIC constellation completed
- PSLV-C37 record launch of 104 satellites (2017)
- Expanded ISRO's commercial launch services

**9. K. Sivan (2018–2022)**
- Oversaw **Chandrayaan-2** (2019) — emotionally impacted by Vikram lander's crash
- PM Modi consoled him in a now-famous embrace when he broke down
- Initiated **Gaganyaan** (human spaceflight) programme
- Dealt with COVID-19 pandemic disruptions

**10. S. Somanath (2022–2025)**
- **Chandrayaan-3 triumph** (Aug 23, 2023) — the mission he guided to success
- **Aditya-L1** launch and L1 orbit insertion
- **SpaDeX** docking experiment success
- **XPoSat** launch
- TV-D1 abort test for Gaganyaan
- Known as a rocket scientist — was director of VSSC and LPSC before becoming Chairman
- Oversaw an extraordinary run of mission successes

**11. V. Narayanan (2025–present)**
- Current ISRO Chairman (assumed office January 2025)
- Previously Director of LPSC — played key role in cryogenic and semi-cryogenic engine development
- Overseeing Gaganyaan programme, upcoming missions

#### Other Key Figures in Indian Space

**APJ Abdul Kalam (1931–2015)**
- Full name: Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam
- **Project Director of SLV-3** — made India's first satellite launch vehicle a success (1980)
- Led the **IGMDP** (Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme) — Agni, Prithvi, Akash, Trishul, Nag missiles
- Known as the **"Missile Man of India"**
- Became the **11th President of India** (2002–2007) — "People's President"
- Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu
- Authored "Wings of Fire" (autobiography), "India 2020"
- Awards: Bharat Ratna (1997), Padma Vibhushan (1990), Padma Bhushan (1981)
- Died: July 27, 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong

**Homi Jehangir Bhabha (1909–1966)**
- **Father of the Indian Nuclear/Atomic Energy Programme**
- Chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission
- Supported Vikram Sarabhai's space vision — INCOSPAR was initially under the Department of Atomic Energy
- Bhabha and Sarabhai together laid the foundation for India's scientific institutions
- Died in a plane crash (Air India Flight 101) on January 24, 1966

**Rakesh Sharma — First Indian in Space**
- **Date**: April 3, 1984
- Mission: **Soyuz T-11** (Soviet mission), went to **Salyut 7** space station
- Spent **7 days, 21 hours, 40 minutes** in space
- The famous exchange: When PM **Indira Gandhi** asked how India looked from space, Sharma replied: **"Saare Jahan Se Achha"** (the best in the world — from a famous patriotic song by Allama Iqbal)
- Was an **Indian Air Force** pilot (Squadron Leader at the time)
- Received **Ashoka Chakra** (India's highest peacetime military decoration)
- He remains the **only Indian citizen** to have gone to space (Kalpana Chawla and Sunita Williams are American citizens of Indian origin)

**Kalpana Chawla (1962–2003)**
- Born in Karnal, Haryana, India
- Became a **US citizen** and NASA astronaut
- **First Indian-origin woman in space**: STS-87 mission (November 1997)
- **Died**: February 1, 2003, in the **Space Shuttle Columbia disaster** (STS-107) — Columbia broke apart during re-entry
- All 7 crew members were killed
- India named the meteorological satellite **Kalpana-1** in her honour (2003)

**Sunita Williams**
- Indian-origin American astronaut (father is of Indian origin from Gujarat)
- Multiple ISS missions, held record for most spacewalks by a female astronaut
- Spent extended durations on the ISS

### Key Facts
- Satish Dhawan took blame for failure but gave credit for success — legendary leadership
- Prof. U R Rao built India's satellite programme; URSC is named after him
- APJ Abdul Kalam was SLV-3 project director, later became President of India
- Rakesh Sharma is the only Indian citizen to go to space (April 3, 1984)
- Kalpana Chawla died in the Columbia disaster (Feb 1, 2003)
- S. Somanath oversaw Chandrayaan-3's historic success
- Current ISRO Chairman is V. Narayanan (2025-present)

### Important Terms
- **IGMDP**: Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (led by Kalam)
- **Bharat Ratna**: India's highest civilian honour (Kalam received it)
- **Ashoka Chakra**: Highest peacetime military decoration (Rakesh Sharma received it)
- **SLV-3**: India's first satellite launch vehicle
- **"Missile Man"**: APJ Abdul Kalam's title
- **"Saare Jahan Se Achha"**: Rakesh Sharma's famous reply from space

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Kalpana Chawla was an Indian astronaut | She was an American citizen of Indian origin, a NASA astronaut |
| Rakesh Sharma went on an Indian spacecraft | He flew on a Soviet Soyuz mission to the Salyut 7 space station |

| Satish Dhawan took credit for SLV-3 success | He took credit for the FAILURE but gave credit for the SUCCESS to the team — this is the key story |
| Vikram Sarabhai saw India launch a satellite | He died in 1971; Aryabhata launched in 1975; SLV-3 succeeded in 1980 |
| The ISRO Chairman reports to the Science Ministry | The Chairman/Secretary DOS reports directly to the Prime Minister |
