## Topic 46: Moon Exploration — International Missions

### Learn This Topic

#### Why the Moon?
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and the closest celestial body (~384,400 km away). It's the most accessible destination for space exploration and serves as a testbed for technologies needed for deeper space travel (Mars and beyond).

#### Timeline of Major Moon Missions

**Robotic Missions (1959–1976):**

| Mission | Country | Year | Achievement |
|---------|---------|------|-------------|
| **Luna 1** | USSR | 1959 | First spacecraft to fly past the Moon |
| **Luna 2** | USSR | 1959 | First spacecraft to reach the Moon (impact) |
| **Luna 3** | USSR | 1959 | First photos of the **far side** of the Moon |
| **Ranger 7** | USA | 1964 | First successful US Moon impact, close-up photos |
| **Luna 9** | USSR | 1966 | **First soft landing** on the Moon |
| **Luna 10** | USSR | 1966 | First spacecraft to orbit the Moon |
| **Surveyor 1** | USA | 1966 | First US soft landing |
| **Luna 16** | USSR | 1970 | First robotic **sample return** from the Moon |
| **Lunokhod 1** | USSR | 1970 | First robotic **rover** on the Moon |
| **Luna 24** | USSR | 1976 | Last Soviet Moon mission (sample return) |

**Human Missions — Apollo (1968–1972):**

| Mission | Date | Achievement |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Apollo 8 | Dec 1968 | First humans to orbit the Moon |
| **Apollo 11** | **Jul 1969** | **First Moon landing** (Armstrong, Aldrin, Collins) |
| Apollo 12 | Nov 1969 | Precision landing near Surveyor 3 |
| Apollo 13 | Apr 1970 | Aborted after oxygen tank explosion; crew returned safely |
| Apollo 14 | Feb 1971 | Alan Shepard hits golf balls on Moon |
| Apollo 15 | Jul 1971 | First use of Lunar Roving Vehicle |

| **Apollo 17** | **Dec 1972** | Last human Moon mission (so far). Eugene Cernan was the last person on the Moon |

**Modern Era (2003–present):**

|---------|---------|------|-------------|

| **Chang'e 1** | China | 2007 | First Chinese lunar orbiter |

| **LCROSS** | USA | 2009 | Confirmed water ice in permanently shadowed craters |
| **Chang'e 3** | China | 2013 | First Chinese lunar lander + Yutu rover |

| **Chang'e 5** | China | 2020 | Lunar sample return (1.73 kg) — first since Luna 24 (1976) |
| **Chandrayaan-3** | India | **2023** | **First landing near lunar south pole!** India = 4th country to soft-land |
| **SLIM** | Japan | 2024 | Japan = 5th country to soft-land; precision landing within 55 m |
| **Chang'e 6** | China | **2024** | **First sample return from the far side** of the Moon (~1.9 kg) |

#### Why the South Pole?
- Permanently shadowed craters may contain **water ice** — potential resource for future bases (drinking, oxygen, rocket fuel)
- Elevated crater rims have **near-continuous sunlight** — ideal for solar power
- Scientific interest: south pole has ancient, undisturbed terrain that can reveal lunar history
- This is why Chandrayaan-3 landing near the south pole was so significant

#### Countries That Have Soft-Landed on the Moon
1. **USSR** — Luna 9 (1966)
2. **USA** — Surveyor 1 (1966), Apollo missions (1969–72)
3. **China** — Chang'e 3 (2013), Chang'e 4 (2019), Chang'e 5 (2020), Chang'e 6 (2024)
4. **India** — Chandrayaan-3 (2023)
5. **Japan** — SLIM (2024)

(Note: Private companies like Intuitive Machines with Odysseus/IM-1 have also landed, Feb 2024)

### Key Facts
- Luna 9 (USSR, 1966): first soft landing on the Moon
- Apollo 11 (USA, 1969): first human Moon landing (Armstrong and Aldrin)
- 12 people have walked on the Moon (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17)
- Chandrayaan-1 (India, 2008): discovered water molecules on the Moon
- Chang'e 4 (China, 2019): first far-side landing
- Chandrayaan-3 (India, 2023): first landing near the lunar south pole
- 5 countries have soft-landed on the Moon: USSR, USA, China, India, Japan
- The south pole is valuable for water ice and near-continuous sunlight

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Chandrayaan-1 landed on the Moon | Chandrayaan-1 was an orbiter. Its Moon Impact Probe (MIP) impacted the surface (not soft-landed) |
| Apollo missions found water on the Moon | They found dry rocks. Water was confirmed by Chandrayaan-1's M³ instrument (2008) and NASA's LCROSS (2009) |
| The "dark side" of the Moon is always dark | The far side gets equal sunlight. "Dark" means "unseen from Earth" (due to tidal locking) |
| Only government agencies have landed on the Moon | Private companies have also achieved landings (Intuitive Machines' Odysseus, 2024) |

## Topic 47: Mars Exploration — International Missions

### Learn This Topic

#### Why Mars?
Mars is the most Earth-like planet and the prime candidate for finding evidence of past life beyond Earth. It once had liquid water, a thicker atmosphere, and possibly habitable conditions.

#### Key Mars Facts
- Distance from Sun: ~228 million km (1.52 AU)
- Distance from Earth: 55–400 million km (varies with orbits)
- Diameter: ~6,791 km (about half of Earth's)
- Day length: ~24 hours 37 minutes (very close to Earth!)
- Year: ~687 Earth days
- Gravity: ~38% of Earth's
- Temperature: -60°C average (range -140°C to +20°C)
- Atmosphere: ~95% CO₂, very thin (~1% of Earth's pressure)
- Two small moons: **Phobos** and **Deimos**

#### Major Mars Missions

| Mission | Country | Year | Achievement |
|---------|---------|------|-------------|
| **Mariner 4** | USA | 1965 | First successful Mars flyby; first close-up photos |
| **Mars 3** | USSR | 1971 | First Mars landing (transmitted for ~20 seconds before failing) |
| **Viking 1 & 2** | USA | 1976 | First successful Mars landers; searched for life (inconclusive results). Viking 1 operated until 1982 |
| **Mars Pathfinder** | USA | 1997 | Carried **Sojourner** — first Mars rover (size of a microwave) |
| **Mars Odyssey** | USA | 2001 | Still orbiting! Found evidence of water ice below surface |
| **Mars Express** | ESA | 2003 | Confirmed water ice at poles. Still operational |
| **Spirit** | USA | 2004–2010 | MER-A rover in Gusev Crater |
| **Opportunity** | USA | 2004–2018 | MER-B rover. Lasted **14+ years** (designed for 90 days!). Travelled 45.16 km — a Mars driving record. Lost during global dust storm |
| **Phoenix** | USA | 2008 | Landed near north pole; confirmed water ice in soil |
| **Curiosity** | USA | 2012–present | MSL rover in Gale Crater. Car-sized. Found ancient lake bed, organic molecules, evidence of past habitable conditions |
| **MAVEN** | USA | 2014 | Studying Mars atmosphere loss |
| **MOM/Mangalyaan** | **India** | **2014** | India's first Mars mission! First Asian nation at Mars. First to succeed on first attempt. Cost: ~$73 million (less than the movie Gravity!) |
| **InSight** | USA | 2018–2022 | First to study Mars interior; detected "marsquakes" |
| **Tianwen-1 / Zhurong** | China | 2021 | Chinese Mars orbiter, lander, and rover. Zhurong explored Utopia Planitia |
| **Perseverance** | USA | 2021–present | Searching for ancient microbial life in Jezero Crater. Collecting samples for future return |
| **Ingenuity** | USA | 2021–2024 | **First helicopter on another planet!** Made 72 flights |

#### The "Mars Curse"
Roughly **half of all Mars missions have failed** — Mars is notoriously difficult to reach. Common failure modes: trajectory errors, entry/descent/landing failures, communication loss.

#### Future Mars Plans
- **Mars Sample Return** (NASA/ESA): Planned mission to bring Perseverance's collected samples back to Earth
- **ExoMars Rosalind Franklin** (ESA): Rover planned for 2028 launch, will drill up to 2 m below surface
- **Human missions**: NASA's long-term goal is crewed Mars missions (2030s–2040s), building on Artemis experience
- Various concepts for permanent Mars habitats, terraforming (highly theoretical)

### Key Facts
- Viking 1 and 2 (1976): first successful Mars landers
- Opportunity (2004–2018): lasted 14+ years, drove 45 km on Mars
- Curiosity (2012–present): found evidence of ancient habitable conditions
- MOM/Mangalyaan (India, 2014): first Asian nation at Mars, first to succeed on first attempt
- Perseverance (2021–present): collecting samples for future return to Earth
- Ingenuity: first helicopter on another planet (72 flights)
- Tianwen-1/Zhurong (China, 2021): China's first Mars landing
- About half of all Mars missions have failed ("Mars Curse")

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Mars is the closest planet to Earth | Venus is sometimes closer. Mars is the most ACCESSIBLE for landing (Venus has crushing atmosphere/temperature) |
| Mars is red because of heat | Mars is red because of iron oxide (rust) on its surface |
| We've already found life on Mars | No life has been confirmed. We've found evidence that Mars was once habitable (water, organic molecules) |
| India's MOM discovered life on Mars | MOM studied the atmosphere and surface, including looking for methane. It did NOT find life |

## Topic 48: Space Debris and Space Sustainability

### Learn This Topic

#### What Is Space Debris?
Space debris (also called orbital debris or space junk) consists of non-functional human-made objects in Earth orbit — spent rocket stages, dead satellites, fragments from collisions and explosions, paint flecks, nuts, bolts, and more.

#### Scale of the Problem (2025–2026 estimates)

| Size Category | Estimated Count |
|---------------|----------------|
| > 10 cm (tracked) | ~45,900 objects regularly tracked |
| 1–10 cm | ~900,000 |
| < 1 cm | ~128 million |
| Active satellites | ~11,000 (of the tracked total) |
| **Total mass in orbit** | ~8,000+ metric tonnes |

Even tiny debris is dangerous because of extreme speeds: objects in LEO travel at ~7–8 km/s. A 1 cm piece of debris has the kinetic energy of a hand grenade!

#### Kessler Syndrome
- Proposed by NASA scientist **Donald Kessler** in 1978
- A theoretical cascade: collisions create debris → more debris → more collisions → runaway chain reaction
- Could make certain orbits unusable for generations
- Two LEO altitude bands (~900–1,000 km and ~1,500 km) may already be past critical density
- Not a sudden event — develops gradually over years/decades

#### Major Debris-Creating Events

**1. Chinese ASAT Test (January 2007)**
- China destroyed its own Fengyun-1C weather satellite at ~865 km altitude
- **Largest single debris-creating event in history**
- Created 2,300+ tracked fragments, 35,000+ pieces ≥1 cm, ~1 million ≥1 mm
- Many fragments will remain in orbit for decades

**2. Cosmos-Iridium Collision (February 2009)**
- First major accidental satellite collision
- Dead Russian Kosmos-2251 hit active US Iridium 33 at ~800 km, relative speed ~11.7 km/s
- Created 2,000+ large debris fragments
- Demonstrated Kessler syndrome risk is real

**3. India's Mission Shakti (March 27, 2019)**
- India's DRDO conducted an **Anti-Satellite (ASAT) test** — destroyed India's own Microsat-R satellite
- Used a ground-based missile (PDV Mark-II interceptor)
- India became the **4th country** with ASAT capability (after USA, Russia, China)
- Test was conducted in **low orbit** (~300 km) so most debris would decay quickly
- 270+ debris pieces tracked; most re-entered within weeks/months
- PM Modi announced this as "Mission Shakti"

**4. Russia's Kosmos-1408 Destruction (November 2021)**
- Russia destroyed an old Kosmos-1408 satellite at ~450 km
- Created 1,500+ trackable debris pieces
- ISS crew had to shelter in capsules as debris passed nearby
- Widely condemned internationally

#### Space Debris Tracking
- **US Space Surveillance Network**: Primary tracking system, catalogues all objects > 10 cm in LEO
- **ESA Space Debris Office**: European tracking and monitoring
- **ISRO IS4OM**: ISRO System for Safe and Sustainable Space Operations Management — India's network for tracking debris and providing collision avoidance warnings

#### Mitigation Guidelines
- **Passivation**: At end of life, drain remaining fuel and depressurise tanks to prevent explosions
- **25-year rule**: Guidelines recommend that LEO satellites de-orbit within 25 years of mission end (new proposals suggest 5 years)
- **Graveyard orbit**: GEO satellites are boosted ~300 km above GEO to a "graveyard" orbit
- **Collision avoidance manoeuvres**: Active satellites dodge debris when conjunction warnings are issued
- ISS has **Whipple shields** (multi-layer protection) and regularly performs debris avoidance manoeuvres

#### Active Debris Removal (ADR) Concepts
- **Nets and harpoons** to capture debris
- **Robotic arms** to grab and de-orbit
- **Lasers** to alter debris orbits
- **ESA's ClearSpace-1**: Planned first active debris removal mission
- **ISRO** has been working on debris mitigation guidelines and tracking

### Key Facts
- ~45,900 objects tracked in orbit; ~128 million pieces < 1 cm
- Kessler syndrome (1978): cascade theory where collisions create runaway debris
- Chinese ASAT (2007): largest single debris event
- Cosmos-Iridium collision (2009): first major accidental satellite collision
- Mission Shakti (2019): India's ASAT test; India became 4th country with ASAT capability
- Even 1 cm debris can be as destructive as a hand grenade at orbital speeds
- 25-year de-orbit guideline for LEO satellites
- ISRO's IS4OM tracks debris and provides collision warnings

### Important Terms
- **ASAT**: Anti-Satellite weapon
- **Kessler Syndrome**: Cascading collision scenario
- **Passivation**: Safely draining/venting a dead satellite's fuel
- **Graveyard Orbit**: Disposal orbit above GEO
- **Whipple Shield**: Multi-layer debris protection on ISS
- **IS4OM**: ISRO System for Safe and Sustainable Space Operations Management
- **Conjunction**: Close approach between two objects in orbit

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Space debris is natural (rocks, etc.) | Space debris is specifically HUMAN-MADE. Natural objects (meteoroids) are different |
| Debris only comes from satellite collisions | Most debris comes from explosions (rocket stages), deliberate destruction (ASAT), and fragmentation — not just collisions |
| Mission Shakti created permanent debris | India deliberately tested at low altitude (~300 km) so debris would decay quickly via atmospheric drag |
| We can easily clean up space debris | Active debris removal is extremely difficult and expensive. No operational cleanup mission has been conducted yet |

## Topic 49: Satellite Applications and Space Technology

### Learn This Topic

#### How Satellites Serve Society
Satellites are not just tools for scientists — they affect everyday life in ways most people don't realise. India's space programme was specifically designed around applications that benefit society.

#### Communication Satellites
- Relay TV signals, phone calls, internet data
- India's INSAT and GSAT/CMS series serve communication needs
- **VSAT** (Very Small Aperture Terminal) networks connect remote areas
- **DTH** (Direct-to-Home) TV uses communication satellites
- **Tele-education**: ISRO's EDUSAT (GSAT-3, 2004) was the first dedicated educational satellite in the world
- **Telemedicine**: Satellites connect remote health centres to specialist hospitals
- Disaster communication: When ground infrastructure fails (earthquakes, floods), satellite phones remain operational

#### Navigation Satellites
- **GPS** (USA): Global Positioning System — 24+ satellites in MEO (~20,200 km). First operational 1995
- **GLONASS** (Russia): Russia's global navigation system
- **Galileo** (ESA/EU): European system, higher precision for civilians
- **BeiDou** (China): Chinese global system, completed 2020
- **NavIC** (India): Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System. 7 satellites. Coverage: India + ~1,500 km around. Dual frequency (restricted and civilian)

**How GPS/Navigation works:** At least 4 satellites must be visible. Each satellite sends its position and time. Your receiver calculates the time delay of each signal, computes distance, and triangulates your position.

**Applications:**
- Vehicle navigation, mapping, ride-sharing apps
- Aviation (flight paths), shipping (vessel tracking)
- Precision agriculture (tractor guidance)
- Military applications (guided weapons, troop tracking)
- Surveying and construction
- Emergency response (locating people in distress)
- Timing for banking, telecom, power grid synchronisation

#### Remote Sensing
Using sensors on satellites to observe Earth from space without physical contact.

**Types of Remote Sensing:**
- **Optical**: Visible light cameras (like taking photos from space)
- **Infrared (IR)**: Detects heat; useful for fire detection, vegetation health
- **Microwave/Radar (SAR)**: Works through clouds and at night; can see surface features, measure terrain

**India's Remote Sensing:**
- India's **IRS** (Indian Remote Sensing) series is one of the world's largest civilian remote sensing constellations
- **Cartosat**: High-resolution mapping
- **Resourcesat**: Agriculture, water resources
- **RISAT**: Radar imaging (works through clouds)
- **NISAR** (NASA-ISRO SAR): Joint mission for Earth observation with both L-band and S-band radar

**Applications:**
- **Agriculture**: Crop monitoring, yield prediction, soil moisture, drought assessment. Indian National Crop Forecast Centre uses satellite data
- **Forestry**: Forest cover mapping, deforestation detection
- **Water resources**: Groundwater mapping, reservoir monitoring
- **Urban planning**: City growth monitoring, infrastructure planning
- **Disaster management**: Flood mapping, earthquake damage assessment, cyclone tracking, forest fire detection. ISRO provides **rapid response maps** during disasters
- **Mining and geology**: Mineral exploration, geological mapping
- **Coastal zone management**: Erosion monitoring, mangrove mapping
- **Potential Fishing Zones (PFZ)**: ISRO provides daily maps of likely fish locations using ocean temperature data, benefiting fishermen

#### Weather Satellites
- Provide real-time imagery for weather forecasting
- **Geostationary** weather satellites give continuous coverage of one region
- **Polar-orbiting** satellites provide global coverage twice daily
- India's INSAT-3D/3DR are advanced weather satellites
- IMD (India Meteorological Department) relies heavily on satellite data
- Cyclone tracking: satellite-based early warnings have reduced cyclone deaths from thousands to dozens in India

#### Space Technology Spinoffs
Technology developed for space has many everyday applications:

| Space Technology | Everyday Application |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Water purification for space | Clean drinking water systems |
| Memory foam (NASA) | Mattresses, pillows, shoe insoles |
| Scratch-resistant lenses | Eyeglass coatings |
| Infrared thermometers | Medical temperature measurement |
| Cordless power tools | Black & Decker developed for Apollo |
| Freeze-dried food | Camping food, emergency rations |
| CMOS image sensors | Smartphone cameras |
| Space suit materials | Firefighter protective gear |
| Satellite communication | Internet, TV, phone |
| GPS technology | Navigation in cars and phones |

#### Disaster Management — Space as a Lifesaver
ISRO's **Decision Support Centre** provides satellite-based information during disasters:
- Flood inundation maps
- Cyclone tracking and prediction
- Earthquake damage assessment
- Forest fire alerts
- Drought monitoring
- Landslide vulnerability mapping
- The **International Charter "Space and Major Disasters"** allows countries to request satellite data during emergencies

### Key Facts
- GPS needs at least 4 satellites visible to determine position
- NavIC is India's regional navigation system (7 satellites, covers India + 1,500 km)
- India's IRS is one of the world's largest civilian remote sensing constellations
- EDUSAT (GSAT-3, 2004) was the world's first dedicated educational satellite
- ISRO provides Potential Fishing Zone maps to help fishermen
- Satellite-based cyclone tracking has dramatically reduced deaths in India
- Many everyday technologies (memory foam, smartphone cameras, scratch-resistant lenses) originated from space research

### Important Terms
- **GPS**: Global Positioning System (USA)
- **NavIC/IRNSS**: Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System
- **GNSS**: Global Navigation Satellite System (umbrella term for GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, NavIC)
- **SAR**: Synthetic Aperture Radar
- **Remote Sensing**: Observing Earth from space without physical contact
- **GIS**: Geographic Information System (used with satellite data for mapping)
- **PFZ**: Potential Fishing Zone
- **DTH**: Direct-to-Home (satellite TV)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| GPS is the only navigation system | There are 5 global/regional systems: GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou, NavIC |
| NavIC is global like GPS | NavIC is REGIONAL — covers India and ~1,500 km around it. GPS is global |
| Remote sensing requires contact | "Remote" sensing means observation from a distance — no physical contact needed |
| Satellites only help scientists | Satellites affect daily life: TV, weather forecasts, maps, ride-sharing, farming, disaster warnings |
| ISRO only does space missions | ISRO's applications (agriculture, disaster management, communication, education) affect millions of Indians daily |

## Topic 50: World Space Week and the "Rocket Revolution" Theme

### Learn This Topic

#### What Is World Space Week?
- **Declared by**: United Nations General Assembly on **December 6, 1999**
- **Dates**: **October 4–10** every year
- **Significance of dates**:
  - **October 4, 1957**: Launch of **Sputnik 1** — first human-made satellite (USSR)
  - **October 10, 1967**: Signing of the **Outer Space Treaty** (Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space)
- **Described as**: The **largest annual space event in the world**
- **Participation**: Celebrated in **95+ nations** with 8,000+ events
- **Coordinated by**: **World Space Week Association (WSWA)**, a non-governmental, non-profit organisation, in collaboration with the United Nations
- **India coordinator**: SPACE India serves as the Regional Coordinator for India

#### World Space Week 2026
- **Theme**: **"Rocket Revolution"**
- **Context**: Celebrating the transformation in rocket technology — from early sounding rockets to reusable launch systems
- **ISRO/URSC event**: Organised by U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC), ISRO, Bengaluru
- **Location**: Lotus English Medium High School, Pulakunta, Hindupur, Sri Sathya Sai District, Andhra Pradesh
- **Date**: October 6, 2026

#### Recent WSW Themes

| Year | Theme |
|------|-------|
| 2019 | The Moon: Gateway to the Stars |
| 2020 | Satellites Improve Life |
| 2021 | Women in Space |
| 2022 | Space and Sustainability |
| 2023 | Space and Entrepreneurship |
| 2024 | Space & Climate Change |
| 2025 | Living In Space |
| **2026** | **Rocket Revolution** |

#### The "Rocket Revolution" — What It Means

**The Beginning of Rocketry:**
- **Ancient China**: Invented gunpowder rockets (~13th century). Used as weapons and fireworks
- **Tipu Sultan**: The **Mysorean rockets** (18th century, India) were among the first iron-cased rockets used as weapons. The British studied captured Mysorean rockets and developed the Congreve rocket
- **Konstantin Tsiolkovsky** (Russia, 1903): Theorised multi-stage rockets and space travel
- **Robert Goddard** (USA, 1926): Launched the **first liquid-fuelled rocket**
- **Hermann Oberth** (Germany): Theoretical foundations for spaceflight
- **Wernher von Braun** (Germany → USA): V-2 rocket (first to reach space, 1944), later designed Saturn V for Apollo

**The Space Age Revolution:**
- **V-2** (1944): First rocket to reach space (~100 km)
- **R-7** (1957): Soviet rocket that launched Sputnik — world's first ICBM turned space launcher
- **Saturn V** (1967–1973): Most powerful rocket ever flown (until recent years). Carried Apollo astronauts to the Moon. Height: ~111 m, thrust: ~34 million N
- **Space Shuttle** (1981–2011): First partially reusable launch system
- **Ariane** (ESA, 1979–present): Europe's workhorse launcher family

**India's Rocket Revolution:**
- **Nike-Apache** (1963): First rocket from Thumba (American-made)
- **Rohini sounding rockets** (1967 onwards): India's first indigenous rockets
- **SLV-3** (1980): India's first satellite launch vehicle (Kalam as project director)
- **ASLV** (1987–1994): Augmented SLV, limited success
- **PSLV** (1993–present): India's workhorse — most reliable launch vehicle with 50+ consecutive successes, launched 104 satellites in one mission (2017)
- **GSLV** (2001–present): Geosynchronous launcher; India mastered indigenous cryogenic engine (2014)
- **LVM3** (2017–present): India's heaviest launcher. Launched Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, and OneWeb commercial satellites
- **SSLV** (2022–present): Small Satellite Launch Vehicle for quick, low-cost launches
- **Future**: Semi-cryogenic engine (SCE-200), Reusable Launch Vehicle (RLV) demonstrator, Next Generation Launch Vehicle (NGLV)

**The Modern Rocket Revolution:**
- **SpaceX Falcon 9**: First orbital-class rocket to land and fly again. Revolutionised costs through reusability
- **SpaceX Falcon Heavy**: Most powerful operational rocket (until Starship)
- **SpaceX Starship**: Largest and most powerful rocket ever built. Fully reusable design
- **Blue Origin New Shepard / New Glenn**: Reusable rockets for tourism and orbital missions
- **Rocket Lab Electron**: Small satellite launcher with reusable first stage
- **Ariane 6** (ESA, 2024): Next-generation European launcher
- **Reusability**: The key revolution — landing and reusing rocket boosters has dramatically reduced launch costs (from ~$10,000/kg to ~$2,700/kg with Falcon 9)

#### The Outer Space Treaty (1967)
- Full name: Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space, Including the Moon and Other Celestial Bodies
- Signed: October 10, 1967 (the date WSW ends)
- Key principles:
  - Space is free for exploration by all nations
  - No nation can claim sovereignty over a celestial body
  - No nuclear weapons or WMDs in space
  - Astronauts are "envoys of mankind"
  - Nations are responsible for their space activities (including private companies)
  - Nations must avoid harmful contamination of space and celestial bodies

#### Sputnik 1 — The Spark That Started It All
- Launched: **October 4, 1957** by the Soviet Union (the date WSW begins)
- World's **first human-made satellite**
- Mass: 83.6 kg, diameter: 58 cm (about the size of a beach ball)
- Orbited Earth every 96 minutes at ~29,000 km/h
- Transmitted a "beep-beep" radio signal for 21 days
- Burned up on re-entry on January 4, 1958
- Launched on a modified **R-7** ICBM from Baikonur Cosmodrome
- Triggered the **Space Race** between USA and USSR
- Led to the creation of NASA (1958) and the entire modern space age

### Key Facts
- World Space Week: October 4–10 (Sputnik launch → Outer Space Treaty signing)
- Declared by UN General Assembly on December 6, 1999
- 2026 theme: "Rocket Revolution"
- Sputnik 1 (USSR, 1957): first satellite, 83.6 kg, transmitted for 21 days
- Outer Space Treaty (1967): no national sovereignty in space, no weapons of mass destruction
- India's rocket journey: Nike-Apache (1963) → Rohini → SLV-3 (1980) → PSLV → GSLV → LVM3
- Reusable rockets (SpaceX Falcon 9) have revolutionised launch costs
- Tipu Sultan's Mysorean rockets were among the first iron-cased military rockets
- Robert Goddard launched the first liquid-fuelled rocket (1926)
- Saturn V was the most powerful rocket of the Apollo era

### Important Terms
- **WSW**: World Space Week (October 4–10)
- **WSWA**: World Space Week Association
- **Outer Space Treaty**: 1967 UN treaty governing space activities
- **Sputnik 1**: First human-made satellite (1957)
- **Reusability**: Landing and reflying rocket stages to reduce costs
- **ICBM**: Intercontinental Ballistic Missile (R-7, the rocket that launched Sputnik, was one)
- **RLV**: Reusable Launch Vehicle
- **NGLV**: Next Generation Launch Vehicle (ISRO's planned future rocket)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| WSW celebrates only Sputnik | It also commemorates the Outer Space Treaty (Oct 10, 1967) |
| The USA launched the first satellite | The USSR launched Sputnik 1. The first US satellite was Explorer 1 (January 31, 1958) |
| Rockets were invented in Europe | Gunpowder rockets were invented in China (13th century). India's Tipu Sultan used advanced rockets in the 18th century |
| All rockets are expendable (single-use) | SpaceX pioneered routine orbital rocket reuse. This is the core of the "Rocket Revolution" |
