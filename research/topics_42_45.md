## Topic 42: Space Stations and Life in Space

### Learn This Topic

#### What Is a Space Station?
A space station is a large spacecraft that remains in orbit for an extended period, providing a home and workplace for astronauts. Unlike a capsule (which is used for short trips), a station is designed for long-duration stays — weeks, months, or even over a year.

#### Major Space Stations in History

| Station | Country/Agency | Years | Key Facts |
|---------|---------------|-------|-----------|
| **Salyut 1** | Soviet Union | 1971 | World's first space station. Crew of Soyuz 11 (3 cosmonauts) tragically died during re-entry due to cabin depressurisation |
| **Skylab** | USA (NASA) | 1973–1979 | First American space station. Launched using a modified Saturn V. Longest stay: 84 days (Skylab 4). Fell back to Earth in 1979, scattering debris over Western Australia |
| **Salyut 6 & 7** | Soviet Union | 1977–1991 | Second-generation Soviet stations. Hosted international visitors including Rakesh Sharma (India, 1984) |
| **Mir** | Soviet Union / Russia | 1986–2001 | First **modular** space station (assembled in orbit from multiple modules). Record: Valeri Polyakov spent **437 days** continuously on Mir (1994–95) — longest single spaceflight ever. De-orbited in 2001 |
| **ISS** | International (15 nations) | 1998–present | Largest structure ever built in space. Continuously inhabited since **November 2, 2000** — over 25 years! |
| **Tiangong** | China (CNSA) | 2021–present | China's modular space station. Completed in 2022 with 3 modules (Tianhe, Wentian, Mengtian). Permanently crewed since June 2022 |

#### The International Space Station (ISS)

**Key Facts:**
- **Orbit altitude:** ~400 km (250 miles) above Earth
- **Speed:** ~28,000 km/h (17,500 mph) — circles Earth every ~90 minutes
- **Size:** About the size of a football field (~109 m end to end)
- **Mass:** ~420,000 kg
- **Pressurised volume:** ~916 m³ (about the size of a 6-bedroom house)
- **Solar panels:** 8 large arrays generating ~240 kW of power
- **Laboratories:** Multiple — US Destiny lab, ESA Columbus lab, JAXA Kibo lab, Russian modules
- **Visitors:** Over 280 people from 26 countries have visited
- **Spacewalks:** Over 259 conducted since 1998
- **Assembly:** First module (Russian Zarya) launched November 1998. Construction involved over 40 assembly flights

**Partner Agencies:**
- NASA (USA)
- Roscosmos (Russia)
- ESA (Europe)
- JAXA (Japan)
- CSA (Canada)

#### China's Tiangong Space Station

- **Modules:** Tianhe (core, 2021), Wentian (lab, Jul 2022), Mengtian (lab, Oct 2022)
- **Mass:** ~100,000 kg (about 1/4 the mass of ISS)
- **Crew:** Normally 3 (up to 6 during handovers)
- **Orbit:** 340–450 km, inclination ~41.5°
- **Notable:** Uses ion thrusters for station-keeping — a first for crewed spacecraft
- **Experiments:** First full rice growth cycle in space (2022), cold atom physics reaching 100 picokelvin
- **Expansion:** Plans to grow from 3 to 6 modules (~180 tonnes) by ~2027
- **Xuntian telescope:** A co-orbiting space telescope (2 m mirror, 300× Hubble's field of view) planned for 2027, will dock with Tiangong for servicing

#### Life on a Space Station

**Daily Routine (ISS):**
- Astronauts work on a roughly 24-hour schedule aligned to UTC
- ~6.5 hours sleep, ~6.5 hours scheduled work, ~2.5 hours exercise, rest for meals and personal time
- **Exercise is mandatory** — about 2–2.5 hours per day to combat muscle and bone loss from microgravity
- Equipment: treadmill (with harness to hold you down), stationary bicycle (CEVIS), resistance exercise device (ARED)

**Eating in Space:**
- Food is mostly dehydrated, thermostabilised, or irradiated
- Water is added to rehydrate food
- No refrigerator (traditionally) — food doesn't spoil because it's specially preserved
- Fresh fruits and vegetables are rare treats (brought on resupply missions)
- Crumbs are dangerous — they float and can clog air filters or get into eyes

**Sleeping in Space:**
- Astronauts sleep in small crew quarters, zipped into sleeping bags attached to the wall
- There is no "up" or "down" — you can sleep in any orientation
- Earplugs are common (station equipment is noisy)

**Hygiene:**
- No showers — astronauts use wet wipes and rinseless shampoo
- Water is precious — ISS recycles about 90% of water (including from sweat and urine!)
- Toothpaste is edible (swallowed, not spat out)

**Health Effects of Microgravity:**
- Bone density loss (~1–2% per month without exercise)
- Muscle atrophy
- Fluid shifts to the head (puffy face, "bird legs")
- Vision changes (some astronauts need glasses after long stays)
- Radiation exposure (higher than on Earth due to thinner protection)
- Immune system changes

**Experiments on ISS:**
- Materials science, protein crystal growth, combustion, fluid physics
- Biology: growing plants (lettuce, radishes, chili peppers), studying bacteria
- Human physiology: effects of long-duration spaceflight
- Earth observation
- Technology demonstrations

### Key Facts
- Salyut 1 (1971) was the world's first space station
- Mir (1986–2001) was the first modular station; Valeri Polyakov's 437-day record was set there
- ISS has been continuously inhabited since November 2, 2000 (~400 km altitude, ~90 min orbit)
- ISS is a collaboration of NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA (15 nations)
- Over 280 people from 26 countries have visited the ISS
- China's Tiangong was completed in 2022 (3 modules: Tianhe, Wentian, Mengtian)
- Astronauts must exercise ~2.5 hours/day to combat bone and muscle loss
- ISS recycles ~90% of water, including from urine

### Important Terms
- **EVA**: Extra-Vehicular Activity (spacewalk)
- **Microgravity**: Near-weightlessness experienced in orbit (not true zero gravity)
- **Pressurised volume**: Habitable interior space of a station
- **Modular**: Built from separate modules launched and connected in orbit
- **Station-keeping**: Small thruster firings to maintain orbit altitude

#### Space Suits
- A space suit is essentially a **one-person spacecraft** — it provides oxygen, removes CO₂, maintains pressure and temperature
- **Intravehicular (IVA) suits**: Worn inside the spacecraft during launch/re-entry (e.g., Russian **Sokol** suit, SpaceX suits). Lighter, less bulky
- **Extravehicular (EVA) suits**: Worn for spacewalks. Much bulkier with multiple protective layers. NASA's current EVA suit is the **EMU** (Extravehicular Mobility Unit), weighing ~127 kg on Earth
- Suits have ~14 layers including thermal insulation, micrometeorite protection, and pressure bladder
- **Visor**: Gold-coated to reflect harmful solar radiation
- Suits operate at lower pressure than the station (~4.3 psi vs ~14.7 psi), so astronauts must "pre-breathe" pure oxygen before EVAs to avoid decompression sickness (like deep-sea divers)
- India is developing its own space suit for **Gaganyaan**; ISRO's humanoid robot **Vyommitra** has tested life-support systems

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Astronauts on ISS experience zero gravity | They experience MICROGRAVITY. Gravity at ISS altitude is about 90% of surface gravity — they float because they're in free fall (orbit) |
| ISS is the only space station | China's Tiangong has been operational since 2021. Historical stations include Salyut, Skylab, and Mir |
| ISS was built by the USA | It's a 15-nation partnership. Russia provided critical early modules and crew transport |
| Tiangong is tiny | At ~100 tonnes it's substantial — about 1/4 ISS mass, and plans exist to double its size |

## Topic 43: Human Spaceflight — History and Key Missions

### Learn This Topic

#### The Space Race (1957–1969)
The competition between the USA and the Soviet Union to achieve firsts in space was driven by Cold War rivalry. The Soviets led early, achieving most "firsts," while the US ultimately achieved the Moon landing.

#### Key Firsts in Human Spaceflight

| Date | Achievement | Person/Mission | Country |
|------|-------------|---------------|---------|
| **12 Apr 1961** | First human in space | **Yuri Gagarin** (Vostok 1) — 108 minutes, single orbit | Soviet Union |
| 5 May 1961 | First American in space (suborbital) | **Alan Shepard** (Freedom 7) | USA |
| 20 Feb 1962 | First American to orbit Earth | **John Glenn** (Mercury-Atlas 6) | USA |
| **16 Jun 1963** | First woman in space | **Valentina Tereshkova** (Vostok 6) | Soviet Union |
| **18 Mar 1965** | First spacewalk (EVA) | **Alexei Leonov** (Voskhod 2) — nearly couldn't get back in due to suit inflation | Soviet Union |
| 3 Jun 1965 | First American spacewalk | **Ed White** (Gemini 4) | USA |
| 15 Dec 1965 | First space rendezvous | Gemini 6A and 7 | USA |
| 16 Mar 1966 | First space docking | Gemini 8 (with Agena vehicle) — commanded by Neil Armstrong | USA |
| **21 Dec 1968** | First humans to orbit the Moon | **Apollo 8** (Borman, Lovell, Anders) | USA |
| **20 Jul 1969** | **First Moon landing** | **Apollo 11** — Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon; Michael Collins orbited above | USA |

#### The Apollo Programme
- Total Moon landings: **6** (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17)
- Apollo 13 (1970): Famous "successful failure" — oxygen tank explosion, crew returned safely
- Apollo 15, 16, 17 used the **Lunar Roving Vehicle** (Moon buggy)
- Last human on the Moon: **Eugene Cernan** (Apollo 17, December 1972)
- Total Moon walkers: **12 people** (all American men)
- Total lunar samples returned: ~382 kg

#### The Space Shuttle Era (1981–2011)
- NASA's reusable spacecraft — launched like a rocket, landed like a glider
- First flight: **STS-1** (April 12, 1981)
- Total missions: **135**
- Five orbiters: Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis, Endeavour
- **Challenger disaster** (January 28, 1986): Broke apart 73 seconds after launch; 7 crew killed. Cause: O-ring seal failure in solid rocket booster in cold weather
- **Columbia disaster** (February 1, 2003): Broke apart during re-entry; 7 crew killed. Cause: foam insulation from external tank damaged heat shield tiles during launch. **Kalpana Chawla** (Indian-American astronaut) was among the crew
- Programme retired in 2011

#### India's Human in Space — Rakesh Sharma
- **Wing Commander Rakesh Sharma** became the first (and so far only) Indian citizen in space
- Flew aboard **Soyuz T-11** to Soviet station **Salyut 7** on **April 3, 1984**
- Spent **7 days 21 hours** in space
- Part of the Soviet Intercosmos programme
- When PM Indira Gandhi asked how India looked from space, he famously replied: **"Saarē jahāṅ se acchā"** (the best in the whole world) — a line from the patriotic poem by Muhammad Iqbal
- Conducted Earth observation and yoga exercises in space

#### First Chinese Astronaut
- **Yang Liwei** flew aboard **Shenzhou 5** on **October 15, 2003**
- China became the **third nation** (after USSR/Russia and USA) to independently launch a human into space

#### Commercial Crew Era (2020s)
- **SpaceX Crew Dragon**: First operational commercial crew transport to ISS (2020). Demo-2 mission (May 2020) carried Doug Hurley and Bob Behnken — first crewed launch from US soil since Shuttle retirement
- **Boeing Starliner**: CST-100 Starliner, also under NASA's Commercial Crew Program; development has been slower than SpaceX
- **Space tourism**: Dennis Tito was the first space tourist (2001, paid ~$20 million for ISS visit)

#### Artemis Programme — Return to the Moon
- NASA's programme to return humans to the Moon
- **Artemis I** (November 2022): Uncrewed test flight of SLS rocket and Orion spacecraft around the Moon
- **Artemis II** (April 2026): First crewed Orion flight around the Moon (~10 days). Crew: NASA's Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and CSA's Jeremy Hansen. Set record for greatest distance humans have traveled from Earth (252,756 miles)
- **Artemis III** (planned 2027): Redesigned as LEO demonstration for rendezvous/docking with commercial lander
- **Artemis IV** (targeted 2028): First planned lunar landing of the programme
- **Lunar Gateway**: Planned small space station in lunar orbit
- **Human Landing Systems**: SpaceX Starship HLS and Blue Origin lander

#### Notable Records
- Longest single spaceflight: **Valeri Polyakov** — 437 days (Mir, 1994–95)
- Most time in space (cumulative): **Gennady Padalka** — 878 days
- Most spacewalks: **Anatoly Solovyev** — 16 EVAs
- First person to orbit Earth: Yuri Gagarin
- Oldest person in space: William Shatner (90 years, 2021, Blue Origin suborbital)
- Youngest orbital astronaut: Hayley Arceneaux (29 years, Inspiration4, 2021)

### Key Facts
- Yuri Gagarin: first human in space (12 Apr 1961, Vostok 1)
- Valentina Tereshkova: first woman in space (16 Jun 1963)
- Neil Armstrong: first person on the Moon (20 Jul 1969, Apollo 11)
- 12 people have walked on the Moon (all on Apollo missions, 1969–1972)
- Rakesh Sharma: first Indian in space (3 Apr 1984, Soyuz T-11 to Salyut 7)
- Yang Liwei: first Chinese astronaut (15 Oct 2003, Shenzhou 5)
- Space Shuttle flew 135 missions (1981–2011); Challenger (1986) and Columbia (2003) disasters
- Kalpana Chawla died in the Columbia disaster (2003)
- Artemis II (April 2026) was the first crewed flight beyond LEO since Apollo

### Important Terms
- **Cosmonaut**: Russian term for astronaut
- **Taikonaut**: Chinese term for astronaut (from 太空 tàikōng = space)
- **Vyomanaut**: Indian term for astronaut (from Sanskrit vyoma = sky/space) — to be used for Gaganyaan crew
- **EVA**: Extra-Vehicular Activity (spacewalk)
- **SLS**: Space Launch System (NASA's heavy-lift rocket for Artemis)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| Neil Armstrong was the first human in space | He was the first on the MOON. Yuri Gagarin was the first in space (1961) |
| The Space Shuttle went to the Moon | The Shuttle only went to low Earth orbit. Only Apollo went to the Moon |
| Kalpana Chawla was an Indian astronaut | She was an INDIAN-AMERICAN astronaut (US citizen of Indian origin). Rakesh Sharma is the first Indian citizen in space |
| No one has been to the Moon since the 1960s | The last Moon landing was Apollo 17 in December 1972, not the 1960s |
| Rakesh Sharma flew on an Indian spacecraft | He flew on a Soviet Soyuz spacecraft. India's own human spaceflight (Gaganyaan) is upcoming |

## Topic 44: NASA and the US Space Programme

### Learn This Topic

#### About NASA
- **Full name**: National Aeronautics and Space Administration
- **Founded**: July 29, 1958 (succeeded NACA — National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics)
- **Headquarters**: Washington, D.C.
- **Purpose**: Aeronautics research, space exploration, Earth science, space technology

#### Key NASA Centres

| Centre | Location | Role |
|--------|----------|------|
| **Kennedy Space Center (KSC)** | Cape Canaveral, Florida | Launch operations (all Shuttle, SLS/Artemis launches) |
| **Johnson Space Center (JSC)** | Houston, Texas | Mission Control, astronaut training, human spaceflight operations. "Houston, we have a problem" comes from here |
| **Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)** | Pasadena, California | Robotic planetary missions (Mars rovers, Voyager, Cassini). Managed by Caltech |
| **Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC)** | Greenbelt, Maryland | Earth science, Hubble, JWST operations |
| **Marshall Space Flight Center** | Huntsville, Alabama | Propulsion, SLS development |
| **Ames Research Center** | Moffett Field, California | Aeronautics, astrobiology |

#### Major NASA Programmes and Missions

**Mercury (1958–1963):** First US human spaceflight programme. Single-astronaut capsule. Alan Shepard (first American in space), John Glenn (first American to orbit).

**Gemini (1965–1966):** Two-astronaut missions. Practised rendezvous, docking, spacewalks — skills needed for Apollo.

**Apollo (1967–1972):** Moon landing programme. Saturn V rocket. 6 successful Moon landings. Greatest achievement in human exploration history.

**Skylab (1973–1979):** First US space station.

**Space Shuttle (1981–2011):** Reusable spacecraft. Built ISS, deployed Hubble, 135 missions.

**Voyager (1977–present):**
- Voyager 1 and 2 launched in 1977 to study the outer planets
- Voyager 1 is the **farthest human-made object from Earth** (over 24 billion km, in interstellar space since 2012)
- Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited all four giant planets (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)
- Both carry the **Golden Record** — a phonograph record with sounds and images of Earth for any extraterrestrial finders

**Hubble Space Telescope (1990–present):** Orbits at ~540 km. 2.4 m mirror. Revolutionised astronomy with deep field images.

**Mars Rovers:**
- Sojourner (1997, Pathfinder mission): First Mars rover
- Spirit (2004–2010) and Opportunity (2004–2018): Twin rovers. Opportunity lasted 14 years!
- Curiosity (2012–present): Car-sized rover in Gale Crater, found evidence of ancient water
- Perseverance (2021–present): Jezero Crater, searching for ancient microbial life, collecting samples for future return to Earth
- **Ingenuity** (2021–2024): First helicopter to fly on another planet! Made 72 flights on Mars

**James Webb Space Telescope (JWST, 2021–present):**
- Launched December 25, 2021 (joint NASA/ESA/CSA)
- 6.5 m gold-coated beryllium mirror (18 hexagonal segments)
- Infrared telescope at Sun-Earth **Lagrange Point L2** (~1.5 million km from Earth)
- Designed to see the earliest galaxies, study exoplanet atmospheres, star formation

**Artemis Programme (2022–present):** Return to the Moon (see Topic 43).

### Key Facts
- NASA was founded on July 29, 1958
- Apollo 11 (July 20, 1969): first Moon landing
- Voyager 1 is the farthest human-made object (interstellar space since 2012)
- Hubble (1990) and JWST (2021) are NASA's most famous space telescopes
- Mars rovers: Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance
- Ingenuity: first helicopter on another planet (72 flights on Mars)
- JWST is at Lagrange Point L2, about 1.5 million km from Earth
- KSC (Florida) for launches, JSC (Houston) for mission control, JPL (California) for robotic missions

### Important Terms
- **NACA**: National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NASA's predecessor)
- **KSC**: Kennedy Space Center (Florida)
- **JSC**: Johnson Space Center (Houston)
- **JPL**: Jet Propulsion Laboratory (California)
- **SLS**: Space Launch System (Artemis rocket)
- **Golden Record**: Sounds and images of Earth on Voyager 1 and 2

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| NASA was founded during the Space Race | NASA was founded in 1958, BEFORE the main Space Race milestones. It was CREATED to compete with the Soviets |
| JPL is part of NASA | JPL is managed by Caltech (a private university) on behalf of NASA — a unique arrangement |
| Hubble and JWST are similar | Hubble is in LEO (optical/UV), JWST is at L2 (infrared). JWST's mirror is 6.5 m vs Hubble's 2.4 m |
| All Mars rovers are still working | Only Curiosity and Perseverance are active. Sojourner, Spirit, and Opportunity have ended their missions |

## Topic 45: International Space Agencies (ESA, Roscosmos, CNSA, JAXA)

### Learn This Topic

#### European Space Agency (ESA)
- **Founded**: 1975
- **Headquarters**: Paris, France
- **Members**: 22 member states
- **Launch site**: Guiana Space Centre, Kourou, French Guiana (near equator, ~5°N — excellent for launches)
- **Key rockets**: Ariane 5 (retired 2023 after 117 flights), **Ariane 6** (first flight 2024), Vega

**Major ESA Missions:**
- **Rosetta** (2004–2016): Orbited comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko. Its lander **Philae** made the first comet landing (November 12, 2014)
- **Huygens** (2005): Landed on Saturn's moon **Titan** — most distant landing ever achieved
- **Gaia** (2013–present): Mapping positions of ~2 billion stars with unprecedented precision
- **Mars Express** (2003–present): Mars orbiter, confirmed water ice at Mars poles
- **ExoMars**: Programme with Roscosmos (complicated by Russia-Ukraine conflict). Rosalind Franklin rover targeting 2028 launch
- **JUICE** (2023): Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer, en route to study Ganymede, Callisto, Europa
- **Columbus**: ESA's laboratory module on ISS
- **Euclid** (2023): Space telescope studying dark energy and dark matter
- **JWST**: ESA is a partner (provided NIRSpec instrument and launch on Ariane 5)

#### Roscosmos (Russia)
- **Founded**: 1992 (successor to the Soviet space programme)
- **Headquarters**: Moscow
- **Launch site**: **Baikonur Cosmodrome**, Kazakhstan (world's first and largest space launch facility, leased from Kazakhstan) and Vostochny Cosmodrome (new, in Russia's Far East)

**Key Achievements:**
- Soviet Union achieved most early "firsts" (Sputnik, Gagarin, Tereshkova, Leonov, first space station)
- **Soyuz** spacecraft: Workhorse of human spaceflight since 1967. Used to ferry crews to ISS for decades. Most-launched rocket family in history
- **Proton** rocket: Heavy-lift vehicle for GEO satellites and space station modules
- **Mir** space station (1986–2001)
- **Luna programme**: Robotic Moon missions including first soft landing (Luna 9, 1966), first sample return (Luna 16, 1970)
- **Venera programme**: Multiple Venus landers — first to land on another planet (Venera 7, 1970), first photos from Venus surface (Venera 9, 1975)
- Provided ISS modules (Zarya, Zvezda) and crew transport for many years

#### China National Space Administration (CNSA)
- **Founded**: 1993
- **Headquarters**: Beijing
- **Launch sites**: Jiuquan, Taiyuan, Xichang, Wenchang (newest, for heavy-lift)
- **Key rockets**: Long March family (Long March 5 is the heavy-lift vehicle)

**Major Achievements:**
- **Shenzhou** (crewed spacecraft): Yang Liwei first Chinese in space (2003, Shenzhou 5)
- **Tiangong Space Station** (2021–present): 3-module station, permanently crewed
- **Chang'e Programme** (Moon):
  - Chang'e 3 (2013): First Chinese lunar lander and Yutu rover
  - Chang'e 4 (2019): **First-ever landing on the far side of the Moon!** With Yutu-2 rover
  - Chang'e 5 (2020): Lunar sample return (~1.73 kg from near side)
  - Chang'e 6 (2024): **First-ever sample return from the far side of the Moon!** (~1.9 kg)
- **Tianwen-1** (2021): Mars orbiter, lander, and **Zhurong rover** — China's first Mars landing
- **BeiDou**: China's global navigation satellite system (like GPS)
- Plans for a permanent lunar base (International Lunar Research Station, with Russia and others)

#### Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA)
- **Founded**: 2003 (merged three predecessors)
- **Headquarters**: Tokyo
- **Launch site**: Tanegashima Space Center
- **Key rockets**: H-IIA, H3 (new generation, first successful flight 2024)

**Major Missions:**
- **Hayabusa** (2003–2010): First mission to return samples from an asteroid (25143 Itokawa)
- **Hayabusa2** (2014–2020): Returned samples from asteroid **Ryugu** (162173). Landed multiple times, used an impactor to create an artificial crater!
- **Kaguya/SELENE** (2007): Lunar orbiter
- **Akatsuki** (2010/2015): Venus orbiter (entered orbit on second attempt)
- **SLIM** (January 2024): **Smart Lander for Investigating Moon** — Japan became the **5th country** to soft-land on the Moon. Landed upside down but still operated! Achieved precision landing within 55 m of target
- **Kibo**: JAXA's laboratory module on ISS (largest single ISS module)
- **MMX** (planned): Mars Moons eXploration mission to Phobos (sample return)

#### Other Notable Space Agencies

| Agency | Country | Notable Achievements |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| **CSA** (Canadian Space Agency) | Canada | Canadarm/Canadarm2 on ISS (robotic arm), Radarsat, Jeremy Hansen on Artemis II |
| **KARI/KASA** (Korea) | South Korea | Nuri rocket (KSLV-II), Danuri lunar orbiter (2022) |
| **ASI** (Italian Space Agency) | Italy | Contributed to ISS (Columbus, Cupola, PMMs), Vega rocket |
| **CNES** (France) | France | Ariane rocket programme (through ESA), many scientific missions, joint missions with ISRO (Megha-Tropiques, SARAL) |
| **DLR** (Germany) | Germany | Contributed to many ESA missions, Philae lander operations |
| **UAE Space Agency** | UAE | Hope/Al-Amal Mars orbiter (2021), Rashid rover on Moon |

### Key Facts
- ESA (22 members, HQ Paris) launches from French Guiana; Rosetta was its landmark comet mission
- Roscosmos (Russia): Soyuz is the most-launched rocket family; Soviet Union achieved most early space "firsts"
- CNSA (China): Chang'e 4 was the first far-side Moon landing (2019); Tiangong is China's space station
- JAXA (Japan): Hayabusa missions returned asteroid samples; SLIM achieved precision Moon landing (2024)
- China's Chang'e 6 (2024) returned the first samples from the Moon's far side
- Baikonur Cosmodrome (Kazakhstan) is the world's first and largest space launch facility
- ESA launches from near the equator in French Guiana for maximum rotational advantage

### Important Terms
- **ESA**: European Space Agency
- **Roscosmos**: Russian Federal Space Agency
- **CNSA**: China National Space Administration
- **JAXA**: Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency
- **Artemis Accords**: US-led international agreements for civil space exploration (signed by 40+ nations)
- **ILRS**: International Lunar Research Station (China-Russia led)

### Common Confusions
| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| ESA is one country | ESA has 22 member countries. It's not a single nation's agency |
| Russia invented the space shuttle | The USSR built Buran (one uncrewed flight, 1988). The reusable Space Shuttle was NASA's. Soyuz is Russia's workhorse |
| China copied ISS | Tiangong is independently designed and built. It uses different architecture (self-contained modules) |
| JAXA is new to space | Japan has been in space since the 1970s. JAXA was formed in 2003 by merging three older agencies |
| Only 3 countries have landed on the Moon | As of 2024: USSR, USA, China, India, and Japan have all achieved soft landings |
