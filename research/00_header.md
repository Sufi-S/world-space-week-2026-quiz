## About the Event

World Space Week (WSW) is an international celebration of science and technology and their contribution to the betterment of the human condition, observed every year from **October 4 to October 10**. These dates commemorate two landmark events in space history:

- **October 4, 1957** -- Launch of *Sputnik 1*, the first human-made satellite, by the Soviet Union.
- **October 10, 1967** -- Signing of the *Treaty on Principles Governing the Activities of States in the Exploration and Use of Outer Space* (the Outer Space Treaty).

The **World Space Week 2026** celebrations are being organized by the **U R Rao Satellite Centre (URSC)**, Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), Bengaluru, under the **Department of Space, Government of India**. URSC is India's premier centre for the design, development, fabrication, and testing of all Indian satellites.

### Competitions

The event features the following competitions for school students:

1. **Quiz Competition** -- Test your knowledge of space science, ISRO missions, and astronomy.
2. **Memory Competition** -- Demonstrate your recall ability on space-related topics.
3. **Pick and Speak** -- Impromptu speaking on space-related topics drawn at random.
4. **Drawing / Spot Painting** -- Express your vision of space through art.

Each school can send **10 students**, out of whom **7 are nominated** across the four events (3 for Quiz + 1 for Memory + 1 for Pick and Speak + 2 for Drawing/Spot Painting).

The event also features an **exhibition of satellite and launch vehicle models**, along with posters and videos showcasing India's space achievements.

## How to Use This Book

This document is your complete preparation guide for the Quiz Competition at World Space Week 2026. It is organized into clear, self-contained topic chapters so you can study systematically. Here is how to get the most out of it:

### Step 1: Read the "Must-Know Space Facts" Below

Start with the quick-reference fact list in the next section. These are the highest-value facts that appear most frequently in space quizzes. Read through them once, then come back to them every day before the competition.

### Step 2: Study One Topic at a Time

Each topic chapter has a **"Learn"** section that explains the subject clearly, with key facts, dates, names, and numbers highlighted. Read the Learn section carefully. Do not rush -- understanding is more important than memorization.

### Step 3: Attempt the Questions

After studying each Learn section, attempt the **questions** that follow. These include:

- **Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)** -- Pick the correct option.
- **True or False** -- Decide if the statement is correct.
- **Fill in the Blanks** -- Recall the missing word or number.
- **Short Answer Questions** -- Write a brief answer in your own words.
- **Match the Following** -- Pair items from two columns.

Try to answer without looking back at the Learn section first. This tests your actual recall.

### Step 4: Check Your Answers

Each chapter includes an **answer key**. Check your answers honestly. For any question you got wrong, go back and re-read the relevant part of the Learn section.

### Step 5: Use the Revision Sections

Before the competition, use the **Must-Know Space Facts** list and the **revision summaries** at the end of each chapter for a final review. Focus on areas where you made mistakes during practice.

### Tips for Quiz Day

- **Read each question carefully** before answering. Many wrong answers come from misreading the question.
- **Pay attention to specific numbers** -- dates, distances, speeds, and altitudes are common quiz questions.
- **Remember Indian achievements** -- ISRO missions, Indian satellites, and Indian space scientists are likely to be heavily featured, especially given that URSC is organizing the event.
- **Know the theme** -- "Rocket Revolution" means extra questions on rockets, launch vehicles, and propulsion are very likely.
- **Stay calm and confident** -- you have prepared well!

## Must-Know Space Facts

This is your high-value revision list. Memorize these facts -- they cover the most commonly asked topics in space quizzes.

### The Eight Planets (in order from the Sun)

| # | Planet | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Mercury** | Smallest planet in the Solar System; closest to the Sun |
| 2 | **Venus** | Hottest planet (surface ~465 degrees C); rotates backwards (retrograde) |
| 3 | **Earth** | Only known planet with life; 71% surface covered by water |
| 4 | **Mars** | Called the Red Planet (iron oxide surface); has the tallest volcano -- Olympus Mons |
| 5 | **Jupiter** | Largest planet; has the Great Red Spot (a giant storm) |
| 6 | **Saturn** | Known for its spectacular ring system; least dense planet (would float on water) |
| 7 | **Uranus** | Rotates on its side (axial tilt ~98 degrees); an ice giant |
| 8 | **Neptune** | Farthest planet from the Sun; has the strongest winds in the Solar System |

*Mnemonic: **M**y **V**ery **E**ducated **M**other **J**ust **S**erved **U**s **N**oodles*

### ISRO -- Key Facts

- **Founded:** August 15, 1969 (Indian Independence Day)
- **Founder:** Dr. Vikram Sarabhai (Father of the Indian Space Programme)
- **Headquarters:** Bengaluru, Karnataka
- **Parent body:** Department of Space, Government of India

### Key ISRO Chairmen

| Chairman | Notable Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Dr. Vikram Sarabhai** (1963--1971) | Founded the Indian space programme; established Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station (TERLS) |
| **Prof. Satish Dhawan** (1972--1984) | Oversaw the launch of India's first satellite Aryabhata and development of SLV-3 |
| **Prof. U R Rao** (1984--1994) | Spearheaded India's satellite programme; URSC is named after him |
| **Dr. K. Kasturirangan** (1994--2003) | Led ISRO during IRS and INSAT satellite expansion |
| **Shri V. Narayanan** (Current) | Leading ISRO through Gaganyaan and next-generation missions |

### Key ISRO Mission Dates

| Mission | Date / Period | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Aryabhata** | April 19, 1975 | India's first satellite (launched by Soviet Union) |
| **Chandrayaan-1** | October 22, 2008 | India's first Moon mission; discovered water molecules on the Moon |
| **Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan)** | Launched: November 5, 2013; Mars orbit: September 24, 2014 | India's first interplanetary mission; cheapest Mars mission (~$73 million); India became the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit |
| **Chandrayaan-3** | Launched: July 14, 2023; Landed: August 23, 2023 | Successful soft landing on the lunar south pole; India became the 4th country to soft-land on the Moon |
| **Aditya-L1** | Launched: September 2, 2023 | India's first solar observatory mission; positioned at Sun-Earth Lagrange Point 1 |
| **SpaDeX** | December 30, 2024 | Space Docking Experiment -- India's first in-orbit docking mission |
| **Gaganyaan** | Upcoming | India's first human spaceflight programme |

### Key Indian Launch Vehicles

| Vehicle | Type | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| **PSLV** (Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle) | Workhorse of ISRO | Most reliable; launched Chandrayaan-1, Mangalyaan, and 100+ foreign satellites |
| **GSLV** (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle) | Uses cryogenic upper stage | Designed for heavier communication satellites in geosynchronous orbit |
| **LVM3** (Launch Vehicle Mark-3) | Heavy-lift vehicle | India's heaviest rocket; launched Chandrayaan-3; will launch Gaganyaan |
