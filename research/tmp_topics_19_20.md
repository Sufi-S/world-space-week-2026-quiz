## Topic 19 – Gravity, Escape Velocity and Microgravity

### Learn This Topic

**Simple Explanation:**
Gravity is the invisible force that pulls every object with mass toward every other object with mass. It is why apples fall from trees, why the Moon orbits Earth, and why Earth orbits the Sun. The stronger an object's mass and the closer you are to it, the stronger the gravitational pull you feel.

Escape velocity is the minimum speed an object needs to break free from a body's gravitational pull without any further thrust. If a rocket reaches escape velocity, it can leave Earth and travel into space without needing to keep firing its engines.

Microgravity is the condition experienced in orbit where everything appears weightless. Astronauts on the International Space Station (ISS) float not because there is no gravity, but because they and the station are in continuous free fall around Earth.

**Key Concepts:**

1. **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:** Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force given by F = G × m1 × m2 / r². Here, G is the gravitational constant, m1 and m2 are the two masses, and r is the distance between their centres.

2. **Gravitational Constant (G):** G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg². This is a universal constant — it is the same everywhere in the universe.

3. **Acceleration due to Gravity (g):** On Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.8 m/s². On the Moon, g ≈ 1.62 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth). On Mars, g ≈ 3.7 m/s².

4. **Escape Velocity:** The formula is v = √(2GM/r), where M is the mass of the body and r is its radius. It depends only on the mass and radius of the body you are escaping from — not on the mass of the escaping object. A marble and a rocket need the same escape velocity from Earth.

5. **Escape Velocities of Different Bodies:**
   - Earth: 11.186 km/s
   - Moon: 2.38 km/s
   - Mars: 5.03 km/s
   - Jupiter: 60.2 km/s
   - Sun: 617.7 km/s

6. **Relationship Between Orbital and Escape Velocity:** Orbital velocity ≈ Escape velocity / √2. For Earth's surface, orbital velocity is about 7.9 km/s while escape velocity is about 11.2 km/s.

7. **Microgravity:** In orbit, gravity is still present (at ISS altitude of ~400 km, gravity is about 90% of surface value). Astronauts feel weightless because they are in continuous free fall — both the astronaut and the spacecraft fall toward Earth at the same rate while moving forward fast enough to keep missing it.

8. **Tidal Forces:** These occur because gravity is stronger on the side of an object closer to a massive body and weaker on the far side. The Moon causes tides on Earth through this differential gravitational pull.

9. **Einstein's General Relativity:** Einstein explained gravity not as a force but as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. Massive objects bend the fabric of spacetime, and other objects follow curved paths through this bent spacetime.

10. **Gravitational Waves:** Ripples in spacetime caused by accelerating massive objects. First directly detected by LIGO (Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory) on September 14, 2015, from two merging black holes about 1.3 billion light years away.

**Examples:**
- If you weigh 60 kg on Earth, you would weigh only about 10 kg on the Moon because the Moon's gravity is 1/6 of Earth's.
- A ball thrown upward at less than 11.2 km/s will come back to Earth; thrown at or above that speed, it escapes Earth's gravity.
- Astronauts on the ISS experience about 0.9g but feel weightless because they are in free fall.

**Terminology:**
- **Gravitational field:** The region around a mass where another mass experiences gravitational force.
- **Weight vs Mass:** Mass is the amount of matter (constant everywhere); weight is the force of gravity on that mass (changes with location).
- **Free fall:** Motion under gravity alone with no other forces acting.
- **Zero-g:** A common term for microgravity conditions, though technically inaccurate.

**Common Mistakes:**
- Thinking there is zero gravity in space — gravity extends throughout the universe; it just gets weaker with distance.
- Confusing mass and weight — your mass is the same on Earth and Moon, but your weight changes.
- Believing escape velocity depends on the mass of the escaping object — it does not.
- Thinking astronauts float because they are far from Earth — at ISS altitude, gravity is still ~90% of surface gravity.
- Confusing orbital velocity with escape velocity — orbital velocity is lower (by a factor of √2).

**Remember This:**
- "F = G m1 m2 / r²" — The universal law of gravitation.
- Escape velocity = √(2GM/r) — only depends on the body you're escaping, not your own mass.
- Earth's escape velocity: 11.2 km/s (remember "11-2" like a date).
- Moon gravity = 1/6 of Earth; Mars gravity ≈ 1/3 of Earth.
- ISS astronauts are weightless because of free fall, NOT because of zero gravity.
- LIGO detected gravitational waves in 2015 — Nobel Prize 2017.

### Important Terms
- **Gravitation:** The attractive force between any two masses in the universe.
- **Gravitational constant (G):** The proportionality constant in Newton's law of gravitation; G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg².
- **Acceleration due to gravity (g):** The acceleration experienced by an object due to gravitational pull at a planet's surface.
- **Escape velocity:** The minimum speed needed for an object to escape a body's gravitational field without further propulsion.
- **Orbital velocity:** The speed needed to maintain a stable orbit around a body.
- **Free fall:** Motion under the influence of gravity alone, with no other forces.
- **Microgravity:** A condition of very weak apparent gravity, as experienced in orbit.
- **Weight:** The force exerted on a mass by gravity (W = mg); varies with location.
- **Mass:** The amount of matter in an object; remains constant regardless of location.
- **Tidal force:** The differential gravitational pull on different parts of an extended body.
- **Spacetime curvature:** The bending of the fabric of space and time by mass and energy, as described by general relativity.
- **Gravitational waves:** Ripples in spacetime caused by the acceleration of massive objects.
- **LIGO:** Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory; the instrument that first detected gravitational waves.
- **General relativity:** Einstein's theory describing gravity as the geometry of spacetime.

### Common Confusions

| Confusion | Clarification |
|-----------|---------------|
| "There is no gravity in space" | Gravity exists everywhere; astronauts float because they are in free fall, not because gravity is absent |
| "Heavier objects need higher escape velocity" | Escape velocity depends only on the mass and radius of the body being escaped, not the escaping object's mass |
| "Weight and mass are the same" | Mass is constant; weight depends on local gravity (W = mg) |
| "Orbital velocity = escape velocity" | Orbital velocity ≈ escape velocity / √2; they are related but different |
| "Astronauts are weightless because they are far from Earth" | At ISS altitude (~400 km), gravity is about 90% of surface gravity; weightlessness comes from free fall |
| "Gravity is only a force (Newton)" | Einstein showed gravity is better understood as the curvature of spacetime |
| "Gravitational waves are sound waves" | Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime itself; they are completely different from sound |

## Topic 20 – History of Space Exploration – Key Milestones

### Learn This Topic

**Simple Explanation:**
The history of space exploration is one of humanity's greatest adventure stories. It began during the Cold War between the USA and the USSR (Soviet Union), when both nations raced to prove their technological superiority. From the first artificial satellite in 1957 to rovers on Mars and private spaceships today, space exploration has grown tremendously. India has also made remarkable contributions, including the successful Chandrayaan-3 lunar landing in 2023.

**Key Concepts:**

1. **The Space Race (1957-1975):** A competition between the USA and USSR that drove rapid advances in space technology. It began with Sputnik and effectively ended with the Apollo-Soyuz Test Project in 1975, when the two nations cooperated in space for the first time.

2. **Sputnik 1 (October 4, 1957):** The world's first artificial satellite, launched by the Soviet Union. It was a small metal sphere (58 cm diameter) that orbited Earth every 96 minutes, transmitting radio signals. This event shocked the world and started the Space Age.

3. **Laika (November 3, 1957):** A stray dog from Moscow who became the first animal to orbit Earth aboard Sputnik 2. Sadly, Laika did not survive the mission, but her flight proved that living beings could survive launch and weightlessness.

4. **Yuri Gagarin (April 12, 1961):** Soviet cosmonaut who became the first human in space aboard Vostok 1. His flight lasted 108 minutes and completed one orbit around Earth. April 12 is now celebrated as International Day of Human Space Flight.

5. **John Glenn (February 20, 1962):** The first American to orbit Earth, completing three orbits aboard Friendship 7 (Mercury-Atlas 6 mission).

6. **Valentina Tereshkova (June 16, 1963):** Soviet cosmonaut who became the first woman in space aboard Vostok 6. She orbited Earth 48 times over nearly three days.

7. **Alexei Leonov (March 18, 1965):** Soviet cosmonaut who performed the first spacewalk (EVA — Extra Vehicular Activity), spending about 12 minutes outside the Voskhod 2 spacecraft.

8. **Luna 9 (February 3, 1966):** Soviet spacecraft that achieved the first soft landing on the Moon, proving that the lunar surface could support a lander (it did not sink into dust as some had feared).

9. **Apollo 11 (July 20, 1969):** NASA mission in which Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the Moon. Armstrong's famous words: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." Michael Collins orbited the Moon in the command module.

10. **Apollo 13 (April 1970):** Known as a "successful failure" — an oxygen tank exploded en route to the Moon, but the crew (Jim Lovell, Jack Swigert, Fred Haise) survived and returned safely thanks to brilliant improvisation by astronauts and ground controllers.

11. **Salyut 1 (1971):** The world's first space station, launched by the Soviet Union.

12. **Apollo-Soyuz Test Project (1975):** The first joint US-USSR space mission, where an Apollo spacecraft docked with a Soyuz spacecraft in orbit. It marked the end of the Space Race and the beginning of international cooperation in space.

13. **Space Shuttle Era (1981-2011):** NASA's Space Shuttle Columbia made the first flight on April 12, 1981. The shuttle was the first reusable spacecraft. Five orbiters were built: Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis, and Endeavour. Two were lost in disasters: Challenger (1986) and Columbia (2003).

14. **Challenger Disaster (January 28, 1986):** The Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch, killing all seven crew members including teacher Christa McAuliffe.

15. **Mir Space Station (1986-2001):** Soviet/Russian space station that operated for 15 years and hosted many international crews.

16. **Hubble Space Telescope (1990):** Launched into orbit; initially had a flawed mirror that was corrected in 1993. Has provided stunning images and revolutionary scientific discoveries for over 30 years.

17. **International Space Station (ISS):** Construction began in 1998 with collaboration between the USA, Russia, Europe, Japan, and Canada. It has been continuously occupied since November 2000.

18. **Columbia Disaster (February 1, 2003):** Space Shuttle Columbia broke apart during re-entry, killing all seven crew members. The cause was damage to the heat shield from insulation foam during launch.

19. **China's First Crewed Flight (2003):** Yang Liwei became China's first person in space aboard Shenzhou 5, making China the third country to independently send humans to space.

20. **SpaceShipOne (2004):** The first privately funded spacecraft to reach space, winning the Ansari X Prize.

21. **Curiosity Rover (August 6, 2012):** NASA's car-sized rover landed on Mars using a daring "sky crane" method. It has been studying Mars's geology and climate for over a decade.

22. **New Horizons at Pluto (July 14, 2015):** NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew past Pluto, providing the first close-up images of the dwarf planet and its moons.

23. **LIGO Detects Gravitational Waves (2015):** First direct detection of gravitational waves, confirming Einstein's 1915 prediction.

24. **First Black Hole Image (April 10, 2019):** The Event Horizon Telescope captured the first-ever image of a black hole — M87*, at the center of galaxy M87.

25. **Ingenuity Helicopter (2021):** The first powered flight on another planet — a small helicopter that flew on Mars alongside the Perseverance rover.

26. **James Webb Space Telescope (December 25, 2021):** Launched on Christmas Day 2021; the most powerful space telescope ever built, observing the universe in infrared.

27. **Chandrayaan-3 (August 23, 2023):** India's ISRO successfully landed on the Moon's south polar region, making India the fourth country to soft-land on the Moon and the first to land near the south pole.

**Terminology:**
- **Cosmonaut:** Russian term for a space traveller.
- **Astronaut:** Term used by NASA and other Western agencies for a space traveller.
- **EVA (Extra Vehicular Activity):** A spacewalk — any activity done by an astronaut outside a spacecraft.
- **Soft landing:** A controlled landing on a surface at low speed, as opposed to a crash.
- **Space station:** A large spacecraft in orbit where people can live and work for extended periods.
- **Reusable spacecraft:** A spacecraft designed to be used for multiple missions (e.g., Space Shuttle, SpaceX Falcon 9).

**Common Mistakes:**
- Thinking Yuri Gagarin walked on the Moon — he was the first human in space (orbit), not on the Moon.
- Confusing Valentina Tereshkova with other female astronauts — she was the first woman in space (1963).
- Thinking Apollo 13 landed on the Moon — it did not; it was a "successful failure" that returned safely.
- Confusing Challenger and Columbia disasters — Challenger was 1986 (during launch), Columbia was 2003 (during re-entry).
- Thinking ISS was built all at once — construction began in 1998 and took many years.
- Mixing up Sputnik 1 (first satellite) and Sputnik 2 (first animal in orbit).

**Remember This:**
- 1957: Sputnik 1 + Laika (both Soviet); 1961: Gagarin; 1963: Tereshkova; 1969: Moon landing.
- "4-12-61" — April 12, 1961, Gagarin in space (and Apr 12, 1981, first Shuttle).
- Apollo 11 = Moon landing; Apollo 13 = "successful failure."
- Challenger = 1986 launch disaster; Columbia = 2003 re-entry disaster.
- Chandrayaan-3 = 2023, India's Moon landing success.
- First satellite → First animal → First human → First woman → First spacewalk → First Moon landing.
